Real Value of Savings After Inflation

BeginnerPersonal FinanceAlso known as: inflation and the real value of savings, purchasing power of cash, real interest rate, inflation erosion
← Real Return (Inflation-Adjusted)Rebalancing Trades →

Finds: what your savings will really buy.

When to use it: Use it to see what savings will actually buy later. When inflation is above the interest rate the real rate is negative and the balance grows on paper but shrinks in purchasing power. Cash at zero interest loses about the inflation rate each year, which is why long-term savings are usually invested, accepting risk, not left in cash.

The formula

Real value\displaystyle \text{Real value}=B(1+i)n(1+π)n\displaystyle =\frac{B(1+i)^{n}}{(1+\pi)^{n}}rreal\displaystyle r_{real}=1+i1+π−1\displaystyle =\frac{1+i}{1+\pi}-1

In words: Real value = Balance x (1 + interest rate)^n / (1 + inflation)^n; Real interest rate = (1 + interest rate) / (1 + inflation) − 1

What each symbol means

BStarting balancemoney
iInterest rate earned on the savingsper year
piInflation rateper year
nNumber of yearsyears

Worked example

You hold 10,000 in a savings account paying 2% a year while inflation runs at 4%. What is it worth in today's money after 10 years?

Nominal balance10,000 × (1 + 0.02)^1012,189.94
Real value12,189.9 ÷ (1 + 0.04)^108,235.09
Real interest rate(1 + 0.02) ÷ (1 + 0.04) − 1−1.92%
Real value if kept as cash earning 0%10,000 ÷ (1 + 0.04)^106,755.64
Years for prices to doubleln(2) ÷ ln(1 + 0.04)17.7
The answer

After 10 years the account holds 12,189.94 in nominal terms, but in today's money that is only 8,235.09. The real interest rate is −1.92%. If the money earned nothing, it would be worth 6,755.64 in today's money, and prices would double in about 17.7 years.
