Interest Rate Cap and Floor Payoffs

Intermediate

Also known as: caplet, floorlet, interest rate cap, interest rate floor, collar on rates

Payments from an interest rate cap and floor. Use it to see what a cap or floor pays. A cap is a series of call options on a rate, so a borrower with floating-rate debt uses it to limit the interest cost. A floor is a series of put options on a rate. The payment is made at the end of each period, based on the rate set at its start.

The formula

Caplet=N τ max⁡(L−K,0),Floorlet=N τ max⁡(K−L,0)\text{Caplet}=N\,\tau\,\max(L-K,0),\quad \text{Floorlet}=N\,\tau\,\max(K-L,0)

Caplet payment = Notional x Accrual fraction x max(Floating rate - Cap rate, 0); Floorlet payment = Notional x Accrual fraction x max(Floor rate - Floating rate, 0); a cap or floor is the sum of its caplets or floorlets

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
NNotionalmoney
tauAccrual fraction of a period (0.25 for quarterly)years
LFloating rate set at the start of each periodper year
KCap or floor strike rateper year

Worked example

A cap and a floor both have a strike of 4% on a notional of 10,000,000 with quarterly resets. The floating rates set for four quarters are 3.5%, 4.2%, 4.8% and 3.9%. What do they pay?

  1. Caplet payments= 0.00, 5,000.00, 20,000.00, 0.00
  2. Floorlet payments= 12,500.00, 0.00, 0.00, 2,500.00
  3. Total cap payment= 25,000.00
  4. Total floor payment= 15,000.00
  5. Cap minus floor = notional x tau x sum(L - K)capsum-15000= 10,000.00

The cap pays 25,000.00 in total, from the quarters where the rate was above 4%. The floor pays 15,000.00, from the quarters below 4%. The cap and floor payments together equal the payments of a swap that pays 4% fixed, less the floating rate: 10,000.00.
