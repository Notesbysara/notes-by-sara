Interest Rate Cap and Floor Payoffs

IntermediateDerivativesAlso known as: caplet, floorlet, interest rate cap, interest rate floor, collar on rates
← Interest Rate Build-UpInterest Rate Swap: Fair Fixed Rate →

Finds: payments from an interest rate cap and floor.

When to use it: Use it to see what a cap or floor pays. A cap is a series of call options on a rate, so a borrower with floating-rate debt uses it to limit the interest cost. A floor is a series of put options on a rate. The payment is made at the end of each period, based on the rate set at its start.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Caplet\displaystyle \text{Caplet}=N τ max⁡(L−K,0)\displaystyle =N\,\tau\,\max(L-K,0)Floorlet\displaystyle \text{Floorlet}=N τ max⁡(K−L,0)\displaystyle =N\,\tau\,\max(K-L,0)

In words: Caplet payment = Notional x Accrual fraction x max(Floating rate − Cap rate, 0); Floorlet payment = Notional x Accrual fraction x max(Floor rate − Floating rate, 0); a cap or floor is the sum of its caplets or floorlets

What each symbol means

NNotionalmoney
tauAccrual fraction of a period (0.25 for quarterly)years
LFloating rate set at the start of each periodper year
KCap or floor strike rateper year

Worked example

A cap and a floor both have a strike of 4% on a notional of 10,000,000 with quarterly resets. The floating rates set for four quarters are 3.5%, 4.2%, 4.8% and 3.9%. What do they pay?

Caplet payments0, 5,000, 20,000, 0
Floorlet payments12,500, 0, 0, 2,500
Total cap payment25,000
Total floor payment15,000
Cap minus floorcapsum − 15,00010,000
The answer

The cap pays 25,000 in total, from the quarters where the rate was above 4%. The floor pays 15,000, from the quarters below 4%. The cap and floor payments together equal the payments of a swap that pays 4% fixed, less the floating rate: 10,000.
