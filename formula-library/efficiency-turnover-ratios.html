Efficiency Ratios: Asset, Inventory, Receivable and Payable Turnover

Beginner

Also known as: asset turnover, inventory turnover, receivables turnover, payables turnover, fixed asset turnover

How many times a year each balance is used up and replaced. Use it to see how hard a company works its assets. A rising turnover means more sales or cost per unit of the balance sheet item. Compare with past years and with peers, since normal levels vary by industry.

The formula

Turnover=FlowAverage balance\text{Turnover}=\frac{\text{Flow}}{\text{Average balance}}

Asset turnover = Revenue / Average total assets; Fixed asset turnover = Revenue / Average net fixed assets; Inventory turnover = COGS / Average inventory; Receivables turnover = Revenue / Average receivables; Payables turnover = COGS / Average payables

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
Revenue, COGSYearly flows from the income statementmoney
Average balanceAverage of the opening and closing figure on the balance sheetmoney

Worked example

A company has revenue of 1,500 and COGS of 900. Average total assets are 1,200, average fixed assets 600, average inventory 150, average receivables 200 and average payables 100.

  1. Asset turnover = revenue / average assets1500 / 1200= 1.25
  2. Fixed asset turnover1500 / 600= 2.50
  3. Inventory turnover = COGS / average inventory900 / 150= 6.00
  4. Receivables turnover = revenue / average receivables1500 / 200= 7.50
  5. Payables turnover = COGS / average payables900 / 100= 9.00

Total assets turn over 1.25 times a year, fixed assets 2.50 times, inventory 6.00 times, receivables 7.50 times and payables 9.00 times.
