Best Execution: Price Improvement and Execution Cost

Beginner

Also known as: MiFID II, best execution, price improvement, execution quality, NBBO

How good an order execution was. Use it to check execution quality, which MiFID II requires firms to monitor and publish. Getting a price better than the best quote means the client gained; paying above the mid-point is the normal cost of trading. It ignores fees, market impact for large orders and the speed of the fill.

The formula

PI=(Pask−Pexec) q (buy),Cost=(Pexec−Pmid) qPI=(P_{ask}-P_{exec})\,q\ \text{(buy)},\quad \text{Cost}=(P_{exec}-P_{mid})\,q

For a buy order: price improvement = (best offer at order time - price paid) x shares; Execution cost vs mid-point = (price paid - mid-point) x shares; Effective half-spread paid = (price paid - mid-point) / mid-point; Firms under MiFID II must show they took steps to get the best result for clients, including price, costs, speed and likelihood of execution

Worked example

A client buys 5,000 shares when the quote is 100.00 bid and 100.06 offered. The order executes at 100.04.

  1. Mid-point(100.06+100) / 2= 100.0300
  2. Price improvement vs best offer(100.06-100.04) x 5000= 100.00
  3. Cost above the mid-point(100.04-100.03) x 5000= 50.00
  4. Effective half-spread(100.04-100.03) / 100.03= 0.010%
  5. Quoted half-spread(100.06-100.03) / 100.03= 0.030%
  6. Share of the half-spread paid(100.04-100.03) / (100.06-100.03)= 33.3%

The mid-point is 100.0300. The client improved on the best offer by 100.00 and paid 50.00 above the mid-point, an effective half-spread of 0.010%. The quoted half-spread was 0.030%, so the order captured 33.3% of the quoted spread.
