Noise Trading: Share of Price Variance Explained by Fundamentals

Intermediate

Also known as: noise traders, price vs fundamental value, excess volatility, noise variance

How much price movement is not explained by value. Use it to ask how much of price movement is news about value and how much is noise from sentiment-driven trading. If prices move much more than fundamentals, noise is large, as in the excess-volatility tests. Fundamental value is estimated, so the split depends on the model, and a high noise share can also reflect errors in the value estimate.

The formula

Var(P)=Var(F)+Var(N)+2 Cov(F,N),R2=Cov(P,F)2Var(P) Var(F)Var(P)=Var(F)+Var(N)+2\,Cov(F,N),\quad R^{2}=\frac{Cov(P,F)^{2}}{Var(P)\,Var(F)}

Noise = price - fundamental value; Variance of price = variance of fundamental + variance of noise + 2 x covariance; Share of price variation explained by fundamentals (R-squared) = Cov(P, F)^2 / (Var(P) x Var(F))

Worked example

Eight periods of prices and estimated fundamental values are in the calculator.

  1. Noise = price - fundamental= 0.00, 2.00, 7.00, 10.00, 5.00, 13.00, 16.00, 10.00
  2. Variance of price= 126.50
  3. Variance of fundamentals= 40.12
  4. Variance of noise= 29.55
  5. Price variance / fundamental variance126.5 / 40.125= 3.15
  6. R-squared of price on fundamentals= 92.5%
  7. Share left to noise and other factors1-0.925397= 7.5%

Price variance is 126.50 against 40.12 for fundamentals, so prices are 3.15 times as variable. Noise has variance 29.55. Fundamentals explain 92.5% of the price variation, leaving 7.5% to noise and other factors.
