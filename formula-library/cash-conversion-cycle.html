Cash Conversion Cycle

IntermediateCorporate FinanceAlso known as: CCC, working capital cycle, DIO DSO DPO, operating cycle
← Carry Trade Return and Break-EvenCash Conversion Cycle from the Financial Statements →

Finds: days cash is tied up in operations.

When to use it: Use it to see how long cash is tied up in the business. A shorter cycle means the company needs less financing for its working capital.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

CCC\displaystyle CCC=DIO+DSO−DPO\displaystyle =DIO+DSO-DPO

In words: Cash conversion cycle = Days inventory + Days sales outstanding − Days payables

What each symbol means

CCCDays between paying suppliers and collecting from customersdays
DIODays inventory outstanding = Inventory / COGS x 365days
DSODays sales outstanding = Receivables / Sales x 365days
DPODays payables outstanding = Payables / COGS x 365days

Worked example

A company has inventory of 400, receivables of 600 and payables of 300. Its annual sales are 7,300 and its cost of goods sold is 3,650. What is the cash conversion cycle?

DIO400 ÷ 3,650 × 36540
DSO600 ÷ 7,300 × 36530
DPO300 ÷ 3,650 × 36530
CCC40 + 30 − 3040
The answer

Inventory takes 40.0 days to sell, customers take 30.0 days to pay, and the company takes 30.0 days to pay suppliers. The cash conversion cycle is 40.0 days.
