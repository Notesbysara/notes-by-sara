Operational Risk: Basic Indicator Capital and Expected Loss

Intermediate

Also known as: basic indicator approach, operational risk capital, loss frequency severity, op risk

Capital and expected loss for operational risk. Use it for a simple measure of operational risk capital. The Basic Indicator Approach ties capital to income as a rough proxy for size. Most banks also model the frequency and severity of internal loss events to find the expected annual loss.

The formula

KBIA=α×GI‾>0,EL=λ×LˉK_{BIA}=\alpha\times\overline{GI}_{>0},\quad EL=\lambda\times\bar{L}

Basic Indicator capital = 15% x Average of the positive annual gross income over three years; Expected annual operational loss = Number of loss events per year x Average loss per event

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
alphaFixed percentage of 15%percent
GIAnnual gross incomemoney
lambdaExpected number of loss events per year (frequency)number
L-barAverage loss per event (severity)money

Worked example

A bank's gross income was 800, 900 and 950 million over three years. It averages 12 operational loss events a year, with an average loss of 0.4 million.

  1. Average of the positive gross income figures= 883.3
  2. Capital = alpha x average gross income0.15 x 883.333= 132.5
  3. Expected annual loss = frequency x severity12 x 0.4= 4.80

Average positive gross income is 883.3, so Basic Indicator capital is 132.5 million. Expected annual operational loss from the frequency and severity data is 4.80 million.
