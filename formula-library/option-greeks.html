Option Greeks: Delta, Gamma, Vega, Theta, Rho

IntermediateDerivativesAlso known as: delta, gamma, vega, theta, rho, option sensitivities
← Optimal Risky Portfolio (Maximum Sharpe, Two Assets)Option Intrinsic Value and Time Value →

Finds: how an option price responds to price, time, volatility and rates.

When to use it: Use it to measure how an option's price reacts to each input. Delta is the hedge ratio, gamma shows how fast delta changes, vega shows the volatility exposure, theta is the daily cost of holding, and rho is rate sensitivity. These are for European options under Black-Scholes.

In words: Call delta = e^(-qT) x N(d1); Put delta = call delta − e^(-qT); Gamma = e^(-qT) x pdf(d1) / (S x sigma x sqrt(T)); Vega = S x e^(-qT) x pdf(d1) x sqrt(T); Call theta = [-S e^(-qT) pdf(d1) sigma / (2 sqrt(T)) − r K e^(-rT) N(d2) + q S e^(-qT) N(d1)]; Call rho = K T e^(-rT) N(d2)

Worked example

A stock is at 100 with a strike of 100, a risk-free rate of 5%, a volatility of 20%, no dividend and one year to expiry. What are the Greeks of the call?

d1(ln(100 ÷ 100) + (0.05 − 0 + 0.2^2 ÷ 2) × 1) ÷ (0.2 × sqrt(1))0.3500
d2d1 − 0.2 × sqrt(1)0.1500

The answer

The call delta is 0.6368, so the option gains about 0.6368 for each 1 rise in the stock, and the put delta is −0.3632. Gamma is 0.01876. A 1 percentage-point rise in volatility adds 0.3752 to either option. Time decay costs the call −0.0176 per day, and a 1 percentage-point rise in rates adds 0.5323 to the call.
