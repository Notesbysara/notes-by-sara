Fundamental Law of Active Management

IntermediateAdvanced Investment PracticeAlso known as: portfolio construction and evaluation, information coefficient, breadth, transfer coefficient, expected active return
← Free Cash Flow vs. Net IncomeFunded Ratio (Liability-Driven Investing) →

Finds: how much skill and breadth an active manager needs.

When to use it: Use it to see what is needed to add value. A small forecasting edge (IC = 0.05 is good) pays off if applied across many independent bets. Constraints such as no short selling lower the transfer coefficient. It does not prove skill: the same arithmetic applied to a manager's record needs enough data to separate skill from luck.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

IR\displaystyle IR=TC×IC×BR\displaystyle =TC\times IC\times\sqrt{BR}α\displaystyle \alpha=IR×TE\displaystyle =IR\times TE

In words: Information ratio (expected) = transfer coefficient x information coefficient x square root of breadth; Expected active return (alpha) = information ratio x tracking error; Information coefficient is the correlation between forecasts and outcomes; Breadth is the number of independent bets per year; Transfer coefficient is how much of the forecast makes it into the portfolio (1 = fully, below 1 with constraints); Breadth needed for a target IR = (IR / (TC x IC))^2

What each symbol means

ICInformation coefficient: correlation between predicted and actual returnsnumber
BRBreadth: independent bets per yearcount
TCTransfer coefficient: how well the portfolio reflects the forecasts after constraints and costsnumber
TETracking error: planned risk against the benchmarkpercent

Worked example

A manager has an IC of 0.05, makes 100 independent bets a year, has a transfer coefficient of 0.8 and runs 4% tracking error.

Expected information ratio0.8 × 0.05 × sqrt(100)0.40
Expected active return0.4 × 0.041.60%
IR with double the breadth0.8 × 0.05 × sqrt(2 × 100)0.57
Breadth needed for the target IR(0.75 ÷ (0.8 × 0.05))^2352
The answer

The expected information ratio is 0.40, giving an expected active return of 1.60% a year. With the same skill, doubling breadth to 200 bets raises the IR to 0.57. To reach an IR of 0.75 the manager would need 352 independent bets.
