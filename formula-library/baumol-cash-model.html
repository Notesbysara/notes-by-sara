Baumol Cash Management Model

Intermediate

Also known as: optimal cash balance, Baumol model, cash conversion size

Cash to hold and transfer. Use it to decide how much cash to hold and how often to top it up. Holding more cash costs interest you could have earned, while topping up often costs transaction fees.

The formula

C∗=2FTkC^{*}=\sqrt{\frac{2FT}{k}}

Optimal cash transfer C = square root of (2 x Fixed cost per transaction x Annual cash needs / Interest rate)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
C*Best amount of cash to raise each timemoney
FFixed cost each time you sell securities for cashmoney
TTotal cash needed over the yearmoney
kInterest rate earned on securities, as a decimalper year

Worked example

A company needs 1,000,000 of cash a year. Each sale of securities costs 50 and securities earn 5%. How much should it raise each time?

  1. C = sqrt(2 x F x T / k)sqrt(2 x 50 x 1,000,000 / 0.05)= 44,721.36
  2. Transfers per year = T / C1,000,000 / 44721.4= 22.4

The best amount to raise is 44,721.36 each time, which means 22.4 transfers a year.
