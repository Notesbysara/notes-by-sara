Model Risk: Pricing Error Metrics

Intermediate

Also known as: model validation, MAE RMSE bias, model error, calibration error

How far a model is from market prices. Use it to test a model against market prices. Bias shows if the model is consistently too high or low. RMSE penalizes large misses more than MAE does, and the largest error shows the worst case. A model that is accurate on average can still fail badly in one corner.

The formula

Bias=Pmodel−Pmkt‾,MAE=∣Pmodel−Pmkt∣‾,RMSE=(Pmodel−Pmkt)2‾Bias=\overline{P_{model}-P_{mkt}},\quad MAE=\overline{|P_{model}-P_{mkt}|},\quad RMSE=\sqrt{\overline{(P_{model}-P_{mkt})^{2}}}

Bias = average of (Model - Market); MAE = average of |Model - Market|; RMSE = square root of the average of (Model - Market)^2; Largest error = max |Model - Market|

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
P_modelPrice from the modelmoney
P_mktObserved market price or quotemoney

Worked example

A model priced six options at 10.2, 8.5, 6.9, 5.1, 3.8 and 2.6. The market prices were 10.0, 8.7, 6.8, 5.3, 3.7 and 2.5.

  1. Error = model - market= 0.2000, -0.2000, 0.1000, -0.2000, 0.1000, 0.1000
  2. Bias = average error= 0.0167
  3. MAE = average absolute error= 0.1500
  4. RMSE= 0.1581
  5. Largest absolute errormax(max([0.2, -0.2, 0.1, -0.2, 0.1, 0.1]), -min([0.2, -0.2, 0.1, -0.2, 0.1, 0.1]))= 0.2000

The bias is 0.0167, so the model prices a little high on average. The average absolute error is 0.1500, the RMSE is 0.1581 and the largest single miss is 0.2000.
