Cointegration: Hedge Ratio, Spread Z-Score and Half-Life

IntermediateQuantitative FinanceAlso known as: cointegration, Engle Granger, mean reversion of spread, half-life of mean reversion
← Coefficient of VariationCollar (Protective Put Financed by a Covered Call) →

Finds: whether a pair behaves like a stable spread and how fast it reverts.

When to use it: Use it to test whether two prices share a long-run link. Each price may wander, but if a mix of them is stable the pair is cointegrated and the gap tends to close. The half-life says how fast. A full test also needs a unit-root test on the spread (Engle-Granger), which this calculator does not run, so treat the result as a screen and not proof.

The formula

β\displaystyle \beta=Cov(Y,X)Var(X),  st=Yt−βXt−α,  st=ϕst−1+ε,  t1/2=−ln⁡2ln⁡ϕ\displaystyle =\frac{Cov(Y,X)}{Var(X)},\; s_{t}=Y_{t}-\beta X_{t}-\alpha,\; s_{t}=\phi s_{t-1}+\varepsilon,\; t_{1/2}=\frac{-\ln 2}{\ln\phi}

In words: Hedge ratio beta = Covariance(Y, X) / Variance(X); Spread = Y − beta x X − alpha; Fit spread today = phi x spread yesterday; Half-life = -ln 2 / ln(phi)

The answer

The hedge ratio is 1.3940 and the intercept is 14.7347. The latest spread sits 1.181 standard deviations from its mean. The spread's persistence is 0.1321, so a gap closes by half in 0.34 periods.
