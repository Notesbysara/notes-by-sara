Adjusted Leverage with Off-Balance-Sheet Items

IntermediateFinancial Statement AnalysisAlso known as: off balance sheet debt, lease adjusted debt, pension deficit, guarantees, debt like items
← Additional Funds Needed (Percent-of-Sales Forecast)After-Tax Cost of Debt →

Finds: debt including hidden obligations.

When to use it: Use it to include debt-like obligations that the balance sheet leaves out. Rating agencies and lenders do the same. If you add lease debt, add the rent back to earnings so both sides treat leases the same way.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Adjusted debt\displaystyle \text{Adjusted debt}=D+L+P+p×G\displaystyle =D+L+P+p\times G

In words: Adjusted debt = Reported debt + Capitalized operating leases + Pension deficit + Probability x Guarantees; Adjusted leverage = Adjusted debt / EBITDAR

What each symbol means

DDebt on the balance sheetmoney
LPresent value of lease commitments not on the balance sheetmoney
PPension deficitmoney
GGuarantees given for others' debtsmoney
pChance the guarantee will have to be paidpercent
EBITDAREBITDA before rent, so the lease cost is excluded from both sidesmoney

Worked example

Reported debt is 600. Off-balance-sheet lease commitments are worth 150, the pension deficit is 80 and the company has guaranteed 20 of a partner's debt, with a 50% chance of payment. EBITDA is 260 and rent expense is 30.

Adjusted debt600 + 150 + 80 + 0.5 × 20840
EBITDAR260 + 30290
Reported debt / EBITDA600 ÷ 2602.31
Adjusted debt / EBITDAR840 ÷ 2902.90
The answer

Adjusted debt is 840, against reported debt of 600. Leverage is 2.31 on reported figures and 2.90 after adjusting debt and adding back rent.
