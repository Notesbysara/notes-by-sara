Dual-Class Shares: Voting Power vs. Ownership

Intermediate

Also known as: dual class shares, supervoting shares, control versus ownership, wedge

Control compared with ownership. Use it to see how a founder can control a company with a small share of its value. The gap between voting power and economic ownership is a governance risk that investors watch.

The formula

Voting power=a vAa vA+b vB,Economic share=aa+b\text{Voting power}=\frac{a\,v_{A}}{a\,v_{A}+b\,v_{B}},\quad \text{Economic share}=\frac{a}{a+b}

Voting power = A shares x A votes / (A shares x A votes + B shares x B votes); Economic share = A shares / (A shares + B shares)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
aNumber of Class A shares held by the foundershares
v_AVotes per Class A sharevotes

v_BVotes per Class B sharevotes

Worked example

A founder holds 1,000,000 Class A shares with 10 votes each. The public holds 9,000,000 Class B shares with 1 vote each. What are the founder's voting power and economic share?

  1. Founder votes = A shares x A votes1,000,000 x 10= 10,000,000
  2. Public votes = B shares x B votes9,000,000 x 1= 9,000,000
  3. Voting power = founder votes / total votes10,000,000 / (10,000,000+9,000,000)= 52.63%
  4. Economic share = A shares / total shares1,000,000 / (1,000,000+9,000,000)= 10.00%
  5. Gap between voting and economic share(0.526316-0.1) x 100= 42.6

The founder owns 10.00% of the company but controls 52.63% of the votes. The gap is 42.6 points.
