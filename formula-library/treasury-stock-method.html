Treasury Stock Method (Diluted Shares)

Intermediate

Also known as: diluted EPS, stock options dilution, in the money options, TSM

Diluted share count and EPS from options. Use it to count the extra shares from stock options in diluted EPS and valuation. It assumes the exercise proceeds are used to buy back shares at the market price, so only the net new shares count. Options with a strike above the share price are ignored.

The formula

Net new shares=Nopt(1−KP)\text{Net new shares}=N_{opt}\left(1-\frac{K}{P}\right)

If options are in the money, Net new shares = Options x (1 - Strike / Share price); Diluted shares = Basic shares + Net new shares

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
N_optNumber of options outstandingnumber
KStrike (exercise) pricemoney
PCurrent share pricemoney

Worked example

A company has 20 million basic shares and 1 million options with a strike of 30. The share price is 45 and net income is 90 million.

  1. Proceeds if exercised = options x strike (0 if out of the money)1 x 30 if 45>30 else 0= 30.00
  2. Shares bought back = proceeds / price30 / 45= 0.667
  3. Net new shares = options - shares bought back (0 if out of the money)1-0.666667 if 45>30 else 0= 0.333
  4. Diluted shares = basic + net new20+0.333333= 20.333
  5. Basic EPS90 / 20= 4.50
  6. Diluted EPS90 / 20.3333= 4.43

Exercise would bring in 30.00 million, which buys back 0.667 million shares. Net new shares are 0.333 million, so diluted shares are 20.333 million. EPS falls from 4.50 to 4.43.
