Treasury Stock Method (Diluted Shares)

IntermediateFinancial Statement AnalysisAlso known as: diluted EPS, stock options dilution, in the money options, TSM
← Tranche Loss Allocation (Attachment and Detachment)Trend Analysis: Index and Growth Rates →

Finds: diluted share count and EPS from options.

When to use it: Use it to count the extra shares from stock options in diluted EPS and valuation. It assumes the exercise proceeds are used to buy back shares at the market price, so only the net new shares count. Options with a strike above the share price are ignored.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Net new shares\displaystyle \text{Net new shares}=Nopt(1−KP)\displaystyle =N_{opt}\left(1-\frac{K}{P}\right)

In words: If options are in the money, Net new shares = Options x (1 − Strike / Share price); Diluted shares = Basic shares + Net new shares

What each symbol means

N_optNumber of options outstandingnumber
KStrike (exercise) pricemoney
PCurrent share pricemoney

Worked example

A company has 20 million basic shares and 1 million options with a strike of 30. The share price is 45 and net income is 90 million.

Proceeds if exercised1 × 30 if 45>30 else 030
Shares bought back30 ÷ 450.667
Net new shares1 − 0.666667 if 45>30 else 00.333
Diluted shares20 + 0.33333320.333
Basic EPS90 ÷ 204.50
Diluted EPS90 ÷ 20.33334.43
The answer

Exercise would bring in 30 million, which buys back 0.667 million shares. Net new shares are 0.333 million, so diluted shares are 20.333 million. EPS falls from 4.50 to 4.43.
