Bitcoin Supply Schedule and Issuance Rate

Beginner

Also known as: Bitcoin halving, 21 million cap, stock-to-flow, block reward, annual inflation of supply

How many coins exist and how fast new ones arrive. Use it to see how Bitcoin's fixed schedule works: issuance halves about every four years until it approaches 21 million. Stock-to-flow is a ratio that some analysts use to compare scarcity, though it has no proven link to price. The 2024 halving cut the reward to 3.125 coins.

The formula

Rk=502k,Sh=210,000∑k=0h−1Rk,SF=Syearly issuanceR_{k}=\frac{50}{2^{k}},\quad S_{h}=210{,}000\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}R_{k},\quad SF=\frac{S}{\text{yearly issuance}}

Block reward after k halvings = 50 / 2^k coins; Each halving period lasts 210,000 blocks; Total supply after h halvings = 210,000 x sum of the rewards over those periods (the limit is 21 million); Yearly issuance = reward x 6 blocks an hour x 24 x 365 (about 52,560 blocks); Annual supply growth = yearly issuance / supply; Stock-to-flow = supply / yearly issuance

Worked example

Four halvings have taken place (the reward is 3.125 coins).

  1. Block reward = 50 / 2^h50 / 2^4= 3.1250
  2. Coins in existence = 210,000 x 50 x (2 - 2^(1 - h))210000 x 50 x (2-2^(1-4))= 19,687,500
  3. Share of 21 million19,687,500 / 21000000= 93.75%
  4. Coins issued per year (52,560 blocks)3.125 x 52560= 164,250
  5. Annual supply growth164250 / 19,687,500= 0.83%
  6. Stock-to-flow19,687,500 / 164250= 119.9

The reward is 3.1250 coins per block. About 19,687,500 coins exist, which is 93.75% of the 21 million cap. Issuance is about 164,250 coins a year, an annual supply growth of 0.83%, and stock-to-flow is 119.9.
