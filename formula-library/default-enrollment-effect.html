Default Enrollment: Effect on Retirement Savings

Beginner

Also known as: nudges and choice architecture, auto-enrollment, opt-out default, participation rate

What making saving the default is worth. Use it to see the size of a "nudge". Making saving the default, and leaving people free to opt out, raises participation sharply because inertia works in favor of saving. The calculation values a change in how a choice is presented without any change in incentives. Real programs also set default contribution rates and investments, and some people lower their own contributions when the default is low, so net effects can be smaller.

The formula

ΔN=N (pout−pin),ΔFV=ΔN×C (1+r)n−1r\Delta N=N\,(p_{out}-p_{in}),\quad \Delta FV=\Delta N\times C\,\frac{(1+r)^{n}-1}{r}

Extra participants = workers x (participation with automatic enrollment - participation with opt-in); Extra savings per year = extra participants x salary x contribution rate; Extra retirement balance = extra participants x yearly contribution x ((1 + r)^n - 1) / r

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
p_in, p_outParticipation under opt-in (people must sign up) and under opt-out (people are signed up and may leave)percent
NNumber of eligible workerscount
CYearly contribution of a participant = salary x contribution ratemoney

Worked example

A firm has 1,000 workers earning 50,000. Participation is 45% with opt-in and 90% with automatic enrollment. Participants save 5% of pay for 30 years at 5%.

  1. Extra participants = N x (p_out - p_in)1000 x (0.9-0.45)= 450
  2. Yearly contribution per participant50000 x 0.05= 2,500.00
  3. Extra savings per year450 x 2500= 1,125,000.00
  4. Value of one participant's contributions after the years2500 x ((1+0.05)^30-1) / 0.05= 166,097.12
  5. Extra retirement balance for the group450 x 166097= 74,743,703.44

Automatic enrollment adds 450 participants and 1,125,000.00 of savings each year. Each participant's contributions grow to 166,097.12 over 30 years, so the group's extra retirement balance is 74,743,703.44.
