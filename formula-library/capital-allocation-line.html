Capital Allocation Line and Optimal Risky Weight

Intermediate

Also known as: CAL, risk free and risky mix, optimal y, complete portfolio

Return, risk and best split between risky and safe assets. Use it to split money between a risky portfolio and a risk-free asset. The line shows every risk and return combination you can reach, and the optimal y picks the point that suits your risk aversion.

The formula

E(Rc)=rf+y [E(Rp)−rf],σc=y σp,y∗=E(Rp)−rfAσp2E(R_{c})=r_{f}+y\,[E(R_{p})-r_{f}],\quad \sigma_{c}=y\,\sigma_{p},\quad y^{*}=\frac{E(R_{p})-r_{f}}{A\sigma_{p}^{2}}

Complete portfolio return = rf + y x (E(Rp) - rf); risk = y x sp; optimal y = (E(Rp) - rf) / (A x sp^2)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
yShare of the money put in the risky portfolio (the rest earns the risk-free rate)percent

Worked example

The risky portfolio has an expected return of 10% and a standard deviation of 15%. The risk-free rate is 3%. If 80% goes into the risky portfolio, what are the return and risk, and what is the best share for an investor with a risk aversion of 3?

  1. Return = rf + y x (rp - rf)0.03+0.8 x (0.1-0.03)= 8.60%
  2. Risk = y x sp0.8 x 0.15= 12.00%
  3. Slope of the line = (rp - rf) / sp(0.1-0.03) / 0.15= 0.467
  4. Optimal y = (rp - rf) / (A x sp^2)(0.1-0.03) / (3 x 0.15^2)= 103.70%

An 80% allocation gives an expected return of 8.60% with a standard deviation of 12.00%. The slope of the line is 0.467. The best share for this investor is 103.70%. Above 100% means borrowing at the risk-free rate to invest more.
