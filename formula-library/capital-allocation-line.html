Capital Allocation Line and Optimal Risky Weight

IntermediateInvestments & Portfolio TheoryAlso known as: CAL, risk free and risky mix, optimal y, complete portfolio
← Callable and Putable Bond ValueCapital Controls: Reserve Requirement Implicit Tax →

Finds: return, risk and best split between risky and safe assets.

When to use it: Use it to split money between a risky portfolio and a risk-free asset. The line shows every risk and return combination you can reach, and the optimal y picks the point that suits your risk aversion.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

E(Rc)\displaystyle E(R_{c})=rf+y [E(Rp)−rf]\displaystyle =r_{f}+y\,[E(R_{p})-r_{f}]σc\displaystyle \sigma_{c}=y σp\displaystyle =y\,\sigma_{p}y∗\displaystyle y^{*}=E(Rp)−rfAσp2\displaystyle =\frac{E(R_{p})-r_{f}}{A\sigma_{p}^{2}}

In words: Complete portfolio return = rf + y x (E(Rp) − rf); risk = y x sp; optimal y = (E(Rp) − rf) / (A x sp^2)

What each symbol means

yShare of the money put in the risky portfolio (the rest earns the risk-free rate)percent
E(R_p)Expected return of the risky portfolioper year

Worked example

The risky portfolio has an expected return of 10% and a standard deviation of 15%. The risk-free rate is 3%. If 80% goes into the risky portfolio, what are the return and risk, and what is the best share for an investor with a risk aversion of 3?

Return0.03 + 0.8 × (0.1 − 0.03)8.60%
Risk0.8 × 0.1512%
Slope of the line(0.1 − 0.03) ÷ 0.150.467
Optimal y(0.1 − 0.03) ÷ (3 × 0.15^2)103.70%
The answer

An 80% allocation gives an expected return of 8.60% with a standard deviation of 12%. The slope of the line is 0.467. The best share for this investor is 103.70%. Above 100% means borrowing at the risk-free rate to invest more.
