Bank Run: Liquidity Shortfall and Fire-Sale Loss

Intermediate

Also known as: bank runs and liquidity crises, fire sale, run threshold, liquid assets coverage

How large a run a bank can survive. Use it to see why a solvent bank can fail in a run. Deposits can leave at once while loans cannot be sold quickly, so a run forces sales at a discount, which eats equity and justifies more fear. The model has two thresholds: the withdrawal level the liquid assets cover, and the level at which fire-sale losses exceed equity. Central bank lending and deposit insurance work by removing the need to sell.

The formula

Shortfall=wD−L,Loss=h1−h Shortfall,w∗=L+E(1−h)/hD\text{Shortfall}=wD-L,\quad \text{Loss}=\frac{h}{1-h}\,\text{Shortfall},\quad w^{*}=\frac{L+E(1-h)/h}{D}

Cash needed = withdrawal fraction x deposits; Shortfall = cash needed - liquid assets; Assets to sell (at book value) = shortfall / (1 - haircut); Fire-sale loss = book value sold - shortfall; Withdrawal fraction that wipes out equity = (liquid assets + equity x (1 - h) / h) / deposits

Worked example

A bank has liquid assets 150, loans 900 and equity 50, so deposits are 1,000. Depositors withdraw 30%. Loans can be sold at a 15% discount.

  1. Deposits = liquid + loans - equity150+900-50= 1,000.00
  2. Cash demanded = w x deposits0.3 x 1000= 300.00
  3. Liquid assets available150= 150.00
  4. Shortfallmax(300-150, 0)= 150.00
  5. Loans to sell (book value) = shortfall / (1 - h)150 / (1-0.15)= 176.47
  6. Fire-sale loss176.471-150= 26.47
  7. Equity after the loss50-26.4706= 23.53
  8. Run the liquid assets can cover = L / D150 / 1000= 15.0%
  9. Withdrawal share that wipes out equity(150+50 x (1-0.15) / 0.15) / 1000= 43.3%

Deposits are 1,000.00. The run demands 300.00, but only 150.00 is liquid, leaving a shortfall of 150.00. Raising that cash means selling 176.47 of loans and realizing a loss of 26.47, which leaves equity of 23.53. Liquid assets alone cover runs of up to 15.0% of deposits, and the bank is wiped out if withdrawals reach 43.3%.
