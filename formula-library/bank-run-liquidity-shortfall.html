Bank Run: Liquidity Shortfall and Fire-Sale Loss

IntermediateBanking & Financial InstitutionsAlso known as: bank runs and liquidity crises, fire sale, run threshold, liquid assets coverage
← Bank Profitability: Income Statement, ROA and ROEBanking Book Interest Rate Risk: EVE and NII →

Finds: how large a run a bank can survive.

When to use it: Use it to see why a solvent bank can fail in a run. Deposits can leave at once while loans cannot be sold quickly, so a run forces sales at a discount, which eats equity and justifies more fear. The model has two thresholds: the withdrawal level the liquid assets cover, and the level at which fire-sale losses exceed equity. Central bank lending and deposit insurance work by removing the need to sell.

The formula

Shortfall\displaystyle \text{Shortfall}=wD−L\displaystyle =wD-LLoss\displaystyle \text{Loss}=h1−h Shortfall\displaystyle =\frac{h}{1-h}\,\text{Shortfall}w∗\displaystyle w^{*}=L+E(1−h)/hD\displaystyle =\frac{L+E(1-h)/h}{D}

In words: Cash needed = withdrawal fraction x deposits; Shortfall = cash needed − liquid assets; Assets to sell (at book value) = shortfall / (1 − haircut); Fire-sale loss = book value sold − shortfall; Withdrawal fraction that wipes out equity = (liquid assets + equity x (1 − h) / h) / deposits

Worked example

A bank has liquid assets 150, loans 900 and equity 50, so deposits are 1,000. Depositors withdraw 30%. Loans can be sold at a 15% discount.

Deposits150 + 900 − 501,000
Cash demanded0.3 × 1,000300
Liquid assets available150150
Shortfallmax(300 − 150, 0)150
Loans to sell (book value)150 ÷ (1 − 0.15)176.47
Fire-sale loss176.471 − 15026.47
Equity after the loss50 − 26.470623.53
Run the liquid assets can cover150 ÷ 1,00015%
Withdrawal share that wipes out equity(150 + 50 × (1 − 0.15) ÷ 0.15) ÷ 1,00043.3%
The answer

Deposits are 1,000. The run demands 300, but only 150 is liquid, leaving a shortfall of 150. Raising that cash means selling 176.47 of loans and realizing a loss of 26.47, which leaves equity of 23.53. Liquid assets alone cover runs of up to 15.0% of deposits, and the bank is wiped out if withdrawals reach 43.3%.
