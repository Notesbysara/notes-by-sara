Price Elasticity of Demand (Midpoint)

Beginner

Also known as: elasticity, revenue effect of price change, elastic vs inelastic

How strongly quantity responds to price. Use it to see how sensitive buyers are to price. If the size of elasticity is above 1, a price rise cuts revenue because volume falls faster. Below 1 (inelastic), a price rise increases revenue. The midpoint method gives the same answer whichever direction the price moves.

The formula

Ed=(Q2−Q1)/Qˉ(P2−P1)/Pˉ,Qˉ=Q1+Q22,  Pˉ=P1+P22E_{d}=\frac{(Q_{2}-Q_{1})/\bar{Q}}{(P_{2}-P_{1})/\bar{P}},\quad \bar{Q}=\frac{Q_{1}+Q_{2}}{2},\;\bar{P}=\frac{P_{1}+P_{2}}{2}

Elasticity = (change in quantity / average quantity) / (change in price / average price). Revenue = price x quantity

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
P_1, P_2Price before and aftermoney
Q_1, Q_2Quantity demanded before and afterunits
E_dPrice elasticity of demand. Usually negative; above 1 in size means demand is elasticnumber

Worked example

A price rises from 10 to 12 and the quantity demanded falls from 100 to 88.

  1. Midpoint elasticity((88-100) / ((100+88) / 2)) / ((12-10) / ((10+12) / 2))= -0.702
  2. Revenue before10 x 100= 1,000.00
  3. Revenue after12 x 88= 1,056.00
  4. Change in revenue1056 / 1000-1= 5.6%

The elasticity is -0.702. Revenue moves from 1,000.00 to 1,056.00, a change of 5.6%. Because the size of the elasticity is below 1, demand is inelastic and the price rise increased revenue.
