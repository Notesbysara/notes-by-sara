Price Elasticity of Demand (Midpoint)

BeginnerEconomics for FinanceAlso known as: elasticity, revenue effect of price change, elastic vs inelastic
← Present Value of an Ordinary AnnuityPrice Improvement (Order Flow) →

Finds: how strongly quantity responds to price.

When to use it: Use it to see how sensitive buyers are to price. If the size of elasticity is above 1, a price rise cuts revenue because volume falls faster. Below 1 (inelastic), a price rise increases revenue. The midpoint method gives the same answer whichever direction the price moves.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Ed\displaystyle E_{d}=(Q2−Q1)/Qˉ(P2−P1)/Pˉ\displaystyle =\frac{(Q_{2}-Q_{1})/\bar{Q}}{(P_{2}-P_{1})/\bar{P}}Qˉ\displaystyle \bar{Q}=Q1+Q22,  Pˉ=P1+P22\displaystyle =\frac{Q_{1}+Q_{2}}{2},\;\bar{P}=\frac{P_{1}+P_{2}}{2}

In words: Elasticity = (change in quantity / average quantity) / (change in price / average price). Revenue = price x quantity

What each symbol means

P_1, P_2Price before and aftermoney
Q_1, Q_2Quantity demanded before and afterunits
E_dPrice elasticity of demand. Usually negative; above 1 in size means demand is elasticnumber

Worked example

A price rises from 10 to 12 and the quantity demanded falls from 100 to 88.

Midpoint elasticity((88 − 100) ÷ ((100 + 88) ÷ 2)) ÷ ((12 − 10) ÷ ((10 + 12) ÷ 2))−0.702
Revenue before10 × 1001,000
Revenue after12 × 881,056
Change in revenue1,056 ÷ 1,000 − 15.6%
The answer

The elasticity is −0.702. Revenue moves from 1,000 to 1,056, a change of 5.6%. Because the size of the elasticity is below 1, demand is inelastic and the price rise increased revenue.
