Family Office Break-Even Size

BeginnerBanking & Financial InstitutionsAlso known as: family offices, single family office, multi-family office, cost of wealth management
← Fama-French Factor Model ReturnFinancial Independence Number and the 4% Rule →

Finds: the wealth level where a family office pays for itself.

When to use it: Use it to judge when a private family office is worth running. Its cost is mostly fixed, so the cost per unit of wealth falls as wealth grows. Below the break-even size an outside provider is cheaper. Cost is not the only factor: control, privacy, and bespoke services such as tax planning and philanthropy also matter.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

AUM∗\displaystyle AUM^{*}=Ffalt−v\displaystyle =\frac{F}{f_{alt}-v}Cost%\displaystyle \text{Cost}\%=FAUM+v\displaystyle =\frac{F}{AUM}+v

In words: Break-even wealth = fixed annual cost / (fee rate of the outside alternative − variable cost rate); Cost as a share of wealth = fixed cost / wealth + variable cost rate

What each symbol means

FFixed yearly running cost: staff, systems, offices, compliancemoney
vVariable cost rate on assets (custody, advisers)percent
f_altFee rate of the alternative, such as a multi-family office or private bankpercent
AUMWealth to be managedmoney

Worked example

A single family office costs 1.2 million a year to run plus 0.15% of assets. A multi-family office charges 0.80%. The family has 300 million.

Break-even wealth1.2 ÷ (0.008 − 0.0015)184.6
Family office cost as a share of assets1.2 ÷ 300 + 0.00150.550%
Family office total cost0.0055 × 3001.65
Alternative total cost0.008 × 3002.40
Saving by running the family office2.4 − 1.650.75
The answer

The break-even wealth is 184.6 million. At 300 million, the family office costs 0.550% of assets, or 1.65 a year, against 2.40 with the alternative, saving 0.75 a year.
