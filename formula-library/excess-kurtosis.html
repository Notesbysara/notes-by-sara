Excess Kurtosis

IntermediateFinance FoundationsAlso known as: kurtosis, fat tails, fourth moment, tail risk
← Ex-Dividend Price DropExpanded Accounting Equation →

Finds: how fat the tails of returns are.

When to use it: Use it to check for fat tails, meaning extreme gains and losses that happen more often than a normal curve predicts. Positive excess kurtosis signals higher tail risk.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Excess kurtosis\displaystyle \text{Excess kurtosis}=1n∑i=1n(Ri−Rˉσ)4−3\displaystyle =\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{R_{i}-\bar{R}}{\sigma}\right)^{4}-3

In words: Excess kurtosis = average of ((Ri − mean) / standard deviation)^4, minus 3

What each symbol means

Excess kurtosisHow much fatter the tails are than a normal distribution (a normal gives 0)number
R_iReturn in period ipercent
R-barMean returnpercent
sigmaStandard deviation of returns, using n (the population version)percent
nNumber of returnsnumber

Worked example

A fund returned 10%, −5%, 15%, 2%, −12% and 8% over six periods. What is the excess kurtosis?

Excess kurtosis−1.175
The answer

The excess kurtosis is −1.175. A value below zero means thinner tails than a normal curve. With only six observations this is only a rough guide.
