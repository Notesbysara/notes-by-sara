Index Fund Tracking Difference and Error

Beginner

Also known as: index funds, passive investing, tracking error of an index fund, expense ratio drag

How closely and how cheaply a fund follows its index. Use it to compare index funds that follow the same index. The average gap is roughly the fund's annual cost, partly offset by income such as securities lending. The tracking error shows consistency. Between two funds on one index, the lower and steadier gap is the better one. It tells you nothing about whether the index itself is a good choice.

The formula

TDt=Rtfund−Rtindex,TE=σ(TD)TD_{t}=R^{fund}_{t}-R^{index}_{t},\quad TE=\sigma(TD)

Tracking difference each year = fund return - index return; Average tracking difference = mean of those differences; Tracking error = standard deviation of the differences; Cumulative gap = growth of the fund / growth of the index - 1

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
R^fundFund return after feespercent
R^indexReturn of the index it followspercent
TDTracking difference: usually close to minus the fund's costspercent
TETracking error: how much the gap variespercent

Worked example

Over five years a fund returned 10.3%, -4.7%, 21.4%, 8.2% and 14.9%. Its index returned 10.5%, -4.4%, 21.6%, 8.4% and 15.3%.

  1. Yearly differences (fund - index)= -0.20%, -0.30%, -0.20%, -0.20%, -0.40%
  2. Average tracking difference= -0.260%
  3. Tracking error (standard deviation of the differences)= 0.089%
  4. Growth of 1 in the fund= 1.5865
  5. Growth of 1 in the index= 1.6055
  6. Cumulative gap = fund growth / index growth - 11.58648 / 1.60551-1= -1.19%

The yearly gaps are -0.20%, -0.30%, -0.20%, -0.20%, -0.40%. The average tracking difference is -0.260% a year and the tracking error is 0.089%. Over five years 1 in the index grew to 1.6055 and 1 in the fund to 1.5865, a cumulative gap of -1.19%.
