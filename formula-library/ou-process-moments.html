Mean-Reverting (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck) Process Moments

IntermediateQuantitative FinanceAlso known as: stochastic processes, mean reversion, OU process, Vasicek dynamics
← MBS Pass-Through Cash Flow for One MonthMental Accounting: Cost of Separate Pots →

Finds: where a mean-reverting variable is expected to be, and how fast.

When to use it: Use it for quantities that wander but are pulled back to a normal level, such as interest rates, spreads and commodity prices. The expected value moves from the start toward the long-run mean, and the variance rises to a ceiling instead of growing forever, unlike a random walk. The half-life is the time to close half the gap.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

E[Xt]\displaystyle E[X_{t}]=θ+(x0−θ)e−κt\displaystyle =\theta+(x_{0}-\theta)e^{-\kappa t}Var[Xt]\displaystyle Var[X_{t}]=σ22κ(1−e−2κt)\displaystyle =\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\kappa}\left(1-e^{-2\kappa t}\right)

In words: Expected value at time t = Long-run mean + (Start − Long-run mean) x e^(-speed x t); Variance = sigma^2 / (2 x speed) x (1 − e^(−2 x speed x t))

What each symbol means

thetaLong-run mean the process is pulled towardnumber
kappaSpeed of mean reversionper year
sigmaVolatility of the random shocksper year
x_0Starting valuenumber

Worked example

A spread starts at 2.0 and is pulled toward 0.5 with a speed of 1.5 per year. Its volatility is 0.4. What is its distribution after 1 year, and what is the half-life of a shock?

Expected value0.5 + (2 − 0.5) × exp(−1.5 × 1)0.8347
Variance0.4^2 ÷ (2 × 1.5) × (1 − exp(−2 × 1.5 × 1))0.05068
Standard deviationsqrt(var)0.2251
Long-run standard deviation0.4 ÷ sqrt(2 × 1.5)0.2309
Half-lifeln(2) ÷ 1.50.462
The answer

After 1 year the expected value is 0.8347, with a standard deviation of 0.2251. In the long run the standard deviation settles at 0.2309. The half-life of a shock is 0.462 years.
