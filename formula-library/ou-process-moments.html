Mean-Reverting (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck) Process Moments

Intermediate

Also known as: stochastic processes, mean reversion, OU process, Vasicek dynamics

Where a mean-reverting variable is expected to be, and how fast. Use it for quantities that wander but are pulled back to a normal level, such as interest rates, spreads and commodity prices. The expected value moves from the start toward the long-run mean, and the variance rises to a ceiling instead of growing forever, unlike a random walk. The half-life is the time to close half the gap.

The formula

E[Xt]=θ+(x0−θ)e−κt,Var[Xt]=σ22κ(1−e−2κt)E[X_{t}]=\theta+(x_{0}-\theta)e^{-\kappa t},\quad Var[X_{t}]=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\kappa}\left(1-e^{-2\kappa t}\right)

Expected value at time t = Long-run mean + (Start - Long-run mean) x e^(-speed x t); Variance = sigma^2 / (2 x speed) x (1 - e^(-2 x speed x t))

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
thetaLong-run mean the process is pulled towardnumber

Worked example

A spread starts at 2.0 and is pulled toward 0.5 with a speed of 1.5 per year. Its volatility is 0.4. What is its distribution after 1 year, and what is the half-life of a shock?

  1. Expected value = theta + (x0 - theta) x e^(-kappa t)0.5+(2-0.5) x exp(-1.5 x 1)= 0.8347
  2. Variance = sigma^2 / (2 kappa) x (1 - e^(-2 kappa t))0.4^2 / (2 x 1.5) x (1-exp(-2 x 1.5 x 1))= 0.05068
  3. Standard deviationsqrt(var)= 0.2251
  4. Long-run standard deviation = sigma / sqrt(2 kappa)0.4 / sqrt(2 x 1.5)= 0.2309
  5. Half-life = ln 2 / kappaln(2) / 1.5= 0.462

After 1 year the expected value is 0.8347, with a standard deviation of 0.2251. In the long run the standard deviation settles at 0.2309. The half-life of a shock is 0.462 years.
