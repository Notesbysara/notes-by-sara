Cash Conversion Cycle from the Financial Statements

Intermediate

Also known as: DIO, DSO, DPO, operating cycle, days payable outstanding, days inventory outstanding

Days cash is tied up in operations. Use it to see how long cash is tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. A shorter cycle frees cash. A negative cycle means suppliers fund the business. This version builds each part from the statements, where the Corporate Finance entry takes the day counts as inputs.

The formula

CCC=DIO+DSO−DPO,DIO=Inv‾COGS×365CCC=DIO+DSO-DPO,\quad DIO=\frac{\overline{Inv}}{COGS}\times 365

DIO = Average inventory / COGS x 365; DSO = Average receivables / Revenue x 365; DPO = Average payables / COGS x 365; Cash conversion cycle = DIO + DSO - DPO

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
DIODays inventory outstanding, days stock sits before being solddays
DSODays sales outstanding, days to collect from customersdays
DPODays payables outstanding, days taken to pay suppliersdays
CCCCash conversion cycle, days that cash is tied up in operationsdays

Worked example

Revenue is 1,500 and COGS is 900. Average inventory is 150, average receivables 200 and average payables 100. Use a 365-day year.

  1. DIO = inventory / COGS x 365150 / 900 x 365= 60.8
  2. DSO = receivables / revenue x 365200 / 1500 x 365= 48.7
  3. DPO = payables / COGS x 365100 / 900 x 365= 40.6
  4. CCC = DIO + DSO - DPO60.8333+48.6667-40.5556= 68.9

DIO is 60.8 days, DSO is 48.7 days and DPO is 40.6 days. The cash conversion cycle is 68.9 days, so cash is tied up for about that long between paying suppliers and collecting from customers.
