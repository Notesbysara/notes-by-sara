Forecast Calibration and Brier Score

BeginnerBehavioral FinanceAlso known as: overconfidence, calibration, hit rate versus confidence, probability forecast accuracy
← Floating Rate Note Coupon and PriceForeign Project NPV: Two Equivalent Methods →

Finds: whether your confidence matches reality.

When to use it: Use it to test your own judgment. If you say 90% for events that happen only 70% of the time, you are overconfident. Overconfident investors trade too often and take too much risk. Keep a log of forecasts and outcomes, and check the score once you have a few dozen, as eight forecasts is too few to conclude much.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Overconfidence\displaystyle \text{Overconfidence}=pˉ−oˉ\displaystyle =\bar{p}-\bar{o}BS\displaystyle BS=1N∑t(pt−ot)2\displaystyle =\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t}(p_{t}-o_{t})^{2}

In words: Overconfidence = average stated probability − share of forecasts that came true; Brier score = average of (stated probability − outcome)^2, where the outcome is 1 if the event happened and 0 if not (0 is perfect and 0.25 is what always saying 50% scores)

What each symbol means

p_tProbability you gave that the event would happenpercent
o_tWhat happened: 1 if it happened, 0 if notnumber
NNumber of forecastscount

Worked example

Eight forecasts were made with confidence of 90, 80, 95, 70, 85, 90, 60 and 75 percent. The events happened in the order 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 1, 0, 1.

Average confidence80.6%
Share that happened62.5%
Overconfidence0.80625 − 0.62518.1%
Brier score0.2372
The answer

Your average stated confidence was 80.6% but only 62.5% of the events happened, so you were overconfident by 18.1%. The Brier score is 0.2372, compared with 0.25 for a forecaster who always says 50%.
