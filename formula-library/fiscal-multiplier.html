Fiscal Multiplier

Intermediate

Also known as: fiscal policy, spending multiplier, tax cut multiplier, balanced budget multiplier

How much output a fiscal change adds. Use it to estimate how much extra output a spending change creates. Each round of spending becomes someone's income, and part leaks into saving, tax and imports. Spending on goods has a larger multiplier than a tax cut of the same size, because part of a tax cut is saved first. Multipliers are smaller when the economy is at full capacity or interest rates rise in response.

The formula

kG=11−c(1−t)+m,kT=−c1−c(1−t)+mk_{G}=\frac{1}{1-c(1-t)+m},\quad k_{T}=-\frac{c}{1-c(1-t)+m}

Spending multiplier = 1 / (1 - MPC x (1 - tax rate) + import share); Tax-cut multiplier = - MPC / (1 - MPC x (1 - tax rate) + import share). Change in GDP = multiplier x change in spending

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
cMarginal propensity to consume: share of extra income spentnumber
tMarginal tax ratepercent
mMarginal propensity to import: share of extra income spent on importsnumber

Worked example

MPC is 0.8, the marginal tax rate 25% and the import share 0.1. The government raises spending by 100.

  1. Denominator = 1 - c(1 - t) + m1-0.8 x (1-0.25)+0.1= 0.500
  2. Spending multiplier1 / 0.5= 2.000
  3. GDP change from extra spending2 x 100= 200.00
  4. Tax-cut multiplier0.8 / 0.5= 1.600
  5. GDP change from a tax cut of the same size1.6 x 100= 160.00
  6. Extra tax revenue = t x GDP change0.25 x 200= 50.00
  7. Net budget cost of the spending100-50= 50.00

The leakage-adjusted denominator is 0.500, so the spending multiplier is 2.000 and 100 of extra spending raises GDP by 200.00. A tax cut of the same size has a multiplier of 1.600 and raises GDP by 160.00. Extra GDP brings in 50.00 of tax, so the net cost of the spending is 50.00.
