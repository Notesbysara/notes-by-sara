Fiscal Multiplier

IntermediateEconomics for FinanceAlso known as: fiscal policy, spending multiplier, tax cut multiplier, balanced budget multiplier
← Finite Difference: Explicit Scheme Step and StabilityFixed Charge Coverage and Net Debt to EBITDA →

Finds: how much output a fiscal change adds.

When to use it: Use it to estimate how much extra output a spending change creates. Each round of spending becomes someone's income, and part leaks into saving, tax and imports. Spending on goods has a larger multiplier than a tax cut of the same size, because part of a tax cut is saved first. Multipliers are smaller when the economy is at full capacity or interest rates rise in response.

The formula

kG\displaystyle k_{G}=11−c(1−t)+m\displaystyle =\frac{1}{1-c(1-t)+m}kT\displaystyle k_{T}=−c1−c(1−t)+m\displaystyle =-\frac{c}{1-c(1-t)+m}

In words: Spending multiplier = 1 / (1 − MPC x (1 − tax rate) + import share); Tax-cut multiplier = − MPC / (1 − MPC x (1 − tax rate) + import share). Change in GDP = multiplier x change in spending

What each symbol means

cMarginal propensity to consume: share of extra income spentnumber
tMarginal tax ratepercent
mMarginal propensity to import: share of extra income spent on importsnumber

Worked example

MPC is 0.8, the marginal tax rate 25% and the import share 0.1. The government raises spending by 100.

Denominator1 − 0.8 × (1 − 0.25) + 0.10.500
Spending multiplier1 ÷ 0.52.000
GDP change from extra spending2 × 100200
Tax-cut multiplier0.8 ÷ 0.51.600
GDP change from a tax cut of the same size1.6 × 100160
Extra tax revenue0.25 × 20050
Net budget cost of the spending100 − 5050
The answer

The leakage-adjusted denominator is 0.500, so the spending multiplier is 2.000 and 100 of extra spending raises GDP by 200. A tax cut of the same size has a multiplier of 1.600 and raises GDP by 160. Extra GDP brings in 50 of tax, so the net cost of the spending is 50.
