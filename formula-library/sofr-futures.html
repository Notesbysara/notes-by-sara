SOFR Futures Price, Rate and Tick Value

Beginner

Also known as: interest rate futures, 3 month SOFR, Eurodollar futures, basis point value of a future

The rate in a SOFR futures price and your gain or loss. Use it to read the market's expected short-term rate from a futures price, and to hedge short-term interest rate risk. The price moves opposite to the rate. A long position gains when rates fall.

The formula

Price=100−Implied rate,Value of 1 bp=Notional×312×0.0001\text{Price}=100-\text{Implied rate},\quad \text{Value of 1 bp}=\text{Notional}\times\frac{3}{12}\times 0.0001

Futures price = 100 - Implied rate (in %); Value of 1 basis point = Notional x 0.25 x 0.0001

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
PriceQuoted futures pricenumber
Implied rateAverage SOFR rate the market expects over the contract's three-month periodper year
NotionalContract size, 1,000,000 for three-month SOFR futuresmoney

Worked example

A SOFR futures contract trades at 95.35. You buy 100 contracts, and the price later falls to 95.20. What is the implied rate and what is your result?

  1. Implied rate then = 100 - price (in %)(100-95.35) / 100= 4.650%
  2. Implied rate now(100-95.2) / 100= 4.800%
  3. Value of one basis point per contract1,000,000 x 0.25 x 0.0001= 25.00
  4. Price change in basis points(95.2-95.35) x 100= -15.0
  5. Profit or loss on a long position-15 x 25 x 100= -37,500.00

The implied rate is 4.650%, and later it is 4.800%. One basis point is worth 25.00 per contract. The price fell -15.0 basis points, so a long position of 100 contracts loses -37,500.00.
