SOFR Futures Price, Rate and Tick Value

BeginnerDerivativesAlso known as: interest rate futures, 3 month SOFR, Eurodollar futures, basis point value of a future
← SkewnessSolow Model Steady State →

Finds: the rate in a SOFR futures price and your gain or loss.

When to use it: Use it to read the market's expected short-term rate from a futures price, and to hedge short-term interest rate risk. The price moves opposite to the rate. A long position gains when rates fall.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Price\displaystyle \text{Price}=100−Implied rate\displaystyle =100-\text{Implied rate}Value of 1 bp\displaystyle \text{Value of 1 bp}=Notional×312×0.0001\displaystyle =\text{Notional}\times\frac{3}{12}\times 0.0001

In words: Futures price = 100 − Implied rate (in %); Value of 1 basis point = Notional x 0.25 x 0.0001

What each symbol means

PriceQuoted futures pricenumber
Implied rateAverage SOFR rate the market expects over the contract's three-month periodper year
NotionalContract size, 1,000,000 for three-month SOFR futuresmoney

Worked example

A SOFR futures contract trades at 95.35. You buy 100 contracts, and the price later falls to 95.20. What is the implied rate and what is your result?

Implied rate then(100 − 95.35) ÷ 1004.650%
Implied rate now(100 − 95.2) ÷ 1004.800%
Value of one basis point per contract1,000,000 × 0.25 × 0.000125
Price change in basis points(95.2 − 95.35) × 100−15
Profit or loss on a long position−15 × 25 × 100−37,500
The answer

The implied rate is 4.650%, and later it is 4.800%. One basis point is worth 25 per contract. The price fell −15.0 basis points, so a long position of 100 contracts loses −37,500.
