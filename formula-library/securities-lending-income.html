Securities Lending Income

Intermediate

Also known as: stock lending, rebate rate, collateral reinvestment, lending fee

Extra income earned by lending shares. Use it to see what an owner earns by lending shares to short sellers. The borrower pays by accepting a lower rebate on its cash. The harder a stock is to borrow, the lower (or more negative) the rebate.

The formula

Income=C (rinv−rrebate) d365×(1−s)\text{Income}=C\,(r_{inv}-r_{rebate})\,\frac{d}{365}\times(1-s)

Income = Collateral x (Reinvestment return - Rebate rate) x Days / 365 x (1 - Agent share)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
CCash collateral the borrower posts, usually a little above the value of the shares lentmoney
r_invReturn earned by reinvesting the cash collateralper year
r_rebateRebate rate, the interest the lender pays back to the borrower on the cashper year
dDays of the loandays

Worked example

A fund lends shares worth 1,000,000 and receives 1,020,000 of cash collateral. It reinvests the cash at 4.5% and pays a rebate of 3.0%. The loan lasts 60 days and the agent keeps 20%. What does the fund earn?

  1. Gross income = collateral x (reinvestment - rebate) x days / 3651,020,000 x (0.045-0.03) x 60 / 365= 2,515.07
  2. Income to the fund = gross x (1 - agent share)2515.07 x (1-0.2)= 2,012.05

The spread between the reinvestment return and the rebate earns 2,515.07 before the agent's cut. The fund keeps 2,012.05.
