Securities Lending Income

IntermediateEquity MarketsAlso known as: stock lending, rebate rate, collateral reinvestment, lending fee
← Secondaries: Price Versus NAV and Buyer IRRSecurity Market Line and Alpha →

Finds: extra income earned by lending shares.

When to use it: Use it to see what an owner earns by lending shares to short sellers. The borrower pays by accepting a lower rebate on its cash. The harder a stock is to borrow, the lower (or more negative) the rebate.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Income\displaystyle \text{Income}=C (rinv−rrebate) d365×(1−s)\displaystyle =C\,(r_{inv}-r_{rebate})\,\frac{d}{365}\times(1-s)

In words: Income = Collateral x (Reinvestment return − Rebate rate) x Days / 365 x (1 − Agent share)

What each symbol means

CCash collateral the borrower posts, usually a little above the value of the shares lentmoney
r_invReturn earned by reinvesting the cash collateralper year
r_rebateRebate rate, the interest the lender pays back to the borrower on the cashper year
dDays of the loandays
sShare of income kept by the lending agentpercent

Worked example

A fund lends shares worth 1,000,000 and receives 1,020,000 of cash collateral. It reinvests the cash at 4.5% and pays a rebate of 3.0%. The loan lasts 60 days and the agent keeps 20%. What does the fund earn?

Gross income1,020,000 × (0.045 − 0.03) × 60 ÷ 3652,515.07
Income to the fund2,515.07 × (1 − 0.2)2,012.05
The answer

The spread between the reinvestment return and the rebate earns 2,515.07 before the agent's cut. The fund keeps 2,012.05.
