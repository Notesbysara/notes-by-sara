Stock Split

BeginnerCorporate FinanceAlso known as: share split, split ratio, stock split price adjustment
← Stablecoin Reserve Backing and Issuer IncomeStock-Based Compensation Adjusted Free Cash Flow →

Finds: shares and price after a split.

When to use it: Use it to see what a split changes: the number of shares and the price per share. The total value of your holding stays the same. A reverse split uses k below 1.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Nnew\displaystyle N_{new}=k N\displaystyle =k\,NPnew\displaystyle P_{new}=Pk\displaystyle =\frac{P}{k}

In words: New shares = k x Old shares; New price = Old price / k, for a k-for-1 split

What each symbol means

kSplit ratio, new shares for each old share (3 for a 3-for-1 split)number
NShares held before the splitshares
PShare price before the splitmoney

Worked example

You hold 100 shares priced at 60. The company announces a 3-for-1 split. What do you hold afterward?

New shares3 × 100300
New price60 ÷ 320
Value before100 × 606,000
Value after300 × 206,000
The answer

You hold 300 shares at 20 each. Your holding is worth 6,000 before and 6,000 after, so nothing has changed in value.
