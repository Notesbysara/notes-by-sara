Put-Call Parity

IntermediateDerivativesAlso known as: synthetic put, synthetic call, conversion arbitrage, parity relationship
← Purchasing Power Parity: Valuation and Expected RateQuantity Theory of Money →

Finds: whether call and put prices fit together.

When to use it: Use it to check whether option prices are consistent, or to build one instrument from the others: a call equals a put plus the stock minus a bond. If the two sides differ, there is an arbitrage. It holds only for European options.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

C−P\displaystyle C-P=S e−qT−K e−rT\displaystyle =S\,e^{-qT}-K\,e^{-rT}

In words: Call price − Put price = Spot x e^(-dividend yield x T) − Strike x e^(-r x T)

What each symbol means

C, PPrices of a European call and put with the same strike and expirymoney
SSpot pricemoney
KStrike pricemoney
rRisk-free rate (continuous)per year
qDividend yield (continuous)per year
TTime to expiryyears

Worked example

A stock trades at 100 and pays no dividend. A 1-year at-the-money call costs 10.45 and the put costs 5.90. The risk-free rate is 5%. Do the prices fit parity?

Present value of the strike100 × exp(−0.05 × 1)95.12
Call - put10.45 − 5.94.55
Stock - PV of strike100 × exp(−0 × 1) − 95.12294.88
Gap4.55 − 4.87706−0.33
Put price consistent with the call10.45 − 4.877065.57
The answer

The left side is 4.55 and the right side is 4.88, a gap of −0.33. Because they differ, these prices are out of line. The put consistent with the call would cost 5.57, against 5.90 quoted.
