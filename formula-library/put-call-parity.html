Put-Call Parity

Intermediate

Also known as: synthetic put, synthetic call, conversion arbitrage, parity relationship

Whether call and put prices fit together. Use it to check whether option prices are consistent, or to build one instrument from the others: a call equals a put plus the stock minus a bond. If the two sides differ, there is an arbitrage. It holds only for European options.

The formula

C−P=S e−qT−K e−rTC-P=S\,e^{-qT}-K\,e^{-rT}

Call price - Put price = Spot x e^(-dividend yield x T) - Strike x e^(-r x T)

What each symbol means

C, PPrices of a European call and put with the same strike and expirymoney

Worked example

A stock trades at 100 and pays no dividend. A 1-year at-the-money call costs 10.45 and the put costs 5.90. The risk-free rate is 5%. Do the prices fit parity?

  1. Present value of the strike = K x e^(-r T)100 x exp(-0.05 x 1)= 95.12
  2. Call - put10.45-5.9= 4.55
  3. Stock - PV of strike = S x e^(-q T) - K x e^(-r T)100 x exp(-0 x 1)-95.1229= 4.88
  4. Gap = left - right (zero if consistent)4.55-4.87706= -0.33
  5. Put price consistent with the call = call - right side10.45-4.87706= 5.57

The left side is 4.55 and the right side is 4.88, a gap of -0.33. Because they differ, these prices are out of line. The put consistent with the call would cost 5.57, against 5.90 quoted.
