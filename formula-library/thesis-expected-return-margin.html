Investment Thesis: Expected Return and Margin of Safety

Intermediate

Also known as: form and defend your own investment thesis, margin of safety, kill criteria, thesis scenario

Whether a thesis has enough edge to act on. Use it to test your own thesis before you commit money. Write down what you believe, the price if you are right, the price if you are wrong and your confidence. A thin margin of safety, or a break-even probability close to your confidence, means there is little room for error. Write the conditions that would make you sell (kill criteria) before you buy.

The formula

MoS=V−PV,E[R]=p(VP−1)+(1−p)(LP−1),Rann=(1+E[R])1/n−1MoS=\frac{V-P}{V},\quad E[R]=p\left(\frac{V}{P}-1\right)+(1-p)\left(\frac{L}{P}-1\right),\quad R_{ann}=(1+E[R])^{1/n}-1

Margin of safety = (estimated value - price) / estimated value; Expected total return = probability the thesis works x its return + probability it fails x its return (a loss); Annualized = (1 + expected return)^(1 / years) - 1; Break-even probability = loss / (gain + loss), the chance the thesis must have to just cover itself

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
VValue you estimate the stock is worth if the thesis is rightmoney
LPrice you expect if the thesis failsmoney
pYour probability that the thesis workspercent
nYears to holdyears

Worked example

You buy at 62. If your thesis is right the stock reaches 95 within 3 years; if wrong it falls to 48. You put 60% on the thesis working.

  1. Margin of safety(95-62) / 95= 34.7%
  2. Gain if right95 / 62-1= 53.2%
  3. Loss if wrong1-48 / 62= 22.6%
  4. Expected total return0.6 x 0.532258-(1-0.6) x 0.225806= 22.9%
  5. Annualized expected return(1+0.229032)^(1 / 3)-1= 7.1%
  6. Break-even probability0.225806 / (0.532258+0.225806)= 29.8%
  7. Your confidence0.6= 60%

The margin of safety is 34.7%. The gain if right is 53.2% and the loss if wrong is 22.6%. The expected return is 22.9%, or 7.1% a year. The thesis breaks even at a probability of 29.8%, compared with your 60% confidence.
