Bond Price

BeginnerFixed IncomeAlso known as: present value of a bond, price of a coupon bond, bond valuation, premium and discount bonds
← Bond Futures Invoice Price and BasisBootstrapping Spot Rates from Par Yields →

Finds: what a bond is worth at a given yield.

When to use it: Use it to find what a bond is worth at a given market yield. When the market yield is above the coupon rate the bond sells at a discount, and when it is below the bond sells at a premium.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

P\displaystyle P=C 1−(1+r)−Nr+F(1+r)N\displaystyle =C\,\frac{1-(1+r)^{-N}}{r}+\frac{F}{(1+r)^{N}}

In words: Price = Coupon x [1 − (1 + r)^-N] / r + Face value / (1 + r)^N, where r = yield / payments per year and N = number of payments

What each symbol means

PPrice of the bond todaymoney
CCoupon payment each periodmoney
FFace value repaid at maturitymoney
rMarket yield per period (annual yield divided by payments per year)per period
NNumber of coupon periods remainingnumber

Worked example

A bond has a face value of 1,000, a 5% coupon paid twice a year and 10 years to maturity. The market yield is 6%. What is the price?

Coupon payment1,000 × 0.05 ÷ 225
Yield per period0.06 ÷ 23.0000%
Number of periods10 × 220
Price25 × (1 − (1 + 0.03)^(−20)) ÷ 0.03 + 1,000 ÷ (1 + 0.03)^20925.61
Price minus face value (negative means a discount)925.613 − 1,000−74.39
The answer

The bond is worth 925.61, which is −74.39 against its face value. It sells at a discount because the 6% market yield is above the 5% coupon.
