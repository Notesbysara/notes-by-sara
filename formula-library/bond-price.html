Bond Price

Beginner

Also known as: present value of a bond, price of a coupon bond, bond valuation, premium and discount bonds

What a bond is worth at a given yield. Use it to find what a bond is worth at a given market yield. When the market yield is above the coupon rate the bond sells at a discount, and when it is below the bond sells at a premium.

The formula

P=C 1−(1+r)−Nr+F(1+r)NP=C\,\frac{1-(1+r)^{-N}}{r}+\frac{F}{(1+r)^{N}}

Price = Coupon x [1 - (1 + r)^-N] / r + Face value / (1 + r)^N, where r = yield / payments per year and N = number of payments

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
PPrice of the bond todaymoney
CCoupon payment each periodmoney
FFace value repaid at maturitymoney
rMarket yield per period (annual yield divided by payments per year)per period
NNumber of coupon periods remainingnumber

Worked example

A bond has a face value of 1,000, a 5% coupon paid twice a year and 10 years to maturity. The market yield is 6%. What is the price?

  1. Coupon payment = face x c / m1000 x 0.05 / 2= 25.00
  2. Yield per period = y / m0.06 / 2= 3.0000%
  3. Number of periods = n x m10 x 2= 20
  4. Price = coupon x [1 - (1 + r)^-N] / r + face / (1 + r)^N25 x (1-(1+0.03)^(-20)) / 0.03+1000 / (1+0.03)^20= 925.61
  5. Price minus face value (negative means a discount)925.613-1000= -74.39

The bond is worth 925.61, which is -74.39 against its face value. It sells at a discount because the 6% market yield is above the 5% coupon.
