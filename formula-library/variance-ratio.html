Variance Ratio (Random Walk Test)

Intermediate

Also known as: Lo MacKinlay, random walk test, mean reversion test, random walk theory

Whether returns look like a random walk. Use it to test whether prices follow a random walk. In a random walk the variance grows in proportion to time, so the ratio is about 1. Below 1 suggests returns reverse (mean reversion), and above 1 suggests they trend.

The formula

VR(q)=σq2q σ12VR(q)=\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{q\,\sigma_{1}^{2}}

Variance ratio = Variance of q-period returns / (q x Variance of 1-period returns)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
VR(q)Variance ratio for q periodsnumber
sigma_qStandard deviation of returns measured over q periodspercent
sigma_1Standard deviation of returns measured over 1 periodpercent
qNumber of periods combinednumber

Worked example

Monthly returns have a standard deviation of 4%. Returns measured over 4 months have a standard deviation of 7.4%. What is the variance ratio?

  1. Variance of 1-period returns0.04^2= 0.001600
  2. Variance of q-period returns0.074^2= 0.005476
  3. VR = vq / (q x v1)0.005476 / (4 x 0.0016)= 0.856

The variance ratio is 0.856. It is below 1, which hints at some mean reversion, although a formal test is needed before concluding anything.
