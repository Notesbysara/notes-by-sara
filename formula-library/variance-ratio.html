Variance Ratio (Random Walk Test)

IntermediateInvestments & Portfolio TheoryAlso known as: Lo MacKinlay, random walk test, mean reversion test, random walk theory
← Variance of a Probability DistributionVasicek Model Zero-Coupon Bond Price →

Finds: whether returns look like a random walk.

When to use it: Use it to test whether prices follow a random walk. In a random walk the variance grows in proportion to time, so the ratio is about 1. Below 1 suggests returns reverse (mean reversion), and above 1 suggests they trend.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

VR(q)\displaystyle VR(q)=σq2q σ12\displaystyle =\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{q\,\sigma_{1}^{2}}

In words: Variance ratio = Variance of q-period returns / (q x Variance of 1-period returns)

What each symbol means

VR(q)Variance ratio for q periodsnumber
sigma_qStandard deviation of returns measured over q periodspercent
sigma_1Standard deviation of returns measured over 1 periodpercent
qNumber of periods combinednumber

Worked example

Monthly returns have a standard deviation of 4%. Returns measured over 4 months have a standard deviation of 7.4%. What is the variance ratio?

Variance of 1-period returns0.04^20.001600
Variance of q-period returns0.074^20.005476
VR0.005476 ÷ (4 × 0.0016)0.856
The answer

The variance ratio is 0.856. It is below 1, which hints at some mean reversion, although a formal test is needed before concluding anything.
