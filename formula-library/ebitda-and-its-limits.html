EBITDA and Its Limits

IntermediateFinancial Statement AnalysisAlso known as: EBITDA, EBITDA minus capex, cash EBITDA
← Earnings per Share (EPS)Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) →

Finds: how much of EBITDA is real cash.

When to use it: Use it to see what EBITDA leaves out. EBITDA ignores the cost of replacing assets, tax, interest and working capital, so it overstates the cash a business generates. A large gap between EBITDA and the cash proxy is a limit on its usefulness.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

EBITDA\displaystyle EBITDA=NI+Tax+Interest+D&A\displaystyle =NI+\text{Tax}+\text{Interest}+D\&ACash proxy\displaystyle \text{Cash proxy}=EBITDA−Tax−CapEx−ΔNWC−Interest\displaystyle =EBITDA-\text{Tax}-\text{CapEx}-\Delta NWC-\text{Interest}

In words: EBITDA = Net income + Tax + Interest + Depreciation and amortization; Cash proxy = EBITDA − Cash tax − Capex − Increase in working capital − Interest

What each symbol means

NINet incomemoney
D&ADepreciation and amortizationmoney
CapExCapital expendituremoney
Delta NWCIncrease in net working capitalmoney

Worked example

A company has net income of 90, tax of 30, interest of 40 and depreciation and amortization of 90. It spent 110 on capital and working capital rose by 20.

EBIT90 + 30 + 40160
EBITDA160 + 90250
Cash proxy250 − 30 − 110 − 20 − 4050
Cash proxy as a share of EBITDA50 ÷ 25020%
EBITDA / interest250 ÷ 406.25
EBIT / interest160 ÷ 404
The answer

EBIT is 160 and EBITDA is 250. After tax, interest, capex and working capital, the cash left is 50, only 20.0% of EBITDA. Interest cover is 6.25 on EBITDA but 4 on EBIT.
