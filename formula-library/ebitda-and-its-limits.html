EBITDA and Its Limits

Intermediate

Also known as: EBITDA, EBITDA minus capex, cash EBITDA

How much of EBITDA is real cash. Use it to see what EBITDA leaves out. EBITDA ignores the cost of replacing assets, tax, interest and working capital, so it overstates the cash a business generates. A large gap between EBITDA and the cash proxy is a limit on its usefulness.

The formula

EBITDA=NI+Tax+Interest+D&A,Cash proxy=EBITDA−Tax−CapEx−ΔNWC−InterestEBITDA=NI+\text{Tax}+\text{Interest}+D\&A,\quad \text{Cash proxy}=EBITDA-\text{Tax}-\text{CapEx}-\Delta NWC-\text{Interest}

EBITDA = Net income + Tax + Interest + Depreciation and amortization; Cash proxy = EBITDA - Cash tax - Capex - Increase in working capital - Interest

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
NINet incomemoney
D&ADepreciation and amortizationmoney
CapExCapital expendituremoney
Delta NWCIncrease in net working capitalmoney

Worked example

A company has net income of 90, tax of 30, interest of 40 and depreciation and amortization of 90. It spent 110 on capital and working capital rose by 20.

  1. EBIT = NI + tax + interest90+30+40= 160.00
  2. EBITDA = EBIT + D&A160+90= 250.00
  3. Cash proxy = EBITDA - tax - capex - increase in NWC - interest250-30-110-20-40= 50.00
  4. Cash proxy as a share of EBITDA50 / 250= 20.0%
  5. EBITDA / interest250 / 40= 6.25
  6. EBIT / interest160 / 40= 4.00

EBIT is 160.00 and EBITDA is 250.00. After tax, interest, capex and working capital, the cash left is 50.00, only 20.0% of EBITDA. Interest cover is 6.25 on EBITDA but 4.00 on EBIT.
