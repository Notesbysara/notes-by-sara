AML/KYC Customer Risk Score

Beginner

Also known as: anti-money laundering, know your customer, risk-based approach, customer due diligence, enhanced due diligence

A customer risk rating and how much extra checking it triggers. Use it to see how a bank applies a risk-based approach under anti-money-laundering rules. Higher-risk customers get more checks (source of funds, senior approval, closer monitoring). The factors, weights and cut-offs are set by each firm and approved by its regulator, so the numbers here are illustrative.

The formula

S=∑iwisi,Rating={HighS≥3.5Medium2.5≤S<3.5LowS<2.5S=\sum_{i}w_{i}s_{i},\quad \text{Rating}=\begin{cases}\text{High}&S\ge 3.5\\\text{Medium}&2.5\le S<3.5\\\text{Low}&S<2.5\end{cases}

Customer risk score = sum of (weight x score) over factors such as country, product, customer type, delivery channel and transaction pattern, each scored 1 (low) to 5 (high), weights adding up to 1; Rating bands set by the firm (here: 3.5 or more is high risk and gets enhanced due diligence)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
w_iWeight the firm gives to risk factor i (adds up to 100%)percent
s_iScore for factor i from 1 to 5number

Worked example

A customer scores 4 on country, 5 on product (cash-intensive business), 2 on customer type, 3 on channel and 1 on transaction pattern. Weights are 30%, 25%, 20%, 15% and 10%.

  1. Weighted contribution of each factor= 1.20, 1.25, 0.40, 0.45, 0.10
  2. Total score= 3.40
  3. Rating (2 = high, 1 = medium, 0 = low)2 if 3.4>=3.5 else (1 if 3.4>=2.5 else 0)= 1
  4. Factor adding most (position)= 2

The weighted score is 3.40, which is rated 1 (2 = high, 1 = medium, 0 = low). Enhanced due diligence applies when the score is 3.5 or above. The factor adding most to the score is number 2 (counting from 1).
