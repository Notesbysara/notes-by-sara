Rational Bubble: Required Growth and Survival

Intermediate

Also known as: bubbles and manias, Blanchard Watson, bubble burst probability, explosive growth

How fast a bubble must grow and how likely it is to last. Use it to see why bubbles are explosive. A price above fundamental value can persist if holders believe it will keep rising fast enough to pay for the burst risk. The required growth is high, so the bubble component becomes large the longer it lasts, while the chance of surviving falls. It shows that a bubble can be consistent with rational expectations, not that it is likely.

The formula

g=1+r1−π−1,P(survive n)=(1−π)n,Bn=B0(1+g)ng=\frac{1+r}{1-\pi}-1,\quad P(\text{survive } n)=(1-\pi)^{n},\quad B_{n}=B_{0}(1+g)^{n}

If a bubble bursts with probability pi each period, holders require the bubble part of the price to grow, while it survives, at g = (1 + r) / (1 - pi) - 1 so that the expected return stays at r; Chance it survives n periods = (1 - pi)^n; Bubble size after n periods if it survives = B0 x (1 + g)^n

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
rRequired return of holdersper year
piChance per period that the bubble burstspercent
B_0Size of the bubble component of the price todaymoney
gGrowth rate the bubble must have to keep holders willingper year

Worked example

Holders require 8% a year. The chance of a burst each year is 10%. The bubble part of the price is 20 today.

  1. Required growth = (1 + r) / (1 - pi) - 1(1+0.08) / (1-0.1)-1= 20.00%
  2. Bubble size after n years if it survives20 x (1+0.2)^5= 49.77
  3. Probability it survives n years(1-0.1)^5= 59.0%
  4. Expected bubble size = survival chance x size0.59049 x 49.7664= 29.39
  5. Expected size growth at r: B0 x (1 + r)^n20 x (1+0.08)^5= 29.39

While the bubble survives it must grow 20.00% a year. After 5 years the bubble part would be 49.77, if it survives, and the chance of surviving that long is 59.0%. The expected bubble part, counting the burst risk, is 29.39, consistent with growth at the required 8%.
