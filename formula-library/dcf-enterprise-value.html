DCF Enterprise Value (Explicit Period plus Terminal Value)

IntermediateValuationAlso known as: DCF, discounted cash flow, DCF model build, enterprise value from cash flows
← DCF Capstone: From Cash Flows to Value per ShareDebits Equal Credits (Trial Balance) →

Finds: business value from forecast cash flows.

When to use it: Use it to value a business from its forecast cash flows. Check what share of the value comes from the terminal value, because a very high share means the answer rests on a distant guess.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

EV\displaystyle EV=∑t=1nFCFFt(1+WACC)t+TVn(1+WACC)n\displaystyle =\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{FCFF_{t}}{(1+WACC)^{t}}+\frac{TV_{n}}{(1+WACC)^{n}}

In words: EV = sum of FCFF_t / (1 + WACC)^t for t = 1 to n, plus Terminal value / (1 + WACC)^n

What each symbol means

EVEnterprise value, the value of the whole businessmoney
FCFF_tFree cash flow to the firm forecast for year tmoney
WACCWeighted average cost of capitalper year
TV_nTerminal value at the end of year nmoney
gGrowth rate used in the terminal valueper year

Worked example

A firm is forecast to produce free cash flows of 100, 110, 120, 130 and 140 over the next 5 years. WACC is 9% and growth after year 5 is 3%. What is its enterprise value?

PV of the explicit cash flows460.08
Terminal value2,403.33
PV of the terminal value1,562
Enterprise value460.076 + 1,5622,022.08
Share of value from the terminal value1,562 ÷ 2,022.0877.25%
The answer

The explicit cash flows are worth 460.08, the terminal value adds 1,562 in present value, and the enterprise value is 2,022.08. The terminal value makes up 77.25% of the total.
