Lease Liability and First-Year Expense (IFRS 16 / ASC 842)

Intermediate

Also known as: right of use asset, operating lease, finance lease, lease accounting

The lease liability and first-year expense. Use it to see how leases appear on the balance sheet. The lessee records a liability and a right-of-use asset at the present value of the payments. Under IFRS 16 (and ASC 842 finance leases) the expense is interest plus depreciation, which is higher in early years. Under ASC 842 an operating lease keeps a single straight-line expense.

The formula

L0=∑t=1nP(1+r)t,Interest1=r L0,Depreciation=ROUnL_{0}=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{P}{(1+r)^{t}},\quad \text{Interest}_{1}=r\,L_{0},\quad \text{Depreciation}=\frac{ROU}{n}

Lease liability = Present value of the lease payments at the discount rate; Year-1 interest = Rate x Liability; Depreciation = Right-of-use asset / Lease term; IFRS 16 expense = Interest + Depreciation

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
PAnnual lease payment, paid at year endmoney
rDiscount rate: the rate implicit in the lease or the lessee's borrowing rateper year
nLease term in yearsyears
ROURight-of-use asset, equal to the liability at the startmoney

Worked example

A company signs a 5-year lease with payments of 100 at the end of each year. The discount rate is 6%.

  1. Liability = payment x [1 - (1 + r)^-n] / r100 x (1-(1+0.06)^(-5)) / 0.06= 421.24
  2. Year-1 interest = r x liability0.06 x 421.236= 25.27
  3. Year-1 depreciation = liability / n421.236 / 5= 84.25
  4. IFRS 16 year-1 expense = interest + depreciation25.2742+84.2473= 109.52
  5. Liability at the end of year 1 = liability + interest - payment421.236+25.2742-100= 346.51

The lease liability and the right-of-use asset start at 421.24. In year one the interest is 25.27 and depreciation is 84.25, so the IFRS 16 expense is 109.52 compared with the cash payment of 100. The expense is front-loaded: it is above the payment early on and below it near the end.
