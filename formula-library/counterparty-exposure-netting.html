Counterparty Exposure, Netting and Potential Future Exposure

Intermediate

Also known as: counterparty credit risk, PFE, netting agreement, exposure at default derivatives

Counterparty exposure with and without netting. Use it to measure what you would lose if a counterparty defaulted today, and how that might grow. Under a netting agreement the trades are added together, so gains on some offset losses on others, which cuts the exposure. CVA (priced in the Derivatives section) turns the exposure profile into a money charge.

The formula

Gross=∑max⁡(MtMi,0),Net=max⁡(∑MtMi,0),PFE=z σt×N\text{Gross}=\sum\max(MtM_{i},0),\quad \text{Net}=\max\left(\sum MtM_{i},0\right),\quad PFE=z\,\sigma\sqrt{t}\times N

Gross exposure = sum of the positive trade values; Net exposure = max(sum of all trade values, 0); Potential future exposure = z-score x volatility x sqrt(time) x Notional

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
MtM_iCurrent market value of trade i with the counterparty. Positive means they owe youmoney
PFEPotential future exposure, a high estimate of how large the exposure could becomemoney
NNotional of the tradesmoney
sigma, tVolatility and time horizonper year, years

Worked example

A bank has five trades with one counterparty, valued at 12, -8, 5, -3 and 10 (millions). The notional is 200 million, the volatility of the underlying is 8% a year, the horizon is 1 year and the confidence is 95%.

  1. Gross exposure = sum of positive values= 27.00
  2. Net exposure = max(sum of all values, 0)= 16.00
  3. Benefit of netting27-16= 11.00
  4. Netting benefit as a share of gross11 / 27= 40.7%
  5. PFE = z x vol x sqrt(t) x notional= 26.32
  6. Conservative exposure at default = net + PFE16+26.3177= 42.32

Without netting the exposure is 27.00 million. With netting it is 16.00 million, a saving of 11.00 (40.7%). The potential future exposure is 26.32 million, so the conservative exposure at default is 42.32 million.
