Counterparty Exposure, Netting and Potential Future Exposure

IntermediateRisk ManagementAlso known as: counterparty credit risk, PFE, netting agreement, exposure at default derivatives
← Cost-Benefit Test of a Financial RuleCountry Risk Premium →

Finds: counterparty exposure with and without netting.

When to use it: Use it to measure what you would lose if a counterparty defaulted today, and how that might grow. Under a netting agreement the trades are added together, so gains on some offset losses on others, which cuts the exposure. CVA (priced in the Derivatives section) turns the exposure profile into a money charge.

The formula

Gross\displaystyle \text{Gross}=∑max⁡(MtMi,0)\displaystyle =\sum\max(MtM_{i},0)Net\displaystyle \text{Net}=max⁡(∑MtMi,0)\displaystyle =\max\left(\sum MtM_{i},0\right)PFE\displaystyle PFE=z σt×N\displaystyle =z\,\sigma\sqrt{t}\times N

In words: Gross exposure = sum of the positive trade values; Net exposure = max(sum of all trade values, 0); Potential future exposure = z-score x volatility x sqrt(time) x Notional

What each symbol means

MtM_iCurrent market value of trade i with the counterparty. Positive means they owe youmoney
PFEPotential future exposure, a high estimate of how large the exposure could becomemoney
NNotional of the tradesmoney
sigma, tVolatility and time horizonper year, years

Worked example

A bank has five trades with one counterparty, valued at 12, −8, 5, −3 and 10 (millions). The notional is 200 million, the volatility of the underlying is 8% a year, the horizon is 1 year and the confidence is 95%.

Gross exposure27
Net exposure16
Benefit of netting27 − 1611
Netting benefit as a share of gross11 ÷ 2740.7%
PFE26.32
Conservative exposure at default16 + 26.317742.32
The answer

Without netting the exposure is 27 million. With netting it is 16 million, a saving of 11 (40.7%). The potential future exposure is 26.32 million, so the conservative exposure at default is 42.32 million.
