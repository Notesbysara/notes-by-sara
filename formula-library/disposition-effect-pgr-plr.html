Disposition Effect: Gains vs Losses Realized

Beginner

Also known as: disposition effect, selling winners too early, Odean PGR PLR, holding losers

Whether you sell winners faster than losers. Use it to check your own selling behavior. Investors tend to sell winners to lock in gains and hold losers hoping to break even, which is the reverse of what tax rules favor. A PGR well above PLR is a sign. Count positions on days when you sold something, as in the original study, so you do not mix in days with no decision.

The formula

PGR=RGRG+PG,PLR=RLRL+PL,Disposition=PGR−PLRPGR=\frac{RG}{RG+PG},\quad PLR=\frac{RL}{RL+PL},\quad \text{Disposition}=PGR-PLR

Proportion of gains realized = realized gains / (realized gains + paper gains); Proportion of losses realized = realized losses / (realized losses + paper losses); Disposition effect = PGR - PLR (positive means winners are sold more readily than losers)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
RG, RLNumber of gains and losses realized (sold) on days when a sale happenedcount
PG, PLNumber of gains and losses still held on paper on those same dayscount

Worked example

On days with a sale, you realized 60 gains and 20 losses, while 240 gains and 280 losses stayed on paper.

  1. PGR = RG / (RG + PG)60 / (60+240)= 20.0%
  2. PLR = RL / (RL + PL)20 / (20+280)= 6.7%
  3. Disposition effect = PGR - PLR0.2-0.0666667= 13.3%
  4. PGR / PLR0.2 / 0.0666667= 3.00

Your PGR is 20.0% and your PLR is 6.7%, so winners are 3.00 times as likely to be sold as losers. The disposition effect measure is 13.3%.
