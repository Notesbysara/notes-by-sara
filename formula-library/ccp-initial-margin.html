Central Counterparty Initial Margin (Value-at-Risk Method)

Intermediate

Also known as: central clearing, CCP margin, margin period of risk, OTC vs exchange margin, initial margin

The collateral a clearing house asks for. Use it to see how a clearing house sets collateral. The central counterparty stands between buyers and sellers. It asks for margin big enough to cover a large loss over the days it would take to close a defaulting member's position. Longer close-out periods, more volatile markets and bigger positions mean more margin.

The formula

IM=z σdMPOR×PositionIM=z\,\sigma_{d}\sqrt{MPOR}\times\text{Position}

Initial margin = z-score x Daily volatility x Square root of margin period of risk (days) x Position size

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
zNumber of standard deviations for the confidence level (2.33 for 99%)number
sigma_dDaily volatility of the position's valuepercent
MPORMargin period of risk, the days needed to close out a defaulting member's position, often 5 for cleared swapsdays
PositionSize of the positionmoney

Worked example

A member holds a 50 million position with a daily volatility of 1.1%. The clearing house uses a 99% level (z = 2.33) and a 5-day margin period of risk. What initial margin is required?

  1. Volatility over the margin period = sd x sqrt(MPOR)sd x sqrt(5)= 2.46%
  2. Initial margin = z x volatility over the period x position2.33 x 0.0245967 x 50,000,000= 2,865,521.11
  3. Margin as a share of the position2,865,521 / 50,000,000= 5.73%
  4. For comparison, a 10-day margin period2.33 x sd x sqrt(10) x 50,000,000= 4,052,458.82

The 5-day volatility is 2.46%, so the initial margin is 2,865,521.11, or 5.73% of the position. With a 10-day margin period it would rise to 4,052,458.82.
