Climate Disclosure: Carbon Intensity and Financed Emissions

IntermediateRegulation, Ethics & ESGAlso known as: climate risk disclosure, TCFD, ISSB, WACI, PCAF, financed emissions, carbon footprint
← Classification Metrics: Precision, Recall and F1CLO Overcollateralization Test →

Finds: A portfolio's carbon exposure.

When to use it: Use it to report climate exposure of a portfolio or loan book in the way supported by TCFD and ISSB. WACI shows how carbon-intensive the holdings are relative to their sales; financed emissions assigns a share of each company's emissions to the investor in proportion to its stake. Data quality varies, since many companies report estimates.

The formula

WACI\displaystyle WACI=∑iwiEiRevi\displaystyle =\sum_{i}w_{i}\frac{E_{i}}{Rev_{i}}Financed\displaystyle \text{Financed}=∑iOiEVICiEi\displaystyle =\sum_{i}\frac{O_{i}}{EVIC_{i}}E_{i}

In words: Weighted average carbon intensity = sum of (portfolio weight x company emissions / company revenue), in metric tons of CO2 equivalent per million of revenue; Financed emissions = sum of (amount invested or lent / company enterprise value including cash) x company emissions; Portfolio footprint per million invested = financed emissions / total invested

Worked example

A portfolio holds four companies with weights 40%, 30%, 20% and 10%. Emissions are 120, 300, 45 and 800 metric tons; revenues are 1,500, 900, 2,000 and 1,000 million. The investor holds 40, 30, 20 and 10 million of companies whose enterprise values are 4,000, 2,500, 6,000 and 2,000 million.

The answer

The WACI is 0.22 metric tons per million of revenue. Financed emissions are 8.95 metric tons on 100.0 million invested, which is 0.09 metric tons per million invested. The most intense holding is number 4 by emissions per unit of revenue.
