Disability Insurance Income Gap

BeginnerPersonal FinanceAlso known as: disability insurance, income replacement, elimination period, waiting period
← Deposit Insurance Coverage and PremiumDiscount for Lack of Marketability (Private Companies) →

Finds: whether cash plus a policy would cover a period of disability.

When to use it: Use it to see whether disability cover would keep you afloat. A policy usually replaces only part of income and pays only after a waiting period, so you need savings to bridge that wait. Whether the benefit is taxed depends on who pays the premiums and on local rules, so this uses the benefit as received.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Gap\displaystyle \text{Gap}=E−p Y\displaystyle =E-p\,YWaiting-period cash\displaystyle \text{Waiting-period cash}=w×E\displaystyle =w\times E

In words: Monthly benefit = replacement % x monthly income; Monthly gap = monthly expenses − benefit; Cash needed for the waiting period = months waiting x monthly expenses

What each symbol means

EMonthly expenses you must still paymoney
pShare of income the policy replaces (often up to 60%)percent
YMonthly income before the disabilitymoney
wWaiting (elimination) period before benefits beginmonths

Worked example

Income is 5,200 a month, expenses are 3,800 and a policy replaces 60% after a 3-month wait. You hold 9,000 in cash.

Monthly benefit0.6 × 5,2003,120
Monthly gap3,800 − 3,120680
Cash for the waiting period3 × 3,80011,400
Months of expenses your cash covers9,000 ÷ 3,8002.4
Shortfall in the waiting periodmax(11,400 − 9,000, 0)2,400
The answer

The policy pays 3,120 a month, which leaves a gap of 680 against expenses (a positive gap is a shortfall, a negative one a surplus). To get through the 3-month wait you need 11,400, and your cash covers 2.4 months of it, leaving 2,400 still to find.
