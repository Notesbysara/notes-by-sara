Dividend Discount Model (Gordon Growth)

Intermediate

Also known as: DDM, Gordon growth, constant growth dividend model, dividend valuation

Value of a steadily growing dividend stream. Use it to value a stable, mature company that pays dividends growing at a steady rate. It is very sensitive to g and r, and it fails when g is close to or above r.

The formula

P0=D0(1+g)r−g=D1r−gP_{0}=\frac{D_{0}(1+g)}{r-g}=\frac{D_{1}}{r-g}

Price = D0 x (1 + g) / (r - g) = D1 / (r - g)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
P_0Intrinsic value of the share todaymoney
D_0Dividend just paidmoney
D_1Dividend expected next yearmoney
gConstant growth rate of dividends, foreverper year
rRequired return on equityper year

Worked example

A company just paid a dividend of 2. Dividends are expected to grow 4% a year forever and the required return is 9%. What is the share worth?

  1. D1 = D0 x (1 + g)2 x (1+0.04)= 2.08
  2. P0 = D1 / (r - g)d1 / (0.09-0.04)= 41.60

Next year's dividend is 2.08, so the share is worth 41.60.
