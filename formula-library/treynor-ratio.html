Treynor Ratio

IntermediateInvestments & Portfolio TheoryAlso known as: reward to volatility, return per unit of beta
← Trend Analysis: Index and Growth RatesTwo-Sample Z-Test for a Difference in Means →

Finds: return per unit of market risk.

When to use it: Use it to compare portfolios that are part of a larger, diversified holding, because it counts only market risk (beta) and ignores risk that diversification removes.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Treynor\displaystyle \text{Treynor}=Rp−rfβp\displaystyle =\frac{R_{p}-r_{f}}{\beta_{p}}

In words: Treynor ratio = (Portfolio return − Risk-free rate) / Portfolio beta

What each symbol means

R_pPortfolio returnper year
r_fRisk-free rateper year
beta_pPortfolio beta, its sensitivity to the marketnumber

Worked example

A fund returned 10% with a beta of 1.2. The risk-free rate is 3%. The market returned 8% with a beta of 1. What are their Treynor ratios?

Treynor(0.1 − 0.03) ÷ 1.25.83%
Market Treynor0.08 − 0.035%
The answer

The fund's Treynor ratio is 5.83%. The market's is 5%. The fund earned more per unit of market risk.
