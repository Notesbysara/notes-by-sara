Value at Risk: Parametric (Variance-Covariance)

IntermediateRisk ManagementAlso known as: VaR, value at risk, normal VaR, delta normal, Monte Carlo VaR
← Value at Risk: Historical SimulationValue Created by an Acquisition →

Finds: A loss limit at a chosen confidence level.

When to use it: Use it for a quick estimate of the loss that should not be exceeded with a chosen confidence over a set period. It assumes returns follow a normal distribution, which understates the chance of extreme losses. Monte Carlo VaR simulates many random paths instead, and historical VaR (next entry) reads the loss from past data.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

VaR\displaystyle VaR=V(zc σt−μt)\displaystyle =V\left(z_{c}\,\sigma\sqrt{t}-\mu t\right)

In words: VaR = Portfolio value x (z-score x Daily volatility x sqrt(days) − Daily mean return x days)

What each symbol means

VPortfolio valuemoney
z_cNumber of standard deviations for the confidence level (2.33 at 99%, 1.645 at 95%)number
sigmaDaily standard deviation of returnsper day
muAverage daily returnper day
tHolding period in daysdays

Worked example

A portfolio is worth 1,000,000. Daily returns have a mean of 0.03% and a standard deviation of 1.2%. What is the 99% VaR over 1 day and over 10 days?

z-score for the confidence level2.326
1-day VaR1,000,000 × (2.32635 × sd − 0.0003)27,616.17
VaR over t days1,000,000 × (2.32635 × sd × sqrt(10) − 0.0003 × 10)85,278.69
The answer

The z-score at 99% is 2.326. The 1-day VaR is 27,616.17 and the 10-day VaR is 85,278.69. We expect to lose more than that on only about 1 day in 100.
