Home Bias Measure and Diversification Cost

Intermediate

Also known as: home bias, domestic overweight, global market cap weight, foregone diversification

How strongly a portfolio leans toward home and what that does to risk. Use it to measure how far a portfolio leans toward home. Investors typically hold far more of their own market than its share of the world, which gives up diversification. A market-value weight is a neutral reference, not a prescription: currency risk, costs and the cost of living in home currency are good reasons to keep some tilt.

The formula

HB=wactual−wmkt1−wmkt,σp=w2σh2+(1−w)2σf2+2w(1−w)ρσhσfHB=\frac{w_{actual}-w_{mkt}}{1-w_{mkt}},\quad \sigma_{p}=\sqrt{w^{2}\sigma_{h}^{2}+(1-w)^{2}\sigma_{f}^{2}+2w(1-w)\rho\sigma_{h}\sigma_{f}}

Home bias = (domestic weight held - domestic share of world market value) / (1 - domestic share of world market value); Portfolio risk is the two-asset formula, evaluated at the actual weight and at the market-value weight

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
w_actualShare of the portfolio held in the home marketpercent
w_mktHome market's share of the global equity marketpercent
sigma_h, sigma_fVolatility of home and foreign equitiesper year
rhoCorrelation between themnumber

Worked example

An investor holds 85% in home equities. The home market is 40% of the world. Home volatility is 16%, foreign 18% and their correlation 0.7.

  1. Home bias = (held - market share) / (1 - market share)(0.85-0.4) / (1-0.4)= 75.0%
  2. Volatility at the actual weightsqrt(0.85^2 x 0.16^2+(1-0.85)^2 x 0.18^2+2 x 0.85 x (1-0.85) x 0.7 x 0.16 x 0.18)= 15.61%
  3. Volatility at the market-value weightsqrt(0.4^2 x 0.16^2+(1-0.4)^2 x 0.18^2+2 x 0.4 x (1-0.4) x 0.7 x 0.16 x 0.18)= 15.95%
  4. Extra volatility from the tilt0.156095-0.159489= -0.34%

Home bias is 75.0%. The portfolio volatility is 15.61% at 85% home, against 15.95% at the market-value weight of 40%, so the tilt adds -0.34% of volatility without any extra expected return in this simple comparison.
