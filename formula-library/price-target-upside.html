Price Target and Upside (Equity Research)

Beginner

Also known as: analyst price target, target price, research report valuation, implied return

The price an analyst's assumptions imply. Use it to understand how a research report arrives at a rating. The target price is only as good as the earnings forecast and the P/E assumption, so test how the answer changes when each is wrong.

The formula

Ptarget=EPSfwd×PEtarget,Upside=PtargetPnow−1P_{target}=EPS_{fwd}\times PE_{target},\quad \text{Upside}=\frac{P_{target}}{P_{now}}-1

Target price = Forward EPS x Target P/E; Upside = Target price / Current price - 1

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
EPS_fwdAnalyst's forecast of earnings per share over the next 12 monthsmoney
PE_targetP/E the analyst thinks the stock should trade on, often from peers or its own historynumber
P_nowCurrent share pricemoney

Worked example

An analyst forecasts earnings of 4.80 per share and thinks a P/E of 16 is fair. The stock trades at 60. What is the target price and the implied total return with a 2% dividend yield?

  1. Target price = EPS x P/E4.8 x 16= 76.80
  2. Upside = target / current - 176.8 / 60-1= 28.0%
  3. Expected total return = upside + dividend yield0.28+0.02= 30.0%

The target price is 76.80, an upside of 28.0%. Adding the dividend yield gives an expected total return of 30.0%.
