Price Target and Upside (Equity Research)

BeginnerEquity MarketsAlso known as: analyst price target, target price, research report valuation, implied return
← Price Improvement (Order Flow)Price-Weighted Index and Divisor →

Finds: the price an analyst's assumptions imply.

When to use it: Use it to understand how a research report arrives at a rating. The target price is only as good as the earnings forecast and the P/E assumption, so test how the answer changes when each is wrong.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Ptarget\displaystyle P_{target}=EPSfwd×PEtarget\displaystyle =EPS_{fwd}\times PE_{target}Upside\displaystyle \text{Upside}=PtargetPnow−1\displaystyle =\frac{P_{target}}{P_{now}}-1

In words: Target price = Forward EPS x Target P/E; Upside = Target price / Current price − 1

What each symbol means

EPS_fwdAnalyst's forecast of earnings per share over the next 12 monthsmoney
PE_targetP/E the analyst thinks the stock should trade on, often from peers or its own historynumber
P_nowCurrent share pricemoney

Worked example

An analyst forecasts earnings of 4.80 per share and thinks a P/E of 16 is fair. The stock trades at 60. What is the target price and the implied total return with a 2% dividend yield?

Target price4.8 × 1676.80
Upside76.8 ÷ 60 − 128%
Expected total return0.28 + 0.0230%
The answer

The target price is 76.80, an upside of 28.0%. Adding the dividend yield gives an expected total return of 30.0%.
