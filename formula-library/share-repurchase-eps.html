Share Repurchase Effect on EPS

Intermediate

Also known as: buyback EPS, share buyback accretion, repurchase shares

EPS after a buyback. Use it to see how a buyback raises earnings per share by shrinking the share count. This simple version ignores the interest lost on the cash spent, so it overstates the benefit.

The formula

Shares bought=AmountP,EPSnew=NIN−Shares bought\text{Shares bought}=\frac{\text{Amount}}{P},\quad EPS_{new}=\frac{NI}{N-\text{Shares bought}}

Shares bought = Amount spent / Price; New EPS = Net income / (Shares before - Shares bought)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
AmountCash spent on the buybackmoney
PPrice paid per sharemoney
NINet income, assumed unchangedmoney
NShares outstanding before the buybackshares

Worked example

A company with net income of 800 and 400 shares outstanding spends 1,000 buying back shares at 20 each. What happens to EPS?

  1. Shares bought = amount / price1000 / 20= 50
  2. EPS before = NI / N800 / 400= 2.00
  3. EPS after = NI / (N - shares bought)800 / (400-50)= 2.29
  4. Increase in EPS2.28571 / 2-1= 14.29%

The company retires 50 shares. EPS rises from 2.00 to 2.29, an increase of 14.29%.
