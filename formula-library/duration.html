Macaulay and Modified Duration

Intermediate

Also known as: bond duration, interest rate sensitivity, weighted average time to cash flows

How sensitive a bond is to interest rates. Use it to measure a bond's sensitivity to interest rates. A bond with a duration of 7 loses about 7% in price if yields rise by 1 percentage point. A higher coupon and a higher yield lower duration, and a longer maturity raises it.

The formula

DMac=1P∑ktk CFk(1+y/m)k,Dmod=DMac1+y/mD_{Mac}=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{k}\frac{t_{k}\,CF_{k}}{(1+y/m)^{k}},\quad D_{mod}=\frac{D_{Mac}}{1+y/m}

Macaulay duration = sum of (time x present value of each cash flow) / Price; Modified duration = Macaulay duration / (1 + yield / payments per year)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
t_kTime of cash flow k, in yearsyears
CF_kCash flow k, a coupon or the coupon plus face value at maturitymoney
PPrice of the bondmoney
D_MacMacaulay duration, the weighted average time to receive the cash flowsyears
D_modModified duration, the percentage price fall for a 1 percentage-point rise in yieldnumber

Worked example

A 10-year bond with a 5% coupon paid twice a year yields 6%. What are its Macaulay and modified duration?

  1. Price= 925.61
  2. Macaulay duration (years)= 7.895
  3. Modified duration = Macaulay / (1 + y / m)7.895 / (1+0.06 / 2)= 7.665

The Macaulay duration is 7.895 years, so on average the holder waits that long for the cash. The modified duration is 7.665, so a 1 percentage-point rise in yield cuts the price by about 7.665%.
