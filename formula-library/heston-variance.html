Heston Model: Average Variance and Feller Condition

Beginner

Also known as: stochastic volatility, Heston, variance mean reversion, vol of vol

The volatility level Heston expects over a horizon. Use it to understand what the Heston model says about volatility. Variance follows a CIR process: it reverts to a long-run level and is random. The expected average variance over the option's life, rather than today's variance, drives the option price level, while rho and xi shape the skew and smile.

The formula

E[vˉT]=θ+(v0−θ)1−e−κTκT,2κθ≥ξ2E[\bar{v}_{T}]=\theta+(v_{0}-\theta)\frac{1-e^{-\kappa T}}{\kappa T},\quad 2\kappa\theta\ge\xi^{2}

Expected average variance over T = theta + (v0 - theta) x (1 - e^(-kappa T)) / (kappa T); variance stays above zero if 2 x kappa x theta >= xi^2

Worked example

Current volatility is 24.5% (variance 0.06) and the long-run volatility is 20% (variance 0.04). The speed is 2, the vol of vol is 0.5 and the horizon is 1 year.

  1. Expected average variance = theta + (v0 - theta)(1 - e^(-kappa T)) / (kappa T)0.04+(0.06-0.04) x (1-exp(-2 x 1)) / (2 x 1)= 0.04865
  2. Average volatility = sqrt(average variance)sqrt(0.0486466)= 22.06%
  3. Current volatilitysqrt(0.06)= 24.49%
  4. Long-run volatilitysqrt(0.04)= 20.00%
  5. 2 x kappa x theta2 x 2 x 0.04= 0.1600
  6. xi squared0.5^2= 0.2500
  7. Feller condition holds? (1 = yes, 0 = no)1 if 0.16>=0.25 else 0= 0

The expected average variance over the year is 0.04865, which is an average volatility of 22.06%, between the current 24.49% and the long-run 20.00%. The Feller test compares 2 kappa theta = 0.1600 with xi squared = 0.2500. The condition fails, so variance can reach zero.
