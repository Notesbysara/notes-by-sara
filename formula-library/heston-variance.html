Heston Model: Average Variance and Feller Condition

BeginnerQuantitative FinanceAlso known as: stochastic volatility, Heston, variance mean reversion, vol of vol
← Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (Industry Concentration)Holding Period Return →

Finds: the volatility level Heston expects over a horizon.

When to use it: Use it to understand what the Heston model says about volatility. Variance follows a CIR process: it reverts to a long-run level and is random. The expected average variance over the option's life, rather than today's variance, drives the option price level, while rho and xi shape the skew and smile.

The formula

E[vˉT]\displaystyle E[\bar{v}_{T}]=θ+(v0−θ)1−e−κTκT\displaystyle =\theta+(v_{0}-\theta)\frac{1-e^{-\kappa T}}{\kappa T}2κθ≥ξ2\displaystyle 2\kappa\theta\ge\xi^{2}

In words: Expected average variance over T = theta + (v0 − theta) x (1 − e^(-kappa T)) / (kappa T); variance stays above zero if 2 x kappa x theta >= xi^2

Worked example

The answer

The expected average variance over the year is 0.04865, which is an average volatility of 22.06%, between the current 24.49% and the long-run 20%. The Feller test compares 2 kappa theta = 0.1600 with xi squared = 0.2500. The condition fails, so variance can reach zero.
