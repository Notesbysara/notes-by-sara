Reading the News: Is This Move Unusual?

BeginnerPractical Skills & CapstoneAlso known as: follow financial news critically, headline move, z-score of a daily move, how often does this happen
← Rational Bubble: Required Growth and SurvivalReal Return (Inflation-Adjusted) →

Finds: how unusual a market move is for that asset.

When to use it: Use it to put a dramatic headline in context. A fall of 3% may be an ordinary week for one stock and a rare event for another. Compare the move with the asset's own typical swings. Be careful: the normal curve understates how often extreme days occur, so treat the frequency as a lower bound.

In words: Size of the move in standard deviations = daily return / typical daily volatility; Chance of a move at least this large in either direction under a normal distribution = 2 x (1 − normal cumulative of |z|); About how many trading days between such moves = 1 / that chance; Real markets have fatter tails, so large moves happen more often than a normal curve suggests

Worked example

The answer

The move is −4.43 standard deviations. Under a normal distribution a move this large happens with a chance of 0.0009% on any day, about once every 105,419 trading days, or once in 418.3 years. The headline is 1 (1 = unusual, beyond 3 standard deviations).
