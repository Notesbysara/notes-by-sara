Quantity Theory of Money

IntermediateMonetary Economics & the Financial SystemAlso known as: equation of exchange, MV=PY, velocity of money, money growth and inflation
← Put-Call ParityQuick Ratio (Acid Test) →

Finds: inflation implied by money growth.

When to use it: Use it to see the link between money and prices. If velocity and output growth are steady, inflation roughly equals money growth minus real growth. In practice velocity is unstable, especially after financial crises or when interest rates change, so the link is loose in the short run and clearer over long periods and in high-inflation countries.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

MV\displaystyle MV=PY\displaystyle =PYV\displaystyle V=PYM\displaystyle =\frac{PY}{M}1+π\displaystyle 1+\pi=(1+gM)(1+gV)1+gY\displaystyle =\frac{(1+g_{M})(1+g_{V})}{1+g_{Y}}

In words: Money supply x velocity = price level x real output (= nominal GDP); Velocity = nominal GDP / money supply; Inflation = (1 + money growth) x (1 + velocity growth) / (1 + real output growth) − 1

What each symbol means

MMoney supplymoney
VVelocity: how many times a unit of money is used in a yearnumber
P, YPrice level and real output; PY is nominal GDPmoney
gGrowth rate of each variablepercent

Worked example

The money supply is 21,000 and nominal GDP 28,000. Money grows 8%, velocity falls 1% and real output grows 2.5%.

Velocity28,000 ÷ 21,0001.333
Inflation(1 + 0.08) × (1+−0.01) ÷ (1 + 0.025) − 14.31%
Approximation gM + gV - gY0.08+−0.01 − 0.0254.50%
Inflation if velocity is steady(1 + 0.08) ÷ (1 + 0.025) − 15.37%
The answer

Velocity is 1.333. With these growth rates inflation is 4.31% (the approximation gM + gV - gY gives 4.50%). If money grew 8% while velocity were steady, inflation would be 5.37%.
