Operating Working Capital Analysis

Intermediate

Also known as: working capital to sales, operating working capital, incremental working capital, NWC intensity

How much working capital growth requires. Use it to see how much money the day-to-day business ties up as it grows. Operating working capital leaves out cash and financial debt, which are financing items. A company with a high incremental intensity needs more funding as it grows.

The formula

OWC=(AR+Inv+Prepaid)−(AP+Accrued),Intensity=OWCSalesOWC=(AR+Inv+\text{Prepaid})-(AP+\text{Accrued}),\quad \text{Intensity}=\frac{OWC}{\text{Sales}}

Operating working capital = (Receivables + Inventory + Prepaid) - (Payables + Accrued liabilities); Intensity = OWC / Sales; Incremental intensity = Change in OWC / Change in sales

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
OWCOperating working capital, excluding cash and debtmoney
IntensityHow much working capital is tied up per unit of salespercent
Incremental intensityExtra working capital needed for each extra unit of salespercent

Worked example

A company has receivables of 200, inventory of 150, prepaid items of 30, payables of 100 and accrued liabilities of 60. Last year the same items gave an operating working capital of 190. Sales were 1,500 this year and 1,400 last year.

  1. Operating working capital200+150+30-100-60= 220.00
  2. Intensity = OWC / sales220 / 1500= 14.67%
  3. Change in OWC220-190= 30.00
  4. Change in sales1500-1400= 100.00
  5. Incremental intensity = change in OWC / change in sales30 / 100= 30.0%

Operating working capital is 220.00, or 14.67% of sales. It grew by 30.00, while sales grew by 100.00, so each extra unit of sales needed 30.0% of working capital.
