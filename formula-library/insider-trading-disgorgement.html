Insider Trading: Profit Gained and Maximum Civil Penalty

BeginnerRegulation, Ethics & ESGAlso known as: insider trading, loss avoided, disgorgement, three times profit
← Infrastructure Project: DSCR and Loan Life CoverInsurance Underwriting Profit and Float →

Finds: the profit and maximum civil penalty in an insider case.

When to use it: Use it to see the scale of the money at stake in an insider trading case. Regulators ask the trader to give up the gain and may add a penalty of up to three times the gain. For a person who sold ahead of bad news, the same maths measures the loss avoided. Courts decide the actual amounts using their own rules.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

G\displaystyle G=q (Pafter−Ptrade)\displaystyle =q\,(P_{after}-P_{trade})Max civil penalty\displaystyle \text{Max civil penalty}=3G\displaystyle =3G

In words: Profit gained (buyer) = shares x (price after the news is public − price paid); Loss avoided (seller) = shares x (price paid originally − price after bad news); Disgorgement = profit gained or loss avoided; The US civil penalty can be up to three times that amount, in addition to disgorgement, and criminal penalties may apply

What each symbol means

qShares tradedshares
P_tradePrice at which the insider tradedmoney
P_afterPrice after the information became publicmoney

Worked example

An employee buys 50,000 shares at 38 before a takeover bid is announced. The price rises to 52.

Gain (disgorgement)50,000 × (52 − 38)700,000
Maximum civil penalty (3 x gain)3 × 700,0002,100,000
Total exposure with disgorgement700,000 + 2,100,0002,800,000
Gain as a share of the amount invested700,000 ÷ (50,000 × 38)36.8%
The answer

The gain is 700,000. A civil penalty of up to three times the gain would be 2,100,000, so the total exposure with disgorgement is up to 2,800,000. The gain is 36.8% of the amount invested.
