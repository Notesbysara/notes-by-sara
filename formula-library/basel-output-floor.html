Basel III Endgame: Output Floor on Risk-Weighted Assets

Intermediate

Also known as: Basel III endgame, 72.5% floor, internal models floor, standardized approach floor

Whether the output floor reduces a bank's capital ratio. Use it to see how the floor stops internal models from producing capital that is too low. A bank with sophisticated models can report much lower risk-weighted assets than the standard method gives. The floor puts a lower bound at 72.5% of the standard figure. It is most important for banks with low-risk mortgage and corporate books under internal models.

The formula

RWA=max⁡ ⁣(RWAinternal, 0.725×RWAstandardised)RWA=\max\!\left(RWA_{internal},\ 0.725\times RWA_{standardised}\right)

Risk-weighted assets used for the capital ratio = the larger of (assets as measured by the bank's own models) and (72.5% of assets as measured by the standardized approach); The floor binds when internal models give less than 72.5% of the standardized amount; Capital ratio = CET1 / floored RWA

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
RWA_internalRisk-weighted assets from the bank's internal modelsmoney

0.725Floor level (phased in over several years)number

Worked example

A bank's internal models give risk-weighted assets of 380. The standardized approach gives 640. CET1 capital is 55.

  1. Floor = 72.5% x standardized RWA0.725 x 640= 464.00
  2. RWA used = max(internal, floor)max(380, 464)= 464.00
  3. CET1 ratio on the floored RWA55 / 464= 11.85%
  4. CET1 ratio on internal models55 / 380= 14.47%
  5. Fall in the ratio (percentage points)(0.144737-0.118534) x 100= 2.62
  6. Extra capital to keep the old ratio0.144737 x 464-55= 12.16

The floor is 464.00. Because the internal figure of 380 is below the floor, the bank must use 464.00. Its CET1 ratio is 11.85% compared with 14.47% on its own models, a fall of 2.62 percentage points. Extra capital to keep the old ratio would be 12.16.
