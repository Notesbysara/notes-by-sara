Minimum-Variance Portfolio (Two Assets)

IntermediateInvestments & Portfolio TheoryAlso known as: Markowitz, mean variance optimization, efficient frontier, global minimum variance
← Minimum-Variance Hedge RatioModel Risk: Pricing Error Metrics →

Finds: the lowest-risk mix of two assets.

When to use it: Use it to find the mix of two assets with the lowest risk. It is the left-most point of the efficient frontier. A negative weight means that asset should be sold short, and this formula allows it.

The formula

w1∗\displaystyle w_{1}^{*}=σ22−ρσ1σ2σ12+σ22−2ρσ1σ2\displaystyle =\frac{\sigma_{2}^{2}-\rho\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}-2\rho\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}

In words: Weight in asset 1 = (s2^2 − rho x s1 x s2) / (s1^2 + s2^2 − 2 x rho x s1 x s2), and asset 2 gets the rest

What each symbol means

w_1*Weight in asset 1 that gives the lowest possible portfolio riskpercent
sigma_1, sigma_2Standard deviations of the two assetsper year
rhoCorrelation between the assetsnumber

Worked example

Asset 1 has a standard deviation of 20% and expected return of 10%. Asset 2 has 12% and 6%. Their correlation is 0.3. What mix has the lowest risk?

The answer

The lowest-risk mix is 18% in asset 1 and 82% in asset 2. Its standard deviation is 11.45%, below that of either asset, with an expected return of 6.72%.
