Policy Rate Pass-Through to Banks and Borrowers

Intermediate

Also known as: how monetary policy reaches banks and borrowers, deposit beta, loan beta, transmission mechanism

How a policy move changes bank income and borrower costs. Use it to follow a central bank's rate move through to the people it affects. Banks pass higher rates to borrowers faster than to savers, so net interest income often rises when rates rise, unless depositors move money to better rates, which pushes the deposit beta up over time. Borrowers on floating loans feel the change at once, which is how policy slows spending.

The formula

Δrloan=βL Δrp,ΔNII=βL Ars Δrp−βD Drs Δrp\Delta r_{loan}=\beta_{L}\,\Delta r_{p},\quad \Delta NII=\beta_{L}\,A_{rs}\,\Delta r_{p}-\beta_{D}\,D_{rs}\,\Delta r_{p}

Change in loan rate = loan beta x change in policy rate; Change in deposit rate = deposit beta x change in policy rate; Change in bank net interest income = loan beta x rate-sensitive loans x change - deposit beta x rate-sensitive deposits x change; Extra annual interest for a floating borrower = loan rate change x balance

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
beta_LLoan beta: share of a policy rate change passed on to loan ratesnumber
beta_DDeposit beta: share passed on to deposit rates (often below the loan beta, so margins widen when rates rise)number
A_rs, D_rsFloating-rate loans and rate-sensitive depositsmoney

Worked example

The policy rate rises 1 point. Loan beta is 0.6 and deposit beta 0.3. The bank has 3,000 of floating loans and 5,000 of rate-sensitive deposits. A borrower has a 300,000 floating loan.

  1. Change in loan rate0.6 x 0.01= 0.60%
  2. Change in deposit rate0.3 x 0.01= 0.30%
  3. Extra interest earned on loans3000 x 0.006= 18.00
  4. Extra interest paid on deposits5000 x 0.003= 15.00
  5. Change in net interest income18-15= 3.00
  6. Extra yearly interest for the borrower300000 x 0.006= 1,800.00
  7. Extra per month1800 / 12= 150.00

Loan rates rise 0.60% and deposit rates 0.30%. The bank earns 18.00 more on loans and pays 15.00 more on deposits, so net interest income changes by 3.00. The floating borrower pays 1,800.00 more a year, or 150.00 a month.
