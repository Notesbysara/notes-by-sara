Bond Convexity

Intermediate

Also known as: curvature of the price-yield curve, second order interest rate risk

The curvature that duration misses. Use it to refine the duration estimate. Convexity is the curve in the price-yield relationship. It is good for the holder, because the price rises more when yields fall than it drops when they rise by the same amount. Zero-coupon and long bonds have more of it.

The formula

C=1P (1+y/m)2 m2∑kk(k+1) CFk(1+y/m)kC=\frac{1}{P\,(1+y/m)^{2}\,m^{2}}\sum_{k}\frac{k(k+1)\,CF_{k}}{(1+y/m)^{k}}

Convexity = [sum over each period k of k x (k + 1) x PV of cash flow k] / [Price x (1 + y/m)^2 x m^2]

Worked example

A 10-year bond with a 5% coupon paid twice a year yields 6%. What is its convexity, and how much does it add to the estimated price change for a 2 percentage-point fall in yields?

  1. Convexity= 71.79
  2. Modified duration= 7.665
  3. Duration part = -D x dy-7.66505 x -0.02= 15.33%
  4. Convexity part = 0.5 x C x dy^20.5 x 71.7854 x -0.02^2= 1.44%

The convexity is 71.79. For a 2 percentage-point fall in yield the convexity term adds 1.44% to the price, on top of the 15.33% from duration.
