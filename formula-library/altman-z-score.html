Altman Z-Score

Intermediate

Also known as: bankruptcy prediction, credit analysis, financial distress score, Z score

How likely a company is to fail. Use it as a quick screen of a listed manufacturing company's chance of failing within about two years. This is the original 1968 version for public manufacturers. Other versions exist for private firms and services. Treat the result as a signal to investigate, not a verdict.

The formula

Z=1.2WCTA+1.4RETA+3.3EBITTA+0.6MVETL+1.0SalesTAZ=1.2\frac{WC}{TA}+1.4\frac{RE}{TA}+3.3\frac{EBIT}{TA}+0.6\frac{MVE}{TL}+1.0\frac{Sales}{TA}

Z = 1.2 x Working capital/Total assets + 1.4 x Retained earnings/Total assets + 3.3 x EBIT/Total assets + 0.6 x Market value of equity/Total liabilities + 1.0 x Sales/Total assets

ZScore. Above 2.99 is the safe zone, below 1.81 is distress, in between is the gray zonenumber

Worked example

A manufacturer has working capital of 200, retained earnings of 350, EBIT of 180, a market value of equity of 900, sales of 1,500, total assets of 1,200 and total liabilities of 700. What is its Z-score?

  1. Working capital / total assets200 / 1200= 0.1667
  2. Retained earnings / total assets350 / 1200= 0.2917
  3. EBIT / total assets180 / 1200= 0.1500
  4. Market equity / total liabilities900 / 700= 1.2857
  5. Sales / total assets1500 / 1200= 1.2500
  6. Z = 1.2 x x1 + 1.4 x x2 + 3.3 x x3 + 0.6 x x4 + 1.0 x x51.2 x 0.166667+1.4 x 0.291667+3.3 x 0.15+0.6 x 1.28571+1.0 x 1.25= 3.12

The Z-score is 3.12. That is above 2.99, which puts the company in the safe zone, so a default looks unlikely on this measure.
