Deposit Insurance Coverage and Premium

Beginner

Also known as: deposit insurance, insured deposits, coverage limit, FDIC premium

How much of the deposit base is protected. Use it to see how much of a bank's deposits are protected. Insured depositors have little reason to run, so a high insured share lowers run risk. Large uninsured balances are the flighty part. Coverage rules (limits, account categories, premium base) differ by country, so enter your own scheme's numbers. Limits here are per depositor per bank.

The formula

Insured=∑jmin⁡(Bj,L),Premium=ρ×Assessment base\text{Insured}=\sum_{j}\min(B_{j},L),\quad \text{Premium}=\rho\times\text{Assessment base}

Insured amount = sum over depositors of the smaller of their balance and the coverage limit; Uninsured = total deposits - insured; Premium = premium rate x assessment base (often insured deposits or total liabilities, depending on the scheme)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
B_jBalance of depositor j at this bankmoney
LCoverage limit per depositor per bankmoney
rhoAnnual premium rate paid by the bankpercent

Worked example

Five depositors have 40,000, 120,000, 260,000, 500,000 and 90,000. The limit is 250,000 per depositor and the bank pays 0.15% a year on insured deposits.

  1. Total deposits= 1,010,000.00
  2. Insured = sum of min(balance, limit)= 750,000.00
  3. Insured share750000 / 1,010,000= 74.3%
  4. Uninsured deposits1,010,000-750000= 260,000.00
  5. Premium = rate x insured deposits0.0015 x 750000= 1,125.00

Total deposits are 1,010,000.00. The insured part is 750,000.00, or 74.3% of the total, leaving 260,000.00 uninsured. The annual premium is 1,125.00.
