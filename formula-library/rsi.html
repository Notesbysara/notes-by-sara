Relative Strength Index (RSI)

IntermediateEquity MarketsAlso known as: technical analysis, overbought oversold, momentum oscillator, RSI 14
← REIT Funds From Operations (FFO) and AFFORelevering Beta →

Finds: recent price momentum on a 0 to 100 scale.

When to use it: Use it as a gauge of recent momentum. Chartists use 70 and 30 as warning levels. This is the simple version with plain averages. Wilder's original smooths the averages over time, so values can differ a little.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

RSI\displaystyle RSI=100−1001+RS\displaystyle =100-\frac{100}{1+RS}RS\displaystyle RS=Average gainAverage loss\displaystyle =\frac{\text{Average gain}}{\text{Average loss}}

In words: RSI = 100 − 100 / (1 + RS), where RS = average of the up-moves / average of the down-moves over the period

What each symbol means

Average gainTotal of the price rises divided by the number of periodsmoney
Average lossTotal of the price falls divided by the number of periodsmoney
RSINumber from 0 to 100. Above 70 is often read as overbought and below 30 as oversoldnumber

Worked example

A stock closed at these prices over 15 days: 44, 44.34, 44.09, 44.15, 43.61, 44.33, 44.83, 45.10, 45.42, 45.84, 46.08, 45.89, 46.03, 45.61, 46.28. What is the 14-day RSI?

Average gain0.2629
Average loss0.1000
RS0.262857 ÷ 0.12.629
RSI100 − 100 ÷ (1 + 2.62857)72.4
The answer

The average gain is 0.2629 and the average loss is 0.1000. The RSI is 72.4. That is in the upper half of the range, so recent momentum has been upward, but it has not reached the usual 70 warning level.
