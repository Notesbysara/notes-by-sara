Buy Now Pay Later: Merchant Break-Even Sales Lift

Intermediate

Also known as: BNPL, pay in four, merchant fee, conversion uplift

Whether BNPL pays for the merchant and the lender. Use it to decide whether a merchant gains from offering pay-in-installments. The fee is typically higher than a card fee, so sales must rise enough to pay it. For the lender, the merchant fee must cover credit losses, funding and late-payment risk. The calculation counts only merchant profit and ignores returns and repeat purchases.

The formula

Δπ=(1+u) (m−f)−m,u∗=fm−f\Delta\pi=(1+u)\,(m-f)-m,\quad u^{*}=\frac{f}{m-f}

Merchant profit per 100 of base sales = base sales x margin; with BNPL: sales x (1 + lift) x (margin - fee); Break-even lift u* = fee / (margin - fee); Customer pays nothing extra when paid on time, so the lender earns from merchant fees and late fees

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
mMerchant gross margin on a salepercent
fBNPL fee charged to the merchantpercent
uIncrease in sales caused by offering BNPLpercent
u*Smallest increase that leaves the merchant no worse offpercent

Worked example

A merchant earns a 25% margin and BNPL costs 4% of each sale. It expects sales to rise 12% with BNPL. The lender sees 2% of balances default.

  1. Profit per 100 of base sales100 x 0.25= 25.00
  2. Profit with BNPL per 100 of base sales100 x (1+0.12) x (0.25-0.04)= 23.52
  3. Change in profit23.52-25= -1.48
  4. Break-even lift = f / (m - f)0.04 / (0.25-0.04)= 19.0%
  5. Lender fee income on one basket100 x 0.04= 4.00
  6. Lender expected default loss on one basket100 x 0.02= 2.00
  7. Lender net before funding and costs4-2= 2.00

Without BNPL the merchant earns 25.00 per 100 of sales. With a 12% lift it earns 23.52, a change of -1.48. The break-even lift is 19.0%. The lender collects 4.00 in fees on a 100 basket and expects to lose 2.00 from defaults, leaving 2.00.
