Buy Now Pay Later: Merchant Break-Even Sales Lift

IntermediateFintech & Digital FinanceAlso known as: BNPL, pay in four, merchant fee, conversion uplift
← Bull Call SpreadBuyout Returns and Value Creation Bridge →

Finds: whether BNPL pays for the merchant and the lender.

When to use it: Use it to decide whether a merchant gains from offering pay-in-installments. The fee is typically higher than a card fee, so sales must rise enough to pay it. For the lender, the merchant fee must cover credit losses, funding and late-payment risk. The calculation counts only merchant profit and ignores returns and repeat purchases.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Δπ\displaystyle \Delta\pi=(1+u) (m−f)−m\displaystyle =(1+u)\,(m-f)-mu∗\displaystyle u^{*}=fm−f\displaystyle =\frac{f}{m-f}

In words: Merchant profit per 100 of base sales = base sales x margin; with BNPL: sales x (1 + lift) x (margin − fee); Break-even lift u* = fee / (margin − fee); Customer pays nothing extra when paid on time, so the lender earns from merchant fees and late fees

What each symbol means

mMerchant gross margin on a salepercent
fBNPL fee charged to the merchantpercent
uIncrease in sales caused by offering BNPLpercent
u*Smallest increase that leaves the merchant no worse offpercent

Worked example

A merchant earns a 25% margin and BNPL costs 4% of each sale. It expects sales to rise 12% with BNPL. The lender sees 2% of balances default.

Profit per 100 of base sales100 × 0.2525
Profit with BNPL per 100 of base sales100 × (1 + 0.12) × (0.25 − 0.04)23.52
Change in profit23.52 − 25−1.48
Break-even lift0.04 ÷ (0.25 − 0.04)19%
Lender fee income on one basket100 × 0.044
Lender expected default loss on one basket100 × 0.022
Lender net before funding and costs4 − 22
The answer

Without BNPL the merchant earns 25 per 100 of sales. With a 12% lift it earns 23.52, a change of −1.48. The break-even lift is 19.0%. The lender collects 4 in fees on a 100 basket and expects to lose 2 from defaults, leaving 2.
