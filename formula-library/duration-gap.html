Duration Gap and Equity Sensitivity

IntermediateBanking & Financial InstitutionsAlso known as: asset liability management, ALM, interest rate risk of a bank, DGAP
← DuPont Analysis (3-Step and 5-Step)DV01 (Dollar Value of a Basis Point) →

Finds: A bank's equity risk from a parallel rate move.

When to use it: Use it to see how a rise in rates changes a bank's equity. A bank that funds long assets with short deposits has a positive duration gap, so higher rates cut asset values by more than they cut liabilities and equity falls. It shows value risk, whereas the repricing gap shows effects on income. It assumes a parallel yield shift and uses approximations that are less accurate for large moves.

The formula

DGAP\displaystyle DGAP=DA−LADL\displaystyle =D_{A}-\frac{L}{A}D_{L}ΔE≈−DGAP×A×Δy1+y\displaystyle \Delta E\approx-DGAP\times A\times\frac{\Delta y}{1+y}

In words: Duration gap = duration of assets − (liabilities / assets) x duration of liabilities; Change in equity value = − duration gap x assets x (change in yield) / (1 + yield)

What each symbol means

D_A, D_LDuration of assets and of liabilities, in yearsyears
A, LMarket value of assets and liabilitiesmoney
Delta yChange in the general level of yieldspercent
DGAPDuration gap: positive means assets are more rate-sensitive than fundingyears

Worked example

Assets are 8,000 with a duration of 4.5 years, liabilities 7,600 with a duration of 2.0 years. Yields are 5% and rise by 1 percentage point.

Duration gap4.5 − (7,600 ÷ 8,000) × 22.600
Change in equity−2.6 × 8,000 × 0.01 ÷ (1 + 0.05)−198.10
Equity8,000 − 7,600400
Change as a share of equity−198.095 ÷ 400−49.5%
Liability duration that gives zero gap4.5 × 8,000 ÷ 7,6004.74
The answer

The duration gap is 2.600 years. A 1 point rise in yields lowers equity value by −198.10, which is −49.5% of the book equity of 400. To fully immunize, liability duration would need to be 4.74 years.
