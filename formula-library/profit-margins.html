Profit Margins

Beginner

Also known as: gross margin, operating margin, net margin, margin ratios

Profit as a share of sales. Use these to see how much of each unit of sales is kept as profit at three levels. Compare them over time or against other companies in the same industry.

The formula

Gross=R−COGSR, Operating=R−COGS−OpexR, Net=NIR\text{Gross}=\frac{R-COGS}{R},\ \text{Operating}=\frac{R-COGS-Opex}{R},\ \text{Net}=\frac{NI}{R}

Gross margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue; Operating margin = (Revenue - COGS - Opex) / Revenue; Net margin = Net income / Revenue

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
RRevenue, sales for the periodmoney
COGSCost of goods sold, the direct cost of the items soldmoney
OpexOperating expenses such as wages, rent and marketingmoney
NINet income, the profit after all expenses, interest and taxmoney

Worked example

A company has revenue of 5,000, cost of goods sold of 3,000, operating expenses of 800 and net income of 600. What are its three margins?

  1. Gross margin = (revenue - COGS) / revenue(5000-3000) / 5000= 40.00%
  2. Operating margin = (revenue - COGS - opex) / revenue(5000-3000-800) / 5000= 24.00%
  3. Net margin = net income / revenue600 / 5000= 12.00%

The gross margin is 40.00%, the operating margin is 24.00% and the net margin is 12.00%.
