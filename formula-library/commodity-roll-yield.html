Commodity Futures: Basis and Roll Yield

Intermediate

Also known as: contango, backwardation, roll return, futures curve

The gain or cost of rolling commodity futures. Use it to see what rolling a futures position costs or earns. In contango (the futures curve slopes up) a long investor sells the cheaper near contract and buys the dearer far one, losing roll yield. In backwardation the curve slopes down and the long investor gains.

The formula

Basis=S−F,Roll yield per roll=FnearFfar−1\text{Basis}=S-F,\quad \text{Roll yield per roll}=\frac{F_{near}}{F_{far}}-1

Basis = Spot - Futures; Roll yield = Near futures price / Next futures price - 1 (negative in contango, positive in backwardation)

Worked example

Oil spot is 80, the near contract is 81.20 and the next contract (one month later) is 82.50. What is the roll yield, and what is it annualized?

  1. Futures minus spot (positive means contango)81.2-80= 1.20
  2. Roll yield per roll = near / far - 181.2 / 82.5-1= -1.58%
  3. Annualized roll yield = (1 + roll yield)^(12 / months) - 1(1+-0.0157576)^(12 / 1)-1= -17.35%

The market is in contango, since futures trade above spot by 1.20. Rolling from the near to the next contract gives a roll yield of -1.58% each month, or about -17.35% a year, which reduces the return of a long position even if spot prices do not change.
