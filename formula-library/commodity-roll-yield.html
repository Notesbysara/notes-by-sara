Commodity Futures: Basis and Roll Yield

IntermediateDerivativesAlso known as: contango, backwardation, roll return, futures curve
← Commercial Paper Price and YieldCommodity Futures: Roll Yield and Total Return →

Finds: the gain or cost of rolling commodity futures.

When to use it: Use it to see what rolling a futures position costs or earns. In contango (the futures curve slopes up) a long investor sells the cheaper near contract and buys the dearer far one, losing roll yield. In backwardation the curve slopes down and the long investor gains.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Basis\displaystyle \text{Basis}=S−F\displaystyle =S-FRoll yield per roll\displaystyle \text{Roll yield per roll}=FnearFfar−1\displaystyle =\frac{F_{near}}{F_{far}}-1

In words: Basis = Spot − Futures; Roll yield = Near futures price / Next futures price − 1 (negative in contango, positive in backwardation)

What each symbol means

SSpot pricemoney
F_nearPrice of the near futures contract that is about to expiremoney
F_farPrice of the next contract that replaces itmoney

Worked example

Oil spot is 80, the near contract is 81.20 and the next contract (one month later) is 82.50. What is the roll yield, and what is it annualized?

Futures minus spot (positive means contango)81.2 − 801.20
Roll yield per roll81.2 ÷ 82.5 − 1−1.58%
Annualized roll yield(1+−0.0157576)^(12 ÷ 1) − 1−17.35%
The answer

The market is in contango, since futures trade above spot by 1.20. Rolling from the near to the next contract gives a roll yield of −1.58% each month, or about −17.35% a year, which reduces the return of a long position even if spot prices do not change.
