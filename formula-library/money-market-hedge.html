Hedging a Foreign Currency Receivable

IntermediateInternational FinanceAlso known as: currency risk for multinationals, transaction exposure, forward hedge vs money market hedge, natural hedge
← Hedge Fund Fees: Management, Performance, Hurdle and High-Water MarkHerding: Information Cascade Posterior →

Finds: what a hedge locks in compared with leaving the exposure open.

When to use it: Use it to compare ways to lock in the value of a foreign receivable. A forward is simplest. A money market hedge borrows the foreign currency now, so the receivable repays the loan, and gives almost the same result when parity holds. The two differ if the quoted forward is off parity or if borrowing costs differ. Staying unhedged leaves the outcome to the future spot rate. Costs and credit limits can shape the choice.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

USDMM\displaystyle USD_{MM}=FC1+rfT S (1+rdT)\displaystyle =\frac{FC}{1+r_{f}T}\,S\,(1+r_{d}T)USDfwd\displaystyle USD_{fwd}=FC×F\displaystyle =FC\times F

In words: Forward hedge: dollars received = foreign amount x forward rate. Money market hedge: borrow the present value of the receivable in foreign currency (amount / (1 + foreign rate x T)), convert it at spot, and invest the dollars at the domestic rate; dollars at the due date = that amount x (1 + domestic rate x T). Unhedged: dollars = foreign amount x the spot rate at the due date

Worked example

A US company will receive 1,000,000 euros in 6 months. Spot is 1.0850 USD per euro, the 6-month forward is 1.0950, the euro rate is 3% and the dollar rate 5%.

Forward hedge: FC x F1,000,000 × 1.0951,095,000
Foreign currency to borrow now1,000,000 ÷ (1 + 0.03 × 0.5)985,221.67
Money market hedge: borrow, convert at spot, invest in USD985,222 × 1.085 × (1 + 0.05 × 0.5)1,095,689.66
Forward minus money market hedge1,095,000 − 1,095,690−689.66
Unhedged, low scenario1,000,000 × 1.051,050,000
Unhedged, high scenario1,000,000 × 1.151,150,000
Range of unhedged outcomes1,150,000 − 1,050,000100,000
The answer

The forward hedge locks in 1,095,000. The money market hedge gives 1,095,689.66, a difference of −689.66. Unhedged, the receipt would be 1,050,000 if the euro falls to 1.05 and 1,150,000 if it rises to 1.15. The hedges remove that range of 100,000.
