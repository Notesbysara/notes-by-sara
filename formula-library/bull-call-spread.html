Bull Call Spread

IntermediateDerivativesAlso known as: vertical spread, debit spread, call spread
← Brownian Motion ProbabilitiesBuy Now Pay Later: Merchant Break-Even Sales Lift →

Finds: the limits and breakeven of a bull call spread.

When to use it: Use it when you expect a moderate rise. The call you sell pays for part of the call you buy, so the cost is lower, but the profit is capped at the higher strike.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Max profit\displaystyle \text{Max profit}=(K2−K1)−(C1−C2)\displaystyle =(K_{2}-K_{1})-(C_{1}-C_{2})Max loss\displaystyle \text{Max loss}=C1−C2\displaystyle =C_{1}-C_{2}

In words: Buy a call at the lower strike K1 and sell a call at the higher strike K2. Net cost = C1 − C2; Max profit = (K2 − K1) − net cost; Breakeven = K1 + net cost

What each symbol means

K_1, K_2Lower and higher strikesmoney
C_1, C_2Premium paid for the lower-strike call and received for the higher-strike callmoney
S_TPrice at expirymoney

Worked example

A stock is at 52. You buy a call with a strike of 50 for 6 and sell a call with a strike of 60 for 2. What are the cost, maximum profit, maximum loss, breakeven and the profit if the stock ends at 57?

Net cost6 − 24
Maximum profit(60 − 50) − 46
Breakeven50 + 454
Profit at expirymax(57 − 50, 0) − max(57 − 60, 0) − 43
The answer

The spread costs 4, which is also the most you can lose. The most you can make is 6, reached at 60 or above. The breakeven is 54. If the stock ends at 57 the profit is 3.
