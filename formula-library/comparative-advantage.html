Comparative Advantage and Gains from Trade

Beginner

Also known as: global trade and supply chains, opportunity cost, terms of trade, specialization

Who should produce what, and the price range at which both gain. Use it to see why trade benefits both sides even when one country is better at everything. What counts is the relative cost, not the absolute cost. Each country should specialize where its opportunity cost is lower and trade for the rest. Real trade also faces transport costs, tariffs, and supply-chain risk, which can outweigh small gains.

The formula

OCWA=aWAaCA,OCWB=aWBaCB,OCWB<TOT<OCWAOC_{W}^{A}=\frac{a_{W}^{A}}{a_{C}^{A}},\quad OC_{W}^{B}=\frac{a_{W}^{B}}{a_{C}^{B}},\quad OC_{W}^{B}<TOT<OC_{W}^{A}

Opportunity cost of one unit of wine, in cloth = hours per wine / hours per cloth. The country with the lower opportunity cost has the comparative advantage. Both gain if they trade at a price (terms of trade) between the two opportunity costs

Worked example

Country A needs 2 hours per cloth and 4 hours per wine. Country B needs 3 hours per cloth and 3 hours per wine.

  1. A: cloth given up per wine = aw / ac4 / 2= 2.000
  2. B: cloth given up per wine = bw / bc3 / 3= 1.000
  3. A: wine given up per cloth2 / 4= 0.500
  4. B: wine given up per cloth3 / 3= 1.000
  5. Lower limit of the terms of trade (cloth per wine)min(2, 1)= 1.000
  6. Upper limit of the terms of trademax(2, 1)= 2.000
  7. A midpoint price(1+2) / 2= 1.500
  8. Cloth saved per wine by the higher-cost producer, at the midpoint2-1.5= 0.500

In wine terms, A gives up 2.000 cloth per wine and B gives up 1.000. Wine is cheaper for B. For cloth, A gives up 0.500 wine per cloth and B gives up 1.000. Any price between 1.000 and 2.000 cloth per wine benefits both: at 1.500, A saves 0.500 cloth per wine it imports.
