Cybersecurity: Return on Security Investment

IntermediateFintech & Digital FinanceAlso known as: cybersecurity and digital fraud, ROSI, risk reduction, expected annual loss
← Customer Lifetime Value to Acquisition Cost (LTV:CAC)Days Sales Outstanding and Receivables Turnover →

Finds: whether a security control is worth its cost.

When to use it: Use it to compare security spending with the loss it prevents. Estimate the yearly expected loss before and after a control (such as multi-factor authentication or staff training). A positive return means the control pays for itself in expected terms, though rare severe events are hard to estimate and one breach can threaten a firm.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

ALE\displaystyle ALE=∑SLE×ARO\displaystyle =\sum SLE\times AROROSI\displaystyle ROSI=(ALEbefore−ALEafter)−CostCost\displaystyle =\frac{(ALE_{before}-ALE_{after})-\text{Cost}}{\text{Cost}}

In words: Expected yearly loss (ALE) = loss per incident x incidents per year, summed over threats; Risk reduction = ALE before − ALE after the control; Return on security investment = (risk reduction − cost of the control) / cost of the control; Break-even reduction = cost of the control / ALE before

What each symbol means

SLELoss from one incidentmoney
AROExpected incidents per yearnumber
ALEExpected loss per yearmoney

Worked example

Phishing and account takeover are expected to cost 1,200,000 a year (3 incidents of 400,000). A control costing 250,000 a year is expected to cut incidents to 0.75 a year.

Expected loss before400,000 × 31,200,000
Expected loss after400,000 × 0.75300,000
Reduction in expected loss1,200,000 − 300,000900,000
Net benefit900,000 − 250,000650,000
Return on security investment650,000 ÷ 250,000260%
Break-even reduction250,000 ÷ 1,200,00020.8%
The answer

Expected loss falls from 1,200,000 to 300,000 a year, a saving of 900,000. After the cost of the control the net benefit is 650,000, a return on security investment of 260%. The control breaks even if it removes 20.8% of the loss.
