Cash Ratio and Defensive Interval

BeginnerFinancial Statement AnalysisAlso known as: liquidity ratios, cash ratio, defensive interval measure, days of cash
← Cash Flow Quality RatiosCash Runway →

Finds: how long a company can pay its way from liquid assets.

When to use it: Use it as the strictest liquidity test, ahead of the current and quick ratios. The cash ratio asks whether cash alone covers short-term debts. The defensive interval asks how many days the company could operate with no new sales.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Cash ratio\displaystyle \text{Cash ratio}=Cash+SecuritiesCurrent liabilities\displaystyle =\frac{\text{Cash}+\text{Securities}}{\text{Current liabilities}}DIM\displaystyle DIM=Cash+Securities+ReceivablesDaily cash expenses\displaystyle =\frac{\text{Cash}+\text{Securities}+\text{Receivables}}{\text{Daily cash expenses}}

In words: Cash ratio = (Cash + Marketable securities) / Current liabilities; Defensive interval = (Cash + Securities + Receivables) / Daily cash operating expenses

What each symbol means

Cash + SecuritiesCash and assets that can be sold for cash at oncemoney
Current liabilitiesDebts due within a yearmoney
Daily cash expensesYearly operating costs paid in cash divided by 365money per day

Worked example

A company has cash of 120, marketable securities of 30, receivables of 210 and current liabilities of 400. Yearly cash operating expenses are 1,900.

Cash ratio(120 + 30) ÷ 4000.38
Daily cash expenses1,900 ÷ 3655.21
Defensive interval (days)(120 + 30 + 210) ÷ 5.2054869.2
The answer

The cash ratio is 0.38, so cash alone covers a little over a third of short-term debts. The company could keep operating for 69.2 days with no new sales, which is comfortable.
