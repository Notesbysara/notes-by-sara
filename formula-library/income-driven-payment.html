Income-Driven Student Loan Payment

IntermediatePersonal FinanceAlso known as: income based repayment, IDR, discretionary income, student loans
← Implied VolatilityIndex Fund Tracking Difference and Error →

Finds: A payment that follows your income.

When to use it: Use it to estimate a payment that scales with income instead of the loan size. The percentage, the protected multiple and the poverty line all differ by plan and change over time, so enter the figures from your own plan. Remember that a low payment can leave interest unpaid, so the balance may grow, and forgiveness rules and any tax on forgiven amounts vary.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Payment\displaystyle \text{Payment}=p×max⁡(Y−m L,0)12\displaystyle =\frac{p\times\max(Y-m\,L,0)}{12}

In words: Monthly payment = percentage x max(income − multiple x poverty line, 0) / 12, where the percentage, the multiple and the poverty line are set by the plan

What each symbol means

pShare of discretionary income the plan asks forpercent
YAnnual incomemoney
mMultiple of the poverty line protected from the calculation (for example 150%)number
LPoverty line for your household sizemoney

Worked example

Annual income is 48,000. Your plan takes 10% of income above 150% of a 15,000 poverty line. The loan is 40,000 at 5.5% over 10 years under the standard plan.

Discretionary incomemax(48,000 − 1.5 × 15,000, 0)25,500
Income-driven payment per month0.1 × 25,500 ÷ 12212.50
Standard plan payment40,000 × (0.055 ÷ 12) ÷ (1 − (1 + 0.055 ÷ 12)^(−10 × 12))434.11
Interest for one month40,000 × 0.055 ÷ 12183.33

The answer

Discretionary income is 25,500. The income-driven payment is 212.50 a month, against 434.11 under the standard 10-year plan. The first month of interest is 183.33, so the income-driven payment 1 cover it.
