Black-Scholes PDE Check

Intermediate

Also known as: Black Scholes partial differential equation, deriving Black Scholes, delta hedged portfolio return

That a Black-Scholes price satisfies the pricing equation. Use it to see how Black-Scholes is derived. A portfolio long one option and short Delta shares has no random shocks, so by no-arbitrage it must earn the risk-free rate. That condition gives this equation. The check shows that the formula's price satisfies it: the terms add to zero.

The formula

∂V∂t+rS∂V∂S+12σ2S2∂2V∂S2−rV=0\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}+rS\frac{\partial V}{\partial S}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}S^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial S^{2}}-rV=0

Theta + r x S x Delta + 0.5 x sigma^2 x S^2 x Gamma - r x V = 0 for any option price V that follows from Black-Scholes

What each symbol means

VOption valuemoney
ThetaRate of change of V as time passes (negative for a long option)money per year
Delta, GammaFirst and second derivative of V with respect to the stock pricenumber
rRisk-free rateper year

Worked example

A call has a stock price of 100, strike of 100, rate of 5%, volatility of 20% and one year to expiry. Check that the Black-Scholes price satisfies the equation.

  1. d1(ln(100 / 100)+(0.05+0.2^2 / 2) x 1) / (0.2 x sqrt(1))= 0.3500
  2. d2d1-0.2 x sqrt(1)= 0.1500
  3. Call value V= 10.4506
  4. Delta = N(d1)= 0.6368
  5. Gamma = pdf(d1) / (S sigma sqrt(T))= 0.01876
  6. Theta = -S pdf(d1) sigma / (2 sqrt(T)) - r K e^(-rT) N(d2)= -6.4140
  7. Theta + r S Delta + 0.5 sigma^2 S^2 Gamma - r V-6.41403+0.05 x 100 x 0.636831+0.5 x 0.2^2 x 100^2 x 0.018762-0.05 x 10.4506= -0.00000000

The call is worth 10.4506 with delta 0.6368, gamma 0.01876 and theta -6.4140 per year. The four terms sum to -0.00000000, which is zero up to rounding, so the price solves the equation. Holding the option and shorting delta shares earns exactly the risk-free rate.
