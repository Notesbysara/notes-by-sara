Black-Scholes PDE Check

IntermediateQuantitative FinanceAlso known as: Black Scholes partial differential equation, deriving Black Scholes, delta hedged portfolio return
← Black-Litterman: Blending a View with Equilibrium (One Asset)Black-Scholes-Merton Option Price →

Finds: that a Black-Scholes price satisfies the pricing equation.

When to use it: Use it to see how Black-Scholes is derived. A portfolio long one option and short Delta shares has no random shocks, so by no-arbitrage it must earn the risk-free rate. That condition gives this equation. The check shows that the formula's price satisfies it: the terms add to zero.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

∂V∂t+rS∂V∂S+12σ2S2∂2V∂S2−rV\displaystyle \frac{\partial V}{\partial t}+rS\frac{\partial V}{\partial S}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}S^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial S^{2}}-rV=0\displaystyle =0

In words: Theta + r x S x Delta + 0.5 x sigma^2 x S^2 x Gamma − r x V = 0 for any option price V that follows from Black-Scholes

What each symbol means

VOption valuemoney
ThetaRate of change of V as time passes (negative for a long option)money per year
Delta, GammaFirst and second derivative of V with respect to the stock pricenumber
rRisk-free rateper year

Worked example

A call has a stock price of 100, strike of 100, rate of 5%, volatility of 20% and one year to expiry. Check that the Black-Scholes price satisfies the equation.

d1(ln(100 ÷ 100) + (0.05 + 0.2^2 ÷ 2) × 1) ÷ (0.2 × sqrt(1))0.3500
d2d1 − 0.2 × sqrt(1)0.1500
Call value V10.4506
Delta0.6368
Gamma0.01876
Theta−6.4140
Theta + r S Delta + 0.5 sigma^2 S^2 Gamma - r V−6.41403 + 0.05 × 100 × 0.636831 + 0.5 × 0.2^2 × 100^2 × 0.018762 − 0.05 × 10.4506−0.00000000
The answer

The call is worth 10.4506 with delta 0.6368, gamma 0.01876 and theta −6.4140 per year. The four terms sum to −0.00000000, which is zero up to rounding, so the price solves the equation. Holding the option and shorting delta shares earns exactly the risk-free rate.
