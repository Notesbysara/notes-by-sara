Balance of Payments Identity

Intermediate

Also known as: balance of payments and capital flows, current account, financial account, saving investment gap

How a current account gap is financed. Use it to see how a country pays for its overseas spending. A current account deficit means the country spends more than it earns abroad and must attract capital inflows of the same size, from borrowing or from foreign investors buying assets. The saving-investment version gives the same message: a deficit means domestic investment exceeds national saving. Persistent deficits are not harmful by themselves, but depend on investor confidence.

The formula

CA=TB+PI+SI,CA+KA+FA+EO=0,CA=S−ICA=TB+PI+SI,\quad CA+KA+FA+EO=0,\quad CA=S-I

Current account = trade balance + primary income (investment income, wages) + secondary income (transfers and remittances); Current account + capital account + financial account + errors = 0, where the financial account is net inflows (+) of capital; Current account = national saving - domestic investment

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
TBTrade balance: exports minus imports of goods and servicesmoney
PI, SINet primary income and net secondary income (transfers)money
KACapital account: small capital transfersmoney
FAFinancial account: net capital inflows (investment and borrowing from abroad), sign shown as inflow positivemoney
EOErrors and omissionsmoney

Worked example

A country has a trade deficit of 420, net primary income of +90, net transfers of +30, a capital account of +2 and errors of -5. GDP is 10,000. National saving is 18% of GDP and investment 21%.

  1. Current account = TB + PI + SI-420+90+30= -300.00
  2. Current account as a share of GDP-300 / 10000= -3.0%
  3. Net capital inflow needed = -(CA + KA + EO)-(-300+2+-5)= 303.00
  4. Current account from saving minus investment(0.18-0.21) x 10000= -300.00
  5. Saving-investment gap as a share of GDP0.18-0.21= -3.0%

The current account is -300.00, or -3.0% of GDP. To balance, the financial account must show net capital inflows of 303.00. The saving-investment gap gives a current account of -300.00, which is -3.0% of GDP and should be close to the first figure if the data are consistent.
