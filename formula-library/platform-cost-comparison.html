Investment Platform Cost Comparison

BeginnerPersonal FinanceAlso known as: choosing a broker, broker fees, platform fee, flat fee vs percentage fee
← Investment in Net Working Capital (Projects)Investment Thesis: Expected Return and Margin of Safety →

Finds: which platform costs less over a holding period.

When to use it: Use it to compare two platforms with different fee structures. A flat fee per trade favors large balances and few trades, while a percentage fee favors small balances. The break-even balance shows where the choice flips. Also compare fund costs, currency conversion fees and the range of investments available, which this does not include.

The formula

Cost\displaystyle \text{Cost}=T f n+p B0 (1+g)n−1g\displaystyle =T\,f\,n+p\,B_{0}\,\frac{(1+g)^{n}-1}{g}B∗\displaystyle B^{*}=T(fA−fB)pB−pA\displaystyle =\frac{T(f_{A}-f_{B})}{p_{B}-p_{A}}

In words: Total cost over n years = trades per year x fee per trade x years + percentage fee x starting balance x ((1 + growth)^n − 1) / growth (percentage fee charged on the balance at the start of each year); Break-even balance = trades x (fee A − fee B) / (percentage B − percentage A)

Worked example

A balance of 40,000 grows 5% a year for 10 years and you make 12 trades a year. Platform A charges nothing per trade but 0.35% a year. Platform B charges 5 per trade and 0.05% a year.

The answer

Over 10 years platform A costs 1,760.90 and platform B costs 851.56, so A minus B is 909.35 (a negative number means A is cheaper). The break-even balance is 20,000: above it the flat-fee platform wins, and below it the percentage-fee one does.
