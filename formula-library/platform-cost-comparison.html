Investment Platform Cost Comparison

Beginner

Also known as: choosing a broker, broker fees, platform fee, flat fee vs percentage fee

Which platform costs less over a holding period. Use it to compare two platforms with different fee structures. A flat fee per trade favors large balances and few trades, while a percentage fee favors small balances. The break-even balance shows where the choice flips. Also compare fund costs, currency conversion fees and the range of investments available, which this does not include.

The formula

Cost=T f n+p B0 (1+g)n−1g,B∗=T(fA−fB)pB−pA\text{Cost}=T\,f\,n+p\,B_{0}\,\frac{(1+g)^{n}-1}{g},\quad B^{*}=\frac{T(f_{A}-f_{B})}{p_{B}-p_{A}}

Total cost over n years = trades per year x fee per trade x years + percentage fee x starting balance x ((1 + growth)^n - 1) / growth (percentage fee charged on the balance at the start of each year); Break-even balance = trades x (fee A - fee B) / (percentage B - percentage A)

Worked example

A balance of 40,000 grows 5% a year for 10 years and you make 12 trades a year. Platform A charges nothing per trade but 0.35% a year. Platform B charges 5 per trade and 0.05% a year.

  1. Sum of year-start balances / starting balance = ((1 + g)^n - 1) / g((1+0.05)^10-1) / 0.05= 12.578
  2. Platform A total cost12 x 0 x 10+0.0035 x 40000 x 12.5779= 1,760.90
  3. Platform B total cost12 x 5 x 10+0.0005 x 40000 x 12.5779= 851.56
  4. Difference (A - B)1760.9-851.558= 909.35
  5. Cheaper platform (1 = A, 2 = B)1 if 1760.9<=851.558 else 2= 2
  6. Break-even balance (year-one view) = trades x (fee A - fee B) / (pct B - pct A)12 x (0-5) / (0.0005-0.0035)= 20,000.00

Over 10 years platform A costs 1,760.90 and platform B costs 851.56, so A minus B is 909.35 (a negative number means A is cheaper). The break-even balance is 20,000.00: above it the flat-fee platform wins, and below it the percentage-fee one does.
