Commodity Hedge: Effective Price, Basis and Hedge Ratio

Intermediate

Also known as: short hedge, basis risk, producer hedge, optimal hedge ratio

What a producer actually receives with a futures hedge. Use it to see what a hedger really receives. A producer sells futures to lock in a price, but the basis (spot minus futures) can move, which is basis risk. The hedge ratio finds the quantity to hedge when spot and futures are not perfectly correlated.

The formula

Peff=S1+F0−F1=F0+b1,h∗=ρσSσFP_{eff}=S_{1}+F_{0}-F_{1}=F_{0}+b_{1},\quad h^{*}=\rho\frac{\sigma_{S}}{\sigma_{F}}

Effective price = Spot at sale + Futures gain = Initial futures price + Final basis; Optimal hedge ratio = Correlation x Std dev of spot changes / Std dev of futures changes

What each symbol means

Worked example

A producer sells 100,000 barrels in 3 months and sells futures at 85 now. At sale the spot price is 78 and the futures price is 80. The correlation between spot and futures changes is 0.92, with volatilities of 30% and 28%. A futures contract covers 1,000 barrels.

  1. Futures gain per barrel = F0 - F185-80= 5.00
  2. Final basis = S1 - F178-80= -2.00
  3. Effective price = S1 + gain78+5= 83.00
  4. Unhedged price78= 78.00
  5. Hedged revenue83 x 100000= 8,300,000.00
  6. Unhedged revenue78 x 100000= 7,800,000.00
  7. Optimal hedge ratio = rho x ss / sf0.92 x 0.3 / 0.28= 0.986
  8. Contracts to sell = h x quantity / contract size0.985714 x 100000 / 1000= 98.6

The futures gain is 5.00 per barrel and the final basis is -2.00, so the effective price is 83.00, against 78.00 unhedged. Revenue is 8,300,000.00 hedged against 7,800,000.00 unhedged. The optimal hedge ratio is 0.986, which means selling 98.6 contracts.
