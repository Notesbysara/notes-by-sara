Bootstrapping Spot Rates from Par Yields

IntermediateFixed IncomeAlso known as: zero curve, discount factors, spot curve, par curve to zero curve
← Bond PriceBreak-Even Analysis →

Finds: zero-coupon rates from a par curve.

When to use it: Use it to turn a curve of par yields, which is how bond markets quote the curve, into zero-coupon rates that can discount any single cash flow. You work from the shortest maturity to the longest, using each answer for the next step.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

DFn\displaystyle DF_{n}=1−yn∑i=1n−1DFi1+yn\displaystyle =\frac{1-y_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}DF_{i}}{1+y_{n}}sn\displaystyle s_{n}=DFn−1/n−1\displaystyle =DF_{n}^{-1/n}-1

In words: Discount factor for year n = (1 − par yield n x sum of earlier discount factors) / (1 + par yield n); Spot rate n = DF^(−1/n) − 1

What each symbol means

y_nPar yield for maturity n (the coupon rate at which a bond of that maturity prices at par), with annual couponsper year
DF_nDiscount factor for year n, the present value of 1 received in year nnumber
s_nSpot (zero-coupon) rate for maturity nper year

Worked example

Par yields with annual coupons are 2.0%, 2.5%, 3.0% and 3.4% for 1 to 4 years. What are the spot rates?

Discount factors0.9804, 0.9517, 0.9146, 0.8735
Spot rates2%, 2.51%, 3.02%, 3.44%
The answer

The discount factors are 0.9804, 0.9517, 0.9146, 0.8735 and the spot rates are 2%, 2.51%, 3.02%, 3.44% for years 1 to 4. Spot rates sit above par yields on an upward curve, as expected.
