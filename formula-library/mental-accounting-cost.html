Mental Accounting: Cost of Separate Pots

Beginner

Also known as: mental accounting, saving while in debt, money is not fungible, opportunity cost of low-interest savings

What keeping savings and debt apart costs per year. Use it when you hold savings in one pot and costly debt in another. Money is fungible, so a pound in the savings account that earns 3% while a card charges 22% is costing you the difference. People often keep the pots separate, because the savings feel "safe" or have a label. Keep the buffer so you do not need to borrow again.

The formula

Net gain=Pay×(rdebt−rsave),Pay=min⁡(D,  S−B)\text{Net gain}=\text{Pay}\times(r_{debt}-r_{save}),\quad \text{Pay}=\min(D,\;S-B)

Amount you could pay off = smaller of the debt and (savings - emergency buffer you decide to keep); Interest saved = amount x debt rate; Interest given up = amount x savings rate; Net gain per year = amount x (debt rate - savings rate)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
DDebt balancemoney
SSavings balancemoney
BBuffer you want to keep as an emergency fundmoney
r_debt, r_saveInterest rate on the debt and on the savingsper year

Worked example

You hold 12,000 in savings at 3% and owe 8,000 on a card at 22%. You want to keep a 3,000 emergency buffer.

  1. Amount to pay off = min(debt, savings - buffer)max(min(8000, 12000-3000), 0)= 8,000.00
  2. Card interest saved per year8000 x 0.22= 1,760.00
  3. Savings interest given up per year8000 x 0.03= 240.00
  4. Net gain per year1760-240= 1,520.00
  5. Per month1520 / 12= 126.67

You could pay off 8,000.00 of the card from savings. That saves 1,760.00 in card interest and gives up 240.00 in savings interest, a net gain of 1,520.00 a year, or 126.67 a month.
