Cumulative Voting: Shares Needed to Elect Directors

IntermediateCorporate FinanceAlso known as: proxy voting, minority shareholder board seats, shares needed to elect a director
← Cumulative Abnormal Return before an AnnouncementCurrency Swap Cash Flows and Value →

Finds: votes needed to win board seats.

When to use it: Use it to see how many shares a minority shareholder needs to win board seats under cumulative voting. This system lets each holder put all their votes on one candidate, which helps minorities.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Shares needed\displaystyle \text{Shares needed}=⌊S×kD+1⌋+1\displaystyle =\left\lfloor\frac{S\times k}{D+1}\right\rfloor+1

In words: Shares needed = rounded down (Total shares x Seats wanted / (Seats up for election + 1)) + 1

What each symbol means

STotal shares votingshares
kNumber of board seats the minority wants to winseats
DNumber of seats up for electionseats

Worked example

A company has 1,000,000 voting shares and 5 board seats up for election. A shareholder group wants to win 2 seats. How many shares do they need?

Shares needed before rounding1,000,000 × 2 ÷ (5 + 1)333,333.33
Round down and add 1333,334
Share of the total vote333,334 ÷ 1,000,00033.33%
The answer

The group needs 333,334 shares, about 33.33% of the vote. With straight voting they would need a majority to win any seat.
