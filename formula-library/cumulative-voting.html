Cumulative Voting: Shares Needed to Elect Directors

Intermediate

Also known as: proxy voting, minority shareholder board seats, shares needed to elect a director

Votes needed to win board seats. Use it to see how many shares a minority shareholder needs to win board seats under cumulative voting. This system lets each holder put all their votes on one candidate, which helps minorities.

The formula

Shares needed=⌊S×kD+1⌋+1\text{Shares needed}=\left\lfloor\frac{S\times k}{D+1}\right\rfloor+1

Shares needed = rounded down (Total shares x Seats wanted / (Seats up for election + 1)) + 1

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
STotal shares votingshares
kNumber of board seats the minority wants to winseats
DNumber of seats up for electionseats

Worked example

A company has 1,000,000 voting shares and 5 board seats up for election. A shareholder group wants to win 2 seats. How many shares do they need?

  1. Shares needed before rounding = S x k / (D + 1)1,000,000 x 2 / (5+1)= 333,333.33
  2. Round down and add 1= 333,334
  3. Share of the total vote333334 / 1,000,000= 33.33%

The group needs 333,334 shares, about 33.33% of the vote. With straight voting they would need a majority to win any seat.
