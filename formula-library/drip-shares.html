Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP)

Beginner

Also known as: DRIP, reinvested dividends, dividend reinvestment plan

Extra shares from reinvesting. Use it to see how reinvesting dividends grows your holding. Each reinvestment buys more shares, which pay more dividends, so the effect compounds.

The formula

New shares=N×DPSP\text{New shares}=\frac{N\times DPS}{P}

New shares = (Shares held x Dividend per share) / Reinvestment price

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
NShares heldshares
DPSDividend per sharemoney
PPrice at which the dividend buys new sharesmoney

Worked example

You hold 200 shares paying a dividend of 1.20 each. The reinvestment price is 30. How many new shares do you get?

  1. Total dividend = N x DPS200 x 1.2= 240.00
  2. New shares = dividend / price240 / 30= 8.00
  3. Shares held after = N + new shares200+8= 208.00

The dividend of 240.00 buys 8.00 new shares, so you hold 208.00 shares after reinvesting.
