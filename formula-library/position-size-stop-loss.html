Position Size from a Stop-Loss

BeginnerEquity MarketsAlso known as: stop order, risk per trade, stop loss position sizing, order types
← Portfolio Variance in Matrix FormPP&E Roll-Forward →

Finds: how many shares fit a risk limit.

When to use it: Use it to decide how many shares to buy when you place a stop order. A stop turns into a market order once the price touches it, so the loss is roughly the gap between entry and stop times the shares. Gaps and fast markets can make it worse.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Q\displaystyle Q=A×rPentry−Pstop\displaystyle =\frac{A\times r}{P_{entry}-P_{stop}}

In words: Shares = (Account size x Risk per trade) / (Entry price − Stop price)

What each symbol means

QNumber of shares to buynumber
AAccount sizemoney
rShare of the account you accept losing if the stop is hitpercent
P_entryPrice you buy atmoney
P_stopPrice of the stop order that sells if the stock falls to itmoney

Worked example

A 50,000 account risks 1% on a trade. You buy at 40 with a stop at 37. How many shares?

Amount at risk50,000 × 0.01500
Shares166
Position cost166 × 406,640
Position as a share of the account6,640 ÷ 50,00013.3%
The answer

You can lose 500 on the trade, so buy 166 shares. That is a 6,640 position, or 13.3% of the account.
