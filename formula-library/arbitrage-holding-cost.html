Limits to Arbitrage: Holding Cost and Adverse Move

Intermediate

Also known as: limits to arbitrage, mispricing persistence, short selling cost, noise trader risk

Whether a mispricing is worth trading and how it could hurt. Use it to see why obvious mispricings can last. A trader who bets against an overpriced stock pays fees every year and must post margin. If the price moves further away before it converges, losses can force the position closed at the worst time, so a mispricing can survive even when prices are wrong. The gap must be large enough to pay for time and risk.

The formula

Net=(P−V)−fPT−r m P T,T∗=P−VP(f+rm)\text{Net}=(P-V)-fP T-r\,m\,P\,T,\quad T^{*}=\frac{P-V}{P(f+rm)}

Net profit per share from shorting an overpriced stock = gap to fundamental value - borrow fee x price x years - financing rate x margin share x price x years; Break-even holding time = gap / (price x (borrow fee + financing rate x margin share)); Loss if the price moves further away by a = a x price

Worked example

A stock trades at 100 and you believe it is worth 90. Borrowing it costs 3% a year, margin is 50% and capital costs 4%. You expect convergence in 1 year, but a 10% adverse move is possible first.

  1. Gain if the price falls to value = P - V100-90= 10.00
  2. Borrow fee = f x P x T0.03 x 100 x 1= 3.00
  3. Cost of margin capital = r x m x P x T0.04 x 0.5 x 100 x 1= 2.00
  4. Net profit10-3-2= 5.00
  5. Return on the margin5 / (0.5 x 100)= 10.0%
  6. Break-even holding time = gap / (P (f + r m))10 / (100 x (0.03+0.04 x 0.5))= 2.00
  7. Loss on an adverse move = a x P0.1 x 100= 10.00
  8. Loss as a share of the margin10 / (0.5 x 100)= 20.0%

Convergence would earn 10.00 a share, but costs are 3.00 in borrowing and 2.00 in capital, so the net profit is 5.00, a return of 10.0% on the margin put up. The trade breaks even only if convergence happens within 2.00 years. A 10% adverse move costs 10.00, which is 20.0% of the margin and could trigger a margin call.
