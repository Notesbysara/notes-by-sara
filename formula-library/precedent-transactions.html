Valuation from Precedent Transactions

IntermediateValuationAlso known as: transaction comps, deal multiples, M&A comparables, control premium valuation
← Utility and Risk AversionValuation from Trading Comparables →

Finds: value implied by prices paid in past deals.

When to use it: Use it to value a company as if it were being bought. Past deal multiples include a control premium, so they are usually higher than trading multiples.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Deal multiple\displaystyle \text{Deal multiple}=EVdealEBITDAtarget\displaystyle =\frac{EV_{deal}}{EBITDA_{target}}EV\displaystyle EV=EBITDA×Median deal multiple\displaystyle =EBITDA\times\text{Median deal multiple}

In words: Deal multiple = Deal EV / Target EBITDA for each past deal; Implied EV = Your EBITDA x Median deal multiple

What each symbol means

EV_dealEnterprise value paid in a past acquisitionmoney
EBITDA_targetEBITDA of the company that was boughtmoney
Median deal multipleMiddle value of the multiples from past dealsnumber

Worked example

Three past deals were done at enterprise values of 900, 1,500 and 600 for targets with EBITDA of 100, 150 and 80. A company with EBITDA of 200 is being sold. What is its implied value?

Deal multiple9, 10, 7.50
Median deal multiple9
Implied EV200 × 91,800
The answer

The deal multiples are 9, 10, 7.50. The median is 9, so the implied enterprise value is 1,800.
