Dollar-Cost Averaging Average Cost

Beginner

Also known as: regular investing, average cost per share, harmonic mean cost, invest monthly

Your average cost when investing a fixed amount regularly. Use it to see what regular fixed-amount investing does to your average cost. Because the same money buys more shares when prices are low, your average cost is below the average price whenever prices vary. This does not mean DCA beats investing a lump sum at once: if prices rise on average, the lump sum usually wins. Its main benefit is behavioral and matches investing from pay.

The formula

Cˉ=n A∑tA/Pt=n∑t1/Pt\bar{C}=\frac{n\,A}{\sum_{t}A/P_{t}}=\frac{n}{\sum_{t}1/P_{t}}

Average cost per share = total invested / total shares bought = n / (sum of 1 / price). It is the harmonic mean of the prices, which is never above the plain average price

Worked example

You invest 300 at the start of each of six months at prices of 50, 40, 25, 40, 50 and 60.

  1. Shares bought each period = A / price= 6.0000, 7.5000, 12.0000, 7.5000, 6.0000, 5.0000
  2. Total shares= 44.0000
  3. Total invested= 1,800.00
  4. Average cost per share = invested / shares1800 / 44= 40.91
  5. Simple average price= 44.17
  6. Saving versus the average price44.1667-40.9091= 3.26
  7. Final price= 60.00
  8. Value of the holding44 x 60= 2,640.00
  9. Gain or loss2640-1800= 840.00

You buy 44.0000 shares for 1,800.00, an average cost of 40.91 per share. The simple average price was 44.17, so you paid 3.26 less than that. At the final price of 60.00 your holding is worth 2,640.00, a gain of 840.00.
