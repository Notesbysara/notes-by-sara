Cash Flow Quality Ratios

IntermediateFinancial Statement AnalysisAlso known as: operating cash flow to net income, cash flow adequacy, CFO to EBITDA
← Cash Conversion Cycle from the Financial StatementsCash Ratio and Defensive Interval →

Finds: whether profit turns into cash and cash covers needs.

When to use it: Use it to judge whether operations pay for themselves. CFO well above net income is usually a sign of good quality (but check for working capital being squeezed). Adequacy above 1 means operating cash covers investment, dividends and debt repayment without outside funding.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

CFONI\displaystyle \frac{CFO}{NI}CFOEBITDA\displaystyle \frac{CFO}{EBITDA}Adequacy\displaystyle \text{Adequacy}=CFOCapEx+Dividends+Debt repaid\displaystyle =\frac{CFO}{\text{CapEx}+\text{Dividends}+\text{Debt repaid}}

In words: CFO / Net income; CFO / EBITDA; Cash flow adequacy = CFO / (Capex + Dividends + Debt repayments)

What each symbol means

CFOCash flow from operationsmoney
NINet incomemoney
EBITDAEarnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortizationmoney

Worked example

A company has operating cash flow of 140, net income of 90 and EBITDA of 260. It spends 80 on capital, 30 on dividends and 20 on debt repayment.

CFO / net income140 ÷ 901.56
CFO / EBITDA140 ÷ 2600.54
Adequacy140 ÷ (80 + 30 + 20)1.08
The answer

CFO is 1.56 of net income and 0.54 of EBITDA. Cash flow adequacy is 1.08, so operations cover all the main uses of cash with a small surplus.
