Cash Flow Quality Ratios

Intermediate

Also known as: operating cash flow to net income, cash flow adequacy, CFO to EBITDA

Whether profit turns into cash and cash covers needs. Use it to judge whether operations pay for themselves. CFO well above net income is usually a sign of good quality (but check for working capital being squeezed). Adequacy above 1 means operating cash covers investment, dividends and debt repayment without outside funding.

The formula

CFONI,CFOEBITDA,Adequacy=CFOCapEx+Dividends+Debt repaid\frac{CFO}{NI},\quad \frac{CFO}{EBITDA},\quad \text{Adequacy}=\frac{CFO}{\text{CapEx}+\text{Dividends}+\text{Debt repaid}}

CFO / Net income; CFO / EBITDA; Cash flow adequacy = CFO / (Capex + Dividends + Debt repayments)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
CFOCash flow from operationsmoney
NINet incomemoney
EBITDAEarnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortizationmoney

Worked example

A company has operating cash flow of 140, net income of 90 and EBITDA of 260. It spends 80 on capital, 30 on dividends and 20 on debt repayment.

  1. CFO / net income140 / 90= 1.56
  2. CFO / EBITDA140 / 260= 0.54
  3. Adequacy = CFO / (capex + dividends + debt repaid)140 / (80+30+20)= 1.08

CFO is 1.56 of net income and 0.54 of EBITDA. Cash flow adequacy is 1.08, so operations cover all the main uses of cash with a small surplus.
