Cost of Fees: Active vs. Passive

Beginner

Also known as: expense ratio impact, fee drag, active versus passive, cost of active management

How fees reduce wealth over time. Use it to see what fees cost over time. Because fees come out of the balance each year, they compound too. An active fund must earn more than its passive rival, by at least the fee difference, just to match it.

The formula

FV=P (1+r−f)nFV=P\,(1+r-f)^{n}

Final value = Amount invested x (1 + gross return - annual fee)^years

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
FVFinal value of the investmentmoney
PAmount invested at the startmoney
rGross return before fees, per yearper year
fAnnual fee as a percentage of assetsper year
nYearsyears

Worked example

You invest 10,000 for 30 years. Before fees the market returns 7% a year. An active fund charges 1.0% and an index fund charges 0.1%. Both earn the market return before fees. What is each worth at the end?

  1. Active fund value = P x (1 + r - fa)^n10000 x (1+0.07-0.01)^30= 57,434.91
  2. Index fund value = P x (1 + r - fp)^n10000 x (1+0.07-0.001)^30= 74,016.95
  3. Cost of the extra fee = index value - active value74016.9-57434.9= 16,582.03
  4. As a share of the index fund value16582 / 74016.9= 22.40%
  5. Break-even outperformance per year = fa - fp0.01-0.001= 0.90%

The active fund grows to 57,434.91 and the index fund to 74,016.95. The fee difference costs 16,582.03, or 22.40% of the index fund's result. The active manager must beat the market by 0.90% a year just to break even.
