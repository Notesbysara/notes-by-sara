Cost of Fees: Active vs. Passive

BeginnerInvestments & Portfolio TheoryAlso known as: expense ratio impact, fee drag, active versus passive, cost of active management
← Cost of Equity with Leverage (Modigliani-Miller Proposition II)Cost of Goods Sold (Inventory Roll-Forward) →

Finds: how fees reduce wealth over time.

When to use it: Use it to see what fees cost over time. Because fees come out of the balance each year, they compound too. An active fund must earn more than its passive rival, by at least the fee difference, just to match it.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

FV\displaystyle FV=P (1+r−f)n\displaystyle =P\,(1+r-f)^{n}

In words: Final value = Amount invested x (1 + gross return − annual fee)^years

What each symbol means

FVFinal value of the investmentmoney
PAmount invested at the startmoney
rGross return before fees, per yearper year
fAnnual fee as a percentage of assetsper year
nYearsyears

Worked example

You invest 10,000 for 30 years. Before fees the market returns 7% a year. An active fund charges 1.0% and an index fund charges 0.1%. Both earn the market return before fees. What is each worth at the end?

Active fund value10,000 × (1 + 0.07 − 0.01)^3057,434.91
Index fund value10,000 × (1 + 0.07 − 0.001)^3074,016.95
Cost of the extra fee74,016.9 − 57,434.916,582.03
As a share of the index fund value16,582 ÷ 74,016.922.40%
Break-even outperformance per year0.01 − 0.0010.90%
The answer

The active fund grows to 57,434.91 and the index fund to 74,016.95. The fee difference costs 16,582.03, or 22.40% of the index fund's result. The active manager must beat the market by 0.90% a year just to break even.
