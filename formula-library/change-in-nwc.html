Investment in Net Working Capital (Projects)

IntermediateCorporate FinanceAlso known as: working capital in project analysis, change in NWC, NWC as percent of sales
← International Fisher EffectInvestment Platform Cost Comparison →

Finds: cash tied up by growth.

When to use it: Use it to include the cash a growing project ties up in inventory and receivables. It is a cash outflow when sales rise, and it is recovered at the end of the project.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

ΔNWCt\displaystyle \Delta NWC_{t}=w (Salest−Salest−1)\displaystyle =w\,(Sales_{t}-Sales_{t-1})

In words: Change in NWC = w x (Sales this year − Sales last year), where w is NWC as a share of sales

What each symbol means

Delta NWC_tExtra cash tied up in working capital in year tmoney
wWorking capital needed per 1 of sales, as a decimaldecimal
Sales_tSales this yearmoney
Sales_(t-1)Sales last yearmoney

Worked example

A project needs working capital equal to 15% of sales. Sales rise from 1,000 to 1,200. How much extra cash is tied up?

NWC last year0.15 × 1,000150
NWC this year0.15 × 1,200180
Change in NWC180 − 15030
The answer

Working capital rises from 150 to 180, so 30 of extra cash is tied up and must be subtracted from that year's cash flow.
