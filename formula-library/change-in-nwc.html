Investment in Net Working Capital (Projects)

Intermediate

Also known as: working capital in project analysis, change in NWC, NWC as percent of sales

Cash tied up by growth. Use it to include the cash a growing project ties up in inventory and receivables. It is a cash outflow when sales rise, and it is recovered at the end of the project.

The formula

ΔNWCt=w (Salest−Salest−1)\Delta NWC_{t}=w\,(Sales_{t}-Sales_{t-1})

Change in NWC = w x (Sales this year - Sales last year), where w is NWC as a share of sales

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
Delta NWC_tExtra cash tied up in working capital in year tmoney
wWorking capital needed per 1 of sales, as a decimaldecimal
Sales_tSales this yearmoney
Sales_(t-1)Sales last yearmoney

Worked example

A project needs working capital equal to 15% of sales. Sales rise from 1,000 to 1,200. How much extra cash is tied up?

  1. NWC last year = w x sales last year0.15 x 1000= 150.00
  2. NWC this year = w x sales this year0.15 x 1200= 180.00
  3. Change in NWC = this year - last year180-150= 30.00

Working capital rises from 150.00 to 180.00, so 30.00 of extra cash is tied up and must be subtracted from that year's cash flow.
