Private Fund Metrics: IRR, MOIC, TVPI, DPI, RVPI

Intermediate

Also known as: TVPI, DPI, RVPI, MOIC, PME, net IRR

How much a private fund has returned and what it is worth in total. Use it to read a private fund report. TVPI shows total value created but part of it is only a valuation, so DPI is the part already in your hands. IRR depends on timing and can look high early on; a multiple (MOIC, TVPI) ignores time. Read all three together and be careful comparing a fund with unrealized value to a finished one.

The formula

DPI=∑D∑P,RVPI=NAV∑P,TVPI=DPI+RVPI,0=∑tDt−Pt+NAVT1t=T(1+IRR)tDPI=\frac{\sum D}{\sum P},\quad RVPI=\frac{NAV}{\sum P},\quad TVPI=DPI+RVPI,\quad 0=\sum_{t}\frac{D_{t}-P_{t}+NAV_{T}\mathbb{1}_{t=T}}{(1+IRR)^{t}}

DPI = total distributions / total paid-in capital (cash actually returned); RVPI = remaining net asset value / paid-in capital (value still held); TVPI = DPI + RVPI (total value per unit paid in, also called the net multiple); IRR = rate that sets the net present value of calls, distributions and final NAV to zero

Worked example

A fund called 30, 30, 20 and 10 in years 0 to 3. It paid back 10 in year 2, 10 in year 3, 25 in year 4 and 40 in year 5, and the remaining stake is valued at 45.

  1. Paid-in capital= 90.00
  2. DPI = distributions / paid-in= 0.94
  3. RVPI = NAV / paid-in45 / 90= 0.50
  4. TVPI = DPI + RVPI0.944444+0.5= 1.44
  5. Net flows with NAV added to the last year= -30.00, -30.00, -10.00, 0.00, 25.00, 40.00
  6. Net IRR= 11.7%

Paid-in capital is 90.00. DPI is 0.94, RVPI is 0.50 and TVPI is 1.44. The net IRR, counting the remaining value as received in the last year, is 11.7%. Only 0.94 of every 1 paid in has come back as cash so far.
