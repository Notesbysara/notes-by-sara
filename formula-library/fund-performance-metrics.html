Private Fund Metrics: IRR, MOIC, TVPI, DPI, RVPI

IntermediateAlternative InvestmentsAlso known as: TVPI, DPI, RVPI, MOIC, PME, net IRR
← Private Equity Distribution Waterfall and CarryProbability from a Normal Distribution →

Finds: how much a private fund has returned and what it is worth in total.

When to use it: Use it to read a private fund report. TVPI shows total value created but part of it is only a valuation, so DPI is the part already in your hands. IRR depends on timing and can look high early on; a multiple (MOIC, TVPI) ignores time. Read all three together and be careful comparing a fund with unrealized value to a finished one.

The formula

In words: DPI = total distributions / total paid-in capital (cash actually returned); RVPI = remaining net asset value / paid-in capital (value still held); TVPI = DPI + RVPI (total value per unit paid in, also called the net multiple); IRR = rate that sets the net present value of calls, distributions and final NAV to zero

What each symbol means

P_tCapital called in year tmoney
D_tDistributions in year tmoney
NAVReported value of the investor's remaining stakemoney

Worked example

A fund called 30, 30, 20 and 10 in years 0 to 3. It paid back 10 in year 2, 10 in year 3, 25 in year 4 and 40 in year 5, and the remaining stake is valued at 45.

Paid-in capital90
DPI0.94
RVPI45 ÷ 900.50
TVPI0.944444 + 0.51.44
Net flows with NAV added to the last year−30, −30, −10, 0, 25, 40
Net IRR11.7%
The answer

Paid-in capital is 90. DPI is 0.94, RVPI is 0.50 and TVPI is 1.44. The net IRR, counting the remaining value as received in the last year, is 11.7%. Only 0.94 of every 1 paid in has come back as cash so far.
