Murabaha Mark-Up and Implied Rate

IntermediateBanking & Financial InstitutionsAlso known as: Islamic banking, cost-plus financing, profit rate, sale on deferred payment
← Municipal Bond Tax-Equivalent Yield (with State Tax)Narrative Contagion (SIR Model) →

Finds: the all-in price and the comparable annual rate of a cost-plus sale.

When to use it: Use it to compare an Islamic cost-plus sale with a conventional loan. The bank buys the asset and sells it to the customer at cost plus an agreed mark-up, payable in installments, so the price is fixed and does not accrue interest on a loan. The implied rate is a comparison tool only: the two contracts are structured differently and differ in legal treatment, risk and late-payment rules.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Price\displaystyle \text{Price}=C(1+m)\displaystyle =C(1+m)PMT\displaystyle PMT=Pricen\displaystyle =\frac{\text{Price}}{n}C\displaystyle C=PMT 1−(1+i)−ni\displaystyle =PMT\,\frac{1-(1+i)^{-n}}{i}

In words: Sale price = cost x (1 + mark-up); Installment = sale price / number of installments; Implied periodic rate i solves: cost = installment x (1 − (1 + i)^(-n)) / i; Effective annual rate = (1 + i)^12 − 1 for monthly installments

What each symbol means

CCost the bank pays for the assetmoney
mAgreed mark-up (profit) over cost, disclosed in advancepercent
nNumber of equal monthly installmentsmonths
iMonthly rate implied by the installments, for comparison with a conventional loanper month

Worked example

A bank buys equipment for 100,000 and sells it for a 12% mark-up payable in 36 equal monthly installments.

Sale price100,000 × (1 + 0.12)112,000
Monthly installment112,000 ÷ 363,111.11
Implied monthly rate (IRR of the cash flows)0.6259%
Effective annual rate(1 + 0.00625888)^12 − 17.77%
Flat annual rate of the mark-up0.12 ÷ (36 ÷ 12)4%
The answer

The sale price is 112,000 and each installment is 3,111.11. Solving for the rate that links the cost to the installments gives 0.6259% a month, or 7.77% as an effective annual rate. The flat mark-up of 12% over 3 years looks like 4% a year, but the true annual cost is higher because the balance falls with each payment.
