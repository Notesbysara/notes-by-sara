Basel III Capital Requirement Stack and Buffers

Intermediate

Also known as: Basel I, II, III, capital conservation buffer, countercyclical buffer, G-SIB surcharge, maximum distributable amount, CET1 requirement

How far a bank is above its capital requirement. Use it to see how much loss a bank can bear before it faces limits on payouts. The minimum is not the real constraint; the buffers on top are. Supervisors raise the countercyclical buffer in booms, so the requirement can change. It ignores other requirements such as leverage ratio, liquidity and bank-specific add-ons.

The formula

Req=4.5%+CCB+CCyB+G-SIB,Buffer=CET1RWA−Req\text{Req}=4.5\%+CCB+CCyB+G\text{-}SIB,\quad \text{Buffer}=\frac{CET1}{RWA}-\text{Req}

Required CET1 ratio = 4.5% minimum + capital conservation buffer (2.5%) + countercyclical buffer (set by the country, 0% to 2.5%) + surcharge for global systemically important banks (1% to 3.5%); Headroom = actual CET1 ratio - requirement (this is the loss the bank can absorb before restrictions); Capital needed = shortfall x risk-weighted assets; Banks inside the buffers face limits on dividends and bonuses

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
CET1Common equity tier 1 capital (shares and retained earnings after deductions)money
RWARisk-weighted assets: assets scaled by their riskinessmoney
CCB, CCyBConservation buffer and countercyclical bufferpercent
G-SIBExtra requirement for the largest global bankspercent

Worked example

A bank has CET1 capital of 12 against risk-weighted assets of 100. The countercyclical buffer is 1% and the bank is in a 1.5% systemic-bank bracket.

  1. CET1 ratio12 / 100= 12.00%
  2. Requirement = 4.5% + 2.5% + CCyB + G-SIB0.045+0.025+0.01+0.015= 9.50%
  3. Headroom0.12-0.095= 2.50%
  4. Loss absorbable before breaching (as % of RWA)0.025 x 100= 2.50
  5. Headroom if RWA rise 10%12 / (100 x 1.1)-0.095= 1.41%

The bank's CET1 ratio is 12.00%. The requirement including buffers is 9.50%, so headroom is 2.50%. Losses of 2.50 of risk-weighted assets would use up the headroom. If RWA rise 10% the headroom becomes 1.41%.
