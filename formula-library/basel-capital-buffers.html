Basel III Capital Requirement Stack and Buffers

IntermediateRegulation, Ethics & ESGAlso known as: Basel I, II, III, capital conservation buffer, countercyclical buffer, G-SIB surcharge, maximum distributable amount, CET1 requirement
← Barrier Option PayoffBasel III Endgame: Output Floor on Risk-Weighted Assets →

Finds: how far a bank is above its capital requirement.

When to use it: Use it to see how much loss a bank can bear before it faces limits on payouts. The minimum is not the real constraint; the buffers on top are. Supervisors raise the countercyclical buffer in booms, so the requirement can change. It ignores other requirements such as leverage ratio, liquidity and bank-specific add-ons.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Req\displaystyle \text{Req}=4.5%+CCB+CCyB+G-SIB\displaystyle =4.5\%+CCB+CCyB+G\text{-}SIBBuffer\displaystyle \text{Buffer}=CET1RWA−Req\displaystyle =\frac{CET1}{RWA}-\text{Req}

In words: Required CET1 ratio = 4.5% minimum + capital conservation buffer (2.5%) + countercyclical buffer (set by the country, 0% to 2.5%) + surcharge for global systemically important banks (1% to 3.5%); Headroom = actual CET1 ratio − requirement (this is the loss the bank can absorb before restrictions); Capital needed = shortfall x risk-weighted assets; Banks inside the buffers face limits on dividends and bonuses

What each symbol means

CET1Common equity tier 1 capital (shares and retained earnings after deductions)money
RWARisk-weighted assets: assets scaled by their riskinessmoney
CCB, CCyBConservation buffer and countercyclical bufferpercent
G-SIBExtra requirement for the largest global bankspercent

Worked example

A bank has CET1 capital of 12 against risk-weighted assets of 100. The countercyclical buffer is 1% and the bank is in a 1.5% systemic-bank bracket.

CET1 ratio12 ÷ 10012%
Requirement0.045 + 0.025 + 0.01 + 0.0159.50%
Headroom0.12 − 0.0952.50%
Loss absorbable before breaching (as % of RWA)0.025 × 1002.50
Headroom if RWA rise 10%12 ÷ (100 × 1.1) − 0.0951.41%
The answer

The bank's CET1 ratio is 12%. The requirement including buffers is 9.50%, so headroom is 2.50%. Losses of 2.50 of risk-weighted assets would use up the headroom. If RWA rise 10% the headroom becomes 1.41%.
