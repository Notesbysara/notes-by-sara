Combined Ratio (Insurers)

Beginner

Also known as: insurance combined ratio, loss ratio, expense ratio, underwriting profit

Underwriting profitability of an insurer. Use it to judge whether an insurer makes money from underwriting alone. Below 100% it makes an underwriting profit. Above 100% it relies on investment income to make a profit.

The formula

Combined ratio=Claims+ExpensesPremiums earned\text{Combined ratio}=\frac{\text{Claims}+\text{Expenses}}{\text{Premiums earned}}

Combined ratio = (Claims paid + Operating expenses) / Premiums earned

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
ClaimsLosses paid and set aside for claimsmoney
ExpensesCosts of selling and running the insurance businessmoney
Premiums earnedInsurance premiums earned in the periodmoney

Worked example

An insurer earned premiums of 1,000, paid claims of 650 and had expenses of 300. What are its loss ratio, expense ratio and combined ratio?

  1. Loss ratio = claims / premiums650 / 1000= 65.00%
  2. Expense ratio = expenses / premiumsexp / 1000= 30.00%
  3. Combined ratio = loss ratio + expense ratio0.65+0.3= 95.00%
  4. Underwriting profit = premiums - claims - expenses1000-650-exp= 50.00

The loss ratio is 65.00%, the expense ratio is 30.00% and the combined ratio is 95.00%. The underwriting profit is 50.00.
