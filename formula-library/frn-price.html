Floating Rate Note Coupon and Price

Intermediate

Also known as: FRN, quoted margin, discount margin, SOFR plus spread, floating rate bond

The price of a floating rate note. Use it to price a floating rate note. If the market requires the same margin as the quoted margin, the price is at par on a reset date. If investors want a higher margin because credit worsened, the price falls below par. This simple form assumes the reference rate stays flat.

The formula

Coupon=(Reference rate+QM)m×F,P=∑C(1+(Ref+DM)/m)k+F(1+(Ref+DM)/m)N\text{Coupon}=\frac{(\text{Reference rate}+QM)}{m}\times F,\quad P=\sum\frac{C}{(1+(\text{Ref}+DM)/m)^{k}}+\frac{F}{(1+(\text{Ref}+DM)/m)^{N}}

Coupon = (Reference rate + Quoted margin) / payments per year x Face value; Price = coupons and face value discounted at (Reference rate + Discount margin) / payments per year

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
Reference rateFloating benchmark such as SOFR, reset each periodper year
QMQuoted margin, the fixed spread added to the reference rateper year
DMDiscount margin, the spread the market now requiresper year
FFace valuemoney
mPayments per yearnumber

Worked example

A 3-year floating rate note pays quarterly at SOFR plus 0.60%. SOFR is 4.5%. The market now requires a margin of 0.90%. Face value is 1,000. What is the next coupon and the price?

  1. Coupon = face x (ref + QM) / m1000 x (0.045+0.006) / 4= 12.75
  2. Price: coupons and face value discounted at ref + DM= 991.74
  3. Shortfall from par1000-991.742= 8.26

The next coupon is 12.75. With a required margin above the quoted margin the note is worth 991.74, below par by 8.26.
