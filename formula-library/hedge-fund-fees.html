Hedge Fund Fees: Management, Performance, Hurdle and High-Water Mark

IntermediateBanking & Financial InstitutionsAlso known as: hedge funds, 2 and 20, incentive fee, net of fee return
← Health Plan Cost ComparisonHedging a Foreign Currency Receivable →

Finds: the net return an investor keeps after fees.

When to use it: Use it to see what investors keep. The classic "2 and 20" takes 2% of assets and 20% of profits, so fees can absorb a large part of the gross return. A hurdle and a high-water mark protect investors by requiring a minimum return and by not charging again for recovering past losses. This version uses a hard hurdle (fee only on the excess), whereas some funds use a soft hurdle where the fee is charged on all profits once the hurdle is cleared.

The formula

Feeperf\displaystyle \text{Fee}_{perf}=p×max⁡ ⁣(NAV−M−max⁡(NAV0(1+h),HWM), 0)\displaystyle =p\times\max\!\left(NAV-M-\max(NAV_{0}(1+h),HWM),\,0\right)

In words: Management fee = rate x starting NAV; Performance fee = performance rate x the amount by which NAV after the management fee exceeds the higher of (starting NAV x (1 + hurdle)) and the high-water mark; Net return = (NAV after both fees / starting NAV) − 1

What each symbol means

NAV_0Net asset value at the start of the yearmoney
hHurdle: return the fund must beat before earning a performance feepercent
HWMHigh-water mark: the highest NAV on which a fee was paid, so investors never pay twice for the same gainsmoney
pPerformance (incentive) fee rate, often 20%percent

Worked example

A fund starts at 100 and earns 14% gross. It charges 2% management and 20% performance above a 4% hurdle and a high-water mark of 100.

NAV before fees100 × (1 + 0.14)114
NAV after the management fee114 − 100 × 0.02112
Fee thresholdmax(100 × (1 + 0.04), 100)104
Performance fee0.2 × max(112 − 104, 0)1.60
Ending NAV after fees112 − 1.6110.40
Net return110.4 ÷ 100 − 110.40%
Fee drag0.14 − 0.1043.60%
The answer

Before fees the fund ends at 114. After the management fee it is 112. The fee threshold is 104, so the performance fee is 1.60 and the investor ends with 110.40, a net return of 10.40% against 14% gross. Fees took 3.60% of the gross return.
