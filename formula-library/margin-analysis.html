Margin Analysis with Year-on-Year Change

Beginner

Also known as: gross margin, operating margin, net margin, EBITDA margin, profitability ratios

Profit as a share of sales at each level, and its trend. Use it to see how much of each unit of sales becomes profit at each stage, and how that changed. A falling gross margin points to pricing or input cost pressure, and a falling operating margin with a steady gross margin points to rising overheads.

The formula

Margin=Profit measureRevenue,Δ=Marginnow−Marginprior\text{Margin}=\frac{\text{Profit measure}}{\text{Revenue}},\quad \Delta=\text{Margin}_{now}-\text{Margin}_{prior}

Gross margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue; Operating margin = Operating profit / Revenue; Net margin = Net income / Revenue; the change is shown in basis points

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
RevenueNet sales for the yearmoney
COGSCost of goods soldmoney
Operating profitRevenue less COGS and operating expenses (EBIT)money
Net incomeProfit after interest and taxmoney

Worked example

This year revenue was 1,500, cost of goods sold 900, operating profit 160 and net income 90. Last year revenue was 1,400, cost of goods sold 820, operating profit 150 and net income 84.

  1. Gross margin = (revenue - COGS) / revenue(1500-900) / 1500= 40.00%
  2. Operating margin = operating profit / revenue160 / 1500= 10.67%
  3. Net margin = net income / revenue90 / 1500= 6.00%
  4. Change in gross margin (basis points)((1500-900) / 1500-(1400-820) / 1400) x 10000= -143
  5. Change in operating margin (basis points)(160 / 1500-150 / 1400) x 10000= -5
  6. Change in net margin (basis points)(90 / 1500-84 / 1400) x 10000= 0

Gross margin is 40.00%, operating margin 10.67% and net margin 6.00%. Compared with last year, gross margin moved -143 basis points and operating margin -5. Net margin moved 0. Costs of goods grew a little faster than sales, but overheads were well controlled.
