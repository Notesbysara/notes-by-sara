RAROC and Risk Limit Utilization

Intermediate

Also known as: risk adjusted return on capital, risk appetite, VaR limit, hurdle rate

Return on risk capital and how much of a risk limit is used. Use it to compare businesses by return per unit of risk, and to track a limit set by the risk appetite. A business is creating value if RAROC beats the hurdle rate. Utilization near 100% means the desk has little room left.

The formula

RAROC=Revenue−Costs−ELEconomic capital,Utilization=VaRLimitRAROC=\frac{\text{Revenue}-\text{Costs}-EL}{\text{Economic capital}},\quad \text{Utilization}=\frac{VaR}{\text{Limit}}

RAROC = (Revenue - Costs - Expected loss) / Economic capital; Limit utilization = Risk used / Limit

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
ELExpected lossmoney
Economic capitalCapital held against unexpected lossesmoney
LimitThe maximum risk the board allows for a desk or business linemoney

Worked example

A business earns revenue of 120 and has costs of 60 and expected loss of 15. It uses 250 of economic capital and the hurdle rate is 12%. Its VaR is 4.2 against a limit of 5.0.

  1. RAROC = (revenue - costs - EL) / economic capital(120-60-15) / 250= 18.0%
  2. Excess over the hurdle0.18-0.12= 6.0%
  3. Limit utilization = VaR / limitvar / 5= 84.0%
  4. Headroom = limit - VaR5-var= 0.80

RAROC is 18.0%, above the hurdle of 12%, so the business adds value beyond its risk cost, with 6.0% of excess return. The VaR limit is 84.0% used, leaving headroom of 0.80.
