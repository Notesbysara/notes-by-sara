RAROC and Risk Limit Utilization

IntermediateRisk ManagementAlso known as: risk adjusted return on capital, risk appetite, VaR limit, hurdle rate
← R-Squared (Coefficient of Determination)Rate Expectations from Policy Rate Futures →

Finds: return on risk capital and how much of a risk limit is used.

When to use it: Use it to compare businesses by return per unit of risk, and to track a limit set by the risk appetite. A business is creating value if RAROC beats the hurdle rate. Utilization near 100% means the desk has little room left.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

RAROC\displaystyle RAROC=Revenue−Costs−ELEconomic capital\displaystyle =\frac{\text{Revenue}-\text{Costs}-EL}{\text{Economic capital}}Utilization\displaystyle \text{Utilization}=VaRLimit\displaystyle =\frac{VaR}{\text{Limit}}

In words: RAROC = (Revenue − Costs − Expected loss) / Economic capital; Limit utilization = Risk used / Limit

What each symbol means

ELExpected lossmoney
Economic capitalCapital held against unexpected lossesmoney
LimitThe maximum risk the board allows for a desk or business linemoney

Worked example

A business earns revenue of 120 and has costs of 60 and expected loss of 15. It uses 250 of economic capital and the hurdle rate is 12%. Its VaR is 4.2 against a limit of 5.0.

RAROC(120 − 60 − 15) ÷ 25018%
Excess over the hurdle0.18 − 0.126%
Limit utilizationvar ÷ 584%
Headroom5 − var0.80
The answer

RAROC is 18.0%, above the hurdle of 12%, so the business adds value beyond its risk cost, with 6.0% of excess return. The VaR limit is 84.0% used, leaving headroom of 0.80.
