Itô's Lemma: Drift and Volatility of a Function

IntermediateQuantitative FinanceAlso known as: Ito formula, stochastic calculus, drift correction, change of variable
← IQR Outlier FencesJ-Curve: Cumulative Net Cash Flow of a Fund →

Finds: the drift and volatility of a function of a random price.

When to use it: Use it to find how a function of a random price behaves. The extra second-derivative term appears because squared random shocks do not average to zero: dW squared is dt. For f = ln S it shows that the log price drifts at mu minus half the variance, which explains why median growth is below mean growth.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

df\displaystyle df=(ft+μSfS+12σ2S2fSS)dt+σSfS dW\displaystyle =\left(f_{t}+\mu S f_{S}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}S^{2}f_{SS}\right)dt+\sigma S f_{S}\,dW

In words: If dS = mu x S dt + sigma x S dW then a function f(S, t) has drift f_t + mu x S x f_S + 0.5 x sigma^2 x S^2 x f_SS and volatility sigma x S x f_S

What each symbol means

f_t, f_S, f_SSFirst derivative in time, first and second derivative in Snumber
muExpected return of Sper year
sigmaVolatility of Sper year
dWRandom shock, normal with variance dtnumber

Worked example

A stock at 100 has an expected return of 9% and a volatility of 25%. For f = ln S, the derivatives are f_t = 0, f_S = 1/S = 0.01 and f_SS = −1/S^2 = −0.0001. For g = S^2, they are 0, 2S = 200 and 2.

Drift of f0 + 0.09 × 100 × 0.01 + 0.5 × 0.25^2 × 100^2 × −0.00015.875%
Volatility of f0.25 × 100 × 0.0125%
Half the variance0.5 × 0.25^23.125%
Drift of S squared, divided by S squared(0.09 × 100 × 200 + 0.5 × 0.25^2 × 100^2 × 2) ÷ (100^2)24.25%
The answer

For ln S the drift is 5.875% per year and the volatility is 25%. The drift is the 9% return less half the variance (3.125%). For S squared, drift over S squared is 24.25%, which is 2 mu + sigma^2.
