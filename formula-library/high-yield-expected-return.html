Expected Return after Defaults: Investment Grade vs. High Yield

Intermediate

Also known as: junk bond, default adjusted yield, promised yield versus expected yield

Return after expected default losses. Use it to compare bonds of different credit quality. A high-yield bond promises more, but defaults take part of it back. What is left, compared with a safe rate, is the real reward for taking credit risk.

The formula

E(r)≈y−PD×(1−R)E(r)\approx y-PD\times(1-R)

Expected return = Promised yield - Probability of default x (1 - Recovery rate)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
yPromised yieldper year
PDAnnual probability of defaultper year
RRecovery ratepercent
E(r)Expected return after allowing for default lossesper year

Worked example

A high-yield bond promises 9% with a 4% annual default probability and 40% recovery. An investment-grade bond promises 5% with a 0.2% default probability and 40% recovery. The risk-free rate is 4%. How do the expected returns compare?

  1. High-yield expected return = y - PD x (1 - R)0.09-0.04 x (1-0.4)= 6.60%
  2. Investment-grade expected return0.05-0.002 x (1-0.4)= 4.88%
  3. High-yield excess over risk-free0.066-0.04= 2.60%
  4. Investment-grade excess over risk-free0.0488-0.04= 0.88%

Expected return is 6.60% for the high-yield bond and 4.88% for the investment-grade bond. Over the risk-free rate they earn 2.60% and 0.88%. The extra promised yield of the high-yield bond is largely eaten by expected defaults.
