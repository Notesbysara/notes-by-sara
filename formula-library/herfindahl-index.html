Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (Industry Concentration)

Intermediate

Also known as: HHI, market concentration, industry analysis, concentration ratio

How concentrated an industry is. Use it in industry analysis to see how competitive a sector is. A concentrated industry may give firms pricing power, which supports profit margins, but it also draws the attention of regulators.

The formula

HHI=∑i=1nsi2,CRk=∑i=1ksiHHI=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}^{2},\quad CR_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}

HHI = sum of the squared market shares (in percent); CR4 = sum of the four largest shares

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
s_iMarket share of company i, in percentage pointspercent
HHIIndex from 0 to 10,000. Close to 0 is many tiny firms and 10,000 is a monopolynumber
CR_kCombined share of the k largest firmspercent

Worked example

Five firms hold market shares of 35%, 25%, 20%, 12% and 8%. How concentrated is the industry?

  1. HHI = sum of squared shares (shares in whole percentage points)= 2,458
  2. CR4 = sum of the four largest shares= 92.0%

The HHI is 2,458. Above 2,500 is usually called highly concentrated. The top four firms hold 92.0% of the market.
