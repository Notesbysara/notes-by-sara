ESG Rating Divergence Between Providers

IntermediateRegulation, Ethics & ESGAlso known as: ESG ratings and their criticisms, rater disagreement, rating correlation, rank correlation
← Equivalent Annual Annuity (EAA)Estate Tax Estimate →

Finds: how much two ESG ratings disagree.

When to use it: Use it to see one reason ESG ratings are criticized: different providers disagree about the same company, because they measure different things, weight them differently and treat missing data differently. If two ratings correlate weakly, a choice of provider can change which companies a fund holds. Use the score as one input, not as a fact.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

ρ\displaystyle \rho=Cov(A,B)σAσB\displaystyle =\frac{Cov(A,B)}{\sigma_{A}\sigma_{B}}Disagreement\displaystyle \text{Disagreement}=1N∑∣rA,i−rB,i∣\displaystyle =\frac{1}{N}\sum|r_{A,i}-r_{B,i}|

In words: Correlation between two providers' scores = covariance of the scores / (standard deviation of A x standard deviation of B); Mean absolute gap = average of |score A − score B|; Share of companies on which the providers rank in opposite halves = count of firms above the median on one and below on the other / number of firms. Credit rating agencies tend to have correlations near 0.99, ESG raters much lower (studies report about 0.5 to 0.6)

What each symbol means

A, BScores for the same companies from two ESG rating providers (scaled to the same range)number
rhoCorrelation between the two sets of scoresnumber

Worked example

Ten companies were scored by two providers; the scores are in the calculator.

Correlation0.11
Mean absolute gap16.80
Share in opposite halves50%
The answer

The correlation between the providers is 0.11. The average gap between scores is 16.80 points. The two providers place 50% of the companies in opposite halves of the ranking (above the median on one and below on the other).
