Commodity Futures: Roll Yield and Total Return

Intermediate

Also known as: commodities and commodity futures, contango, backwardation, roll return, convenience yield

What rolling futures costs or earns and what the curve implies. Use it to see why returns on a commodity futures fund can differ from the spot price. A futures investor sells the contract that is expiring and buys the next one. When the curve slopes up (contango), each roll costs money; when it slopes down (backwardation), each roll adds money. Over years roll yield can matter as much as the price change.

The formula

Roll=Fnear−FfarFnear,Rtotal=Rspot+Roll+Rcollateral,y=r+u−1Tln⁡FS\text{Roll}=\frac{F_{near}-F_{far}}{F_{near}},\quad R_{total}=R_{spot}+\text{Roll}+R_{collateral},\quad y=r+u-\frac{1}{T}\ln\frac{F}{S}

Roll yield per roll = (near futures price - next futures price) / near futures price (negative in contango, positive in backwardation); Annualized roll = (1 + roll)^(12 / months between contracts) - 1; Total return of a futures investment = spot price change + roll yield + interest on the cash collateral; Implied convenience yield y = interest rate + storage cost - (1/T) x ln(F / S)

Worked example

Spot is 80. The near contract (1 month) is 81.0 and the next (4 months) is 82.4. Cash collateral earns 4%. Assume spot is flat over the year and the curve stays the same.

  1. Roll yield per roll(81-82.4) / 81= -1.73%
  2. Annualized roll yield(1+-0.017284)^(12 / 3)-1= -6.74%
  3. Total return = spot + roll + collateral0+-0.067364+0.04= -2.74%
  4. Implied convenience yield0.04+0.02-ln(82.4 / 80) / (4 / 12)= -2.87%

Each roll gives -1.73%, which is a cost because the curve is in contango. Annualized, the roll is -6.74%. With spot flat and 4% on collateral, the total return is -2.74%. The implied convenience yield, with storage costs of 2% and a 4-month contract, is -2.87%.
