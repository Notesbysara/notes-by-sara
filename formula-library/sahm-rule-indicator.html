Sahm Rule Recession Indicator

IntermediateEconomics for FinanceAlso known as: recessions and their warning signs, unemployment trigger, yield curve inversion
← Rule of 72Sample Size for Estimating a Mean →

Finds: two classic recession warning signals.

When to use it: Use it as one warning sign, alongside an inverted yield curve and falling surveys. The Sahm rule picks up a quick rise in unemployment, which has historically happened early in recessions. Neither signal is a forecast: they have had false alarms and different lags, and the rule was fitted to US data. Enter at least 15 monthly readings, oldest first.

In words: Sahm indicator = latest 3-month average unemployment rate − the lowest 3-month average of the previous 12 months. A reading of 0.5 percentage points or more has signaled the start of past US recessions. Yield curve spread = 10-year yield − 3-month yield (negative means inverted)

The answer

The latest 3-month average is 4.50% against a prior 12-month low of 3.60%, so the indicator is 0.90% points. The yield curve spread is −0.60%. Signal flags: Sahm 1 and curve inverted 1 (1 = yes, 0 = no).
