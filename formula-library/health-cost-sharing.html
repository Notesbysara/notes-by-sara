Health Plan Cost Comparison

BeginnerPersonal FinanceAlso known as: deductible, out of pocket maximum, coinsurance, health insurance plan comparison
← Growth Accounting and ProductivityHedge Fund Fees: Management, Performance, Hurdle and High-Water Mark →

Finds: which health plan costs less at a given level of use.

When to use it: Use it to compare health plans at different levels of use. A plan with high premiums and low deductibles tends to cost less in a heavy-use year, and a cheaper plan tends to win in a light year. Try your expected bills and a bad-year scenario. Real plans differ in networks and co-payments, which this simple model ignores.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

You pay\displaystyle \text{You pay}=min⁡ ⁣[M,  min⁡(B,D)+c max⁡(B−D,0)]\displaystyle =\min\!\left[M,\;\min(B,D)+c\,\max(B-D,0)\right]Total cost\displaystyle \text{Total cost}=You pay+Premiums\displaystyle =\text{You pay}+\text{Premiums}

In words: What you pay = the smaller of (out-of-pocket maximum) and [the part of the bill up to the deductible + coinsurance % x the part of the bill above the deductible]; Total yearly cost = what you pay + annual premiums

What each symbol means

BMedical bills for the year (allowed charges)money
DDeductible: what you pay firstmoney
cCoinsurance: your share of costs above the deductiblepercent
MOut-of-pocket maximummoney

Worked example

Plan A: deductible 1,500, coinsurance 20%, out-of-pocket maximum 5,000, premiums 3,600 a year. Plan B: deductible 500, coinsurance 10%, maximum 3,000, premiums 6,000. Bills for the year are 12,000.

Plan A: you paymin(5,000, min(12,000, 1,500) + 0.2 × max(12,000 − 1,500, 0))3,600
Plan B: you paymin(3,000, min(12,000, 500) + 0.1 × max(12,000 − 500, 0))1,650
Plan A total including premiums3,600 + 3,6007,200
Plan B total including premiums1,650 + 6,0007,650

Plan A total with no bills3,6003,600
Plan B total with no bills6,0006,000
The answer

At bills of 12,000, you pay 3,600 under plan A and 1,650 under plan B. With premiums, the yearly total is 7,200 for A and 7,650 for B. The cheaper plan this year is number 1 (1 = A, 2 = B), and with no medical bills the totals would be 3,600 and 6,000.
