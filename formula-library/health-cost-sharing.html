Health Plan Cost Comparison

Beginner

Also known as: deductible, out of pocket maximum, coinsurance, health insurance plan comparison

Which health plan costs less at a given level of use. Use it to compare health plans at different levels of use. A plan with high premiums and low deductibles tends to cost less in a heavy-use year, and a cheaper plan tends to win in a light year. Try your expected bills and a bad-year scenario. Real plans differ in networks and co-payments, which this simple model ignores.

The formula

You pay=min⁡ ⁣[M,  min⁡(B,D)+c max⁡(B−D,0)],Total cost=You pay+Premiums\text{You pay}=\min\!\left[M,\;\min(B,D)+c\,\max(B-D,0)\right],\quad \text{Total cost}=\text{You pay}+\text{Premiums}

What you pay = the smaller of (out-of-pocket maximum) and [the part of the bill up to the deductible + coinsurance % x the part of the bill above the deductible]; Total yearly cost = what you pay + annual premiums

What each symbol means

BMedical bills for the year (allowed charges)money

Worked example

Plan A: deductible 1,500, coinsurance 20%, out-of-pocket maximum 5,000, premiums 3,600 a year. Plan B: deductible 500, coinsurance 10%, maximum 3,000, premiums 6,000. Bills for the year are 12,000.

  1. Plan A: you paymin(5000, min(12000, 1500)+0.2 x max(12000-1500, 0))= 3,600.00
  2. Plan B: you paymin(3000, min(12000, 500)+0.1 x max(12000-500, 0))= 1,650.00
  3. Plan A total including premiums3600+3600= 7,200.00
  4. Plan B total including premiums1650+6000= 7,650.00
  5. Cheaper plan (1 = A, 2 = B)1 if 7200<=7650 else 2= 1
  6. Plan A total with no bills3600= 3,600.00
  7. Plan B total with no bills6000= 6,000.00

At bills of 12,000, you pay 3,600.00 under plan A and 1,650.00 under plan B. With premiums, the yearly total is 7,200.00 for A and 7,650.00 for B. The cheaper plan this year is number 1 (1 = A, 2 = B), and with no medical bills the totals would be 3,600.00 and 6,000.00.
