Cost of Skipping an Early-Payment Discount

IntermediateCorporate FinanceAlso known as: 2/10 net 30, trade credit cost, early payment discount, payables management
← Cost of Preferred StockCost-Benefit Test of a Financial Rule →

Finds: the true cost of not paying early.

When to use it: Use it to decide whether to pay early. A "2/10 net 30" term gives 2% off if paid within 10 days, otherwise the full amount is due in 30. Skipping the discount is like borrowing at a very high rate.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Cost\displaystyle \text{Cost}=(1+d1−d)365/(N−m)−1\displaystyle =\left(1+\frac{d}{1-d}\right)^{365/(N-m)}-1

In words: Effective annual cost = (1 + d / (1 − d))^(365 / (Final due day − Discount day)) − 1

What each symbol means

dDiscount percentage offered for early payment, as a decimaldecimal
mLast day to pay and get the discountdays
NDay the full amount is duedays

Worked example

A supplier offers terms of 2/10 net 30. What is the effective annual cost of skipping the discount and paying on day 30?

Cost for the extra days0.02 ÷ (1 − 0.02)2.04%
Extra days of credit30 − 1020
Effective annual cost(1 + 0.0204082)^(365 ÷ 20) − 144.59%
The answer

Skipping the discount costs 2.04% for 20 extra days, which is about 44.59% a year. It is usually cheaper to borrow from a bank and take the discount.
