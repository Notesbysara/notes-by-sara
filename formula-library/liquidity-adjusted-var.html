Liquidity-Adjusted VaR

IntermediateRisk ManagementAlso known as: LVaR, exogenous liquidity cost, bid ask cost in VaR, funding gap
← Linking the Three Statements: Cash Flow and Balance CheckLoan Payment (Amortization) →

Finds: market risk plus the cost of exiting.

When to use it: Use it when positions cannot be sold at the mid price. VaR assumes you can trade at the quoted price, but selling costs half the spread. In a crisis spreads widen, so the liquidity cost can be a large part of the total. This is the simple version with a fixed spread.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

LVaR\displaystyle LVaR=VaR+12 V s\displaystyle =VaR+\tfrac{1}{2}\,V\,s

In words: Liquidity-adjusted VaR = VaR + 0.5 x Position value x Relative bid-ask spread

What each symbol means

VaRMarket-risk VaR over the holding periodmoney
VPosition valuemoney
sRelative bid-ask spread (spread divided by mid price)percent

Worked example

A 5,000,000 position has a daily volatility of 1.2%. The holding period is 10 days at 99% confidence, and the relative bid-ask spread is 0.4%, which widens to 1.5% in stress.

VaR441,393.47
Liquidation cost0.5 × 5,000,000 × 0.00410,000
LVaRvar + 10,000451,393.47
Liquidation cost in stress0.5 × 5,000,000 × 0.01537,500
LVaR in stressvar + 37,500478,893.47
The answer

The 10-day VaR is 441,393.47. The liquidation cost is 10,000 in normal markets, giving a liquidity-adjusted VaR of 451,393.47. If the spread widens to 1.5% the cost rises to 37,500 and the adjusted VaR to 478,893.47.
