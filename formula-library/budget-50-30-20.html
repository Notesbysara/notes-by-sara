50/30/20 Budget and Zero-Based Check

BeginnerPersonal FinanceAlso known as: budgeting, needs wants savings, zero-based budget, budget split
ABS Excess Spread and Overcollateralization →

Finds: whether your spending matches a target split and what is left unassigned.

When to use it: Use it to check whether spending is balanced. The 50/30/20 split is a starting rule, not a law: in a high-rent city needs may take 60%, and the goal is then to shift the other two. Zero-based budgeting goes further and gives every unit of income a job, so the unassigned amount should end at zero.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Needs\displaystyle \text{Needs}=0.50 I\displaystyle =0.50\,IWants\displaystyle \text{Wants}=0.30 I\displaystyle =0.30\,ISavings\displaystyle \text{Savings}=0.20 I\displaystyle =0.20\,IUnassigned\displaystyle \text{Unassigned}=I−Needs−Wants−Savings\displaystyle =I-\text{Needs}-\text{Wants}-\text{Savings}

In words: Targets: needs 50%, wants 30%, savings and debt repayment 20% of after-tax income. Zero-based check: income − needs − wants − savings should equal 0

What each symbol means

IMonthly income after taxmoney
NeedsRent, utilities, groceries, minimum debt payments, insurance, transport to workmoney
WantsEverything optional: dining out, subscriptions, travelmoney
SavingsSavings, investing and extra debt repaymentmoney

Worked example

Take-home pay is 4,200 a month. You spend 2,300 on needs, 1,100 on wants and put 700 into savings.

Needs target4,200 × 0.52,100
Wants target4,200 × 0.31,260
Savings target4,200 × 0.2840
Actual needs share of income2,300 ÷ 4,20054.8%
Actual wants share1,100 ÷ 4,20026.2%
Actual savings share700 ÷ 4,20016.7%
Savings minus target700 − 840−140
Unassigned4,200 − 2,300 − 1,100 − 700100
The answer

The targets are 2,100 for needs, 1,260 for wants and 840 for savings. You actually spend 54.8% on needs, 26.2% on wants and save 16.7%. Savings are −140 against the target, and 100 of income has not been assigned to anything yet.
