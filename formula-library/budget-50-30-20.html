50/30/20 Budget and Zero-Based Check

Beginner

Also known as: budgeting, needs wants savings, zero-based budget, budget split

Whether your spending matches a target split and what is left unassigned. Use it to check whether spending is balanced. The 50/30/20 split is a starting rule, not a law: in a high-rent city needs may take 60%, and the goal is then to shift the other two. Zero-based budgeting goes further and gives every unit of income a job, so the unassigned amount should end at zero.

The formula

Needs=0.50 I,Wants=0.30 I,Savings=0.20 I,Unassigned=I−Needs−Wants−Savings\text{Needs}=0.50\,I,\quad \text{Wants}=0.30\,I,\quad \text{Savings}=0.20\,I,\quad \text{Unassigned}=I-\text{Needs}-\text{Wants}-\text{Savings}

Targets: needs 50%, wants 30%, savings and debt repayment 20% of after-tax income. Zero-based check: income - needs - wants - savings should equal 0

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
IMonthly income after taxmoney
NeedsRent, utilities, groceries, minimum debt payments, insurance, transport to workmoney
WantsEverything optional: dining out, subscriptions, travelmoney
SavingsSavings, investing and extra debt repaymentmoney

Worked example

Take-home pay is 4,200 a month. You spend 2,300 on needs, 1,100 on wants and put 700 into savings.

  1. Needs target = income x needs share4200 x 0.5= 2,100.00
  2. Wants target4200 x 0.3= 1,260.00
  3. Savings target4200 x 0.2= 840.00
  4. Actual needs share of income2300 / 4200= 54.8%
  5. Actual wants share1100 / 4200= 26.2%
  6. Actual savings share700 / 4200= 16.7%
  7. Savings minus target700-840= -140.00
  8. Unassigned = income - needs - wants - savings4200-2300-1100-700= 100.00

The targets are 2,100.00 for needs, 1,260.00 for wants and 840.00 for savings. You actually spend 54.8% on needs, 26.2% on wants and save 16.7%. Savings are -140.00 against the target, and 100.00 of income has not been assigned to anything yet.
