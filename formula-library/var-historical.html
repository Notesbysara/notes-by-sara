Value at Risk: Historical Simulation

Intermediate

Also known as: historical VaR, empirical VaR, percentile VaR

A loss limit read from past returns. Use it when you do not want to assume a normal distribution. It uses actual past returns, so it captures fat tails and skew in the sample, but the answer depends on the window used and cannot see a loss worse than any in the history.

The formula

VaR=−V×Q1−c(R)VaR=-V\times Q_{1-c}(R)

VaR = - Portfolio value x the (1 - confidence) percentile of past returns

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
VPortfolio valuemoney
Q_(1-c)The (1 - c) percentile of the historical returns, so the worst 5% of days for 95% confidencepercent
RList of past returnspercent

Worked example

A portfolio of 1,000,000 had these 20 daily returns (%): -1.8, 0.6, 1.1, -0.4, 0.9, -2.6, 0.3, 1.4, -0.9, 0.2, -3.1, 0.8, 1.0, -0.2, 0.5, -1.2, 1.6, -0.6, 0.4, -2.0. What is the 90% one-day VaR?

  1. The (1 - confidence) percentile of returns= -2.06%
  2. VaR = -V x percentile-1,000,000 x -0.0206= 20,600.00

The 10th percentile of the returns is -2.06%, so the 90% one-day historical VaR is 20,600.00. On the worst 10% of days the loss was at least that big.
