VaR Backtest: Exceptions and Kupiec Test

Intermediate

Also known as: backtesting VaR, traffic light test, proportion of failures, VaR exceptions

Whether a VaR model has the right number of exceptions. Use it to check whether a VaR model is calibrated. A 99% VaR should be exceeded about 1 day in 100. Too many exceptions means risk is understated, and too few means capital is wasted. The Basel traffic light for 250 days at 99% is green for 0 to 4 exceptions, yellow for 5 to 9 and red for 10 or more.

The formula

LRPOF=−2ln⁡[(1−p)N−xpx]+2ln⁡[(1−xN)N−x(xN)x]LR_{POF}=-2\ln\left[(1-p)^{N-x}p^{x}\right]+2\ln\left[\left(1-\tfrac{x}{N}\right)^{N-x}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)^{x}\right]

Kupiec statistic = -2 x ln[(1-p)^(N-x) x p^x] + 2 x ln[(1 - x/N)^(N-x) x (x/N)^x]; compare with 3.84 (5% level, 1 degree of freedom)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
NNumber of days testeddays
xNumber of days on which the loss exceeded VaR (exceptions)number
pExpected exception rate: 1 minus the VaR confidencepercent
LRLikelihood-ratio statistic. Above 3.84 rejects the model at the 5% levelnumber

Worked example

Over 250 days a 99% VaR model was exceeded 6 times.

  1. Expected exception rate p = 1 - confidence1-0.99= 1.00%
  2. Expected exceptions = N x p250 x 0.01= 2.50
  3. Observed rate = x / N6 / 250= 2.40%
  4. Kupiec LR statistic-2 x ln((1-0.01)^(250-6) x 0.01^6)+2 x ln((1-6 / 250)^(250-6) x (6 / 250)^6)= 3.555
  5. Reject the model at 5%? (1 = yes, 0 = no)1 if 3.55535>3.841 else 0= 0

With 99% confidence, about 2.50 exceptions were expected and 6 occurred (an observed rate of 2.40%). The Kupiec statistic is 3.555, just below the 3.84 cut-off, so the model is not rejected at the 5% level. Under the Basel traffic light, 6 exceptions fall in the yellow zone.
