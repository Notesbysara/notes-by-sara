Accretion/Dilution Analysis (Stock Deal)

IntermediateCorporate FinanceAlso known as: EPS accretion, pro forma EPS, merger EPS impact
← ABS Excess Spread and OvercollateralizationAccrual Ratio (Earnings Quality) →

Finds: EPS effect of a share-funded acquisition.

When to use it: Use it to see whether paying for a target with shares raises (accretive) or lowers (dilutive) the buyer's earnings per share. EPS impact is not the same as value created, but the market watches it.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

EPSpro forma\displaystyle EPS_{pro\ forma}=NIA+NITNA+Nnew\displaystyle =\frac{NI_{A}+NI_{T}}{N_{A}+N_{new}}Nnew\displaystyle N_{new}=Price paidPA\displaystyle =\frac{\text{Price paid}}{P_{A}}

In words: Pro forma EPS = (Acquirer NI + Target NI) / (Acquirer shares + New shares), where New shares = Price paid / Acquirer share price

Worked example

A buyer earns 800 with 400 shares (EPS 2). It buys a target earning 200 for 3,000, paid entirely in new shares at 40 each. Is the deal accretive?

New shares3,000 ÷ 4075
EPS before800 ÷ 4002
Pro forma EPS(800 + 200) ÷ (400 + 75)2.11
Change in EPS2.10526 ÷ 2 − 15.26%
The answer

The buyer issues 75 new shares. Pro forma EPS is 2.11 against 2 before, so the deal is accretive, raising EPS by 5.26%.
