Growth Accounting and Productivity

Intermediate

Also known as: productivity and long-run growth, Solow residual, total factor productivity, labor productivity

How much growth comes from better productivity. Use it to split growth into more inputs and better use of inputs. Long-run living standards rise mainly through productivity. The residual is whatever the inputs cannot explain, so measurement errors in capital and labor end up in it. A simple doubling-time check shows how a small growth gap compounds.

The formula

gA=gY−α gK−(1−α) gLg_{A}=g_{Y}-\alpha\,g_{K}-(1-\alpha)\,g_{L}

Productivity growth (Solow residual) = output growth - capital share x capital growth - (1 - capital share) x labor growth; Labor productivity growth = output growth - labor growth

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
g_YGrowth in real outputpercent
g_K, g_LGrowth in the capital stock and in labor inputpercent
alphaCapital's share of income (about one third)percent
g_AGrowth in total factor productivity: output growth not explained by more inputspercent

Worked example

Output grows 3%, capital 4% and labor 1%. Capital's share of income is 35%.

  1. Capital contribution = alpha x g_K0.35 x 0.04= 1.40%
  2. Labor contribution = (1 - alpha) x g_L(1-0.35) x 0.01= 0.65%
  3. Productivity growth = g_Y - capital - labor0.03-0.014-0.0065= 0.95%
  4. Labor productivity growth = g_Y - g_L0.03-0.01= 2.00%
  5. Years to double at the output growth rateln(2) / ln(1+0.03)= 23.4

Capital contributes 1.40% and labor 0.65% to growth, so productivity growth is 0.95%. Labor productivity grows 2.00% a year. At 3% growth an economy doubles in 23.4 years.
