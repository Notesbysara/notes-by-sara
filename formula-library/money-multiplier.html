Money Multiplier and Deposit Expansion

Beginner

Also known as: fractional reserve banking, deposit creation, reserve requirement, excess reserves

How far new reserves can multiply into money. Use it to see how banks create deposits by lending part of what they receive. Each loan becomes a deposit elsewhere, so a reserve injection supports several times its amount. Leakages into cash and extra reserves cut the multiplier. In practice, banks lend when they see good borrowers and capital allows, and are not limited by reserves, so this is a teaching model.

The formula

msimple=1rr,m=1+cc+rr+e,ΔD=ΔRrrm_{simple}=\frac{1}{rr},\quad m=\frac{1+c}{c+rr+e},\quad \Delta D=\frac{\Delta R}{rr}

Simple deposit multiplier = 1 / reserve ratio; Money multiplier with leakages = (1 + currency ratio) / (currency ratio + reserve ratio + excess reserve ratio); Deposit expansion from new reserves = new reserves / reserve ratio

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
rrRequired reserve ratio: share of deposits banks must hold in reservepercent
cCurrency ratio: cash held by the public per unit of depositsnumber
eExcess reserve ratio: reserves banks hold above the requirementnumber
Delta RNew reserves injectedmoney

Worked example

The required reserve ratio is 10%, the public holds 8 of cash per 100 of deposits and banks hold 2% excess reserves. The central bank adds 100 of reserves.

  1. Simple multiplier = 1 / rr1 / 0.1= 10.00
  2. Deposit expansion = new reserves / rr100 / 0.1= 1,000.00
  3. Multiplier with leakages = (1 + c) / (c + rr + e)(1+0.08) / (0.08+0.1+0.02)= 5.400
  4. Money supply change = m x new reserves5.4 x 100= 540.00

The simple multiplier is 10.00 and full deposit expansion from 100 of reserves would be 1,000.00. With leakages the multiplier falls to 5.400, so the money supply rises by 540.00.
