Solow Model Steady State

IntermediateEconomics for FinanceAlso known as: capital per worker, golden rule saving rate, long-run growth model
← SOFR Futures Price, Rate and Tick ValueSortino Ratio →

Finds: the long-run level of output per worker.

When to use it: Use it to see why economies converge to a level of income per worker, not to ever-faster growth. Saving more raises the level of income but not its long-run growth rate; only productivity growth lifts that. Consumption is maximized at the golden-rule saving rate, and saving above it is wasteful. It is a stylized model.

The formula

In words: Steady-state capital per worker k* = (s x A / (depreciation + labor force growth))^(1 / (1 − alpha)); Output per worker y* = A x k*^alpha; Consumption per worker c* = (1 − s) x y*. Consumption is highest when the saving rate s equals alpha

AProductivity levelnumber

Worked example

Saving rate 25%, productivity A = 1, depreciation 5%, labor force growth 1%, capital share 33%.

k*(0.25 × 1 ÷ (0.05 + 0.01))^(1 ÷ (1 − 0.33))8.415

The answer

Steady-state capital per worker is 8.415, giving output per worker of 2.020 and consumption per worker of 1.515. The golden-rule saving rate equals the capital share, 33%, so this economy is saving −8.0% the golden-rule level.
