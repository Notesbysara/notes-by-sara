Solow Model Steady State

Intermediate

Also known as: capital per worker, golden rule saving rate, long-run growth model

The long-run level of output per worker. Use it to see why economies converge to a level of income per worker, not to ever-faster growth. Saving more raises the level of income but not its long-run growth rate; only productivity growth lifts that. Consumption is maximized at the golden-rule saving rate, and saving above it is wasteful. It is a stylized model.

The formula

k∗=(sAδ+n)11−α,y∗=A (k∗)α,c∗=(1−s) y∗k^{*}=\left(\frac{sA}{\delta+n}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}},\quad y^{*}=A\,(k^{*})^{\alpha},\quad c^{*}=(1-s)\,y^{*}

Steady-state capital per worker k* = (s x A / (depreciation + labor force growth))^(1 / (1 - alpha)); Output per worker y* = A x k*^alpha; Consumption per worker c* = (1 - s) x y*. Consumption is highest when the saving rate s equals alpha

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
sSaving (investment) ratepercent
AProductivity levelnumber
deltaDepreciation rate of capitalper year
nGrowth rate of the labor forceper year
alphaCapital share of incomepercent

Worked example

Saving rate 25%, productivity A = 1, depreciation 5%, labor force growth 1%, capital share 33%.

  1. k* = (s A / (delta + n))^(1 / (1 - alpha))(0.25 x 1 / (0.05+0.01))^(1 / (1-0.33))= 8.415
  2. y* = A x k*^alpha1 x 8.41507^0.33= 2.020
  3. c* = (1 - s) x y*(1-0.25) x 2.01962= 1.515
  4. Golden-rule saving rate = alpha0.33= 33%
  5. Saving rate minus golden rule (negative = below)0.25-0.33= -8.0%

Steady-state capital per worker is 8.415, giving output per worker of 2.020 and consumption per worker of 1.515. The golden-rule saving rate equals the capital share, 33%, so this economy is saving -8.0% the golden-rule level.
