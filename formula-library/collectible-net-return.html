Collectibles: Repeat-Sale Return after Costs

Beginner

Also known as: art investing, collectibles and alternatives, buyer premium seller commission, holding costs of art, repeat sales

What a collectible really earns after costs. Use it before buying art, wine, watches or other collectibles. They pay no income, trade rarely and cost a lot to buy and sell, so the price has to rise a good deal just to break even. Compare the net result with what a stock index would have returned. Reported auction results show only the items that sold, so published returns can look better than a typical buyer's experience.

The formula

r=[S(1−cs)B(1+cb)]1/n−1−h,h=i+kr=\left[\frac{S(1-c_{s})}{B(1+c_{b})}\right]^{1/n}-1-h,\quad h=i+k

Annual return before holding costs = [ (sale price x (1 - seller cost)) / (purchase price x (1 + buyer cost)) ] ^ (1 / years) - 1; Net return = that return - insurance rate - storage rate; Break-even sale price = purchase price x (1 + buyer cost) x (1 + hurdle)^years / (1 - seller cost)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
B, SPurchase price and sale pricemoney
c_b, c_sBuyer premium and seller commission as a share of pricepercent
i, kYearly insurance and storage cost as a share of valuepercent
nYears heldyears

Worked example

A painting is bought for 1,000,000 with a 5% buyer premium and sold 8 years later for 1,800,000 with a 12% seller commission. Insurance costs 0.35% and storage 0.15% each year. Compare with equities at 7%.

  1. Annual return after trading costs(1,800,000 x (1-0.12) / (1,000,000 x (1+0.05)))^(1 / 8)-1= 5.27%
  2. Holding costs per year0.0035+0.0015= 0.50%
  3. Net return per year0.0527391-0.005= 4.77%
  4. Sale price needed to match the alternative1,000,000 x (1+0.05) x (1+0.07+0.005)^8 / (1-0.12)= 2,128,013.31

After buying and selling costs the price return is 5.27% a year. Holding costs take 0.50%, so the net return is 4.77%, against 7% for equities. To match equities the painting would have to sell for at least 2,128,013.31.
