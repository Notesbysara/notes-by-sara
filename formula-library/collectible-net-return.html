Collectibles: Repeat-Sale Return after Costs

BeginnerAlternative InvestmentsAlso known as: art investing, collectibles and alternatives, buyer premium seller commission, holding costs of art, repeat sales
← Collar (Protective Put Financed by a Covered Call)Combined Ratio (Insurers) →

Finds: what a collectible really earns after costs.

When to use it: Use it before buying art, wine, watches or other collectibles. They pay no income, trade rarely and cost a lot to buy and sell, so the price has to rise a good deal just to break even. Compare the net result with what a stock index would have returned. Reported auction results show only the items that sold, so published returns can look better than a typical buyer's experience.

The formula

In words: Annual return before holding costs = [ (sale price x (1 − seller cost)) / (purchase price x (1 + buyer cost)) ] ^ (1 / years) − 1; Net return = that return − insurance rate − storage rate; Break-even sale price = purchase price x (1 + buyer cost) x (1 + hurdle)^years / (1 − seller cost)

What each symbol means

B, SPurchase price and sale pricemoney
c_b, c_sBuyer premium and seller commission as a share of pricepercent
i, kYearly insurance and storage cost as a share of valuepercent
nYears heldyears

Worked example

A painting is bought for 1,000,000 with a 5% buyer premium and sold 8 years later for 1,800,000 with a 12% seller commission. Insurance costs 0.35% and storage 0.15% each year. Compare with equities at 7%.

Annual return after trading costs(1,800,000 × (1 − 0.12) ÷ (1,000,000 × (1 + 0.05)))^(1 ÷ 8) − 15.27%
Holding costs per year0.0035 + 0.00150.50%
Net return per year0.0527391 − 0.0054.77%
Sale price needed to match the alternative1,000,000 × (1 + 0.05) × (1 + 0.07 + 0.005)^8 ÷ (1 − 0.12)2,128,013.31
The answer

After buying and selling costs the price return is 5.27% a year. Holding costs take 0.50%, so the net return is 4.77%, against 7% for equities. To match equities the painting would have to sell for at least 2,128,013.31.
