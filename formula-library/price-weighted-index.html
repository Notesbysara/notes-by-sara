Price-Weighted Index and Divisor

BeginnerEquity MarketsAlso known as: Dow Jones, stock split divisor, index divisor, Nikkei
← Price Target and Upside (Equity Research)Price-Yield Relationship: Duration and Convexity Estimate →

Finds: an index level and its divisor after a split.

When to use it: Use it for an index like the Dow, where a high-priced stock counts for more than a low-priced one, whatever the company's size. When a stock splits, its price falls, so the divisor is lowered to stop the index from dropping.

The formula

I\displaystyle I=∑Pid\displaystyle =\frac{\sum P_{i}}{d}dnew\displaystyle d_{new}=∑Pi,afterI\displaystyle =\frac{\sum P_{i,after}}{I}

In words: Index = Sum of the share prices / Divisor; New divisor after a split = New sum of prices / Index level before the split

What each symbol means

P_iShare price of company imoney

Worked example

An index holds three stocks priced 120, 45 and 80, with a divisor of 3. The first stock splits 2 for 1. What is the index before the split, and what is the new divisor?

Index81.67
Sum of prices after the split of the first stock185
New divisor185 ÷ 81.66672.2653
The answer

The index is 81.67 before the split. After the split the prices add up to 185, so the divisor falls to 2.2653 to keep the index at 81.67.
