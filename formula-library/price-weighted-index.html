Price-Weighted Index and Divisor

Beginner

Also known as: Dow Jones, stock split divisor, index divisor, Nikkei

An index level and its divisor after a split. Use it for an index like the Dow, where a high-priced stock counts for more than a low-priced one, whatever the company's size. When a stock splits, its price falls, so the divisor is lowered to stop the index from dropping.

The formula

I=∑Pid,dnew=∑Pi,afterII=\frac{\sum P_{i}}{d},\quad d_{new}=\frac{\sum P_{i,after}}{I}

Index = Sum of the share prices / Divisor; New divisor after a split = New sum of prices / Index level before the split

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
P_iShare price of company imoney
dDivisor, a number that keeps the index continuous when something like a split changes the pricesnumber
IIndex levelpoints

Worked example

An index holds three stocks priced 120, 45 and 80, with a divisor of 3. The first stock splits 2 for 1. What is the index before the split, and what is the new divisor?

  1. Index = sum of prices / divisor= 81.67
  2. Sum of prices after the split of the first stock= 185.00
  3. New divisor = new sum / index185 / 81.6667= 2.2653

The index is 81.67 before the split. After the split the prices add up to 185.00, so the divisor falls to 2.2653 to keep the index at 81.67.
