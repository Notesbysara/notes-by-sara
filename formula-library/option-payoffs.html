Call and Put Payoffs and Profit

Beginner

Also known as: option profit, breakeven, long call, short call, long put, short put, payoff diagram

What an option pays at expiry and the profit after cost. Use it to draw or calculate the result of holding an option to expiry. The buyer's loss is limited to the premium. The seller's loss on a call is unlimited, and on a put it is limited to the strike minus the premium. The breakeven is where profit crosses zero.

The formula

Call payoff=max⁡(ST−K,0),Put payoff=max⁡(K−ST,0)\text{Call payoff}=\max(S_{T}-K,0),\quad \text{Put payoff}=\max(K-S_{T},0)

Call payoff = max(Spot at expiry - Strike, 0); Put payoff = max(Strike - Spot at expiry, 0); Profit = Payoff - Premium paid (a seller's profit is the opposite)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
S_TPrice of the underlying at expirymoney
KStrike pricemoney
PremiumPrice paid for the option todaymoney

Worked example

A call and a put both have a strike of 50. The call cost 3.20 and the put cost 2.10. The stock ends at 55. What are the payoffs, profits and breakevens?

  1. Call payoff = max(ST - K, 0)max(55-50, 0)= 5.00
  2. Call buyer profit = payoff - premium5-3.2= 1.80
  3. Put payoff = max(K - ST, 0)max(50-55, 0)= 0.00
  4. Put buyer profit = payoff - premium0-2.1= -2.10
  5. Call breakeven = K + premium50+3.2= 53.20
  6. Put breakeven = K - premium50-2.1= 47.90

The call pays 5.00 for a profit of 1.80 to the buyer and a loss of the same size to the seller. The put expires worthless, so the buyer loses the premium of 2.10 (profit -2.10) and the seller keeps it. The call breaks even at 53.20 and the put at 47.90.
