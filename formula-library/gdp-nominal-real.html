GDP: Expenditure Approach, Real GDP and Deflator

Beginner

Also known as: GDP and how it is measured, C+I+G+NX, GDP deflator, real growth

GDP, real GDP and growth. Use it to measure the size of an economy and separate real growth from price rises. Nominal GDP can rise purely because prices rose, so real GDP at constant prices is used to measure growth. Imports are subtracted because they are already inside C, I and G but were not produced at home.

The formula

Y=C+I+G+(X−M),Real GDP=Nominal GDPDeflator/100,g=RealtRealt−1−1Y=C+I+G+(X-M),\quad \text{Real GDP}=\frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Deflator}/100},\quad g=\frac{\text{Real}_{t}}{\text{Real}_{t-1}}-1

GDP = Consumption + Investment + Government spending + (Exports - Imports); Real GDP = Nominal GDP / (GDP deflator / 100); Growth = Real GDP this year / Real GDP last year - 1

Worked example

In a year: consumption 14,000, investment 3,600, government 3,700, exports 2,500, imports 3,000. The deflator is 108 (base year 100). Real GDP last year was 19,200.

  1. Net exports = X - M2500-3000= -500.00
  2. Nominal GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)14000+3600+3700+-500= 20,800.00
  3. Real GDP = nominal / (deflator / 100)20800 / (108 / 100)= 19,259.26
  4. Real growth = real GDP / last year's - 119259.3 / 19200-1= 0.31%

Nominal GDP is 20,800.00. Divided by the deflator it is 19,259.26 in constant prices, so real growth is 0.31%. Net exports are -500.00, a deficit that subtracts from GDP.
