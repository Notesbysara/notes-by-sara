Diversification: Risk of an Equally Weighted Portfolio

IntermediateInvestments & Portfolio TheoryAlso known as: systematic risk, unsystematic risk, idiosyncratic risk, number of stocks needed
← Disposition Effect: Gains vs Losses RealizedDividend Discount Model (Gordon Growth) →

Finds: how much risk diversification can remove.

When to use it: Use it to see how adding assets cuts risk. The first term (the risk specific to each asset) shrinks as n grows. The second term (the risk common to all assets) does not, and it is the part you cannot diversify away.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

σp2\displaystyle \sigma_{p}^{2}=σ2n+(1−1n)ρσ2\displaystyle =\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\rho\sigma^{2}

In words: Variance of n equal-weighted assets = s^2 / n + (1 − 1/n) x rho x s^2

What each symbol means

sigmaStandard deviation of each asset, assumed equalper year
rhoAverage correlation between any two assetsnumber
nNumber of assets in the portfolionumber

Worked example

Each stock has a standard deviation of 30% and the average correlation between stocks is 0.3. What is the risk of a portfolio of 20 equal-weighted stocks, and what is the lowest it can ever reach?

Asset-specific part0.3^2 ÷ 200.00450
Common part(1 − 1 ÷ 20) × 0.3 × 0.3^20.02565
Portfolio standard deviationsqrt(0.0045 + 0.02565)17.36%
Floor as n grows without limit0.3 × sqrt(0.3)16.43%
The answer

With 20 stocks the portfolio's standard deviation is 17.36%, down from 30% for one stock. The floor, however many stocks you add, is 16.43%. That floor is the systematic risk.
