Diversification: Risk of an Equally Weighted Portfolio

Intermediate

Also known as: systematic risk, unsystematic risk, idiosyncratic risk, number of stocks needed

How much risk diversification can remove. Use it to see how adding assets cuts risk. The first term (the risk specific to each asset) shrinks as n grows. The second term (the risk common to all assets) does not, and it is the part you cannot diversify away.

The formula

σp2=σ2n+(1−1n)ρσ2\sigma_{p}^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}+\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\rho\sigma^{2}

Variance of n equal-weighted assets = s^2 / n + (1 - 1/n) x rho x s^2

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
sigmaStandard deviation of each asset, assumed equalper year
rhoAverage correlation between any two assetsnumber
nNumber of assets in the portfolionumber

Worked example

Each stock has a standard deviation of 30% and the average correlation between stocks is 0.3. What is the risk of a portfolio of 20 equal-weighted stocks, and what is the lowest it can ever reach?

  1. Asset-specific part = s^2 / n0.3^2 / 20= 0.00450
  2. Common part = (1 - 1/n) x rho x s^2(1-1 / 20) x 0.3 x 0.3^2= 0.02565
  3. Portfolio standard deviation = square root of the sumsqrt(0.0045+0.02565)= 17.36%
  4. Floor as n grows without limit = s x square root of rho0.3 x sqrt(0.3)= 16.43%

With 20 stocks the portfolio's standard deviation is 17.36%, down from 30% for one stock. The floor, however many stocks you add, is 16.43%. That floor is the systematic risk.
