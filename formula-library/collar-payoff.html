Collar (Protective Put Financed by a Covered Call)

IntermediateDerivativesAlso known as: zero cost collar, protective put, covered call, floor and cap
← Cointegration: Hedge Ratio, Spread Z-Score and Half-LifeCollectibles: Repeat-Sale Return after Costs →

Finds: the floor, cap and profit of a collar.

When to use it: Use it to protect a share you own at low cost. The put sets a floor under the value, and selling a call pays for the put but caps the upside. A collar with a net premium near zero is called a zero-cost collar.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Value\displaystyle \text{Value}=min⁡(max⁡(ST,Kp),Kc)\displaystyle =\min\left(\max(S_{T},K_{p}),K_{c}\right)

In words: Collar value at expiry = min( max(Price at expiry, Put strike), Call strike ); Profit = Value − Stock price paid − Net premium

What each symbol means

K_pStrike of the put you buy, the floormoney
K_cStrike of the call you sell, the capmoney
S_0Price paid for the stockmoney
Net premiumPut premium paid minus call premium receivedmoney

Worked example

You own a stock bought at 100. You buy a 95 put for 2.40 and sell a 110 call for 2.60. What are the worst case, the best case and the profit if the stock ends at 118?

Net premium2.4 − 2.6−0.20
Worst-case profit95 − 100−−0.2−4.80
Best-case profit110 − 100−−0.210.20
Value at expirymin(max(118, 95), 110)110
Profit110 − 100−−0.210.20
The answer

The net premium is −0.20, so the collar is slightly profitable up front. The worst outcome is a profit of −4.80 and the best is 10.20. With the stock at 118, the value is capped at 110 and the profit is 10.20.
