Free Cash Flow vs. Net Income

BeginnerFinancial Statement AnalysisAlso known as: FCF conversion, cash conversion of earnings, free cash flow yield
← Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF)Fundamental Law of Active Management →

Finds: cash left after investment compared with reported profit.

When to use it: Use it to see how much profit is left as cash after spending needed to maintain and grow the business. A company can report profits and still burn cash if capital spending is heavy. Conversion well below 100% for several years is a concern.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

FCF\displaystyle FCF=CFO−CapEx\displaystyle =CFO-\text{CapEx}Conversion\displaystyle \text{Conversion}=FCFNI\displaystyle =\frac{FCF}{NI}

In words: Free cash flow = Cash flow from operations − Capital expenditure; Conversion = FCF / Net income; FCF yield = FCF / Market cap

What each symbol means

CFOCash flow from operationsmoney
CapExCapital expendituremoney
NINet incomemoney
Market capValue of the equitymoney

Worked example

A company has cash flow from operations of 140, capital expenditure of 80 and net income of 90. Its market cap is 900.

FCF140 − 8060
Conversion60 ÷ 9066.7%
FCF yield60 ÷ 9006.67%
The answer

Free cash flow is 60. That is 66.7% of net income, and a yield of 6.67% on the market cap.
