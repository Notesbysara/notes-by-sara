Stablecoin Reserve Backing and Issuer Income

IntermediateFintech & Digital FinanceAlso known as: stablecoins, reserve ratio, collateralization, T-bill income, de-peg buffer
← SRISK (Systemic Risk Contribution)Stock Split →

Finds: A stablecoin's backing, income and run risk.

When to use it: Use it to judge how safe a fiat-backed stablecoin is and how the issuer makes money. Full backing in cash and short bills should let every holder redeem at par, but a run on redemptions can exceed the cash on hand. The issuer earns interest on reserves, which is why the business is profitable when rates are high.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

CR\displaystyle CR=RL\displaystyle =\frac{R}{L}Buffer\displaystyle \text{Buffer}=R−L\displaystyle =R-LIncome\displaystyle \text{Income}=Rtb×y\displaystyle =R_{tb}\times y

In words: Backing ratio = reserve assets / coins in circulation; Buffer = reserves − coins in circulation; Issuer income per year = government bills held x yield (holders usually receive none); Run loss = coins redeemed in a run that cannot be paid from cash = max(0, redemptions − cash)

What each symbol means

RReserve assets (cash and short government bills)money
LCoins in circulation, each redeemable for 1money
yYield on the billsper year

Worked example

An issuer has 30bn in cash and 70bn in Treasury bills against 98bn of coins. Bills yield 4.3%. In a run, holders try to redeem 20% of coins at once.

The answer

The backing ratio is 102.04% and the buffer is 2,000,000,000. Annual income from bills is 3,010,000,000. A run of 20% means 19,600,000,000 to be paid out, which is 1 by cash on hand (1 = cash is enough, 0 = bills would have to be sold quickly). Cash covers 100.0% of that run.
