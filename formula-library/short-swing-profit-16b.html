Short-Swing Profit Recovery (US Section 16(b))

IntermediateRegulation, Ethics & ESGAlso known as: Securities Exchange Act 1934, insider short-swing rule, lowest-in highest-out
← Short Sale Profit and ReturnSimple Interest →

Finds: the profit a corporate insider must pay back.

When to use it: Use it as an example of how the 1934 Act limits profits by insiders (officers, directors, holders of more than 10%) from quick in-and-out trading. Liability does not depend on whether the insider used secret information. This simplified version matches the lowest purchase with the highest sale and does not include the full rules on derivatives or exemptions.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Recoverable\displaystyle \text{Recoverable}=∑matched(Psale−Pbuy)×q\displaystyle =\sum_{matched}(P_{sale}-P_{buy})\times q

In words: For a company insider, a purchase and a sale of the company's stock within six months can be matched; the profit to be paid back to the company is calculated by matching the lowest-priced purchases with the highest-priced sales; Recoverable profit = sum over matched shares of (sale price − purchase price) x shares matched; Losses on other matches cannot be used to offset it

What each symbol means

qShares matched between a purchase and a saleshares
P_sale, P_buyPrices of the matched sale and purchasemoney

Worked example

A director buys 10,000 shares at 42 and 5,000 at 45, then sells 12,000 shares at 51 within six months.

Shares matched at the lower pricemin(12,000, 10,000)10,000
Shares matched at the higher pricemin(12,000 − 10,000, 5,000)2,000
Recoverable profit10,000 × (51 − 42) + 2,000 × (51 − 45)102,000
The answer

The 12,000 shares sold are matched with the cheapest purchases first: 10,000 shares at 42 and 2,000 shares at 45. The recoverable profit is 102,000. The insider's own overall profit on the sale and the leftover shares is a separate matter.
