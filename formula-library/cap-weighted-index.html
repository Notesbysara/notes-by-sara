Market-Cap-Weighted Index

Intermediate

Also known as: S&P 500 method, value weighted index, free float index, index weights

An index level and the weight of each company. Use it for indices like the S&P 500 and FTSE 100, where each company counts in proportion to its size. A big company moves the index more than a small one.

The formula

It=I0×∑Pi,tQi∑Pi,0QiI_{t}=I_{0}\times\frac{\sum P_{i,t}Q_{i}}{\sum P_{i,0}Q_{i}}

Index level = Starting level x (Total market value now / Total market value at the start)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
P_iShare price of company imoney
Q_iNumber of shares of company i counted in the index (the free float)number
I_0Index level at the start, often 100 or 1,000points
w_iWeight of company i = its market value / total market valuepercent

Worked example

A three-stock index started at 100 when prices were 50, 20 and 10 and shares in issue (millions) were 40, 100 and 300. Prices are now 55, 18 and 12. What is the index, and what are the weights?

  1. Market value at the start = sum of price x shares= 7,000.0
  2. Market value now= 7,600.0
  3. Index = i0 x v1 / v0100 x 7600 / 7000= 108.57
  4. Current weights = price x shares / total= 28.95%, 23.68%, 47.37%

Total market value went from 7,000.0 to 7,600.0, so the index is 108.57. The current weights are 28.95%, 23.68%, 47.37%.
