Barrier Option Payoff

Intermediate

Also known as: knock-out option, knock-in option, up and out call, down and in put

What a knock-out call pays. Use it to see how a barrier changes a call's payoff. Knock-out options are cheaper than standard options because they can disappear. A knock-in plus a knock-out with the same barrier and strike equals one ordinary option.

The formula

Up-and-out call={0max⁡(St)≥Bmax⁡(ST−K,0)otherwise\text{Up-and-out call}=\begin{cases}0 & \max(S_{t})\ge B\\ \max(S_{T}-K,0) & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}

An up-and-out call pays max(ST - K, 0) only if the price never touched the barrier B; an up-and-in call pays it only if the barrier was touched

Worked example

The prices on observation dates are 100, 104, 98, 106, 110 and 108. A call has a strike of 100 and a barrier of 115 (up-and-out), and another has a barrier of 109 (up-and-out). What does each pay?

  1. Highest price reachedmax([100, 104, 98, 106, 110, 108])= 110.00
  2. Payoff with the first barrier0 if 110>=115 else max([100, 104, 98, 106, 110, 108][-1]-100, 0)= 8.00
  3. Payoff with the second barrier0 if 110>=109 else max([100, 104, 98, 106, 110, 108][-1]-100, 0)= 0.00

The highest price was 110.00. The option with a barrier of 115 never reached it, so it pays 8.00. The option with a barrier of 109 was knocked out because the price reached 110, so it pays 0.00.
