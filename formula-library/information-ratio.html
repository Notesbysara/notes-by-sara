Information Ratio and Tracking Error

IntermediateInvestments & Portfolio TheoryAlso known as: IR, tracking error, active return per unit of active risk
← Inflation-Linked Bond (TIPS) Cash Flows and Breakeven InflationInfrastructure Project: DSCR and Loan Life Cover →

Finds: reward for active risk against a benchmark.

When to use it: Use it to judge an active manager against a benchmark. Tracking error shows how far the portfolio strays from the benchmark, and the information ratio shows whether the extra risk was rewarded.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

TE\displaystyle TE=σ(Rp−Rb)\displaystyle =\sigma(R_{p}-R_{b})IR\displaystyle IR=Rp−Rb‾TE\displaystyle =\frac{\overline{R_{p}-R_{b}}}{TE}

In words: Tracking error = standard deviation of (Portfolio return − Benchmark return); IR = average active return / tracking error

What each symbol means

R_p - R_bActive return, the portfolio return minus the benchmark return in each periodpercent
TETracking error, the standard deviation of the active returnspercent
IRInformation ratio, active return earned per unit of active risknumber

Worked example

Over six months a fund returned 2.5%, −1.0%, 3.0%, 1.5%, −2.0% and 4.0%, while its benchmark returned 2.0%, −1.5%, 2.2%, 1.8%, −1.2% and 3.1%. What are the tracking error and information ratio?

Active return each period0.50%, 0.50%, 0.80%, −0.30%, −0.80%, 0.90%
Average active return0.27%
Tracking error0.67%
Information ratio0.00266667 ÷ 0.006713170.397
Annualized tracking error0.00671317 × sqrt(12)2.33%
Annualized IR0.397229 × sqrt(12)1.376
The answer

The average active return is 0.27% a month with a tracking error of 0.67%, which is 2.33% annualized. The information ratio is 0.397 per month, or 1.376 annualized.
