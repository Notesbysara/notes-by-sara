Forward Exchange Rate and Forward Points

Intermediate

Also known as: spot and forward exchange rates, forward premium, forward discount, swap points

The forward rate and its premium or discount. Use it to price a forward exchange contract. The forward is set so that no one can profit from borrowing in one currency and lending in the other: the currency with the higher interest rate trades at a forward discount. Forward points are the quoted difference, added to the spot. The forward is not a prediction of the future spot rate. Day-count conventions vary by currency.

The formula

F=S 1+rdT/3601+rfT/360,Points=(F−S)×10,000F=S\,\frac{1+r_{d}T/360}{1+r_{f}T/360},\quad \text{Points}=(F-S)\times10{,}000

Forward rate = spot x (1 + domestic rate x days / 360) / (1 + foreign rate x days / 360), where rates are quoted as domestic currency per 1 unit of foreign currency; Forward points = (forward - spot) x 10,000; Annualized forward premium = (F / S - 1) x 360 / days

Worked example

EUR/USD spot is 1.0850 (USD per EUR). The 6-month USD rate is 5% and the EUR rate is 3%, with 180 days to the forward date.

  1. Forward rate1.085 x (1+0.05 x 180 / 360) / (1+0.03 x 180 / 360)= 1.09569
  2. Forward points = (F - S) x 10,000(1.09569-1.085) x 10000= 106.9
  3. Annualized forward premium = (F / S - 1) x 360 / days(1.09569 / 1.085-1) x 360 / 180= 1.97%

The 180-day forward rate is 1.09569, which is 106.9 points above spot. The euro trades at a forward premium of 1.97% a year because USD interest rates are higher. Locking in the forward means a euro buyer pays 1.09569 for each euro at the forward date.
