Deferred Tax from Accelerated Depreciation

Intermediate

Also known as: deferred tax liability, temporary difference, tax expense vs tax paid, DTL

Why tax expense differs from tax paid. Use it to see why the tax expense in the income statement differs from the tax actually paid. When the tax authority allows faster depreciation than the accounts use, tax paid now is lower and a deferred tax liability builds up, which reverses later. Analysts treat a growing deferred tax liability on a growing business as close to permanent.

The formula

Tax expense=t×PBT,Current tax=t×(PBT−Extra tax depreciation),Deferred tax=Expense−Current\text{Tax expense}=t\times PBT,\quad \text{Current tax}=t\times(PBT-\text{Extra tax depreciation}),\quad \text{Deferred tax}=\text{Expense}-\text{Current}

Tax expense = Tax rate x Pre-tax book profit; Current tax payable = Tax rate x (Pre-tax profit - Extra depreciation allowed for tax); Deferred tax = Tax expense - Current tax

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
PBTPre-tax profit in the accountsmoney
tTax ratepercent
Extra tax depreciationDepreciation claimed on the tax return above the depreciation in the accountsmoney
DTLDeferred tax liability, tax that is postponed and will be paid later as the difference reversesmoney

Worked example

A company has pre-tax profit of 200 and a tax rate of 25%. For tax it claimed 60 more depreciation than in its accounts.

  1. Tax expense = t x PBT0.25 x 200= 50.00
  2. Current tax payable = t x (PBT - extra depreciation)0.25 x (200-60)= 35.00
  3. Deferred tax = expense - currentexp-35= 15.00
  4. Temporary difference x t (increase in the deferred tax liability)60 x 0.25= 15.00

The income statement shows tax expense of 50.00, but only 35.00 is payable now. The difference of 15.00 is added to the deferred tax liability. The balance rises by 15.00.
