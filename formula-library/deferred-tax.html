Deferred Tax from Accelerated Depreciation

IntermediateFinancial Statement AnalysisAlso known as: deferred tax liability, temporary difference, tax expense vs tax paid, DTL
← Default Probability and Recovery from Credit SpreadDeFi Automated Market Maker: Constant-Product Swap →

Finds: why tax expense differs from tax paid.

When to use it: Use it to see why the tax expense in the income statement differs from the tax actually paid. When the tax authority allows faster depreciation than the accounts use, tax paid now is lower and a deferred tax liability builds up, which reverses later. Analysts treat a growing deferred tax liability on a growing business as close to permanent.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Tax expense\displaystyle \text{Tax expense}=t×PBT\displaystyle =t\times PBTCurrent tax\displaystyle \text{Current tax}=t×(PBT−Extra tax depreciation)\displaystyle =t\times(PBT-\text{Extra tax depreciation})Deferred tax\displaystyle \text{Deferred tax}=Expense−Current\displaystyle =\text{Expense}-\text{Current}

In words: Tax expense = Tax rate x Pre-tax book profit; Current tax payable = Tax rate x (Pre-tax profit − Extra depreciation allowed for tax); Deferred tax = Tax expense − Current tax

What each symbol means

PBTPre-tax profit in the accountsmoney
tTax ratepercent
Extra tax depreciationDepreciation claimed on the tax return above the depreciation in the accountsmoney
DTLDeferred tax liability, tax that is postponed and will be paid later as the difference reversesmoney

Worked example

A company has pre-tax profit of 200 and a tax rate of 25%. For tax it claimed 60 more depreciation than in its accounts.

Tax expense0.25 × 20050
Current tax payable0.25 × (200 − 60)35
Deferred taxexp − 3515
Temporary difference x t (increase in the deferred tax liability)60 × 0.2515
The answer

The income statement shows tax expense of 50, but only 35 is payable now. The difference of 15 is added to the deferred tax liability. The balance rises by 15.
