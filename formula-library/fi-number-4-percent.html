Financial Independence Number and the 4% Rule

Beginner

Also known as: financial independence, FIRE, 4 percent rule, safe withdrawal rate, 25 times expenses

The portfolio size that supports your spending and the years to reach it. Use it to estimate how large a portfolio must be to fund spending, and how long saving will take. The 4% rule comes from historical US data for a 30-year retirement; a longer retirement, lower expected returns or high fees point to a lower rate such as 3 to 3.5%. It is a guide, not a guarantee, and it does not include pensions or taxes. Working in real terms keeps the arithmetic free of inflation.

The formula

FI=Ew,n=ln⁡ ⁣[(FI r+s)/(P r+s)]ln⁡(1+r)FI=\frac{E}{w},\quad n=\frac{\ln\!\left[(FI\,r+s)/(P\,r+s)\right]}{\ln(1+r)}

Financial independence number = annual spending / safe withdrawal rate (4% gives 25 x spending); Years to get there = ln[(FI x r + s) / (current portfolio x r + s)] / ln(1 + r), where s is the yearly saving and r is the real return

Worked example

Annual spending is 36,000 and you assume a 4% withdrawal rate. You have 150,000, save 24,000 a year from an income of 80,000, and earn a real return of 5%.

  1. FI number = spending / withdrawal rate36000 / 0.04= 900,000.00
  2. Progress = portfolio / FI number150000 / 900000= 16.7%
  3. Savings rate = saving / income24000 / 80000= 30.0%
  4. Years to reach FImax(ln((900000 x 0.05+24000) / (150000 x 0.05+24000)) / ln(1+0.05), 0)= 16.1
  5. FI number at a 3.5% withdrawal rate36000 / 0.035= 1,028,571.43

The financial independence number is 900,000.00. You are 16.7% of the way there, saving 30.0% of your income. At this pace it takes 16.1 years. At a 3.5% withdrawal rate the number would be 1,028,571.43.
