Stress Test Loss and Capital Buffer

Beginner

Also known as: scenario analysis, reverse stress test, shock scenario, stress testing

The loss in a named scenario and how it compares with capital. Use it to see what happens in a specific bad scenario, such as an equity crash or a credit shock, without relying on a statistical model. A reverse stress test works the other way: it asks how large the shock must be to wipe out capital.

The formula

Loss=V∑iwi si,Multiple to breach=CapitalLoss\text{Loss}=V\sum_{i}w_{i}\,s_{i},\quad \text{Multiple to breach}=\frac{\text{Capital}}{\text{Loss}}

Scenario loss = Portfolio value x sum of (Weight of asset i x Shock to asset i); Multiple of the shock that exhausts capital = Capital / Loss

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
w_iShare of the portfolio in asset class ipercent
s_iPercentage price move assumed for asset class i in the scenariopercent
CapitalLoss-absorbing capital or risk budgetmoney

Worked example

A 100 million portfolio holds 40% in shares, 30% in corporate bonds, 20% in property and 10% in government bonds. A crisis scenario assumes moves of -30%, -10%, -15% and +4%. Capital is 12 million.

  1. Portfolio return in the scenario = sum of weight x shock= -17.60%
  2. Loss in money = -V x portfolio return-100 x -0.176= 17.60
  3. Capital as a share of the loss = capital / loss12 / 17.6= 68.2%
  4. Loss as a multiple of capital17.6 / 12= 1.47
  5. Shock multiple that uses up all capital = capital / loss12 / 17.6= 0.68

The scenario gives a portfolio return of -17.60%, a loss of 17.60 million. The capital buffer of 12 million covers only 68.2% of that loss, so the loss is 1.47 times the buffer. Capital would be exhausted by a shock about 0.68 times the size assumed, which here is less than one, so the buffer already fails.
