Repricing Gap and Net Interest Income

Beginner

Also known as: asset liability management, rate sensitivity gap, NII sensitivity

How a rate move changes next-year income. Use it to see how a rate change affects income over the next year. A negative gap (more liabilities than assets repricing) means a rate rise cuts income, because funding costs go up faster than yields. It assumes every item reprices fully and by the same amount, which is not true of deposits, so use the pass-through version for a more realistic picture.

The formula

GAP=RSA−RSL,ΔNII=GAP×ΔrGAP=RSA-RSL,\quad \Delta NII=GAP\times\Delta r

Repricing gap = rate-sensitive assets - rate-sensitive liabilities (those that reset within the horizon); Change in net interest income = gap x change in interest rates

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
RSAAssets that reprice within the horizon: floating loans, maturing securitiesmoney
RSLLiabilities that reprice within the horizon: short deposits and borrowingsmoney
Delta rChange in interest ratespercent

Worked example

Rate-sensitive assets are 3,000 and rate-sensitive liabilities are 3,800 over the next year. Rates rise 1 percentage point.

  1. Gap = RSA - RSL3000-3800= -800.00
  2. Change in NII for the rate change-800 x 0.01= -8.00
  3. Change in NII if rates fall by the same amount--800 x 0.01= 8.00
  4. Gap as a share of total assets-800 / 8000= -10.0%

The one-year gap is -800.00, a negative gap. A 1 point rise in rates changes net interest income by -8.00, and a 1 point fall changes it by 8.00. The gap is -10.0% of total assets.
