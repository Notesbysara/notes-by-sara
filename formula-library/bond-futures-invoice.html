Bond Futures Invoice Price and Basis

Intermediate

Also known as: conversion factor, cheapest to deliver, treasury bond futures, delivery

The cash for delivery and the basis of a bond. Use it to see what the short side of a Treasury bond future receives when delivering a bond. The short chooses which bond to deliver and picks the one with the smallest basis, the cheapest to deliver.

The formula

Invoice=F×CF+AI,Basis=Pclean−F×CF\text{Invoice}=F\times CF+AI,\quad \text{Basis}=P_{clean}-F\times CF

Invoice price = Futures price x Conversion factor + Accrued interest; Basis = Clean price of the bond - Futures price x Conversion factor

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
FFutures settlement price (per 100)number
CFConversion factor, which puts each deliverable bond on a 6%-yield basisnumber
AIInterest accrued on the delivered bond since its last coupon (per 100)number
P_cleanQuoted price of the bond (per 100)number

Worked example

Treasury bond futures settle at 110.50. A deliverable bond has a conversion factor of 0.9120, accrued interest of 1.25 per 100 and a clean price of 101.30. A contract covers 100,000 face value. What does the short side receive and what is the basis?

  1. Invoice price per 100 = F x CF + AI110.5 x 0.912+1.25= 102.0260
  2. Cash received for the contract102.026 x 100000 / 100= 102,026.00
  3. Basis = clean price - F x CF101.3-110.5 x 0.912= 0.5240

The invoice price per 100 is 102.0260, so the short side receives 102,026.00. The basis is 0.5240. The bond with the lowest basis among the deliverables is the cheapest to deliver.
