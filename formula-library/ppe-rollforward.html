PP&E Roll-Forward

Beginner

Also known as: fixed asset schedule, capex from balance sheets, supporting schedule

Closing PP&E, or the capex implied by two balance sheets. Use it to build a forecast, with capex and depreciation as drivers, or to work out capex from two balance sheets when the cash flow statement is not available. A roll-forward ties each balance to the flows that changed it, and is the base of every supporting schedule in a model.

The formula

PPEend=PPEbegin+CapEx−Depreciation−DisposalsPPE_{end}=PPE_{begin}+\text{CapEx}-\text{Depreciation}-\text{Disposals}

Ending PP&E = Opening PP&E + Capex - Depreciation - Net book value of disposals; so Capex = Ending - Opening + Depreciation + Disposals

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
PPENet property, plant and equipment on the balance sheetmoney
CapExCapital expenditure for the yearmoney
DepreciationDepreciation charge for the yearmoney
DisposalsNet book value of assets sold or scrappedmoney

Worked example

Opening PP&E is 1,000. The company spent 110 on capex, charged 90 of depreciation and disposed of assets with a net book value of 10. Separately, another company shows opening PP&E of 800 and closing PP&E of 860, depreciation of 70 and no disposals.

  1. Closing PP&E = opening + capex - depreciation - disposals1000+110-90-10= 1,010.00
  2. Implied capex = closing - opening + depreciation860-800+70= 130.00

Closing PP&E is 1,010.00. For the second company, capex can be worked out as 130.00: closing minus opening plus depreciation.
