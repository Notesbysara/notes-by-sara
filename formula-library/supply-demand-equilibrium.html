Linear Supply and Demand Equilibrium

Beginner

Also known as: supply, demand and prices, market clearing price, price ceiling shortage, demand shift

The market price, and the effect of a shift or a ceiling. Use it to see how a price is set where buyers and sellers agree. A rise in demand (a larger a) raises both the price and the quantity. A price ceiling below the equilibrium price creates a shortage, because buyers want more than sellers will supply. Real curves are not straight lines, so treat this as an illustration of direction and rough size.

The formula

Qd=a−bP,  Qs=c+dP  ⇒  P∗=a−cb+d,  Q∗=a−bP∗Q_{d}=a-bP,\; Q_{s}=c+dP\;\Rightarrow\; P^{*}=\frac{a-c}{b+d},\; Q^{*}=a-bP^{*}

Demand: Qd = a - b x P. Supply: Qs = c + d x P. Equilibrium price P* = (a - c) / (b + d); equilibrium quantity Q* = a - b x P*

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
aQuantity demanded when the price is zero (the demand intercept)units
bHow much quantity demanded falls for each 1 rise in priceunits per price
cQuantity supplied when the price is zero (can be negative)units
dHow much quantity supplied rises for each 1 rise in priceunits per price

Worked example

Demand is Qd = 100 - 2P and supply is Qs = 10 + 3P. Find the equilibrium. Then demand rises so the intercept becomes 120, and a ceiling of 15 is set on the original market.

  1. Equilibrium price = (a - c) / (b + d)(100-10) / (2+3)= 18.00
  2. Equilibrium quantity = a - b x price100-2 x 18= 64.00
  3. Price after demand shifts to a2(120-10) / (2+3)= 22.00
  4. Quantity after the shift10+3 x 22= 76.00
  5. Change in price22 / 18-1= 22.2%
  6. Quantity demanded at the ceiling100-2 x 15= 70.00
  7. Quantity supplied at the ceiling10+3 x 15= 55.00
  8. Shortage at the ceilingmax(70-55, 0)= 15.00

The equilibrium price is 18.00 and the quantity is 64.00. After the demand rise the price is 22.00 and the quantity 76.00, a price rise of 22.2%. A ceiling of 15 in the original market makes buyers want 70.00 but sellers offer only 55.00, a shortage of 15.00.
