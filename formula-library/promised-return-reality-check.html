Promised Return Reality Check

BeginnerPersonal FinanceAlso known as: avoiding scams and fraud, Ponzi scheme warning, too good to be true, guaranteed returns
← Project Operating Cash FlowProperty Insurance Coinsurance Penalty →

Finds: what a promised return implies per year and versus the market.

When to use it: Use it to turn a promise into the annual return it implies and compare that with what markets deliver. A promise of steady, high, guaranteed returns with little risk is a classic warning sign, as no legitimate investment offers all three together. This does not prove an offer is a scam, but if the numbers are far above market returns, check the seller's registration with your financial regulator and be wary of pressure to act fast or to bring in friends.

The formula

EAR\displaystyle EAR=(1+m)12−1\displaystyle =(1+m)^{12}-1Vn\displaystyle V_{n}=P(1+m)12n\displaystyle =P(1+m)^{12n}tdouble\displaystyle t_{double}=ln⁡2ln⁡(1+m)\displaystyle =\frac{\ln 2}{\ln(1+m)}

In words: Equivalent annual rate = (1 + monthly return)^12 − 1; Value after n years = Principal x (1 + monthly return)^(12 x n); Months to double = ln 2 / ln(1 + monthly return)

Worked example

An offer promises 8% a month on 10,000. Long-run stock market returns are around 8% a year.

Equivalent annual rate(1 + 0.08)^12 − 1151.8%
Value after n years at the promised rate10,000 × (1 + 0.08)^(12 × 5)1,012,570.64
Value after n years at the market return10,000 × (1 + 0.08)^514,693.28
Promised value / market value1,012,571 ÷ 14,693.368.9
Months for the money to doubleln(2) ÷ ln(1 + 0.08)9
The answer

A promise of 8% a month equals 151.8% a year. After 5 years 10,000 would become 1,012,570.64, against 14,693.28 at the market return. That is 68.9 times as much, and your money would double every 9.0 months. Returns like that cannot be paid out of real profits for long.
