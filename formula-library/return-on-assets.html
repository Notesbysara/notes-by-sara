Return on Assets (ROA)

Beginner

Also known as: ROA, asset profitability, unlevered ROA

Profit earned per unit of assets. Use it to see how well a company turns its assets into profit. Net income is after interest, so a heavily indebted firm looks worse. The unlevered version adds back after-tax interest to compare firms with different debt levels.

The formula

ROA=NITA‾,ROAunlevered=NI+Interest(1−t)TA‾ROA=\frac{NI}{\overline{TA}},\quad ROA_{unlevered}=\frac{NI+\text{Interest}(1-t)}{\overline{TA}}

ROA = Net income / Average total assets; Unlevered ROA = (Net income + Interest x (1 - tax rate)) / Average total assets

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
NINet incomemoney
Average TAAverage of the opening and closing total assetsmoney
tTax ratepercent
InterestInterest expensemoney

Worked example

A company earned 90 after paying interest of 40. The tax rate is 25%. Total assets were 1,100 at the start of the year and 1,300 at the end.

  1. Average assets(1100+1300) / 2= 1,200.00
  2. ROA = net income / average assets90 / 1200= 7.50%
  3. Unlevered ROA = (NI + interest x (1 - t)) / average assets(90+40 x (1-0.25)) / 1200= 10.00%

Average assets are 1,200.00. ROA is 7.50%. Adding back after-tax interest, the unlevered ROA is 10.00%, which shows what the assets earn before financing costs.
