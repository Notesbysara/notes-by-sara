Forward Rate from Spot Rates

Intermediate

Also known as: implied forward rate, forward interest rate, no arbitrage forward rate

The rate for a future period implied by today's curve. Use it to find the interest rate implied for a future period. Locking in the long rate gives the same result as locking in the short rate and then the forward rate, so any other forward rate would let someone make a risk-free profit.

The formula

(1+s2)t2=(1+s1)t1 (1+f)t2−t1(1+s_{2})^{t_{2}}=(1+s_{1})^{t_{1}}\,(1+f)^{t_{2}-t_{1}}

Forward rate = [(1 + spot rate to t2)^t2 / (1 + spot rate to t1)^t1]^(1 / (t2 - t1)) - 1

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
s_1, s_2Spot (zero-coupon) rates to times t1 and t2per year
t_1, t_2Times in years, with t2 later than t1years
fForward rate, the rate that applies between t1 and t2, locked in todayper year

Worked example

The 1-year spot rate is 3.0% and the 3-year spot rate is 4.2%. What is the implied 2-year rate starting in one year?

  1. Growth to t2 = (1 + s2)^t2(1+0.042)^3= 1.13137
  2. Growth to t1 = (1 + s1)^t1(1+0.03)^1= 1.03000
  3. Forward rate = (g2 / g1)^(1 / (t2 - t1)) - 1(1.13137 / 1.03)^(1 / (3-1))-1= 4.805%
  4. Spot rate to t10.03= 3.00%
  5. Spot rate to t20.042= 4.20%

The implied forward rate for the two years starting in one year is 4.805%. Investing for 3 years at 4.20% equals investing 1 year at 3.00% and then 2 years at 4.805%.
