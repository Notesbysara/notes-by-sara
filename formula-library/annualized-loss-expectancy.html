Annualized Loss Expectancy (Cyber Risk)

Beginner

Also known as: ALE, SLE, ARO, cyber risk quantification, security investment return

The yearly expected cost of a cyber risk and whether a control pays. Use it to put a money value on a cyber risk and decide whether a control is worth buying. A control is worth it if the drop in expected yearly loss exceeds its yearly cost.

The formula

ALE=SLE×ARO,SLE=AV×EFALE=SLE\times ARO,\quad SLE=AV\times EF

Annualized loss expectancy = Single loss expectancy x Annual rate of occurrence; Single loss expectancy = Asset value x Exposure factor

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
AVValue of the asset at riskmoney
EFShare of the asset lost in one incidentpercent
SLESingle loss expectancy, the cost of one incidentmoney
AROAnnual rate of occurrence, expected incidents per yearnumber

Worked example

A customer database is worth 5,000,000. A breach would expose 30% of its value, and a breach is expected once every 5 years. A control costing 120,000 a year cuts the rate to once every 20 years.

  1. SLE = asset value x exposure factor5,000,000 x 0.3= 1,500,000.00
  2. ALE now = SLE x ARO1,500,000 x 0.2= 300,000.00
  3. ALE with the control1,500,000 x 0.05= 75,000.00
  4. Reduction in expected loss300000-75000= 225,000.00
  5. Net benefit = saving - cost225000-120000= 105,000.00

One incident costs 1,500,000.00, so the expected yearly loss is 300,000.00. With the control it falls to 75,000.00, a saving of 225,000.00 against a cost of 120,000, so the control gives a net benefit of 105,000.00.
