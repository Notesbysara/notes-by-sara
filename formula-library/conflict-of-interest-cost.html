Conflicted Advice: Long-Run Cost of a Hidden Commission

Beginner

Also known as: ethics, fiduciary standards and conflicts of interest, fiduciary vs suitability, commission cost, best interest standard

What a conflict of interest costs over time. Use it to see why fiduciary duty (acting in the client's best interest) matters. A suitability standard allows an adviser to sell a product that is suitable but pays them more. A one-percentage-point difference in yearly cost looks small but compounds into a large share of the final amount over many years.

The formula

C=V0[(1+r−f)n−(1+r−f−c)n]C=V_{0}\left[(1+r-f)^{n}-(1+r-f-c)^{n}\right]

Value with a fiduciary adviser who charges f = V0 x (1 + r - f)^n; Value with a product that also pays the adviser a yearly commission c = V0 x (1 + r - f - c)^n; Cost of the conflict = difference between the two; Share of ending value lost = 1 - (1 + r - f - c)^n / (1 + r - f)^n

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
V_0Amount investedmoney
rReturn before costsper year
fCost of the cleaner productper year
cExtra yearly commission or fee paid to the adviserper year
nYearsyears

Worked example

A client invests 100,000 for 20 years at 7% before costs. A low-cost fund charges 0.25%. A product recommended by a commission-paid adviser charges an extra 1.00% a year.

  1. Ending value, low-cost product100000 x (1+0.07-0.0025)^20= 369,281.60
  2. Ending value, commission product100000 x (1+0.07-0.0025-0.01)^20= 305,919.75
  3. Cost of the conflict369282-305920= 63,361.85
  4. Share of ending value lost63361.9 / 369282= 17.2%
  5. Cost as a multiple of the amount invested63361.9 / 100000= 0.63

The low-cost fund grows to 369,281.60 and the commission product to 305,919.75, so the conflict costs the client 63,361.85, or 17.2% of what they would have had. That is 0.63 times the amount they invested in the first place that they gave up.
