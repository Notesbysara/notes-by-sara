Reinsurance: Quota Share and Excess of Loss

Intermediate

Also known as: reinsurance, ceded loss, retention, layer

What the insurer keeps after reinsurance. Use it to see how reinsurance changes the loss an insurer keeps. A quota share reduces every loss in proportion and gives capital relief. Excess of loss protects against large single losses and leaves small ones with the insurer, and a loss beyond retention plus limit comes back to the insurer. Real programs add reinstatement premiums and exclusions.

The formula

CededQS=q L,RecoveryXL=min⁡ ⁣(max⁡(L−R,0), Limit)\text{Ceded}_{QS}=q\,L,\quad \text{Recovery}_{XL}=\min\!\left(\max(L-R,0),\,\text{Limit}\right)

Quota share: the reinsurer takes a fixed share q of every loss and premium; Excess of loss: the reinsurer pays the part of a loss above the retention R, up to the limit; Net retained loss = gross loss - recovery

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
LGross loss from one eventmoney
qQuota share cededpercent
RRetention: the loss the insurer keeps before the cover paysmoney
LimitMost the excess-of-loss cover paysmoney

Worked example

A 8.0 million loss hits an insurer. It has a 30% quota share, and separately an excess-of-loss cover paying 5.0 million above a 2.0 million retention.

  1. Quota share recovery = q x L0.3 x 8= 2.40
  2. Net retained, quota share only8-2.4= 5.60
  3. Excess-of-loss recovery = min(max(L - R, 0), limit)min(max(8-2, 0), 5)= 5.00
  4. Net retained, excess of loss only8-5= 3.00
  5. Loss above the layer (retention + limit)max(8-2-5, 0)= 1.00
  6. Net retained with XL first, then quota share on what remains(8-5) x (1-0.3)= 2.10

Under the quota share the reinsurer pays 2.40 and the insurer keeps 5.60. Under the excess-of-loss cover the reinsurer pays 5.00 and the insurer keeps 3.00. The XL leaves 1.00 above the layer with the insurer. If both apply, with the quota share on the excess cover's retained loss, the insurer keeps 2.10.
