Letter of Credit and Discounting: All-In Cost

Beginner

Also known as: trade finance, letter of credit fees, banker's acceptance, receivables discounting

What early payment under a letter of credit really costs. Use it to see what a seller pays to be paid early on an export under a letter of credit. The bank pays the seller now, less a discount, and collects the buyer's bank payment later. A bank-quoted discount rate understates the true cost, since it applies to face value and not to the amount actually advanced, and fees add to it. Compare all-in rates, not quoted rates.

The formula

Discount=F d t360,All-in rate=CostsF−Costs×365t\text{Discount}=F\,d\,\frac{t}{360},\quad \text{All-in rate}=\frac{\text{Costs}}{F-\text{Costs}}\times\frac{365}{t}

Discount = face value x discount rate x days / 360; Total cost = discount + issuance fee (% of face) + confirmation fee (% a year x days / 360 x face); Net proceeds = face - total cost; All-in annualized cost = total cost / net proceeds x 365 / days

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
FFace value of the letter of credit or acceptancemoney
dBank discount rate (quoted on face value, 360-day year)per year
tDays until payment (usance period)days
feesIssuance fee and confirmation feepercent

Worked example

A seller discounts a 90-day, 500,000 letter of credit at a 6% bank discount rate, with a 0.75% issuance fee and a confirmation fee of 0.30% a year.

  1. Discount = F x d x t / 360500000 x 0.06 x 90 / 360= 7,500.00
  2. Issuance and confirmation fees500000 x 0.0075+500000 x 0.003 x 90 / 360= 4,125.00
  3. Total cost7500+4125= 11,625.00
  4. Net proceeds500000-11625= 488,375.00
  5. All-in annualized cost = cost / net x 365 / t11625 / 488375 x 365 / 90= 9.65%

The discount is 7,500.00. Adding the issuance fee and confirmation fee brings total costs to 11,625.00, so the seller receives 488,375.00. The all-in cost is 9.65% annualized, against the quoted 6%.
