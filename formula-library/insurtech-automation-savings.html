Insurtech: Underwriting Result after Automation

Intermediate

Also known as: insurtech, claims automation, loss ratio, expense ratio, straight-through processing

How much technology changes an insurer's underwriting profit. Use it to size what technology can do to an insurer's profit. Better pricing and fraud detection act on the loss ratio; automated claims and onboarding act on the expense ratio. A combined ratio below 100% is an underwriting profit. The gains are estimates from pilots and may not carry across the whole book.

The formula

CR=LR+ER,ΔCR=− aLLR−aEER,UW=P(1−CR)CR=LR+ER,\quad \Delta CR=-\,a_{L}LR-a_{E}ER,\quad UW=P(1-CR)

Combined ratio = loss ratio + expense ratio; Underwriting profit = premiums x (1 - combined ratio); New loss ratio = old x (1 - claims improvement from better pricing and fraud detection); New expense ratio = old x (1 - expense saving from automation)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
LRClaims paid divided by premiumspercent
EROperating expenses divided by premiumspercent
a_L, a_EShare by which technology cuts claims cost and expensespercent

Worked example

An insurer collects 800m of premiums, pays 620m of claims and spends 210m on expenses. Technology is expected to cut claims by 4% and expenses by 10%.

  1. Loss ratio now620,000,000 / 800,000,000= 77.5%
  2. Expense ratio now210,000,000 / 800,000,000= 26.2%
  3. Combined ratio now0.775+0.2625= 103.8%
  4. Underwriting result now800,000,000 x (1-1.0375)= -30,000,000.00
  5. Loss ratio after0.775 x (1-0.04)= 74.4%
  6. Expense ratio after0.2625 x (1-0.1)= 23.6%
  7. Combined ratio after0.744+0.23625= 98.0%
  8. Underwriting result after800,000,000 x (1-0.98025)= 15,800,000.00
  9. Gain15,800,000--30,000,000= 45,800,000.00

The combined ratio is 103.8% and the underwriting result is -30,000,000.00. After the changes the loss ratio is 74.4%, the expense ratio 23.6%, and the combined ratio 98.0%, giving an underwriting result of 15,800,000.00, a gain of 45,800,000.00.
