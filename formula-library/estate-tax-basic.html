Estate Tax Estimate

Beginner

Also known as: estate planning basics, inheritance tax, exemption threshold, taxable estate

A rough estate tax and what is left. Use it for a rough idea of whether an estate might be taxed. Many estates fall below the threshold and owe nothing. Rules vary a great deal between countries and states, and some places tax the heirs (inheritance tax) and not the estate, so the exemption and rate here are illustrative inputs that you should replace. Planning tools such as wills, trusts and gifts should be set up with a qualified professional.

The formula

Tax=t×max⁡ ⁣(E−D−C−X, 0)\text{Tax}=t\times\max\!\left(E-D-C-X,\,0\right)

Taxable estate = gross estate - debts and expenses - charitable gifts; Tax = tax rate x the part of the taxable estate above the exemption threshold

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
EGross estate: everything owned at death, including life insurance owned by youmoney
DDebts and costs of administering the estatemoney
CGifts to charity (usually deductible)money
XExemption threshold below which no tax is duemoney
tEstate tax rate on the excesspercent

Worked example

A gross estate is 4,500,000 with 200,000 of debts and costs and 100,000 left to charity. The exemption is 3,000,000 and the rate on the excess is 40%.

  1. Taxable estate = gross - debts - charitymax(4,500,000-200000-100000, 0)= 4,200,000.00
  2. Amount above the exemptionmax(4,200,000-3,000,000, 0)= 1,200,000.00
  3. Tax = rate x excess0.4 x 1,200,000= 480,000.00
  4. Tax as a share of the gross estate480000 / 4,500,000= 10.67%
  5. Left after debts and tax4,500,000-200000-480000= 3,820,000.00

The taxable estate is 4,200,000.00, of which 1,200,000.00 is above the exemption. The tax is 480,000.00, an effective rate of 10.67% on the gross estate, leaving 3,820,000.00 for heirs and charity.
