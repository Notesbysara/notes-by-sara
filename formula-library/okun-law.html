Okun's Law

Beginner

Also known as: unemployment and growth, output loss from unemployment, growth needed to keep unemployment steady

A rough change in unemployment from growth. Use it as a rough rule linking growth and unemployment. Growth below trend raises unemployment and growth above trend lowers it. The coefficient and trend growth vary by country and period, so treat the output as a guide. It is a statistical regularity, not a law.

The formula

Δu=−β (g−g∗)\Delta u=-\beta\,(g-g^{*})

Change in the unemployment rate = - beta x (real GDP growth - trend growth), with beta about 0.5 in the United States

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
Delta uChange in the unemployment rate, in percentage pointspoints
betaOkun coefficient: points of unemployment change per point of growth gap (about 0.5)number
gReal GDP growthpercent
g*Trend growth: the pace that keeps unemployment steadypercent

Worked example

Unemployment is 4.2%. Real GDP shrinks 1.0% in a year against trend growth of 2.0% and an Okun coefficient of 0.5.

  1. Growth gap = g - trend-0.01-0.02= -3.00%
  2. Change in unemployment = -beta x gap-0.5 x -0.03= 1.50%
  3. New unemployment rate0.042+0.015= 5.70%
  4. Output gap per 1 point of unemployment = 1 / beta1 / 0.5= 2.0

The growth gap is -3.00%, so unemployment is predicted to change by 1.50% points, to 5.70%. Trend growth of 2.0% is what it takes to hold unemployment steady, and each point of unemployment is associated with about 2.0 of lost output.
