Skewness

IntermediateFinance FoundationsAlso known as: asymmetry of returns, third moment, fat tail direction
← Simulated Price Step (Monte Carlo)SOFR Futures Price, Rate and Tick Value →

Finds: whether returns are lopsided.

When to use it: Use it to see whether big moves tend to be gains or losses. Negative skewness means occasional large losses, which a standard deviation alone would hide.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Skew\displaystyle \text{Skew}=1n∑i=1n(Ri−Rˉσ)3\displaystyle =\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{R_{i}-\bar{R}}{\sigma}\right)^{3}

In words: Skewness = average of ((Ri − mean) / standard deviation)^3

What each symbol means

SkewSkewness, a measure of how lopsided the returns arenumber
R_iReturn in period ipercent
R-barMean returnpercent
sigmaStandard deviation of returns, using n (the population version)percent
nNumber of returnsnumber

Worked example

A fund returned 10%, −5%, 15%, 2%, −12% and 8% over six periods. What is the skewness of its returns?

Skewness−0.362
The answer

The skewness is −0.362. It is negative, so the bigger moves lean toward losses, though only mildly.
