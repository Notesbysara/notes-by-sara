IPO Underwriting Spread and Greenshoe

BeginnerBanking & Financial InstitutionsAlso known as: investment banking, underwriting fees, gross spread, overallotment option, money left on the table
← IPO Underpricing (Initial Return)IQR Outlier Fences →

Finds: what an offering raises and costs.

When to use it: Use it to see what an offering costs the issuer and what the banks earn. The spread pays for advisory work, risk-taking by the underwriters and selling to investors. The greenshoe lets the banks sell extra shares and cover them from the issuer if the price rises, or from the market if it falls, which supports the price. First-day pops show underpricing, a cost to the issuer.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Gross spread\displaystyle \text{Gross spread}=s P N\displaystyle =s\,P\,NNet proceeds\displaystyle \text{Net proceeds}=PN(1−s)−Costs\displaystyle =PN(1-s)-\text{Costs}Left on table\displaystyle \text{Left on table}=(P1−P)N\displaystyle =(P_{1}-P)N

In words: Gross spread = spread % x offer price x shares; Net proceeds to the issuer = price x shares x (1 − spread %) − other costs; Greenshoe adds up to 15% more shares if demand is strong; Money left on the table = (first-day close − offer price) x shares sold

What each symbol means

POffer pricemoney
NShares sold in the base dealmillions
sGross spread: the underwriters' fee as a share of proceedspercent
P_1Price at the end of the first day of tradingmoney

Worked example

An IPO sells 50 million shares at 20 with a 5% spread, 3 million of other costs and a greenshoe of 15%. The shares close at 23.60 on the first day.

Gross proceeds20 × 501,000
Underwriting spread0.05 × 1,00050
Net proceeds to the issuer1,000 − 50 − 3947
Extra net proceeds if the greenshoe is fully used0.15 × 50 × 20 × (1 − 0.05)142.50
First-day return23.6 ÷ 20 − 118%
Money left on the table(23.6 − 20) × 50180
The answer

The base deal raises 1,000 and the underwriters earn 50, leaving the issuer 947 after other costs. If the greenshoe is fully used, 7.5 million more shares add 142.50 of net proceeds. The first-day gain of 18.0% left 180 on the table.
