Greenwashing Check: Claimed vs Aligned Share

Beginner

Also known as: greenwashing, taxonomy alignment, green revenue share, claim gap, green claims

How far a green claim runs ahead of the evidence. Use it to compare what a firm or a fund says with what can be verified. A large gap between the claimed green share and the share that meets a recognized taxonomy is a warning of greenwashing. Regulators in several places require evidence for green claims. A small gap does not prove the claims are right, since the taxonomy has limits too.

The formula

A=∑Aligned revenueTotal revenue,Gap=C−AA=\frac{\sum \text{Aligned revenue}}{\text{Total revenue}},\quad \text{Gap}=C-A

Aligned share = revenue from activities that meet an official green taxonomy criteria / total revenue; Claim gap = share claimed in marketing - aligned share; Overstatement ratio = claimed share / aligned share; Fund version: share of holdings that meet the fund's stated sustainability criteria vs share claimed

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
AShare of revenue (or holdings) that meets the taxonomy criteriapercent
CShare the company or fund claims is greenpercent

Worked example

A company with 2,400 million revenue claims that 60% of its business is green. Activities that meet the taxonomy bring in 300 million and 180 million.

  1. Aligned share= 20.0%
  2. Claim gap (percentage points)(0.6-0.2) x 100= 40.0
  3. Claim / aligned share0.6 / 0.2= 3.00
  4. Flag (1 = gap above 10 points)1 if (0.6-0.2)>0.10 else 0= 1

The aligned share is 20.0%. The claimed share is 60%, so the gap is 40.0 percentage points, and the claim is 3.00 times the aligned share. The flag is 1 (1 = claim is more than 10 percentage points above the aligned share).
