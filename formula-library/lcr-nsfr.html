Basel Liquidity Ratios: LCR and NSFR

Intermediate

Also known as: liquidity coverage ratio, net stable funding ratio, HQLA, available stable funding

Whether a bank meets the Basel liquidity rules. Use it to check a bank against the two main Basel III liquidity rules. The LCR is short-term: can liquid assets cover 30 days of stress outflows? The NSFR is long-term: is the balance sheet funded with stable sources over one year? Regulators require both at 100% or above. The Level 2 cap (40% of HQLA) is ignored here for simplicity.

The formula

LCR=HQLAOutflows−min⁡(Inflows,0.75 Outflows)≥100%,NSFR=ASFRSF≥100%LCR=\frac{HQLA}{\text{Outflows}-\min(\text{Inflows},0.75\,\text{Outflows})}\ge100\%,\quad NSFR=\frac{ASF}{RSF}\ge100\%

LCR = High-quality liquid assets / (30-day outflows - min(30-day inflows, 75% of outflows)); NSFR = Available stable funding / Required stable funding; both must be at least 100%

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
HQLAStock of liquid assets after haircuts: Level 1 at 100%, Level 2A at 85%, Level 2B at 50%money
ASFAvailable stable funding: each funding source times its stability factormoney
RSFRequired stable funding: each asset times the factor for how hard it is to sellmoney

Worked example

A bank holds Level 1 assets of 800, Level 2A of 200 and Level 2B of 50. Expected 30-day outflows are 1,100 and inflows 500. Funding sources of 100, 400, 500 and 300 carry stability factors of 100%, 95%, 90% and 50%. Assets of 200, 600 and 500 need stable funding at 5%, 65% and 85%.

  1. HQLA = Level 1 + 85% of Level 2A + 50% of Level 2B800+0.85 x 200+0.5 x 50= 995.0
  2. Net outflows = outflows - min(inflows, 75% of outflows)1100-min(500, 0.75 x 1100)= 600.0
  3. LCR = HQLA / net outflows995 / 600= 165.8%
  4. ASF = sum of funding x factor= 1,080.0
  5. RSF = sum of assets x factor= 825.0
  6. NSFR = ASF / RSF1080 / 825= 130.9%

HQLA after haircuts is 995.0 and net outflows are 600.0, so the LCR is 165.8%. Available stable funding is 1,080.0 against required funding of 825.0, so the NSFR is 130.9%. Both are above 100%.
