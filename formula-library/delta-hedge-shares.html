Delta Hedge and Hedging Error

IntermediateDerivativesAlso known as: delta neutral, dynamic hedging, gamma risk, hedge a short call
← Degree of Operating Leverage (DOL)Deposit Insurance Coverage and Premium →

Finds: shares needed to hedge options and the leftover gamma loss.

When to use it: Use it to make a short option position insensitive to small moves in the stock by holding delta shares per option. The hedge breaks down for large moves because delta changes (gamma), so it is rebalanced as the stock moves. The leftover loss from gamma is the hedging error.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Shares to hold\displaystyle \text{Shares to hold}=Δ×N×M\displaystyle =\Delta\times N\times MΔC≈Δ ΔS+12Γ(ΔS)2\displaystyle \Delta C\approx\Delta\,\Delta S+\tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma(\Delta S)^{2}

In words: Shares held to hedge short options = Delta x Number of options x Shares per option; Option price change = Delta x Stock change + 0.5 x Gamma x Stock change^2

What each symbol means

Worked example

You sell 20 call contracts (100 shares each) with a delta of 0.6368 and gamma of 0.0188. How many shares do you buy to hedge? If the stock then rises 5, what is the net result?

Shares to hold0.6368 × 20 × 1001,274
Option price change0.6368 × 5 + 0.5 × 0.0188 × 5^23.4190
Loss on the short options3.419 × 20 × 1006,838
Gain on the shares1,273.6 × 56,368
Net result6,368 − 6,838−470
The answer

You buy 1,274 shares. If the stock rises 5, each option gains about 3.4190 in price, so the short options lose 6,838, while the shares gain 6,368. The net result is −470, a small loss from gamma. The bigger the move, the larger the loss.
