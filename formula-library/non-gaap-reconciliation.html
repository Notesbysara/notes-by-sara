Non-GAAP Earnings Reconciliation

Intermediate

Also known as: adjusted earnings, adjusted EPS, pro forma earnings, add backs

How far adjusted earnings are above reported earnings. Use it to rebuild a company's adjusted figure from its reported one. Companies publish adjusted metrics that exclude items they call one-off. Check the size of the gap and whether the items recur year after year. A large, persistent gap means the adjusted number is flattering.

The formula

NIadj=NI+∑Ai (1−t),EPSadj=NIadjSharesNI_{adj}=NI+\sum A_{i}\,(1-t),\quad EPS_{adj}=\frac{NI_{adj}}{\text{Shares}}

Adjusted net income = GAAP net income + sum of the pre-tax add-backs x (1 - tax rate); Adjusted EPS = Adjusted net income / Shares

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
NIGAAP (reported) net incomemoney
A_iPre-tax items the company adds back, such as restructuring, amortization of acquired intangibles and stock compensationmoney
tTax rate applied to the add-backspercent

Worked example

GAAP net income is 90 and there are 20 million shares. Add-backs before tax are restructuring 20, amortization of acquired intangibles 15 and stock compensation 25. The tax rate is 25%.

  1. Total add-backs before tax= 60.00
  2. After-tax add-backs = total x (1 - t)60 x (1-0.25)= 45.00
  3. Adjusted net income90+45= 135.00
  4. Adjusted EPS135 / 20= 6.75
  5. Reported EPS90 / 20= 4.50
  6. Adjusted vs reported EPS6.75 / 4.5-1= 50.0%

The add-backs total 60.00 before tax, or 45.00 after tax. Adjusted net income is 135.00, and adjusted EPS is 6.75 against 4.50 reported. The adjusted figure is 50.0% higher, so judge whether those costs really are one-off.
