FX Forward Hedge and Covered Interest Parity

IntermediateRisk ManagementAlso known as: forward exchange rate, currency hedge, covered interest parity, forward points, hedging receivables
← Futures Price: Cost of CarryGARCH(1,1) Variance Forecast →

Finds: A locked-in exchange rate and what hedging is worth.

When to use it: Use it to fix the home-currency value of a future foreign payment. The forward rate follows from interest rate parity, so it differs from spot by the interest difference, not by a forecast of where the currency is going. Hedging removes the risk, and the opportunity of a favorable move.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

F\displaystyle F=S 1+rd t1+rf t\displaystyle =S\,\frac{1+r_{d}\,t}{1+r_{f}\,t}

In words: Forward rate = Spot rate x (1 + home rate x time) / (1 + foreign rate x time), with rates quoted per year and the rate quoted as home currency per unit of foreign currency

What each symbol means

SSpot rate in home currency per unit of foreign currencynumber
FForward rate for delivery at time tnumber
r_d, r_fInterest rates in the home and foreign currenciesper year
tTime to settlement in yearsyears

Worked example

A US company will receive 2,000,000 euros in 6 months. The spot rate is 1.10 dollars per euro, the dollar rate is 4.5% and the euro rate is 2.5%. If it does not hedge, it expects to convert at 1.04.

Forward1.1 × (1 + 0.045 × 0.5) ÷ (1 + 0.025 × 0.5)1.11086
Hedged receipt2,000,000 × 1.110862,221,728.40
Unhedged receipt at the possible spot rate2,000,000 × 1.042,080,000
Hedged minus unhedged2,221,728 − 2,080,000141,728.40
The answer

The 6-month forward rate is 1.11086, higher than spot because dollar interest rates are higher. Locking it in gives 2,221,728.40. If the spot rate ends at 1.04 the unhedged receipt would be 2,080,000, which is 141,728.40 less.
