Money Market Fund NAV and Breaking the Buck

IntermediateBanking & Financial InstitutionsAlso known as: shadow banking, stable NAV, run on a money fund, redemptions at par
← Modified Internal Rate of Return (MIRR)Money Multiplier and Deposit Expansion →

Finds: how redemptions at par shift losses onto remaining holders.

When to use it: Use it to see why money funds and other shadow-bank vehicles are run-prone. Investors who redeem early get 1.00 while the true value is below it, so those who stay absorb the loss, which gives everyone a reason to leave first. That first-mover advantage turned small losses into a run in 2008. Rules since then allow fees and gates, and some funds now float their NAV.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

NAV\displaystyle NAV=A(1−ℓ)S\displaystyle =\frac{A(1-\ell)}{S}NAVafter\displaystyle NAV_{after}=A(1−ℓ)−wS⋅1S(1−w)\displaystyle =\frac{A(1-\ell)-wS\cdot 1}{S(1-w)}

In words: Net asset value per share = value of assets after losses / shares outstanding; If investors redeem a share fraction w at the stable price of 1 while the true value is lower, the NAV left for remaining holders = (assets after losses − w x shares x 1) / (shares x (1 − w)). The fund breaks the buck if NAV falls below 0.995

What each symbol means

AFund assets at market value before the lossmoney
lLoss on assets, as a share of their valuepercent

wShare of the fund redeemed at 1.00percent

Worked example

A fund has assets of 1,000 and 1,000 shares priced at 1. Its assets lose 0.6%. Then 30% of shares are redeemed at 1.

NAV after the loss1,000 × (1 − 0.006) ÷ 1,0000.9940

NAV for remaining holders after redemptions at 1.00(1,000 × (1 − 0.006) − 0.3 × 1,000 × 1) ÷ (1,000 × (1 − 0.3))0.9914
Loss per share with no redemptions1 − 0.9940.60%

The answer

After the loss the true NAV is 0.9940, below 0.995, so the fund has broken the buck. If 30% redeem at 1, the NAV of the remaining shares drops to 0.9914, a loss of 0.86% for those who stayed, versus 0.60% if no one had left.
