Money Market Fund NAV and Breaking the Buck

Intermediate

Also known as: shadow banking, stable NAV, run on a money fund, redemptions at par

How redemptions at par shift losses onto remaining holders. Use it to see why money funds and other shadow-bank vehicles are run-prone. Investors who redeem early get 1.00 while the true value is below it, so those who stay absorb the loss, which gives everyone a reason to leave first. That first-mover advantage turned small losses into a run in 2008. Rules since then allow fees and gates, and some funds now float their NAV.

The formula

NAV=A(1−ℓ)S,NAVafter=A(1−ℓ)−wS⋅1S(1−w)NAV=\frac{A(1-\ell)}{S},\quad NAV_{after}=\frac{A(1-\ell)-wS\cdot 1}{S(1-w)}

Net asset value per share = value of assets after losses / shares outstanding; If investors redeem a share fraction w at the stable price of 1.00 while the true value is lower, the NAV left for remaining holders = (assets after losses - w x shares x 1.00) / (shares x (1 - w)). The fund breaks the buck if NAV falls below 0.995

What each symbol means

AFund assets at market value before the lossmoney

Worked example

A fund has assets of 1,000 and 1,000 shares priced at 1.00. Its assets lose 0.6%. Then 30% of shares are redeemed at 1.00.

  1. NAV after the loss = A(1 - l) / S1000 x (1-0.006) / 1000= 0.9940
  2. Broke the buck? (1 = yes, 0 = no)1 if 0.994<0.995 else 0= 1
  3. NAV for remaining holders after redemptions at 1.00(1000 x (1-0.006)-0.3 x 1000 x 1) / (1000 x (1-0.3))= 0.9914
  4. Loss per share with no redemptions1-0.994= 0.60%
  5. Loss per share for the holders who stay1-0.991429= 0.86%

After the loss the true NAV is 0.9940, below 0.995, so the fund has broken the buck. If 30% redeem at 1.00, the NAV of the remaining shares drops to 0.9914, a loss of 0.86% for those who stayed, versus 0.60% if no one had left.
