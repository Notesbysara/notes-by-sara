Backtest Summary: Return, Risk, Drawdown and Information Ratio

IntermediatePractical Skills & CapstoneAlso known as: construct and backtest a diversified portfolio, backtest report, CAGR volatility Sharpe drawdown, tracking error
← Backtest Performance MetricsBalance of Payments Identity →

Finds: A full performance report for a tested strategy.

When to use it: Use it to report a backtest in full rather than just the return. A good result must beat a simple benchmark after costs, in data the rule was not tuned on. Two years of monthly data, as here, is too short to say much; use many years and different market conditions, and keep the test free of look-ahead.

The formula

CAGR\displaystyle CAGR=WT12/n−1\displaystyle =W_{T}^{12/n}-1σ\displaystyle \sigma=s12\displaystyle =s\sqrt{12}Sharpe\displaystyle Sharpe=12(rˉ−rf)σ\displaystyle =\frac{12(\bar{r}-r_{f})}{\sigma}IR\displaystyle IR=12 (r−b)‾TE\displaystyle =\frac{12\,\overline{(r-b)}}{TE}

In words: Growth of 1 = product of (1 + monthly return); Annualized return (CAGR) = growth^(12 / months) − 1; Volatility = monthly standard deviation x square root of 12; Sharpe = 12 x (average monthly return − monthly risk-free rate) / volatility; Maximum drawdown = largest fall from a previous peak; Tracking error = standard deviation of monthly excess returns x square root of 12; Information ratio = annualized average excess return / tracking error

What each symbol means

r, bMonthly return of the portfolio and of the benchmarkpercent
r_fMonthly risk-free ratepercent
nNumber of months in the testcount

The answer

The portfolio grew 1.170 times, an annual return of 8.19% against 4.33% for the benchmark. Volatility is 5.67%, the Sharpe ratio 0.79, and the worst fall from a peak 2.4%. Tracking error is 0.38% and the information ratio 9.73.
