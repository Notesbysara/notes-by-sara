Futures Price: Cost of Carry

IntermediateDerivativesAlso known as: cost of carry, convenience yield, storage cost, fair value of a futures, basis
← Futures Daily Settlement and Margin AccountFX Forward Hedge and Covered Interest Parity →

Finds: the no-arbitrage futures price.

When to use it: Use it to find the no-arbitrage futures price. Holding the asset costs interest and storage, and pays an income or convenience yield. The futures price equals the spot price grown at the net cost of carry. If the market price differs, buying the cheap one and selling the expensive one locks in a risk-free profit.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

F0\displaystyle F_{0}=S0 e(r+u−y)T\displaystyle =S_{0}\,e^{(r+u-y)T}

In words: Futures price = Spot x e^((risk-free rate + storage cost − yield) x time)

What each symbol means

S_0Spot pricemoney
rRisk-free rate (continuously compounded)per year
uStorage and insurance cost as a share of the price, per yearper year
yIncome yield: dividend yield for shares, convenience yield for commoditiesper year
TTime to deliveryyears

Worked example

Gold trades at 2,000. The risk-free rate is 4%, storage and insurance cost 0.5% a year and there is no yield. What is the 9-month futures price, and what would it be for a share index at 4,000 with a 1.8% dividend yield and no storage cost?

Net cost of carry0.04 + 0.005 − 04.50%
Futures price2,000 × exp(0.045 × 0.75)2,068.65
Futures price minus spot2,068.65 − 2,00068.65
Index futures price4,000 × exp((0.04 − 0.018) × 0.75)4,066.55
Index futures price minus spot4,066.55 − 4,00066.55
The answer

The 9-month gold futures price is 2,068.65, a basis of 68.65 above spot. The index futures price is 4,066.55, which is 66.55 above spot, since the dividend yield offsets part of the interest cost.
