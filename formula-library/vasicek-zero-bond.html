Vasicek Model Zero-Coupon Bond Price

Intermediate

Also known as: interest rate models, Vasicek, short rate model, mean reverting rate

A zero-coupon bond price and yield from a short-rate model. Use it to price bonds when the short rate is mean reverting with normal (Gaussian) shocks. It gives a full yield curve from three parameters. Its known weakness is that rates can go negative. The yield is a weighted mix of today's short rate and the long-run mean.

The formula

P(0,T)=A e−Br0,  B=1−e−κTκ,  A=exp⁡ ⁣[(θ−σ22κ2)(B−T)−σ2B24κ]P(0,T)=A\,e^{-B r_{0}},\; B=\frac{1-e^{-\kappa T}}{\kappa},\; A=\exp\!\left[\left(\theta-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2\kappa^{2}}\right)(B-T)-\frac{\sigma^{2}B^{2}}{4\kappa}\right]

Bond price = A x e^(-B x r0), where B = (1 - e^(-kappa T)) / kappa and A = exp[(theta - sigma^2 / (2 kappa^2)) x (B - T) - sigma^2 x B^2 / (4 kappa)]

r_0Current short rateper year

Worked example

The short rate is 3%, the long-run mean is 5%, the speed is 0.8, the volatility is 1% and the bond matures in 5 years.

  1. B = (1 - e^(-kappa T)) / kappa(1-exp(-0.8 x 5)) / 0.8= 1.22711
  2. A = exp[(theta - sigma^2 / (2 kappa^2)) (B - T) - sigma^2 B^2 / (4 kappa)]exp((0.05-0.01^2 / (2 x 0.8^2)) x (1.22711-5)-0.01^2 x 1.22711^2 / (4 x 0.8))= 0.82829
  3. Bond price = A x e^(-B r0)0.828286 x exp(-1.22711 x 0.03)= 0.79835
  4. Yield = -ln(price) / T-ln(0.798348) / 5= 4.504%

B is 1.22711 and A is 0.82829, so the 5-year zero-coupon bond costs 0.79835 per 1 of face value. Its yield is 4.504%, between the 3% short rate and the 5% long-run mean.
