Justified Price-to-Book Ratio (Banks)

IntermediateValuationAlso known as: P/B, price to book, bank valuation, justified P/B
← Jensen's AlphaKelly Criterion →

Finds: price-to-book a bank deserves.

When to use it: Use it to value banks and other financial firms, whose book value is close to market value. A bank earning more than its cost of equity should trade above book value.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

PB\displaystyle \frac{P}{B}=ROE−gr−g\displaystyle =\frac{ROE-g}{r-g}

In words: Justified P/B = (ROE − g) / (r − g)

What each symbol means

P/BPrice-to-book ratio the fundamentals justifynumber
ROEReturn on equityper year
gSustainable growth rateper year
rRequired return on equityper year

Worked example

A bank has a return on equity of 14%, expected growth of 4% and a required return of 10%. What price-to-book ratio is justified, and what is the value if book value per share is 30?

P/B(0.14 − 0.04) ÷ (0.1 − 0.04)1.67
Value per share1.66667 × 3050
The answer

The justified P/B is 1.67, so the share is worth 50. The bank earns more than its cost of equity, so it is worth more than book value.
