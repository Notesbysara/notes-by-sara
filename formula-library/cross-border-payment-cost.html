Cross-Border Payment All-In Cost

BeginnerBanking & Financial InstitutionsAlso known as: correspondent banking and SWIFT, remittance cost, FX margin, intermediary bank fees
← Cross Rates and Triangular ArbitrageCrowdfunding: Net Proceeds after Fees →

Finds: what a cross-border payment costs in all.

When to use it: Use it to see what a payment really costs. A transfer over SWIFT may pass through one or more correspondent banks that each take a fee, and the exchange-rate margin is often the largest cost but is not shown as a fee. Compare the amount received with what you would get at the mid-market rate. Fees and rates differ by route and provider.

The formula

Received\displaystyle \text{Received}=(A−fs−fc) ebank−fr\displaystyle =(A-f_{s}-f_{c})\,e_{bank}-f_{r}Cost\displaystyle \text{Cost}=1−ReceivedA emid\displaystyle =1-\frac{\text{Received}}{A\,e_{mid}}

In words: Amount received = (amount sent − sender fee − correspondent fee) x bank exchange rate − receiving bank fee; All-in cost = 1 − amount received / (amount sent x mid-market exchange rate)

Worked example

You send 5,000 with a sender fee of 25, a correspondent fee of 15 and a receiving bank fee of 10 (in the destination currency). The mid-market rate is 0.92 and the bank applies 0.90.

Amount received(5,000 − 25 − 15) × 0.9 − 104,454
Ideal amount at the mid-market rate5,000 × 0.924,600
Total lost to fees and margin4,600 − 4,454146
All-in cost146 ÷ 4,6003.17%
Exchange-rate margin as a share of the amount1 − 0.9 ÷ 0.922.17%
The answer

The recipient receives 4,454, against 4,600 at the mid-market rate with no fees. The all-in cost is 3.17%, of which the exchange-rate margin accounts for 2.17% of the amount sent and the fees account for the rest.
