Cross-Border Payment All-In Cost

Beginner

Also known as: correspondent banking and SWIFT, remittance cost, FX margin, intermediary bank fees

What a cross-border payment costs in all. Use it to see what a payment really costs. A transfer over SWIFT may pass through one or more correspondent banks that each take a fee, and the exchange-rate margin is often the largest cost but is not shown as a fee. Compare the amount received with what you would get at the mid-market rate. Fees and rates differ by route and provider.

The formula

Received=(A−fs−fc) ebank−fr,Cost=1−ReceivedA emid\text{Received}=(A-f_{s}-f_{c})\,e_{bank}-f_{r},\quad \text{Cost}=1-\frac{\text{Received}}{A\,e_{mid}}

Amount received = (amount sent - sender fee - correspondent fee) x bank exchange rate - receiving bank fee; All-in cost = 1 - amount received / (amount sent x mid-market exchange rate)

Worked example

You send 5,000 with a sender fee of 25, a correspondent fee of 15 and a receiving bank fee of 10 (in the destination currency). The mid-market rate is 0.92 and the bank applies 0.90.

  1. Amount received(5000-25-15) x 0.9-10= 4,454.00
  2. Ideal amount at the mid-market rate5000 x 0.92= 4,600.00
  3. Total lost to fees and margin4600-4454= 146.00
  4. All-in cost146 / 4600= 3.17%
  5. Exchange-rate margin as a share of the amount1-0.9 / 0.92= 2.17%

The recipient receives 4,454.00, against 4,600.00 at the mid-market rate with no fees. The all-in cost is 3.17%, of which the exchange-rate margin accounts for 2.17% of the amount sent and the fees account for the rest.
