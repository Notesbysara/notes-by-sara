Bond Cash Flows: Par, Coupon, Maturity

BeginnerFixed IncomeAlso known as: coupon payment, face value, par value, bond basics
← Bollinger BandsBond Convexity →

Finds: the payments a bond makes.

When to use it: Use it to lay out what a bond will pay. The coupon rate is fixed on the face value and does not change with the market price. The bond repays the face value on the maturity date along with the last coupon.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Coupon\displaystyle \text{Coupon}=c×Fm\displaystyle =\frac{c\times F}{m}N\displaystyle N=n×m\displaystyle =n\times m

In words: Coupon payment = Coupon rate x Face value / Payments per year; Number of payments = Years x Payments per year

What each symbol means

FFace (par) value, repaid at maturitymoney
cAnnual coupon rateper year
mCoupon payments per year (2 for semi-annual)number
nYears to maturityyears
NTotal number of coupon paymentsnumber

Worked example

A bond has a face value of 1,000, pays a 6% coupon twice a year and matures in 5 years. What does it pay?

Coupon payment1,000 × 0.06 ÷ 230
Number of payments5 × 210
Total coupons30 × 10300
Total received300 + 1,0001,300
The answer

The bond pays 30 every period for 10 periods. Coupons add up to 300, and with the face value repaid at the end the holder receives 1,300 in all.
