Bond Cash Flows: Par, Coupon, Maturity

Beginner

Also known as: coupon payment, face value, par value, bond basics

The payments a bond makes. Use it to lay out what a bond will pay. The coupon rate is fixed on the face value and does not change with the market price. The bond repays the face value on the maturity date along with the last coupon.

The formula

Coupon=c×Fm,N=n×m\text{Coupon}=\frac{c\times F}{m},\quad N=n\times m

Coupon payment = Coupon rate x Face value / Payments per year; Number of payments = Years x Payments per year

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
FFace (par) value, repaid at maturitymoney
cAnnual coupon rateper year
mCoupon payments per year (2 for semi-annual)number
nYears to maturityyears
NTotal number of coupon paymentsnumber

Worked example

A bond has a face value of 1,000, pays a 6% coupon twice a year and matures in 5 years. What does it pay?

  1. Coupon payment = face x c / m1000 x 0.06 / 2= 30.00
  2. Number of payments = n x m5 x 2= 10
  3. Total coupons = payment x number30 x 10= 300.00
  4. Total received = coupons + face value300+1000= 1,300.00

The bond pays 30.00 every period for 10 periods. Coupons add up to 300.00, and with the face value repaid at the end the holder receives 1,300.00 in all.
