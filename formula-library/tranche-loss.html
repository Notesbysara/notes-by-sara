Tranche Loss Allocation (Attachment and Detachment)

IntermediateFixed IncomeAlso known as: tranching, waterfall losses, attachment point, detachment point, equity mezzanine senior tranche
← Traditional vs Roth Account After TaxTreasury Stock Method (Diluted Shares) →

Finds: which tranche absorbs how much of a pool loss.

When to use it: Use it to see who bears credit losses in a securitization. Losses hit the lowest (equity) tranche first, then the middle (mezzanine), and the senior tranche last. A tranche is like a slice of the pool, so it only loses when pool losses pass its attachment point.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Tranche loss\displaystyle \text{Tranche loss}=min⁡(max⁡(L−A,0), D−A)D−A\displaystyle =\frac{\min\left(\max(L-A,0),\,D-A\right)}{D-A}

In words: Share of the tranche lost = min( max(Pool loss − Attachment point, 0), Detachment point − Attachment point ) / (Detachment point − Attachment point)

What each symbol means

LLoss on the whole pool, as a share of the poolpercent
AAttachment point, the pool loss at which this tranche starts to lose moneypercent
DDetachment point, the pool loss at which the tranche is wiped outpercent

Worked example

A pool loses 8% of its value. Tranche A covers losses from 0% to 5%, tranche B from 5% to 15% and tranche C from 15% to 100%. The pool is 1,000 in size. How are the losses shared?

Equity tranche loss share (0% to 5%)min(max(0.08 − 0, 0), 0.05) ÷ 0.05100%
Mezzanine tranche loss share (5% to 15%)min(max(0.08 − 0.05, 0), 0.10) ÷ 0.1030%
Senior tranche loss share (15% to 100%)min(max(0.08 − 0.15, 0), 0.85) ÷ 0.850%
Equity tranche loss in money1 × 0.05 × 1,00050
Mezzanine tranche loss in money0.3 × 0.10 × 1,00030
Senior tranche loss in money0 × 0.85 × 1,0000
Total losses allocated50 + 30 + 080
The answer

The equity tranche (0% to 5%) loses 100.0% of its value, the mezzanine tranche (5% to 15%) loses 30.0%, and the senior tranche loses 0.0%. In money that is 50, 30 and 0, adding up to the pool loss of 80.
