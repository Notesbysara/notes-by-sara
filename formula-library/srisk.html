SRISK (Systemic Risk Contribution)

IntermediateRisk ManagementAlso known as: systemic risk, capital shortfall, LRMES, too big to fail
← Sovereign Wealth Fund Permanent IncomeStablecoin Reserve Backing and Issuer Income →

Finds: A firm's capital shortfall in a market crash.

When to use it: Use it to estimate how much capital a financial firm would be short if the whole market crashed. A firm with a large SRISK would need rescuing at the worst moment, when no one else has capital to give, which is what systemic risk means. The figure is the sum across firms of the expected shortfalls.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

SRISK\displaystyle SRISK=max⁡[k D−(1−k)(1−LRMES) W, 0]\displaystyle =\max\left[k\,D-(1-k)(1-LRMES)\,W,\ 0\right]

In words: SRISK = max[ Capital ratio x Debt − (1 − Capital ratio) x (1 − LRMES) x Market value of equity, 0 ]

What each symbol means

kPrudential capital ratio, such as 8%percent
DBook value of debtmoney
WMarket value of equitymoney
LRMESLong-run marginal expected shortfall: the equity loss expected if the whole market falls by more than 40% over six monthspercent

Worked example

A bank has debt of 900 and a market value of equity of 60. The prudential capital ratio is 8% and the equity falls by 40% in a market crash (LRMES = 40%).

Equity after the crash(1 − 0.4) × 6036
Capital required0.08 × 90072
SRISKmax(0.08 × 900 − (1 − 0.08) × 36, 0)38.88
The answer

In a crash the equity is expected to be worth 36. The capital required against the debt is 72, so the shortfall is 38.88. A positive value like this one is capital that markets, or the state, would have to supply.
