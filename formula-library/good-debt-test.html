Good Debt vs Bad Debt Test

BeginnerPersonal FinanceAlso known as: good debt bad debt, borrowing to invest, return on borrowed money, after-tax cost of debt
← Geometric Mean ReturnGoodwill from Purchase Price Allocation →

Finds: whether a loan is likely to pay for itself.

When to use it: Use it to compare what a loan costs with what it earns. Debt is closer to "good" when it buys something whose return is likely to exceed its cost and the downside is survivable, such as education that raises income. A card balance at 22% almost never passes. The expected return is an estimate, so a thin positive spread is not a safe margin.

The formula

Spread\displaystyle \text{Spread}=rasset−i(1−t)\displaystyle =r_{asset}-i(1-t)Annual gain\displaystyle \text{Annual gain}=D×Spread\displaystyle =D\times\text{Spread}

In words: After-tax borrowing cost = interest rate x (1 − tax rate on deductible interest); Spread = expected return of what the debt buys − after-tax borrowing cost; Annual gain = amount borrowed x spread

What each symbol means

DAmount borrowedmoney
iInterest rate on the loanper year
tTax rate at which the interest is deductible (0 if it is not)percent
r_assetExpected return of what the money buys: rent, higher earnings or investment return, after costsper year

Worked example

You borrow 30,000 at 5.5% to buy something that you expect to return 8% a year. The interest is not tax deductible. Compare with borrowing the same amount at 22% on a card.

After-tax cost0.055 × (1 − 0)5.50%
Spread0.08 − 0.0552.50%
Annual gain30,000 × 0.025750
Spread if borrowed on the card0.08 − 0.22 × (1 − 0)−14%
Annual gain or loss on the card30,000 × −0.14−4,200
The answer

The after-tax cost of the loan is 5.50% against an expected return of 8%, a spread of 2.50%, or 750 a year. On a card at 22% the spread is −14%, a yearly loss of −4,200, which is bad debt by this test.
