Two-Sample Z-Test for a Difference in Means

BeginnerData, Statistics & Computational SkillsAlso known as: quantitative foundations, compare two strategies, difference in average returns, p-value
← Treynor RatioUncovered Interest Parity and Unhedged Return →

Finds: whether two averages differ by more than chance.

When to use it: Use it to judge whether two averages really differ or the gap is plausibly noise, such as two funds, two strategies, or two periods. It assumes independent groups and large samples (roughly 30 or more each). A small p-value says the gap is hard to explain by chance; it does not say the gap is large or that it will persist. Testing many strategies and reporting the best inflates false positives.

Worked example

The answer

The difference is 1.50% with a standard error of 1.049%, so z is 1.430 and the two-sided p-value is 0.1527. At the usual 5% level the gap 0.
