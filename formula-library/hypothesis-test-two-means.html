Two-Sample Z-Test for a Difference in Means

Beginner

Also known as: quantitative foundations, compare two strategies, difference in average returns, p-value

Whether two averages differ by more than chance. Use it to judge whether two averages really differ or the gap is plausibly noise, such as two funds, two strategies, or two periods. It assumes independent groups and large samples (roughly 30 or more each). A small p-value says the gap is hard to explain by chance; it does not say the gap is large or that it will persist. Testing many strategies and reporting the best inflates false positives.

The formula

z=xˉ1−xˉ2s12/n1+s22/n2z=\frac{\bar{x}_{1}-\bar{x}_{2}}{\sqrt{s_{1}^{2}/n_{1}+s_{2}^{2}/n_{2}}}

z = (average of group 1 - average of group 2) / sqrt(sd1^2 / n1 + sd2^2 / n2); two-sided p-value = 2 x (1 - N(|z|))

What each symbol means

Worked example

Strategy A averaged 9% a year over 60 observations with a standard deviation of 6%. Strategy B averaged 7.5% over 50 observations with a standard deviation of 5%.

  1. Difference in averages0.09-0.075= 1.50%
  2. Standard error = sqrt(s1^2 / n1 + s2^2 / n2)sqrt(0.06^2 / 60+0.05^2 / 50)= 1.049%
  3. z = difference / standard error0.015 / 0.0104881= 1.430
  4. Two-sided p-value = 2 x (1 - N(|z|))= 0.1527
  5. Significant at 5%? (1 = yes, 0 = no)1 if 0.152661<0.05 else 0= 0

The difference is 1.50% with a standard error of 1.049%, so z is 1.430 and the two-sided p-value is 0.1527. At the usual 5% level the gap 0.
