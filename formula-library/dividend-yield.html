Dividend Yield and Total Shareholder Yield

BeginnerCorporate FinanceAlso known as: yield, buyback yield, shareholder yield
← Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP)Dividend Yield on Cost →

Finds: cash returned to owners as a percentage of value.

When to use it: Use it to compare the cash a company returns to owners, counting both dividends and buybacks. A company that only pays dividends can look less generous than one that also repurchases shares.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Dividend yield\displaystyle \text{Dividend yield}=DPSP\displaystyle =\frac{DPS}{P}Buyback yield\displaystyle \text{Buyback yield}=BuybacksMarket cap\displaystyle =\frac{\text{Buybacks}}{\text{Market cap}}

In words: Dividend yield = Dividend per share / Price; Buyback yield = Buybacks / Market capitalization; Total shareholder yield = the sum

Worked example

A share costs 40 and pays dividends of 1.60 a year. The company has a market value of 4,000 and spent 100 on buybacks. What is the total shareholder yield?

Dividend yield1.6 ÷ 404%
Buyback yield100 ÷ 4,0002.50%
Total shareholder yield0.04 + 0.0256.50%
The answer

The dividend yield is 4%, the buyback yield is 2.50%, and the total shareholder yield is 6.50%.
