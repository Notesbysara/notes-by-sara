Degree of Combined Leverage (DCL)

Intermediate

Also known as: DCL, total leverage, DOL times DFL

Combined sensitivity to sales from fixed costs and debt. Use it to see the total effect of fixed operating costs and borrowing together. A firm with both high fixed costs and high debt has very volatile earnings.

The formula

DCL=DOL×DFLDCL=DOL\times DFL

DCL = DOL x DFL

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
DCLHow many percent earnings before tax change for each 1% change in salesnumber
DOLDegree of operating leveragenumber
DFLDegree of financial leveragenumber

Worked example

A firm sells 5,000 units at 25, has a variable cost of 15 per unit, fixed costs of 30,000 and interest of 8,000. What is its degree of combined leverage?

  1. Contribution margin = Q x (P - VC)5000 x (25-15)= 50,000.00
  2. EBIT = contribution margin - FC50000-30000= 20,000.00
  3. DOL = contribution margin / EBIT50000 / 20000= 2.50
  4. DFL = EBIT / (EBIT - I)20000 / (20000-8000)= 1.67
  5. DCL = DOL x DFL2.5 x 1.66667= 4.17
  6. Earnings change for a 10% sales change4.16667 x 0.10= 41.67%

DOL is 2.50 and DFL is 1.67, so the DCL is 4.17. A 10% fall in sales cuts earnings before tax by about 41.67%.
