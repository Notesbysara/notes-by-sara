Geometric Brownian Motion: Terminal Price Distribution

Intermediate

Also known as: lognormal price, GBM, median vs mean price, probability above strike

Mean, median and chances for a price after some years. Use it to describe where a price following GBM is likely to be. Prices are lognormal: never negative and skewed to the upside. The mean is above the median, and the gap grows with volatility and time. A single step of the model is in the Foundations section.

The formula

ST=S0e(μ−σ2/2)T+σTZ,E[ST]=S0eμT,Median=S0e(μ−σ2/2)TS_{T}=S_{0}e^{(\mu-\sigma^{2}/2)T+\sigma\sqrt{T}Z},\quad E[S_{T}]=S_{0}e^{\mu T},\quad \text{Median}=S_{0}e^{(\mu-\sigma^{2}/2)T}

Price at T = S0 x e^((mu - sigma^2 / 2) x T + sigma x sqrt(T) x Z), where Z is a standard normal; Mean = S0 x e^(mu T); Median = S0 x e^((mu - sigma^2 / 2) T); P(S_T > K) = N([ln(S0 / K) + (mu - sigma^2 / 2) T] / (sigma sqrt(T)))

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
S_0Starting pricemoney
muExpected return (drift)per year
sigmaVolatilityper year
TTime horizonyears
ZStandard normal random numbernumber

Worked example

A stock at 100 has an expected return of 8% and volatility of 20%. What is the distribution after 2 years, and what is the chance it ends above 120?

  1. Mean = S0 e^(mu T)100 x exp(0.08 x 2)= 117.35
  2. Median = S0 e^((mu - sigma^2 / 2) T)100 x exp((0.08-0.2^2 / 2) x 2)= 112.75
  3. Standard deviation = mean x sqrt(e^(sigma^2 T) - 1)mean x sqrt(exp(0.2^2 x 2)-1)= 33.87
  4. P(S_T > K) = N([ln(S0 / K) + (mu - sigma^2 / 2) T] / (sigma sqrt(T)))= 41.28%

The mean price after 2 years is 117.35 and the median is 112.75. The standard deviation is 33.87. The chance of ending above 120 is 41.28%.
