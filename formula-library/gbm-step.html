Simulated Price Step (Monte Carlo)

Intermediate

Also known as: geometric Brownian motion, random price path, monte carlo price, lognormal price simulation

One simulated price step. Use it as one step of a Monte Carlo simulation. Repeating it many times with different random z values produces thousands of possible price paths, from which you can estimate risks and option values.

The formula

St+Δt=Stexp⁡ ⁣[(μ−σ22)Δt+σΔt z]S_{t+\Delta t}=S_{t}\exp\!\left[\left(\mu-\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\right)\Delta t+\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,z\right]

S(next) = S x exp((mu - sigma^2 / 2) x dt + sigma x sqrt(dt) x z)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
SPrice at the start of the stepmoney
S(next)Simulated price at the end of the stepmoney
muExpected annual return, as a decimalper year
sigmaAnnual volatility, as a decimalper year
dtLength of the step in yearsyears
zA random draw from a standard normal distribution (mean 0, standard deviation 1)number

Worked example

A share is priced at 100, with an expected return of 8% and a volatility of 20% a year. For a one-year step and a random draw of z = 0.5, what is the simulated price?

  1. Drift = (mu - sigma^2 / 2) x dt(0.08-0.2^2 / 2) x 1= 0.0600
  2. Shock = sigma x sqrt(dt) x z0.2 x sqrt(1) x 0.5= 0.1000
  3. New price = starting price x exp(drift + shock)100 x exp(0.06+0.1)= 117.35

With z = 0.5 the simulated price is 117.35. A different random z gives a different price.
