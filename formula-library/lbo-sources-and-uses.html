LBO Sources and Uses (Equity Check)

IntermediateValuationAlso known as: sources and uses, LBO financing, debt capacity, equity contribution
← LBO Returns (MOIC and IRR)Lease Liability and First-Year Expense (IFRS 16 / ASC 842) →

Finds: how an LBO is funded.

When to use it: Use it to set up an LBO model. Total sources must equal total uses. The debt is sized from EBITDA, and the sponsor's equity fills the gap.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Uses\displaystyle \text{Uses}=EV+Fees\displaystyle =EV+\text{Fees}Debt\displaystyle \text{Debt}=EBITDA×Leverage\displaystyle =\text{EBITDA}\times\text{Leverage}Equity\displaystyle \text{Equity}=Uses−Debt\displaystyle =\text{Uses}-\text{Debt}

In words: Uses = Purchase EV + Fees; Debt = EBITDA x Leverage multiple; Equity check = Uses − Debt

What each symbol means

UsesAll the money needed: the purchase price and feesmoney
SourcesWhere the money comes from: debt and sponsor equitymoney
Leverage multipleDebt as a multiple of EBITDAnumber
Equity checkThe sponsor's own money in the dealmoney

Worked example

A fund buys a company for an enterprise value of 1,000. Fees are 30. The company has EBITDA of 150 and lenders will lend 5 times EBITDA. How much equity does the fund need?

Total uses1,000 + 301,030
Debt150 × 5750
Equity check1,030 − 750280
Equity share of total sources280 ÷ 1,03027.18%
The answer

Total uses are 1,030. Lenders provide 750, so the fund's equity check is 280, which is 27.18% of the total.
