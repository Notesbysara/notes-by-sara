LBO Sources and Uses (Equity Check)

Intermediate

Also known as: sources and uses, LBO financing, debt capacity, equity contribution

How an LBO is funded. Use it to set up an LBO model. Total sources must equal total uses. The debt is sized from EBITDA, and the sponsor's equity fills the gap.

The formula

Uses=EV+Fees,Debt=EBITDA×Leverage,Equity=Uses−Debt\text{Uses}=EV+\text{Fees},\quad \text{Debt}=\text{EBITDA}\times\text{Leverage},\quad \text{Equity}=\text{Uses}-\text{Debt}

Uses = Purchase EV + Fees; Debt = EBITDA x Leverage multiple; Equity check = Uses - Debt

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
UsesAll the money needed: the purchase price and feesmoney
SourcesWhere the money comes from: debt and sponsor equitymoney
Leverage multipleDebt as a multiple of EBITDAnumber
Equity checkThe sponsor's own money in the dealmoney

Worked example

A fund buys a company for an enterprise value of 1,000. Fees are 30. The company has EBITDA of 150 and lenders will lend 5 times EBITDA. How much equity does the fund need?

  1. Total uses = EV + fees1000+30= 1,030.00
  2. Debt = EBITDA x leverage multiple150 x 5= 750.00
  3. Equity check = uses - debt1030-750= 280.00
  4. Equity share of total sources280 / 1030= 27.18%

Total uses are 1,030.00. Lenders provide 750.00, so the fund's equity check is 280.00, which is 27.18% of the total.
