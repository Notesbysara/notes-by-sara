Merchant Cost of a Digital Payment

BeginnerFintech & Digital FinanceAlso known as: digital payments, mobile wallets, merchant discount rate, interchange, effective payment cost
← Mental Accounting: Cost of Separate PotsMerton Credit Model (Equity as a Call on Assets) →

Finds: what a payment costs a merchant.

When to use it: Use it to see what accepting cards or a wallet really costs. The percentage is split between the issuing bank, the network and the processor, and the fixed fee makes small payments cost a higher percentage. Compare payment methods by their effective rate at your average basket size.

The formula

Cost\displaystyle \text{Cost}=a×MDR+f\displaystyle =a\times MDR+fMDR\displaystyle MDR=i+s+m\displaystyle =i+s+mEffective rate\displaystyle \text{Effective rate}=Costa\displaystyle =\frac{\text{Cost}}{a}

In words: Merchant discount rate (MDR) = interchange (paid to the card issuer) + scheme fee (card network) + acquirer margin; Cost of one payment = amount x MDR + fixed fee per transaction; Effective rate = cost / amount; Break-even amount against a flat-fee method = flat fee / (MDR − 0)

What each symbol means

aPayment amountmoney
MDRTotal percentage the merchant pays the payment processorpercent
i, s, mInterchange, scheme fee, acquirer marginpercent
fFixed fee per transactionmoney

Worked example

A shop takes an 80 payment. The MDR is 1.8%, made of 1.2% interchange, 0.15% scheme fee and the rest acquirer margin. There is also a fixed fee of 0.10.

The answer

The MDR is 1.80%. One payment costs 1.54, an effective rate of 1.93%. Of the percentage fee, 0.96 goes to the issuing bank, 0.12 to the network and 0.46 to the processor. On a 5 payment the effective rate would be 3.80%.
