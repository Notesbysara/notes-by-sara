Merchant Cost of a Digital Payment

Beginner

Also known as: digital payments, mobile wallets, merchant discount rate, interchange, effective payment cost

What a payment costs a merchant. Use it to see what accepting cards or a wallet really costs. The percentage is split between the issuing bank, the network and the processor, and the fixed fee makes small payments cost a higher percentage. Compare payment methods by their effective rate at your average basket size.

The formula

Cost=a×MDR+f,MDR=i+s+m,Effective rate=Costa\text{Cost}=a\times MDR+f,\quad MDR=i+s+m,\quad \text{Effective rate}=\frac{\text{Cost}}{a}

Merchant discount rate (MDR) = interchange (paid to the card issuer) + scheme fee (card network) + acquirer margin; Cost of one payment = amount x MDR + fixed fee per transaction; Effective rate = cost / amount; Break-even amount against a flat-fee method = flat fee / (MDR - 0)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
aPayment amountmoney
MDRTotal percentage the merchant pays the payment processorpercent
i, s, mInterchange, scheme fee, acquirer marginpercent
fFixed fee per transactionmoney

Worked example

A shop takes an 80 payment. The MDR is 1.8%, made of 1.2% interchange, 0.15% scheme fee and the rest acquirer margin. There is also a fixed fee of 0.10.

  1. MDR = interchange + scheme + margin0.012+0.0015+0.0045= 1.80%
  2. Cost = amount x MDR + fixed fee80 x 0.018+0.1= 1.54
  3. Effective rate = cost / amount1.54 / 80= 1.93%
  4. To the issuing bank80 x 0.012= 0.96
  5. To the network80 x 0.0015= 0.12
  6. To the processor (margin plus fixed fee)80 x 0.0045+0.1= 0.46
  7. Effective rate on a 5 payment(5 x 0.018+0.1) / 5= 3.80%

The MDR is 1.80%. One payment costs 1.54, an effective rate of 1.93%. Of the percentage fee, 0.96 goes to the issuing bank, 0.12 to the network and 0.46 to the processor. On a 5 payment the effective rate would be 3.80%.
