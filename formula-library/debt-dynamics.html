Government Debt Dynamics

IntermediateEconomics for FinanceAlso known as: fiscal policy, debt to GDP, primary balance, r minus g, debt sustainability
← Goodwill Impairment TestGraham Number →

Finds: whether debt is rising and what surplus stops it.

When to use it: Use it to judge whether debt is on a stable path. When the interest rate is above growth (r > g), debt keeps rising unless the government runs a primary surplus. When growth is above the rate, debt can fall even with a small primary deficit. This is why the r minus g gap matters so much. It assumes constant r and g, whereas rates are refinanced over time.

The formula

In words: Next year's debt-to-GDP = this year's x (1 + interest rate) / (1 + GDP growth) − primary balance (as a share of GDP; a surplus is positive); The primary balance that holds debt steady = debt ratio x (r − g) / (1 + g)

What each symbol means

dDebt as a share of GDPpercent
rAverage interest rate on government debtper year
gNominal GDP growthper year
pbPrimary balance: revenue minus spending excluding interest. Positive = surpluspercent of GDP

Worked example

Debt is 90% of GDP, the average interest rate is 4%, nominal growth 2% and the primary balance is a deficit of 1% of GDP.

Growth factor of debt(1 + 0.04) ÷ (1 + 0.02)1.01961
Debt ratio next year0.9 × 1.01961−−0.0192.76%
Primary balance that stabilizes debt0.9 × (0.04 − 0.02) ÷ (1 + 0.02)1.76%
Debt ratio after 10 years0.9 × 1.01961^10−−0.01 × (1.01961^10 − 1) ÷ (1.01961 − 1)120.2%
Debt after 10 years with the stabilizing surplus0.9 × 1.01961^10 − 0.0176471 × (1.01961^10 − 1) ÷ (1.01961 − 1)90%
The answer

Next year debt rises to 92.76%. To hold the ratio steady the government would need a primary surplus of 1.76%. If the deficit of 1% persisted, debt would reach 120.2% in 10 years, and with the stabilizing surplus it would stay near 90.0%.
