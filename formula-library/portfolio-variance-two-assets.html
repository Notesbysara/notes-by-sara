Risk of a Two-Asset Portfolio

IntermediateInvestments & Portfolio TheoryAlso known as: portfolio standard deviation, portfolio variance, diversification benefit, two asset portfolio risk
← Rights Issue: Ex-Rights Price and Value of a RightRisk Parity Weights (Inverse Volatility) →

Finds: portfolio risk and diversification benefit.

When to use it: Use it to see how combining two assets changes risk. When the correlation is below 1, the portfolio is less risky than the weighted average of the two risks. That gap is the benefit of diversification.

Worked example

A portfolio has 60% in asset 1 (standard deviation 20%) and 40% in asset 2 (standard deviation 12%). The correlation between them is 0.3. What is the portfolio's standard deviation?

The answer

The portfolio's standard deviation is 14.20%. The weighted average of the two risks is 16.80%, so diversification removes 2.60% of risk.
