Risk of a Two-Asset Portfolio

Intermediate

Also known as: portfolio standard deviation, portfolio variance, diversification benefit, two asset portfolio risk

Portfolio risk and diversification benefit. Use it to see how combining two assets changes risk. When the correlation is below 1, the portfolio is less risky than the weighted average of the two risks. That gap is the benefit of diversification.

The formula

σp2=w12σ12+w22σ22+2w1w2ρ12σ1σ2\sigma_{p}^{2}=w_{1}^{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}+w_{2}^{2}\sigma_{2}^{2}+2w_{1}w_{2}\rho_{12}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}

Portfolio variance = w1^2 x s1^2 + w2^2 x s2^2 + 2 x w1 x w2 x rho x s1 x s2

Worked example

A portfolio has 60% in asset 1 (standard deviation 20%) and 40% in asset 2 (standard deviation 12%). The correlation between them is 0.3. What is the portfolio's standard deviation?

  1. Weight of asset 2 = 1 - w11-0.6= 40.00%
  2. Portfolio variance from the formula0.6^2 x 0.2^2+0.4^2 x 0.12^2+2 x 0.6 x 0.4 x 0.3 x 0.2 x 0.12= 0.02016
  3. Portfolio standard deviation = square root of variancesqrt(var)= 14.20%
  4. Weighted average of the two risks = w1 x s1 + w2 x s20.6 x 0.2+0.4 x 0.12= 16.80%
  5. Risk removed by diversification = average - portfolio0.168-sd= 2.60%

The portfolio's standard deviation is 14.20%. The weighted average of the two risks is 16.80%, so diversification removes 2.60% of risk.
