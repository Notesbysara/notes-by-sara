Convertible Bond: Conversion Value and Premiums

Intermediate

Also known as: conversion ratio, conversion price, conversion premium, bond floor, parity

What a convertible bond is worth against its parts. Use it to judge a convertible bond. Its value is at least the larger of its straight bond value (the bond floor) and what it would be worth if converted. The market pays a premium above both because the holder keeps the upside of the shares while having the safety of the bond.

The formula

Conversion value=CR×S,Conversion price=FCR,Premium=PconvCR×S−1\text{Conversion value}=CR\times S,\quad \text{Conversion price}=\frac{F}{CR},\quad \text{Premium}=\frac{P_{conv}}{CR\times S}-1

Conversion value = Conversion ratio x Share price; Conversion price = Face value / Conversion ratio; Premium over conversion value = Bond price / Conversion value - 1; Floor = higher of conversion value and straight bond value

Worked example

A convertible bond has a face value of 1,000 and converts into 25 shares. The shares trade at 36. The bond trades at 1,050 and a similar non-convertible bond would be worth 880. What are the conversion value and the premiums?

  1. Conversion price = face / conversion ratio1000 / 25= 40.00
  2. Conversion value = conversion ratio x share price25 x 36= 900.00
  3. Lower bound = higher of conversion value and straight valuemax(900, 880)= 900.00
  4. Premium over conversion value = price / conversion value - 11050 / 900-1= 16.7%
  5. Premium over straight bond value1050 / 880-1= 19.3%

The conversion price is 40.00, above the share price of 36. The conversion value is 900.00 and the bond floor is 900.00. The bond trades 16.7% above its conversion value and 19.3% above its straight bond value. That is the price paid for the option to convert.
