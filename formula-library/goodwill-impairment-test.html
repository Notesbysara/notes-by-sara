Goodwill Impairment Test

Intermediate

Also known as: impairment loss, recoverable amount, cash generating unit, carrying amount

The write-down when a unit is worth less than its book value. Use it to see how an impairment loss arises. Goodwill is not amortized under IFRS or US GAAP for most firms, so it is tested at least yearly. If the unit's value falls below its book value, the difference is written off, first against goodwill. A sudden impairment often signals that an acquisition was overpriced.

The formula

Recoverable=max⁡(FVLCD,VIU),Impairment=max⁡(Carrying−Recoverable,0)\text{Recoverable}=\max(FVLCD,VIU),\quad \text{Impairment}=\max(\text{Carrying}-\text{Recoverable},0)

Recoverable amount = higher of (Fair value less costs of disposal) and (Value in use); Impairment loss = Carrying amount - Recoverable amount, if positive; it first reduces goodwill

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
Carrying amountBook value of the unit's assets, including goodwillmoney
FVLCDFair value less costs of disposal, what the unit would fetch if soldmoney
VIUValue in use, the present value of cash flows from keeping itmoney
GoodwillGoodwill allocated to the unitmoney

Worked example

A business unit has a carrying amount of 500 including goodwill of 120. Its fair value less costs of disposal is 430 and its value in use is 410.

  1. Recoverable amount = higher of the twomax(430, 410)= 430.00
  2. Impairment = carrying - recoverable (if positive)max(500-430, 0)= 70.00
  3. Charged to goodwill firstmin(70, 120)= 70.00
  4. Goodwill remaining120-70= 50.00
  5. Charged to other assets70-70= 0.00

The recoverable amount is 430.00, the higher of the two. The impairment is 70.00. Goodwill absorbs it first, so 70.00 is written off goodwill, leaving goodwill of 50.00 and 0.00 to be spread over other assets.
