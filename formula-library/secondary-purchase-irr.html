Secondaries: Price Versus NAV and Buyer IRR

Intermediate

Also known as: secondaries and continuation funds, discount to NAV, LP stake sale, unfunded commitment

What a buyer earns on a fund stake bought at a discount. Use it to judge a secondary purchase: buying an existing stake in a private fund from a seller who wants cash. The buyer pays a discount to NAV and receives the fund's distributions from then on, so the investment is already partly mature and the J-curve is shorter. The buyer still takes on any unfunded commitment, and the NAV may itself be too high.

The formula

P=NAV(1−d),0=−P+∑tDt−Ut(1+IRR)t,MOIC=∑DP+∑UP=NAV(1-d),\quad 0=-P+\sum_{t}\frac{D_{t}-U_{t}}{(1+IRR)^{t}},\quad MOIC=\frac{\sum D}{P+\sum U}

Price paid = reported NAV x (1 - discount); Buyer cash flows = distributions received - further capital the buyer must fund (unfunded commitment); Buyer IRR solves NPV = 0; Buyer multiple = total distributions / (price + unfunded capital called)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
NAVNet asset value reported by the fundmoney
dDiscount to NAV the buyer negotiatespercent
D_tDistributions the buyer expects in year tmoney
U_tFurther capital called from the buyer in year tmoney

Worked example

A buyer pays at a 12% discount to a NAV of 100, and expects distributions of 10, 15, 22, 28, 34 and 18 in years 1 to 6. A further 15 is called in year 2.

  1. Price = NAV x (1 - discount)100 x (1-0.12)= 88.00
  2. Total distributions= 127.00
  3. Total invested (price + call)88+15= 103.00
  4. Multiple = distributions / invested127 / 103= 1.23
  5. Buyer cash flows by year= -88.00, 10.00, 0.00, 22.00, 28.00, 34.00, 18.00
  6. Buyer IRR= 6.1%
  7. Cash flows if distributions are 10% lower= -88.00, 9.00, -1.50, 19.80, 25.20, 30.60, 16.20
  8. IRR in that case= 2.9%

The price is 88.00. The buyer receives 127.00 in total and funds 103.00 (price plus the call), a multiple of 1.23. The IRR is 6.1%. If the NAV is overstated by 10% (distributions fall by 10%), the IRR becomes 2.9%.
