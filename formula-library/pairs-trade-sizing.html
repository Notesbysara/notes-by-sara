Pairs Trade Sizing and Reversion Profit

IntermediateQuantitative FinanceAlso known as: statistical arbitrage, stat arb, pairs trading, spread trade
← P/E Ratio and Earnings YieldPayback Period →

Finds: how big to make a pairs trade and what reversion would earn.

When to use it: Use it to size a trade once a spread has been identified. When the z-score is large and positive the spread is rich: short Y and buy beta units of X. When it is large and negative, do the reverse. Profit assumes the spread fully reverts, and ignores costs, financing and the chance that the relationship has broken, so it is an upper bound, not a forecast.

In words: z-score = (spread − mean spread) / sd of spread; Units of the pair = floor(capital / (price Y + hedge ratio x price X)); Profit if the spread returns to its mean = Units x |spread − mean|

What each symbol means

s-bar, sigma_sMean and standard deviation of the spreadmoney

Worked example

The spread has a mean of 0.40 and a standard deviation of 0.80 and is now 2.40. The hedge ratio is 1.5, Y trades at 120 and X at 70, and 50,000 of capital is allocated to gross exposure.

The answer

The z-score is 2.50, so the spread is rich: short Y and buy X. Each unit of the pair uses 225 of gross exposure, so 222 units fit in the capital. Reversion to the mean would earn 444, or 0.89% of the capital.
