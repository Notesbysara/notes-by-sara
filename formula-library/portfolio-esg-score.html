Portfolio ESG Score and Tilt vs Benchmark

BeginnerRegulation, Ethics & ESGAlso known as: ESG investing, weighted average ESG score, ESG tilt, negative screening
← Policy Rate Pass-Through to Banks and BorrowersPortfolio Variance in Matrix Form →

Finds: how a portfolio compares on ESG scores.

When to use it: Use it to describe how a portfolio compares on environmental, social and governance scores. A tilt toward higher-scoring companies or a screen that removes low scorers changes the holdings and may change risk and return. Scores differ between providers, so the same portfolio can look better or worse depending on the source.

The formula

Sp\displaystyle S_{p}=∑iwisi\displaystyle =\sum_{i}w_{i}s_{i}Tilt\displaystyle \text{Tilt}=Sp−Sb\displaystyle =S_{p}-S_{b}

In words: Portfolio ESG score = sum of (weight x ESG score) over holdings; Benchmark score = same with benchmark weights; Tilt = portfolio score − benchmark score; Share excluded by a screen = weight of holdings below a minimum score

What each symbol means

Worked example

A portfolio holds five stocks with weights 30%, 25%, 20%, 15% and 10% and scores 72, 55, 81, 40 and 66. The benchmark holds them equally. A screen excludes any stock scoring below 50.

Portfolio score64.15
Benchmark score62.80
Tilt64.15 − 62.81.35
Weight excluded by the screen15%
Score after screening and re-weighting68.41
The answer

The portfolio score is 64.15 against 62.80 for the benchmark, a tilt of 1.35. The screen removes holdings with a combined weight of 15.0%, and the score of the remaining holdings, re-weighted, is 68.41.
