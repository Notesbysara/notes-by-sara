REIT Funds From Operations (FFO) and AFFO

BeginnerAlternative InvestmentsAlso known as: FFO, AFFO, P/FFO, REIT payout ratio
← Reinsurance: Quota Share and Excess of LossRelative Strength Index (RSI) →

Finds: A REIT's cash earnings, valuation multiple and dividend cover.

When to use it: Use it to assess a REIT instead of ordinary earnings. Accounting depreciation falls on buildings that often hold or gain value, so net income understates the cash a REIT earns. FFO adds it back; AFFO goes further and removes the spending needed to keep the properties let. The payout ratio against AFFO shows whether the dividend is covered.

The formula

FFO\displaystyle FFO=NI+D&A−G\displaystyle =NI+D\&A-GAFFO\displaystyle AFFO=FFO−CapEx−SLR\displaystyle =FFO-CapEx-SLRPFFO\displaystyle \frac{P}{FFO}

In words: FFO = net income + depreciation and amortization − gains on property sales; AFFO = FFO − recurring maintenance capital spending − straight-line rent adjustments; Price / FFO = share price / FFO per share; Payout ratio = dividend per share / AFFO per share

What each symbol means

NINet incomemoney
D&ADepreciation and amortization of buildings (a non-cash charge that overstates real wear on property)money
GGains on property sales (one-off, removed)money
CapExRecurring spending to keep buildings leasablemoney
SLRStraight-line rent adjustment: rent booked as income but not yet received in cashmoney

Worked example

A REIT reports net income of 120m, depreciation of 90m and a 25m gain on property sales. Maintenance capex is 30m and the straight-line rent adjustment is 5m. It has 50m shares at a price of 40 and pays a dividend of 2.20.

FFO120 + 90 − 25185
AFFO185 − 30 − 5150
FFO per share185 ÷ 503.70
AFFO per share150 ÷ 503
Price / FFO40 ÷ 3.710.81
Payout ratio2.2 ÷ 373.3%
Dividend covered? (11 if 2.2<=3 else 01
The answer

FFO is 185m and AFFO is 150m. Per share, FFO is 3.70 and AFFO is 3. The price is 10.81 times FFO. The dividend uses 73.3% of AFFO, so it is 1 (1 = covered by AFFO, 0 = not covered).
