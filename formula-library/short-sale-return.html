Short Sale Profit and Return

Intermediate

Also known as: short selling, borrowing shares, short position return, cost to borrow

The result of a short sale. Use it to work out the result of betting against a stock. You gain if the price falls and lose if it rises, with no upper limit on the loss. Dividends and borrow fees are costs on top.

The formula

Profit=(Psell−Pcover)Q−D Q−Fee,Fee=Psell Q f d365\text{Profit}=(P_{sell}-P_{cover})Q-D\,Q-\text{Fee},\quad \text{Fee}=P_{sell}\,Q\,f\,\frac{d}{365}

Profit = (Sale price - Buy-back price) x Shares - Dividends owed - Borrow fee; Borrow fee = Sale value x Fee rate x Days / 365

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
P_sellPrice at which you sold the borrowed sharesmoney
P_coverPrice at which you buy them back to return themmoney
QShares sold shortnumber
DDividend per share paid while you were short, which you owe to the lendermoney
fAnnual fee to borrow the sharesper year
dDays the position is opendays

Worked example

You short 100 shares at 50 and buy them back 90 days later at 40. A dividend of 1 per share was paid. The borrow fee is 2% a year, and you put up margin of 50% of the sale value. What is the profit and return?

  1. Gain from the price move = (sale - buy-back) x shares(50-40) x 100= 1,000.00
  2. Dividends owed = dividend x shares1 x 100= 100.00
  3. Borrow fee = sale value x fee x days / 36550 x 100 x 0.02 x 90 / 365= 24.66
  4. Profit = gain - dividends - fee1000-100-24.6575= 875.34
  5. Margin posted = margin x sale value0.5 x 50 x 100= 2,500.00
  6. Return on margin = profit / margin875.342 / 2500= 35.01%

The price move earned 1,000.00. After the dividend of 100.00 and a borrow fee of 24.66, the profit is 875.34. On the margin of 2,500.00 that is a return of 35.01%.
