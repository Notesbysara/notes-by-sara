Immunization: Matching Duration to a Liability

Intermediate

Also known as: liability matching, duration matching, bullet and barbell, bond portfolio strategies

How to split money between two bonds to match a liability. Use it to protect against interest rate changes when you must meet a payment on a known date. Set the portfolio's present value equal to the liability's and its duration equal to the liability's duration. Then a change in yields moves assets and liability by about the same amount. Rebalance as durations drift.

The formula

w1=D2−DLD2−D1,w2=1−w1w_{1}=\frac{D_{2}-D_{L}}{D_{2}-D_{1}},\quad w_{2}=1-w_{1}

Weight in bond 1 = (Duration of bond 2 - Duration of the liability) / (Duration of bond 2 - Duration of bond 1); weight in bond 2 = 1 - weight in bond 1

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
D_LDuration of the liability, the time until it is due for a single paymentyears
D_1, D_2Durations of the two bonds, one shorter and one longer than the liabilityyears
w_1, w_2Shares of the portfolio value held in each bondpercent

Worked example

A fund must pay 1,000,000 in 6 years, worth 744,000 today. It can buy a bond with a duration of 3 years or a bond with a duration of 10 years. How much goes in each to immunize the liability?

  1. Weight in bond 1 = (D2 - DL) / (D2 - D1)(10-6) / (10-d1)= 57.14%
  2. Weight in bond 2 = 1 - w11-0.571429= 42.86%
  3. Amount in bond 10.571429 x 744000= 425,142.86
  4. Amount in bond 20.428571 x 744000= 318,857.14

Put 57.14% of the fund in the short bond and 42.86% in the long bond. In money that is 425,142.86 and 318,857.14, which adds up to the present value of the liability.
