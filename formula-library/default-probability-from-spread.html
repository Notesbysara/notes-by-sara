Default Probability and Recovery from Credit Spread

Intermediate

Also known as: credit triangle, expected loss, loss given default, cumulative default probability

Default probability and expected loss from a spread. Use it to turn a credit spread into a rough default probability, or to estimate expected loss on a loan or bond. It treats the whole spread as compensation for default, ignoring liquidity and risk premiums, so it overstates default risk.

The formula

PD≈s1−R,PDn=1−(1−PD)n,EL=PD×(1−R)×EADPD\approx\frac{s}{1-R},\quad PD_{n}=1-(1-PD)^{n},\quad EL=PD\times(1-R)\times EAD

Annual default probability = Spread / (1 - Recovery rate); Cumulative default probability over n years = 1 - (1 - PD)^n; Expected loss = PD x (1 - R) x Exposure

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
sCredit spreadper year
RRecovery rate, the share of face value recovered after defaultpercent
PDAnnual probability of defaultper year
EADExposure at default, the amount owedmoney
ELExpected lossmoney

Worked example

A bond has a credit spread of 1.7% and an expected recovery rate of 40%. You hold 2,000,000 of it. What default probability is implied, what is the chance of default within 5 years, and what is the expected loss over that period?

  1. Annual PD = spread / (1 - R)0.017 / (1-0.4)= 2.833%
  2. Cumulative PD = 1 - (1 - PD)^n1-(1-0.0283333)^5= 13.39%
  3. Expected loss = cumulative PD x (1 - R) x exposure0.133863 x (1-0.4) x 2,000,000= 160,635.77

The implied annual default probability is 2.833% and the chance of defaulting within 5 years is 13.39%. Expected loss over 5 years is 160,635.77.
