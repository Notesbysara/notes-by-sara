Default Probability and Recovery from Credit Spread

IntermediateFixed IncomeAlso known as: credit triangle, expected loss, loss given default, cumulative default probability
← Default Enrollment: Effect on Retirement SavingsDeferred Tax from Accelerated Depreciation →

Finds: default probability and expected loss from a spread.

When to use it: Use it to turn a credit spread into a rough default probability, or to estimate expected loss on a loan or bond. It treats the whole spread as compensation for default, ignoring liquidity and risk premiums, so it overstates default risk.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

PD≈s1−R\displaystyle PD\approx\frac{s}{1-R}PDn\displaystyle PD_{n}=1−(1−PD)n\displaystyle =1-(1-PD)^{n}EL\displaystyle EL=PD×(1−R)×EAD\displaystyle =PD\times(1-R)\times EAD

In words: Annual default probability = Spread / (1 − Recovery rate); Cumulative default probability over n years = 1 − (1 − PD)^n; Expected loss = PD x (1 − R) x Exposure

What each symbol means

sCredit spreadper year
RRecovery rate, the share of face value recovered after defaultpercent
PDAnnual probability of defaultper year

ELExpected lossmoney

Worked example

A bond has a credit spread of 1.7% and an expected recovery rate of 40%. You hold 2,000,000 of it. What default probability is implied, what is the chance of default within 5 years, and what is the expected loss over that period?

Annual PD0.017 ÷ (1 − 0.4)2.833%
Cumulative PD1 − (1 − 0.0283333)^513.39%
Expected loss0.133863 × (1 − 0.4) × 2,000,000160,635.77
The answer

The implied annual default probability is 2.833% and the chance of defaulting within 5 years is 13.39%. Expected loss over 5 years is 160,635.77.
