Degree of Operating Leverage (DOL)

IntermediateCorporate FinanceAlso known as: DOL, operating leverage, fixed cost leverage
← Degree of Financial Leverage (DFL)Delta Hedge and Hedging Error →

Finds: sensitivity of operating profit to sales.

When to use it: Use it to see how much a firm's operating profit swings when sales change. The higher the fixed costs, the higher the DOL, the bigger the swing in both directions.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

DOL\displaystyle DOL=Q(P−VC)Q(P−VC)−FC\displaystyle =\frac{Q(P-VC)}{Q(P-VC)-FC}

In words: DOL = Contribution margin / EBIT = Q x (P − VC) / (Q x (P − VC) − FC)

What each symbol means

DOLHow many percent EBIT changes for each 1% change in salesnumber
QUnits soldunits
PPrice per unitmoney per unit
VCVariable cost per unitmoney per unit
FCFixed costsmoney

Worked example

A firm sells 5,000 units at 25, with a variable cost of 15 per unit and fixed costs of 30,000. What is its degree of operating leverage?

Contribution margin5,000 × (25 − 15)50,000
EBIT50,000 − 30,00020,000
DOL50,000 ÷ 20,0002.50
EBIT change for a 10% sales change2.5 × 0.1025%
The answer

The DOL is 2.50, so a 10% change in sales changes EBIT by about 25%.
