PMI Diffusion Index and Data Surprise

Beginner

Also known as: economic indicators, jobs report surprise, CPI surprise, consensus forecast

Whether a survey signals expansion and how big a data surprise was. Use it to read two things markets focus on. A diffusion index above 50 means more firms are improving than worsening. Prices react to the surprise (actual minus consensus), not the level, because the consensus is already in the price. Dividing by the typical surprise puts different indicators on the same scale.

The formula

PMI=%better+0.5×%same,Z=Actual−ConsensusσsurprisePMI=\%_{better}+0.5\times\%_{same},\quad Z=\frac{\text{Actual}-\text{Consensus}}{\sigma_{surprise}}

Diffusion index (PMI) = % of firms reporting better + 0.5 x % reporting no change (above 50 means expansion); Surprise z-score = (actual - consensus forecast) / standard deviation of past surprises

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
%better, %sameShares of survey respondents reporting an improvement or no changepercent
Actual, ConsensusPublished value and the average economists' forecastnumber
sigmaTypical size of past surprises for that indicatornumber

Worked example

In a survey 38% of firms report better conditions, 44% the same and 18% worse. Separately, payrolls came in at 210,000 versus a 180,000 consensus, and past surprises have had a standard deviation of 60,000.

  1. Diffusion index = % better + 0.5 x % same, scaled to 0-100100 x (0.38+0.5 x 0.44)= 60.0
  2. Surprise = actual - consensus210-180= 30.0
  3. Surprise z-score30 / sd= 0.50
  4. Size of the surprise, absolute= 0.50

The diffusion index is 60.0, which signals expansion. The payrolls surprise is 30.0 thousand, a z-score of 0.50, which is a 0.50 standard-deviation surprise in size.
