Net Advantage to Leasing (Lease vs. Buy)

Intermediate

Also known as: NAL, lease or buy, lease versus buy decision

Whether leasing beats buying. Use it to decide whether to lease or buy an asset. The lessee gives up the depreciation tax shield but saves the purchase cost. This version has no salvage value and pays leases at the end of each year.

The formula

NAL=C−∑L(1−T)(1+rd(1−T))t−∑D T(1+rd(1−T))tNAL=C-\sum\frac{L(1-T)}{(1+r_{d}(1-T))^{t}}-\sum\frac{D\,T}{(1+r_{d}(1-T))^{t}}

NAL = Asset cost - PV of after-tax lease payments - PV of depreciation tax shields given up

Worked example

An asset costs 10,000 and lasts 5 years with no salvage value. A lease costs 2,300 a year. The tax rate is 25% and the pre-tax borrowing rate is 6%. Should the company lease?

  1. After-tax discount rate = rd x (1 - T)0.06 x (1-0.25)= 4.50%
  2. Annuity factor at that rate(1-(1+0.045)^(-5)) / 0.045= 4.3900
  3. PV of after-tax lease payments = L x (1 - T) x factor2300 x (1-0.25) x 4.38998= 7,572.71
  4. Yearly depreciation = C / n10000 / 5= 2,000.00
  5. PV of depreciation tax shields = D x T x factor2000 x 0.25 x 4.38998= 2,194.99
  6. NAL = C - PV of leases - PV of tax shields10000-7572.71-2194.99= 232.30

The NAL is 232.30. It is positive, so leasing is cheaper than buying.
