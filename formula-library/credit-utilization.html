Credit Utilization Ratio

Beginner

Also known as: credit scores, credit score factors, revolving utilization, credit limit usage

How much of your available credit you are using. Use it to see one of the factors that scoring models weigh heavily. Scores also reward paying on time and a long history, and penalize many new applications, so utilization is only part of the picture. Scoring details vary by country and provider, so treat 30% as a guideline, not a rule.

The formula

Utilization=∑Balances∑Credit limits\text{Utilization}=\frac{\sum \text{Balances}}{\sum \text{Credit limits}}

Credit utilization = total revolving balances / total credit limits. A common guideline is to keep it under 30%, and lower is better

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
BalancesAmounts currently owed on each credit card or revolving linemoney
Credit limitsThe maximum you may borrow on eachmoney

Worked example

You have three cards with balances of 1,800, 600 and 0 and limits of 5,000, 3,000 and 2,000. You want to stay under 30%.

  1. Total balances= 2,400.00
  2. Total limits= 10,000.00
  3. Overall utilization = balances / limits2400 / 10000= 24.0%
  4. Utilization by card= 36.00%, 20.00%, 0.00%
  5. Total limit needed for the target at current balances2400 / 0.3= 8,000.00
  6. Balance to pay down to reach the target nowmax(2400-0.3 x 10000, 0)= 0.00

Overall utilization is 24.0%. The share used on each card is 36.00%, 20.00%, 0.00%. The limit needed to put current balances at 30% is 8,000.00, and you would have to pay off 0.00 to reach it now.
