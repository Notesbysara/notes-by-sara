LBO Capstone: Debt Paydown with a Cash Sweep

IntermediatePractical Skills & CapstoneAlso known as: build an LBO model, cash sweep, leverage ratio over time, debt schedule
← Lag-1 AutocorrelationLBO Returns (MOIC and IRR) →

Finds: how fast a buyout pays down its debt.

When to use it: Use it as the debt schedule of a leveraged buyout model. Cash that is not needed goes to repay debt, so leverage falls as earnings grow and interest costs fall. This version leaves out depreciation, working capital and fees, which a full model must add.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Dt\displaystyle D_{t}=Dt−1−min⁡(Dt−1, s [EBITDAt−CapExt−rDt−1−τ (EBITDAt−CapExt−rDt−1)])\displaystyle =D_{t-1}-\min\big(D_{t-1},\ s\,[EBITDA_{t}-CapEx_{t}-r D_{t-1}-\tau\,(EBITDA_{t}-CapEx_{t}-r D_{t-1})]\big)

In words: Interest = opening debt x rate; Cash available for repayment = EBITDA − capital spending − interest − tax, where tax = tax rate x (EBITDA − capital spending − interest) (depreciation is ignored to keep it simple); Repayment = sweep share x cash available, but no more than the debt; Closing debt = opening debt − repayment; Leverage = closing debt / EBITDA

What each symbol means

D_tDebt at the end of year tmoney
sShare of spare cash that goes to repay debt (cash sweep)percent
r, tauInterest rate and tax ratepercent

Worked example

A buyout carries 480 of debt at 8.5% with a 100% cash sweep and a 25% tax rate. EBITDA over 5 years is 120, 132, 145, 158 and 170 with capital spending of 25, 25, 26, 26 and 27.

Closing debt after the last year152.41
Total repaid480 − 152.41327.59
Share of starting debt repaid327.59 ÷ 48068.2%
Opening leverage (debt / first-year EBITDA)480 ÷ [120, 132, 145, 158, 170][0]4
Closing leverage (debt / last-year EBITDA)152.41 ÷ [120, 132, 145, 158, 170][−1]0.90
Total interest paid159.21
The answer

Debt falls from 480 to 152.41 after five years, a repayment of 327.59 or 68.2% of the starting debt. Leverage goes from 4 times EBITDA in year 1 to 0.90 times at the end. Total interest over the period is 159.21.
