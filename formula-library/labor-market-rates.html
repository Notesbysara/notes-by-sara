Unemployment, Participation and Employment Rates

Beginner

Also known as: unemployment and the labor market, labor force participation, discouraged workers, employment population ratio

What a jobs report headline hides. Use it to read a jobs report. The unemployment rate can fall because people find jobs or because they stop looking (they then leave the labor force). The participation rate and employment-population ratio reveal which. Markets watch both along with wage growth, since a tight labor market can push inflation up.

The formula

u=UE+U,LFPR=E+UN,EPOP=ENu=\frac{U}{E+U},\quad LFPR=\frac{E+U}{N},\quad \text{EPOP}=\frac{E}{N}

Unemployment rate = unemployed / labor force, where labor force = employed + unemployed; Participation rate = labor force / working-age population; Employment-population ratio = employed / working-age population

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
EEmployed peoplemillions
UUnemployed: without work, available and actively lookingmillions
NWorking-age populationmillions

Worked example

The working-age population is 260 million, 158 million are employed and 7 million are unemployed. Suppose 3 million of the unemployed stop looking for work.

  1. Labor force = E + U158+7= 165.0
  2. Unemployment rate = U / labor force7 / 165= 4.24%
  3. Participation rate = labor force / population165 / 260= 63.46%
  4. Employment-population ratio = E / population158 / 260= 60.77%
  5. Unemployment rate after some stop looking(7-3) / (165-3)= 2.47%
  6. Participation rate after some stop looking(165-3) / 260= 62.31%

The labor force is 165.0. The unemployment rate is 4.24%, participation is 63.46% and the employment-population ratio is 60.77%. If 3 million stop looking, unemployment falls to 2.47% even though no one found a job, and participation drops to 62.31%.
