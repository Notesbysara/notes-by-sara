Rate Expectations from Policy Rate Futures

Intermediate

Also known as: how markets react to economic data, FedWatch, implied policy rate, probability of a rate hike

The odds markets assign to a policy move. Use it to read what the market expects from the central bank. The futures price embeds an average expected rate for the month, and removing the days before the meeting isolates the post-meeting rate. A data release that changes the probability moves bonds and currencies. Assumes only two outcomes (no move or one move of the stated size), and ignores small risk premiums.

The formula

iimpl=100−P,rnew=iimplD−r0dD−d,p=rnew−r0Δi_{impl}=100-P,\quad r_{new}=\frac{i_{impl}D-r_{0}d}{D-d},\quad p=\frac{r_{new}-r_{0}}{\Delta}

Implied average rate for the month = 100 - futures price; Rate after the meeting = (implied rate x days in month - current rate x days before the meeting) / days after the meeting; Probability of a move = (rate after meeting - current rate) / size of the move

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
PPrice of the policy-rate futures contract for the meeting monthprice
r_0Current policy ratepercent
D, dDays in the month and days before the meetingdays
DeltaSize of the possible rate move (for example 0.25)percent

Worked example

The futures price is 95.45 and the policy rate is 4.33%. The meeting is on day 12 of a 30-day month and the possible move is 0.25%.

  1. Implied average rate = 100 - price100-95.45= 4.550
  2. Expected rate after the meeting(4.55 x 30-4.33 x 12) / (30-12)= 4.697
  3. Probability of the move(4.69667-4.33) / 0.25= 147%

The implied monthly average is 4.550. Stripping out the days before the meeting, the market expects 4.697 afterward. That is a probability of 147% of a 0.25% hike.
