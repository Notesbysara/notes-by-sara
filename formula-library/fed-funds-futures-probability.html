Rate Expectations from Policy Rate Futures

IntermediateEconomics for FinanceAlso known as: how markets react to economic data, FedWatch, implied policy rate, probability of a rate hike
← RAROC and Risk Limit UtilizationRational Bubble: Required Growth and Survival →

Finds: the odds markets assign to a policy move.

When to use it: Use it to read what the market expects from the central bank. The futures price embeds an average expected rate for the month, and removing the days before the meeting isolates the post-meeting rate. A data release that changes the probability moves bonds and currencies. Assumes only two outcomes (no move or one move of the stated size), and ignores small risk premiums.

The formula

iimpl\displaystyle i_{impl}=100−P\displaystyle =100-Prnew\displaystyle r_{new}=iimplD−r0dD−d\displaystyle =\frac{i_{impl}D-r_{0}d}{D-d}p\displaystyle p=rnew−r0Δ\displaystyle =\frac{r_{new}-r_{0}}{\Delta}

In words: Implied average rate for the month = 100 − futures price; Rate after the meeting = (implied rate x days in month − current rate x days before the meeting) / days after the meeting; Probability of a move = (rate after meeting − current rate) / size of the move

What each symbol means

PPrice of the policy-rate futures contract for the meeting monthprice
r_0Current policy ratepercent
D, dDays in the month and days before the meetingdays
DeltaSize of the possible rate move (for example 0.25)percent

Worked example

The futures price is 95.45 and the policy rate is 4.33%. The meeting is on day 12 of a 30-day month and the possible move is 0.25%.

Implied average rate100 − 95.454.550
Expected rate after the meeting(4.55 × 30 − 4.33 × 12) ÷ (30 − 12)4.697
Probability of the move(4.69667 − 4.33) ÷ 0.25147%
The answer

The implied monthly average is 4.550. Stripping out the days before the meeting, the market expects 4.697 afterward. That is a probability of 147% of a 0.25% hike.
