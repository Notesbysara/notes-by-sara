Tail Risk: Cornish-Fisher VaR

IntermediateRisk ManagementAlso known as: skewness kurtosis adjusted VaR, modified VaR, fat tails, black swan, tail risk
← t-Statistic for a Mean (Hypothesis Test)Taylor Rule →

Finds: A VaR that allows for fat tails and skew.

When to use it: Use it to correct a normal VaR for fat tails and negative skew. Financial returns usually have both, so the normal VaR understates the true tail loss. This is an approximation that works for moderate skew and kurtosis. It cannot predict events with no precedent in the data, which is the black swan problem.

The formula

zCF\displaystyle z_{CF}=z+z2−16S+z3−3z24K−2z3−5z36S2\displaystyle =z+\frac{z^{2}-1}{6}S+\frac{z^{3}-3z}{24}K-\frac{2z^{3}-5z}{36}S^{2}

In words: Adjusted z = z + (z^2 − 1) x Skew / 6 + (z^3 − 3z) x Excess kurtosis / 24 − (2z^3 − 5z) x Skew^2 / 36; VaR = V x (adjusted z x sd − mean)

What each symbol means

zNormal z-score for the confidence levelnumber
SSkewness of returns, negative when big losses are more common than big gainsnumber
KExcess kurtosis, how much fatter the tails are than a normal distributionnumber
VPortfolio valuemoney

Worked example

A portfolio of 1,000,000 has daily returns with a mean of 0.03% and a standard deviation of 1.2%, a skewness of −0.8 and an excess kurtosis of 3.0. What is the 99% one-day VaR with and without the adjustment?

Normal z-score2.326
Cornish-Fisher z2.32635 + (2.32635^2 − 1) × −0.8 ÷ 6 + (2.32635^3 − 3 × 2.32635) × 3 ÷ 24 − (2 × 2.32635^3 − 5 × 2.32635) × −0.8^2 ÷ 362.199
Normal VaR1,000,000 × (2.32635 × sd − 0.0003)27,616.17
Adjusted VaR1,000,000 × (2.1986 × sd − 0.0003)26,083.23
Increase26,083.2 ÷ 27,616.2 − 1−5.6%
The answer

The normal 99% VaR is 27,616.17. With the skew and kurtosis adjustment the z-score rises from 2.326 to 2.199, and the VaR becomes 26,083.23, −5.6% higher. The normal model understates the tail.
