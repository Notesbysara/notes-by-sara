Age-Based Asset Allocation and Portfolio Risk

Beginner

Also known as: asset allocation by age and goals, 110 minus age, glide path, equity share

A starting stock and bond mix for your age, with its expected return and risk. Use it to turn a rule of thumb into numbers. The idea is that you hold less in equities as retirement nears, because there is less time to recover from a fall. The rule is a starting point, not advice: your goals, other income, risk tolerance and time horizon matter more than age. The return and risk inputs are assumptions you should set yourself.

The formula

weq=B−age100,σp=w2σe2+(1−w)2σb2+2w(1−w)ρσeσbw_{eq}=\frac{B-\text{age}}{100},\quad \sigma_{p}=\sqrt{w^{2}\sigma_{e}^{2}+(1-w)^{2}\sigma_{b}^{2}+2w(1-w)\rho\sigma_{e}\sigma_{b}}

Equity share = (base number - age) / 100, kept between 0% and 100%; Portfolio return = equity share x equity return + (1 - equity share) x bond return; Portfolio risk uses the two-asset variance formula

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
BBase number in the rule of thumb (100, 110 or 120, higher for more risk)number
wShare held in equitiespercent
sigma_e, sigma_bVolatility of equities and of bondsper year
rhoCorrelation between themnumber

Worked example

A 35-year-old uses the 110 minus age rule. Equities are assumed to return 7% with 17% volatility, bonds 3.5% with 6% volatility, and their correlation is 0.2.

  1. Equity share = (base - age) / 100, limited to 0 to 100%min(max((110-35) / 100, 0), 1)= 75.0%
  2. Bond share1-0.75= 25.0%
  3. Expected return0.75 x 0.07+0.25 x 0.035= 6.13%
  4. Volatilitysqrt(0.75^2 x 0.17^2+0.25^2 x 0.06^2+2 x 0.75 x 0.25 x 0.2 x 0.17 x 0.06)= 13.13%
  5. Equity share at age 65min(max((110-65) / 100, 0), 1)= 45.0%

The rule gives 75.0% in equities and 25.0% in bonds. The portfolio's expected return is 6.13% with volatility of 13.13%. At age 65 the same rule would hold 45.0% in equities.
