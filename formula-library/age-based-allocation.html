Age-Based Asset Allocation and Portfolio Risk

BeginnerPersonal FinanceAlso known as: asset allocation by age and goals, 110 minus age, glide path, equity share
← After-Tax Salvage ValueAI Fraud Model: Net Value of Alerts →

Finds: A starting stock and bond mix for your age, with its expected return and risk.

When to use it: Use it to turn a rule of thumb into numbers. The idea is that you hold less in equities as retirement nears, because there is less time to recover from a fall. The rule is a starting point, not advice: your goals, other income, risk tolerance and time horizon matter more than age. The return and risk inputs are assumptions you should set yourself.

The formula

weq\displaystyle w_{eq}=B−age100\displaystyle =\frac{B-\text{age}}{100}σp\displaystyle \sigma_{p}=w2σe2+(1−w)2σb2+2w(1−w)ρσeσb\displaystyle =\sqrt{w^{2}\sigma_{e}^{2}+(1-w)^{2}\sigma_{b}^{2}+2w(1-w)\rho\sigma_{e}\sigma_{b}}

In words: Equity share = (base number − age) / 100, kept between 0% and 100%; Portfolio return = equity share x equity return + (1 − equity share) x bond return; Portfolio risk uses the two-asset variance formula

What each symbol means

BBase number in the rule of thumb (100, 110 or 120, higher for more risk)number
wShare held in equitiespercent
sigma_e, sigma_bVolatility of equities and of bondsper year
rhoCorrelation between themnumber

Worked example

A 35-year-old uses the 110 minus age rule. Equities are assumed to return 7% with 17% volatility, bonds 3.5% with 6% volatility, and their correlation is 0.2.

Equity sharemin(max((110 − 35) ÷ 100, 0), 1)75%
Bond share1 − 0.7525%
Expected return0.75 × 0.07 + 0.25 × 0.0356.13%
Volatilitysqrt(0.75^2 × 0.17^2 + 0.25^2 × 0.06^2 + 2 × 0.75 × 0.25 × 0.2 × 0.17 × 0.06)13.13%
Equity share at age 65min(max((110 − 65) ÷ 100, 0), 1)45%
The answer

The rule gives 75.0% in equities and 25.0% in bonds. The portfolio's expected return is 6.13% with volatility of 13.13%. At age 65 the same rule would hold 45.0% in equities.
