Minimum-Variance Hedge Ratio

IntermediateCorporate FinanceAlso known as: hedge ratio, number of futures contracts, optimal hedge
← Merton Credit Model (Equity as a Call on Assets)Minimum-Variance Portfolio (Two Assets) →

Finds: how much to hedge and with how many contracts.

When to use it: Use it to size a hedge when the hedging instrument is not a perfect match for the exposure. A lower correlation means the hedge removes less of the risk.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

h∗\displaystyle h^{*}=ρ σSσF\displaystyle =\rho\,\frac{\sigma_{S}}{\sigma_{F}}N\displaystyle N=h∗×ExposureContract size\displaystyle =h^{*}\times\frac{\text{Exposure}}{\text{Contract size}}

In words: Hedge ratio h = correlation x (Volatility of the exposure / Volatility of the hedge); Contracts N = h x Exposure / Contract size

What each symbol means

h*Hedge ratio, the fraction of the exposure to hedgenumber
rhoCorrelation between changes in the exposure and in the hedge instrumentnumber
sigma_SVolatility of the price changes of the exposurepercent
sigma_FVolatility of the price changes of the hedge instrumentpercent
ExposureValue of what is being hedgedmoney
Contract sizeValue covered by one contractmoney

Worked example

A company has an exposure of 5,000,000. The hedge instrument has a correlation of 0.9 with the exposure. The exposure's volatility is 24% and the hedge instrument's is 20%. One contract covers 100,000. How many contracts should it use?

h0.9 × 0.24 ÷ 0.21.080
Contracts1.08 × exp ÷ 100,00054
The answer

The hedge ratio is 1.080, so the company should use about 54.0 contracts. Hedging reduces risk but does not remove it, because the correlation is below 1.
