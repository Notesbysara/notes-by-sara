Grant Element of a Concessional Loan

Intermediate

Also known as: development banks, multilateral lending, concessionality, subsidy in a soft loan

How much of a soft loan is a grant. Use it to measure how much of a below-market loan is effectively a grant. The lower the interest rate relative to the market rate, the longer the maturity and the larger the grace period, the bigger the subsidy. Development agencies often count a loan as aid only if the grant element is above a threshold such as 25%. The discount rate is a policy choice, so results differ by lender.

The formula

GE=F−PVF,PV=∑tc F(1+d)t+F(1+d)NGE=\frac{F-PV}{F},\quad PV=\sum_{t}\frac{c\,F}{(1+d)^{t}}+\frac{F}{(1+d)^{N}}

Grant element = (face value of the loan - present value of its repayments discounted at a commercial rate) / face value. For a loan that pays interest each year and the principal at the end: PV = coupon x annuity factor + face / (1 + d)^N

What each symbol means

FLoan amountmoney
cInterest rate on the concessional loanpercent
dCommercial discount rate: what the borrower would pay in the marketpercent

Worked example

A development bank lends 100 for 20 years at 2% with the principal paid at the end. The commercial rate used for comparison is 10%.

  1. PV of repayments = interest annuity + principal100 x 0.02 x (1-(1+0.1)^(-20)) / 0.1+100 / (1+0.1)^20= 31.89
  2. Grant element = (F - PV) / F(100-31.8915) / 100= 68.1%
  3. Value of the subsidy = F - PV100-31.8915= 68.11
  4. Grant element at the market rate (check = 0)(100-(100 x 0.1 x (1-(1+0.1)^(-20)) / 0.1+100 / (1+0.1)^20)) / 100= 0.0000

The present value of the repayments at 10% is 31.89, so the grant element is 68.1%. The borrower receives about 68.11 of subsidy on each 100 borrowed. A market-rate loan at 10% would have a grant element of 0.0000.
