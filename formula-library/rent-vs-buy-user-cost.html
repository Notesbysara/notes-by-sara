Rent vs Buy: Annual Cost of Owning

Intermediate

Also known as: renting vs buying a home, user cost of housing, price to rent ratio, break even rent

Whether owning costs more or less than renting, per year. Use it for a first comparison of renting and buying. Owning costs interest, tax, upkeep and the return lost on the down payment, offset by price growth. If the annual cost of owning is below annual rent, buying looks cheaper, but price growth is uncertain. It leaves out buying and selling costs, tax relief and how long you will stay, which matter a lot over short stays.

The formula

Cost of owning=P[i(1−d)+o d+τ+m−g],Price-to-rent=P12R\text{Cost of owning}=P\left[i(1-d)+o\,d+\tau+m-g\right],\quad \text{Price-to-rent}=\frac{P}{12R}

Annual cost of owning = Price x [mortgage rate x (1 - down payment share) + opportunity return x down payment share + property tax rate + maintenance rate - price growth rate]; Price-to-rent ratio = Price / (12 x monthly rent)

What each symbol means

Worked example

A home costs 400,000 with 20% down at a 6.5% mortgage rate. Property tax is 1.1%, maintenance 1.0%, expected price growth 3% and the down payment could otherwise earn 5%. Comparable rent is 2,200 a month.

  1. Annual cost of owning = P x [i(1 - d) + o d + tax + maintenance - growth]400000 x (0.065 x (1-0.2)+0.05 x 0.2+0.011+0.01-0.03)= 21,200.00
  2. Per month21200 / 12= 1,766.67
  3. Annual rent12 x 2200= 26,400.00
  4. Price-to-rent ratio400000 / 26400= 15.2
  5. Owning cheaper than renting? (1 = yes, 0 = no)1 if 21200<26400 else 0= 1
  6. Break-even monthly rent = cost of owning / 1221200 / 12= 1,766.67
  7. Monthly cost of owning if growth is 1%400000 x (0.065 x (1-0.2)+0.05 x 0.2+0.011+0.01-0.01) / 12= 2,433.33

Owning costs 21,200.00 a year, or 1,766.67 a month, against rent of 26,400.00 a year. The price-to-rent ratio is 15.2. The break-even rent is 1,766.67 a month: if your rent is above that, buying looks cheaper. If prices grew only 1% the monthly cost of owning would be 2,433.33.
