Straddle and Strangle

IntermediateDerivativesAlso known as: long volatility, option strategies, straddle breakeven, strangle breakeven
← Stock-Based Compensation Adjusted Free Cash FlowStraight-Line Depreciation →

Finds: profit and breakevens for a bet on a big move.

When to use it: Use it when you expect a big move but not its direction. Both strategies lose the premium if the price stays near the strikes, and profit from a large move either way. A strangle costs less than a straddle but needs a bigger move.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Straddle profit\displaystyle \text{Straddle profit}=∣ST−K∣−(C+P)\displaystyle =|S_{T}-K|-(C+P)

In words: Straddle (buy a call and a put at the same strike): Profit = |ST − K| − total premium; Strangle (put at a lower strike, call at a higher strike): Profit = max(Kp − ST, 0) + max(ST − Kc, 0) − total premium

What each symbol means

KStrike of both options in a straddlemoney
K_p, K_cStrikes of the put and call in a strangle, with Kp below Kcmoney
C, PPremiums paidmoney
S_TPrice at expirymoney

Worked example

A stock is at 100. A straddle at strike 100 costs 5 for the call and 4 for the put. A strangle with a 95 put and 105 call costs 1.80 and 2.50. The stock ends at 112. What are the profits and breakevens?

Straddle cost5 + 49
Straddle lower breakeven100 − 991
Straddle upper breakeven100 + 9109
Straddle profit at expiry3
Strangle cost2.5 + 1.84.30
Strangle lower breakeven95 − 4.390.70
Strangle upper breakeven105 + 4.3109.30
Strangle profit at expirymax(95 − 112, 0) + max(112 − 105, 0) − 4.32.70
The answer

The straddle costs 9 and its breakevens are 91 and 109. At 112 it earns 3. The strangle costs 4.30, with breakevens at 90.70 and 109.30, and earns 2.70.
