Straddle and Strangle

Intermediate

Also known as: long volatility, option strategies, straddle breakeven, strangle breakeven

Profit and breakevens for a bet on a big move. Use it when you expect a big move but not its direction. Both strategies lose the premium if the price stays near the strikes, and profit from a large move either way. A strangle costs less than a straddle but needs a bigger move.

The formula

Straddle profit=∣ST−K∣−(C+P)\text{Straddle profit}=|S_{T}-K|-(C+P)

Straddle (buy a call and a put at the same strike): Profit = |ST - K| - total premium; Strangle (put at a lower strike, call at a higher strike): Profit = max(Kp - ST, 0) + max(ST - Kc, 0) - total premium

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
KStrike of both options in a straddlemoney
K_p, K_cStrikes of the put and call in a strangle, with Kp below Kcmoney
C, PPremiums paidmoney
S_TPrice at expirymoney

Worked example

A stock is at 100. A straddle at strike 100 costs 5 for the call and 4 for the put. A strangle with a 95 put and 105 call costs 1.80 and 2.50. The stock ends at 112. What are the profits and breakevens?

  1. Straddle cost = call + put5+4= 9.00
  2. Straddle lower breakeven = K - cost100-9= 91.00
  3. Straddle upper breakeven = K + cost100+9= 109.00
  4. Straddle profit at expiry= 3.00
  5. Strangle cost2.5+1.8= 4.30
  6. Strangle lower breakeven = Kp - cost95-4.3= 90.70
  7. Strangle upper breakeven = Kc + cost105+4.3= 109.30
  8. Strangle profit at expirymax(95-112, 0)+max(112-105, 0)-4.3= 2.70

The straddle costs 9.00 and its breakevens are 91.00 and 109.00. At 112 it earns 3.00. The strangle costs 4.30, with breakevens at 90.70 and 109.30, and earns 2.70.
