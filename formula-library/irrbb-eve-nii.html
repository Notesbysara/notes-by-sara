Banking Book Interest Rate Risk: EVE and NII

IntermediateRisk ManagementAlso known as: duration gap, repricing gap, economic value of equity, net interest income sensitivity
← Bank Run: Liquidity Shortfall and Fire-Sale LossBarrier Option Payoff →

Finds: A bank's value and earnings sensitivity to rates.

When to use it: Use it to see how a bank's earnings and value react to rates. A bank that funds long assets with short liabilities loses value when rates rise (duration gap) and loses earnings if liabilities reprice before assets (repricing gap). Regulators call this IRRBB.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

ΔEVE≈−(DAA−DLL)Δy1+y\displaystyle \Delta EVE\approx-(D_{A}A-D_{L}L)\frac{\Delta y}{1+y}ΔNII\displaystyle \Delta NII=(RSA−RSL) Δr\displaystyle =(RSA-RSL)\,\Delta r

In words: Change in economic value of equity = -(Asset duration x Assets − Liability duration x Liabilities) x Rate change / (1 + yield); Change in net interest income = (Rate-sensitive assets − Rate-sensitive liabilities) x Rate change

What each symbol means

D_A, D_LDurations of assets and liabilitiesyears
A, LMarket values of assets and liabilitiesmoney
RSA, RSLAssets and liabilities that reprice within the periodmoney
Delta yChange in interest ratesper year

Worked example

A bank has assets of 1,000 (duration 3.5) and liabilities of 900 (duration 1.5), so equity is 100. The yield is 4%. Within one year 400 of assets and 600 of liabilities reprice. Rates rise by 1 percentage point.

Equity1,000 − 900100
Duration gap in money3.5 × 1,000 − 1.5 × 9002,150
Change in EVE−2,150 × 0.01 ÷ (1 + 0.04)−20.67
As a share of equity−20.6731 ÷ 100−20.7%
Repricing gap400 − 600−200
Change in NII−200 × 0.01−2
The answer

The duration gap makes economic value fall by −20.67, which is −20.7% of equity. The repricing gap is −200.0, so net interest income falls by −2 in the first year.
