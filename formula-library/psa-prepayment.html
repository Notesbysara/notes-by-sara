PSA Prepayment Speed, CPR and SMM

Intermediate

Also known as: PSA benchmark, conditional prepayment rate, single monthly mortality, prepayment risk, extension risk, contraction risk

Prepayment speed in annual and monthly terms. Use it to describe how fast borrowers prepay. The benchmark starts at 0.2% CPR in month one and rises 0.2 points each month to 6% CPR at month 30, then stays there. Faster speeds shorten the life of the security (contraction risk) and slower speeds lengthen it (extension risk).

The formula

CPR=PSA×6%×min⁡(t30,1),SMM=1−(1−CPR)1/12CPR=\text{PSA}\times 6\%\times\min\left(\frac{t}{30},1\right),\quad SMM=1-(1-CPR)^{1/12}

CPR = PSA speed x 6% x min(month / 30, 1); SMM = 1 - (1 - CPR)^(1/12)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
PSASpeed as a multiple of the standard benchmark, where 100% PSA is the benchmarkpercent
tMonths since the mortgages were made (age of the pool)months
CPRConditional prepayment rate, the annualized share of the pool prepaidper year
SMMSingle monthly mortality, the monthly version of CPRper month

Worked example

A pool is 12 months old and is prepaying at 150% PSA. What are the CPR and SMM?

  1. Benchmark CPR = 6% x min(t / 30, 1)0.06 x min(12 / 30, 1)= 2.40%
  2. CPR = PSA x benchmark1.5 x 0.024= 3.60%
  3. SMM = 1 - (1 - CPR)^(1/12)1-(1-0.036)^(1 / 12)= 0.305%
  4. Level CPR after month 30 = PSA x 6%1.5 x 0.06= 9.00%

At month 12 the benchmark CPR is 2.40%. At 150% PSA the CPR is 3.60% and the SMM is 0.305%. By month 30 the CPR would level out at 9.00%.
