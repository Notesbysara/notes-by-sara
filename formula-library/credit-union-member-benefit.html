Credit Union Member Benefit and Net Worth Ratio

Beginner

Also known as: credit unions, cooperative bank, member dividend, net worth ratio

What membership is worth and how strong the credit union's capital is. Use it to see what member ownership is worth. A credit union is owned by its members and not by outside shareholders, so profits go back as lower loan rates and higher savings rates. The net worth ratio shows whether it has enough capital, which it can build only by retaining earnings, since it cannot sell shares. Membership rules, product range and branch access also matter.

The formula

Benefit=(ibank−icu)L+(dcu−dbank)S,Net worth ratio=Net worthAssets\text{Benefit}=(i_{bank}-i_{cu})L+(d_{cu}-d_{bank})S,\quad \text{Net worth ratio}=\frac{\text{Net worth}}{\text{Assets}}

Yearly member benefit = (bank loan rate - credit union loan rate) x loans + (credit union savings rate - bank savings rate) x savings; Net worth ratio = net worth / total assets (7% or more counts as well capitalized in the US)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
L, SMember loans and savingsmoney
i_bank, i_cuLoan rates at a bank and at the credit unionpercent

Worked example

A member has a 20,000 loan at 6.5% (a bank charges 8.0%) and 10,000 of savings at 3.0% (a bank pays 1.0%). The credit union has net worth of 8.0 million on assets of 100 million.

  1. Saving on the loan(0.08-0.065) x 20000= 300.00
  2. Extra earned on savings(0.03-0.01) x 10000= 200.00
  3. Total yearly member benefit300+200= 500.00
  4. Net worth ratio8 / 100= 8.0%
  5. Well capitalized? (1 = yes, 0 = no)1 if 0.08>=0.07 else 0= 1

The member saves 300.00 on the loan and earns 200.00 more on savings, a total benefit of 500.00 a year. The net worth ratio is 8.0%, which is 1 (1 = at or above the 7% well-capitalized level, 0 = below).
