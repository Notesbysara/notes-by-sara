Credit Union Member Benefit and Net Worth Ratio

BeginnerBanking & Financial InstitutionsAlso known as: credit unions, cooperative bank, member dividend, net worth ratio
← Credit Spread and Spread DurationCredit Utilization Ratio →

Finds: what membership is worth and how strong the credit union's capital is.

When to use it: Use it to see what member ownership is worth. A credit union is owned by its members and not by outside shareholders, so profits go back as lower loan rates and higher savings rates. The net worth ratio shows whether it has enough capital, which it can build only by retaining earnings, since it cannot sell shares. Membership rules, product range and branch access also matter.

The formula

Benefit\displaystyle \text{Benefit}=(ibank−icu)L+(dcu−dbank)S\displaystyle =(i_{bank}-i_{cu})L+(d_{cu}-d_{bank})SNet worth ratio\displaystyle \text{Net worth ratio}=Net worthAssets\displaystyle =\frac{\text{Net worth}}{\text{Assets}}

In words: Yearly member benefit = (bank loan rate − credit union loan rate) x loans + (credit union savings rate − bank savings rate) x savings; Net worth ratio = net worth / total assets (7% or more counts as well capitalized in the US)

Worked example

A member has a 20,000 loan at 6.5% (a bank charges 8.0%) and 10,000 of savings at 3.0% (a bank pays 1.0%). The credit union has net worth of 8.0 million on assets of 100 million.

Saving on the loan(0.08 − 0.065) × 20,000300
Extra earned on savings(0.03 − 0.01) × 10,000200
Total yearly member benefit300 + 200500
Net worth ratio8 ÷ 1008%
Well capitalized? (11 if 0.08>=0.07 else 01
The answer

The member saves 300 on the loan and earns 200 more on savings, a total benefit of 500 a year. The net worth ratio is 8.0%, which is 1 (1 = at or above the 7% well-capitalized level, 0 = below).
