Asset Manager Revenue and Fee Sensitivity

Beginner

Also known as: asset management firms, AUM growth, fee compression, operating margin

How AUM, fees and margin drive profit. Use it to see what drives an asset manager's earnings. Revenue rises with markets and net inflows, and falls with fee cuts, since costs are largely fixed, so profit moves more than revenue. The shift to cheaper passive funds has been compressing fee rates for years, so the fee sensitivity is worth checking.

The formula

AUM1=AUM0(1+r)+F,Revenue=AUMˉ×f,Profit=Revenue×mAUM_{1}=AUM_{0}(1+r)+F,\quad \text{Revenue}=\bar{AUM}\times f,\quad \text{Profit}=\text{Revenue}\times m

Ending AUM = starting AUM x (1 + market return) + net flows; Revenue = average AUM x fee rate; Operating profit = revenue x operating margin

Worked example

A manager starts with 50 billion, the market returns 6% and net flows are +1.5 billion. The average fee is 0.45% and the operating margin 35%. Test a cut to 0.40%.

  1. Ending AUM50 x (1+0.06)+1.5= 54.50
  2. AUM growth54.5 / 50-1= 9.0%
  3. Average AUM(50+54.5) / 2= 52.25
  4. Revenue = average AUM x fee52.25 x 0.0045= 0.235
  5. Operating profit = revenue x margin0.235125 x 0.35= 0.082
  6. Revenue at the lower fee52.25 x 0.004= 0.209
  7. Profit at the lower fee, with costs unchanged = new revenue - old costs0.209-0.235125 x (1-0.35)= 0.056
  8. Fall in profit0.0561687 / 0.0822937-1= -31.7%

Ending AUM is 54.50, up 9.0%, and average AUM is 52.25. Revenue is 0.235 and operating profit 0.082. If the fee rate fell to 0.40% with costs unchanged, revenue would be 0.209 and profit 0.056, a fall of -31.7% in profit.
