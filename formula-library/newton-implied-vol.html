Newton's Method for Implied Volatility

Intermediate

Also known as: Newton Raphson, root finding, iterative solution, optimization methods

How an iterative solver closes in on an answer. Use it to see how a numerical solver finds an answer that has no formula. Newton's method follows the slope: from the current guess, move to where the tangent line would hit the target. It usually settles in three to five steps. The same idea drives optimization, where it is applied to the derivative.

The formula

σn+1=σn−BS(σn)−PriceV(σn)\sigma_{n+1}=\sigma_{n}-\frac{BS(\sigma_{n})-\text{Price}}{\mathcal{V}(\sigma_{n})}

Next guess = Current guess - (Model price at the guess - Market price) / Vega at the guess

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
sigma_nCurrent guess for the volatilityper year
BS()Black-Scholes price at that volatilitymoney
VegaSensitivity of the option price to volatility, used as the slopemoney

Worked example

A call (stock 100, strike 100, 1 year, rate 5%, no dividend) costs 12.50. Start from a guess of 30% volatility and do three Newton steps.

  1. Starting guess0.3= 30.0000%
  2. Step 1: guess - (price at guess - target) / vega= 25.4373%
  3. Step 2= 25.4333%
  4. Step 3= 25.4333%
  5. Size of the last change= 0.0000%
  6. Price at the final guess= 12.50

The guesses go 30.0000% to 25.4373%, then 25.4333% and 25.4333%. The last step changed the answer by only 0.0000%, and the price at the final guess is 12.50, matching the 12.50 target.
