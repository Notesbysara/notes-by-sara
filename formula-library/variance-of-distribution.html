Variance of a Probability Distribution

Intermediate

Also known as: scenario variance, standard deviation of expected return, risk across scenarios

Risk measured across scenarios. Use it to measure risk when you have scenarios with probabilities, rather than a history of past returns. It uses probability weights, so it divides by nothing.

The formula

σ2=∑i=1npi (xi−E(X))2\sigma^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\,(x_{i}-E(X))^{2}

Variance = sum of pi x (xi - E(X))^2

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
sigma^2Variance of the outcomespercent squared
p_iProbability of outcome i, with all probabilities adding to 1number
x_iValue of outcome ipercent
E(X)Expected value, the probability-weighted averagepercent

Worked example

A stock could return 20% in a boom (probability 0.3), 8% in a normal year (0.5) or -10% in a recession (0.2). What are its variance and standard deviation?

  1. Expected value = sum of p x outcome= 8.00%
  2. Deviation of each outcome from the expected value= 12.00%, 0.00%, -18.00%
  3. Square each deviation= 0.0144, 0.0000, 0.0324
  4. Variance = sum of p x squared deviation= 0.0108
  5. Standard deviation = square root of variancesqrt(var)= 10.39%

The expected return is 8.00% and the standard deviation is 10.39%.
