Break-Even Analysis

Beginner

Also known as: break even units, accounting break-even, contribution margin, break-even point

Sales needed to cover costs. Use it to find the sales volume at which the project makes neither a profit nor a loss. Compare it with your realistic sales forecast to see how much room for error there is.

The formula

Q∗=FCP−VCQ^{*}=\frac{FC}{P-VC}

Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost per unit)

What each symbol means

SymbolMeaningUnit
Q*Number of units that must be sold to cover all costsunits
FCFixed costs for the periodmoney
PSelling price per unitmoney per unit
VCVariable cost per unitmoney per unit

Worked example

A product sells for 25. It costs 15 per unit to make and the company has fixed costs of 50,000 a year. How many units must it sell to break even, and what if it expects to sell 7,000?

  1. Contribution margin per unit = P - VC25-15= 10.00
  2. Break-even units = FC / contribution margin50000 / 10= 5,000
  3. Break-even sales = break-even units x P5000 x 25= 125,000.00
  4. Profit at expected sales = (P - VC) x Q - FC10 x 7000-50000= 20,000.00
  5. Margin of safety = (Q - break-even) / Q(7000-5000) / 7000= 28.57%

Break-even is 5,000 units, or 125,000.00 in sales. At 7,000 units the profit is 20,000.00, a margin of safety of 28.57%.
