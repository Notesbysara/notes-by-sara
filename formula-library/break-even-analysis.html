Break-Even Analysis

BeginnerCorporate FinanceAlso known as: break even units, accounting break-even, contribution margin, break-even point
← Bootstrapping Spot Rates from Par YieldsBrownian Motion Probabilities →

Finds: sales needed to cover costs.

When to use it: Use it to find the sales volume at which the project makes neither a profit nor a loss. Compare it with your realistic sales forecast to see how much room for error there is.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Q∗\displaystyle Q^{*}=FCP−VC\displaystyle =\frac{FC}{P-VC}

In words: Break-even quantity = Fixed costs / (Price − Variable cost per unit)

What each symbol means

Q*Number of units that must be sold to cover all costsunits
FCFixed costs for the periodmoney
PSelling price per unitmoney per unit
VCVariable cost per unitmoney per unit

Worked example

A product sells for 25. It costs 15 per unit to make and the company has fixed costs of 50,000 a year. How many units must it sell to break even, and what if it expects to sell 7,000?

Contribution margin per unit25 − 1510
Break-even units50,000 ÷ 105,000
Break-even sales5,000 × 25125,000
Profit at expected sales10 × 7,000 − 50,00020,000
Margin of safety(7,000 − 5,000) ÷ 7,00028.57%
The answer

Break-even is 5,000 units, or 125,000 in sales. At 7,000 units the profit is 20,000, a margin of safety of 28.57%.
