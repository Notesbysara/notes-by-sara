Traditional vs Roth Account After Tax

BeginnerPersonal FinanceAlso known as: tax-advantaged investing, pre-tax vs after-tax contributions, Roth account, tax deferred
← Trade-Off Theory Firm ValueTranche Loss Allocation (Attachment and Detachment) →

Finds: which tax treatment leaves more money after tax.

When to use it: Use it to compare saving before tax with saving after tax. The growth factor is the same in both, so the answer depends only on whether the tax rate now is above or below the rate in retirement: pay tax at the lower rate. If the rates are equal, the two are the same. Real accounts differ by country, with limits, match rules and withdrawal rules, so this shows the principle, not a prediction.

FormulaExampleCalculator

The formula

Traditional\displaystyle \text{Traditional}=C(1+g)n(1−tw)\displaystyle =C(1+g)^{n}(1-t_{w})Roth\displaystyle \text{Roth}=C(1−t0)(1+g)n\displaystyle =C(1-t_{0})(1+g)^{n}

In words: Traditional (tax-deferred) after-tax value = Pre-tax contribution x (1 + growth)^n x (1 − tax rate at withdrawal); Roth (tax-free) value = Pre-tax contribution x (1 − tax rate now) x (1 + growth)^n

What each symbol means

CPre-tax amount you could contributemoney
t_0Tax rate nowpercent
t_wTax rate when you withdrawpercent
gAnnual growthper year
nYears investedyears

Worked example

You can put 5,000 before tax into either account. Your tax rate is 24% now and you expect 18% in retirement. The account grows at 6% for 30 years.

Traditional value before tax5,000 × (1 + 0.06)^3028,717.46
Traditional after withdrawal tax28,717.5 × (1 − 0.18)23,548.31
Amount invested in the Roth5,000 × (1 − 0.24)3,800
Roth value3,800 × (1 + 0.06)^3021,825.27
Traditional minus Roth23,548.3 − 21,825.31,723.05
Better option (11 if 23,548.3>=21,825.3 else 21
Withdrawal tax rate at which they tie0.2424%
The answer

The traditional account grows to 28,717.46 before tax, or 23,548.31 after a withdrawal tax of 18%. The Roth account starts with 3,800 after tax and grows to 21,825.27. Traditional minus Roth is 1,723.05, so a negative number means the Roth is ahead; the better option is number 1 (1 = traditional, 2 = Roth). The two tie if the tax rate at withdrawal is 24.0%.
