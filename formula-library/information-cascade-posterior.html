Herding: Information Cascade Posterior

Intermediate

Also known as: herding, information cascade, follow the crowd, Bayesian updating from others

How strongly the crowd should sway you. Use it to see how herding can be rational yet fragile. Early actions are informative, but once the lead is big enough later traders ignore their own information, so a cascade rests on only a few real signals and can reverse on small news. The model assumes every earlier action reflects one independent signal, which is why it is a stylized picture.

The formula

P(good∣d)=qdqd+(1−q)d,d=buys−sellsP(\text{good}\mid d)=\frac{q^{d}}{q^{d}+(1-q)^{d}},\quad d=\text{buys}-\text{sells}

If each earlier trader acted on a private signal that is right with probability q, the probability the asset is good after observing d more buys than sells = q^d / (q^d + (1 - q)^d). When d reaches 2, your own opposing signal cannot change the decision, so it is rational to follow the crowd, and the crowd stops adding information

What each symbol means

qAccuracy of each private signalpercent

P(good)Probability that the asset is good, given what you have seenpercent

Worked example

Each trader's signal is right 70% of the time. You see 4 buys and 1 sell. You get a private signal that says sell.

  1. Net lead = buys - sells4-1= 3
  2. P(good) from the crowd0.7^3 / (0.7^3+(1-0.7)^3)= 92.7%
  3. P(good) after your own sell signal0.7^(3-1) / (0.7^(3-1)+(1-0.7)^(3-1))= 84.5%
  4. Still above 50%? (1 = yes)1 if 0.844828>0.5 else 0= 1
  5. Cascade flag (lead of 2 or more)1 if 3>=2 else 0= 1

The net lead is 3. The probability that the asset is good, given the crowd, is 92.7%. If you add your own sell signal the probability is 84.5%, still 1 (1 = above 50%, so buying remains the better choice, 0 = below). A cascade starts at a lead of 2, so the flag is 1.
