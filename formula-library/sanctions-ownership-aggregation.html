Sanctions: 50% Ownership Aggregation Rule

BeginnerRegulation, Ethics & ESGAlso known as: financial sanctions, OFAC 50 percent rule, blocked persons, ownership screening
← Sample Size for Estimating a MeanSDR Basket Valuation →

Finds: whether an entity counts as sanctioned through its owners.

When to use it: Use it to screen customers and counterparties. A company with no sanctioned person above 50% can still be blocked if two sanctioned owners together reach 50%. This rule covers ownership only; control without ownership can also matter, and other regimes (EU, UK, UN) apply their own tests that should be checked.

In words: Under the US Treasury (OFAC) 50 percent rule, an entity owned 50% or more in total by one or more sanctioned persons is itself treated as sanctioned; Add up the stakes of all sanctioned owners (each stake alone can be below 50%); Total = sum of the stakes of blocked owners; Check whether total is 50% or more

Worked example

A company has three owners: a sanctioned person with 30%, another sanctioned person with 25%, and an unsanctioned investor with 15%. The rest is widely held.

The answer

The sanctioned owners together hold 55.0%. That is 5.0% above or below the 50% line, so the company is 1 (1 = treated as blocked, 0 = not). The largest single sanctioned stake is 30.0%, which alone would not trigger the rule.
