Averaging returns

The usual average works for heights and prices. For returns that compound, it overstates how much you actually made. The geometric mean tells the truth.

Describing data · Lesson 2 of 8 · 5 minutesBefore this: Mean, median and mode, Exponents

Up 50%, down 50%

Example, with made-up numbers. A fund rises 50% one year and falls 50% the next. The average of +50% and −50% is 0%. So did you break even?

The idea

Follow $100 through it. Up 50% takes it to $150. Down 50% of $150 takes it to $75. You lost a quarter of your money, even though the "average return" was zero.

The simple average says you'd still have $100 (dashed). You really have $75.

The fall happens on a bigger amount than the rise. Returns multiply, they don't add, so they need an average that multiplies: the geometric mean.

Geometric mean = ((1 + r1) × (1 + r2) × … × (1 + rn))1/n − 1

Turn each return into a growth factor (+50% is 1.50, −50% is 0.50), multiply them, take the nth root, then subtract 1. Here: (1.50 × 0.50)1/2 − 1 = 0.751/2 − 1 = −13.4% a year. Check: $100 × 0.866 × 0.866 = $75.

Quick check

A share doubles (+100%), then halves (−50%). What is its geometric mean return?

Worked example

Example, with made-up numbers. A fund returns +20%, −10%, +15% and +5% over four years. What did it really earn a year?

1. Growth factors1.20, 0.90, 1.15, 1.05
2. Multiply them1.20 × 0.90 × 1.15 × 1.05 = 1.3041
3. 4th root, then subtract 11.30411/4 − 1
Geometric mean return6.86% a year

The simple average is 7.50%. At 6.86% a year, $10,000 grows to $13,041, exactly what the fund delivered. At 7.50% it would have been $13,355, money the fund never made. The geometric mean is the same as the CAGR, the compound annual growth rate.

Which average to use

Looking back"How much did this investment earn a year?" Use the geometric mean. It matches the money you actually have.
Looking ahead"What return should I expect next year?" The simple average of past years is the usual starting estimate.

The simple average is always at least as big as the geometric mean. The more returns jump around, the bigger the gap, which is one reason volatility costs investors money.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Using the simple average to describe past growth. It overstates what you earned whenever returns go up and down.
  • Multiplying the returns instead of the growth factors. Multiply 1.20 × 0.90, not 0.20 × −0.10.
  • Forgetting to take the root. 1.3041 is the growth over four years. The yearly figure needs the 4th root.

Remember

  • Returns compound, so the simple average overstates real growth.
  • Geometric mean: multiply the growth factors (1 + r), take the nth root, subtract 1. It's the same as the CAGR.
  • Use it to describe past performance. The bigger the swings, the bigger the gap.
