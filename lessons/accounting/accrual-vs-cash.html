Accrual vs cash accounting

Cash accounting records money when it moves. Accrual accounting records revenue when it's earned and costs when they're used up. Companies report on the accrual basis, which is why profit and cash are not the same thing.

The system · Lesson 3 of 3 · 6 minutesBefore this: Double-entry bookkeeping

Did December make money?

Example, with made-up numbers. In December the coffee cart caters a $2,000 office party, but the office pays in January. It pays $1,200 rent in advance for December, January and February. Staff work in December but get paid in January. And customers buy $600 of gift cards to use next year. How much did the cart earn in December?

The idea

Under accrual accounting, two rules decide the timing:

Revenue when earnedCount the sale when the goods or service are delivered, whenever the cash arrives
Expenses when incurredCount a cost in the period it helps earn revenue. This is the matching principle

When cash and earning happen in different months, the gap is held on the balance sheet:

Circles show when cash moves. Green bars show when revenue is earned, gold bars when a cost is used up.

Accruals: earned or used first, cash laterAccrued revenue becomes a receivable (asset). Accrued expenses, like wages owed, become a payable (liability).
Deferrals: cash first, earned or used laterPrepaid rent is an asset until used. Cash received for gift cards is deferred revenue, a liability until the coffee is served.

Quick check

A company pays $1,200 on January 1 for a year of insurance. Under accrual accounting, what's January's insurance expense?

Worked example: December, two ways

The cart also sold $5,000 of coffee for cash and paid $1,500 for beans used in December. Wages for December were $900.

Cash basis: money in − money out(5,000 + 600) − (1,500 + 1,200) = 2,900
Accrual basis: revenue earned5,000 + 2,000 catering = 7,000
Expenses incurred1,500 beans + 400 rent + 900 wages = 2,800
Profit7,000 − 2,800 = 4,200
December profit, accrual vs cash$4,200 vs $2,900

The accrual figure shows what December really earned. The cash figure is lower because the catering money hasn't arrived and two months of rent were paid early. It also counted gift cards before any coffee was served.

At month end, adjusting entries move the amounts into the right period:

Adjusting entryDebitCredit
Catering earned, not yet paid
Dr Accounts receivable2,000
Cr Revenue2,000
December wages owed
Dr Wages expense900
Cr Wages payable900
One month of the prepaid rent used
Dr Rent expense400
Cr Prepaid rent400

The gift cards were recorded as Dr Cash 600, Cr Deferred revenue 600 when sold. They move to revenue as customers redeem them.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Treating profit as cash. A profitable company can run out of cash if customers pay slowly. Always look at the cash flow statement too.
  • Calling customer prepayments revenue. Cash received before delivery is deferred revenue, a liability: the company still owes the goods or service.
  • Forgetting period-end adjustments. Without them, wages owed and services delivered but not billed simply go missing from the period.

Remember

  • Accrual: revenue when earned, expenses when incurred, matched to the same period.
  • Accruals (earned or used first) and deferrals (cash first) bridge the timing gap on the balance sheet.
  • Adjusting entries at period end put each amount in the right period. Public companies must use accrual accounting.

    Show answer

  3. Write the entry for selling $700 of goods on credit (ignore the cost of the goods).

    Show answer

  4. Write the entry for paying a supplier $300 already owed.

    Show answer

  5. Which normally has a debit balance: accounts receivable, revenue or a bank loan?

    Show answer
    Double-entry bookkeepingAccounts receivable, an asset. Revenue and loans are credit balances.

  6. A $500 sale was posted to the wrong customer's account. Will the trial balance show the error?

    Show answer
    Double-entry bookkeepingNo. Debits still equal credits. The trial balance only catches unequal or one-sided entries.
