Solving for rate and time

The growth formula can answer more than "how much will I have?" Run it backward and it tells you how fast something grew, or how long a goal will take.

Growth and equations · Lesson 7 of 7 · 6 minutesBefore this: Exponents, Logarithms

How fast, and how long?

Example, with made-up numbers. Your savings grew from $5,000 to $8,000 in 6 years. What yearly rate is that? And if it keeps growing at that rate, when will you reach $12,000?

The idea

Compound growth has four parts. If you know any three, you can find the fourth:

FV = PV × (1 + r)n
To findUndo what's in the way
FV, the end amountNothing to undo. Just multiply.
PV, the start amountDivide by (1 + r)n.
r, the rateThe rate is inside a power, so take a root.
n, the timeThe time is the power itself, so use a logarithm.

At 8.15% a year, $5,000 becomes $8,000 in 6 years and reaches $12,000 after about 11.2 years.

Finding the rate

Divide to get the total growth factor. Then take the nth root to turn it into a yearly factor, and subtract 1:

r = (FV ÷ PV)1/n − 1
1. Total growth factor8,000 ÷ 5,000 = 1.6
2. Yearly factor (6th root)1.61/6 = 1.0815
3. Subtract 10.0815
Yearly growth rate8.15%

On a calculator, the 6th root is the power 1/6: type 1.6 ^ (1 ÷ 6). To check, 1.08156 ≈ 1.6.

Quick check

A price rose from $100 to $121 over 2 years. What was the yearly rate?

Finding the time

When the unknown is the power, logarithms bring it down. Take the log of the growth needed, and divide by the log of one year's growth:

n = ln(FV ÷ PV) ÷ ln(1 + r)

You now have $8,000, growing at 8.15%. How long until $12,000?

1. Growth needed12,000 ÷ 8,000 = 1.5
2. Log of the growth neededln 1.5 = 0.4055
3. Log of one year's growthln 1.0815 = 0.0783
4. Divide0.4055 ÷ 0.0783
Time needed5.2 years

Any log works, as long as you use the same one on top and bottom. log10 gives the same answer.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Dividing total growth by the years. 60% over 6 years is not 10% a year. Compounding means it's 8.15%.
  • Forgetting the "− 1". 1.61/6 = 1.0815 is the yearly factor. The rate is 0.0815, or 8.15%.
  • Mixing periods. A monthly rate gives a time in months. Keep the rate and the time in the same units.

Remember

  • Know three of PV, FV, r and n, and you can find the fourth.
  • To find the rate, take a root: r = (FV ÷ PV)1/n − 1.
  • To find the time, use logs: n = ln(FV ÷ PV) ÷ ln(1 + r).

Growth and equations
10 mixed problems from lessons 6 to 12 · about 15 minutes

The problems aren't labeled with a lesson, so part of the work is choosing the right tool. Work each one on paper, then open the answer.

  1. $1,000 grows at 6% a year for 3 years. What is it worth?

    Show answer
    Exponents1,000 × 1.063 = 1,000 × 1.1910 = $1,191.02

  2. How much do you need to invest today at 5% a year to have $10,000 in 4 years?

    Show answer
    Exponents, solving for the start amount10,000 ÷ 1.054 = 10,000 ÷ 1.2155 = $8,227.02

  3. At 10% a year, how many years does it take money to double?

    Show answer
    Logarithmsln 2 ÷ ln 1.10 = 0.6931 ÷ 0.0953 = 7.27 years. The rule of 72 gives 7.2, which is close.

  4. A café has fixed costs of $3,000 a month. Each coffee sells for $4 and costs $1.50 to make. How many coffees must it sell to break even?

    Show answer
    Linear equationsEach coffee covers 4 − 1.50 = $2.50 of fixed costs. 3,000 ÷ 2.50 = 1,200 coffees.

  5. A phone plan costs $20 a month plus $0.10 a minute. Write the cost as an equation, and find the cost of 150 minutes.

    Show answer
    Linear equationsCost = 20 + 0.10 × minutes. For 150 minutes: 20 + 15 = $35. The slope, $0.10, is the cost of each extra minute.

  6. Profit margin = Net income ÷ Revenue. A company's margin is 8% on revenue of $500,000. What is its net income?

    Show answer
    Reading formulasRearrange: Net income = Margin × Revenue = 0.08 × 500,000 = $40,000.

  7. f(x) = 1,000 × 1.05x. What is f(2), and what does it mean?

    Show answer
    Functions1,000 × 1.052 = $1,102.50: the balance after 2 years of $1,000 growing at 5%.

  8. A house bought for $250,000 sells for $400,000 ten years later. What was its yearly growth rate?

    Show answer
    Solving for rate400,000 ÷ 250,000 = 1.6, and 1.61/10 = 1.0481. That's 4.81% a year.

  9. A share went from $50 to $100 in 5 years. A friend says "that's 20% a year." Is that right?

    Show answer
    Solving for rateNo. 21/5 = 1.1487, so it's 14.87% a year. "20%" divides the 100% total by 5 years and ignores compounding.

  10. You have $15,000 and need $20,000. At 4% a year, how long will it take?

    Show answer
    Solving for timeln(20,000 ÷ 15,000) ÷ ln 1.04 = 0.2877 ÷ 0.0392 = 7.33 years.
