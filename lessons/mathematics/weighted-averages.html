Weighted averages

Portfolio returns, the average price you paid for a share, a company's cost of capital: all of them are weighted averages. Here's why a plain average gets them wrong.

Numbers and percentages · Lesson 5 of 5 · 5 minutesBefore this: Ratios and proportions

What did you really earn?

Example, with made-up numbers. You put $9,000 in Fund A, which returns 4%, and $1,000 in Fund B, which returns 20%. The average of 4% and 20% is 12%. Did your money really grow 12%?

The idea

A simple average treats every item as equally important. A weighted average lets each item count in proportion to its size. Here, Fund A holds 90% of your money, so its return should count nine times as much as Fund B's.

Fund A earns $360 (4% of $9,000) and Fund B earns $200 (20% of $1,000). That's $560 on $10,000, or 5.6%, not 12%.

The circles are sized by how much money each fund holds. The weighted average sits close to the bigger one, the way a seesaw balances near the heavier side.

The formula

Weighted average = w1x1 + w2x2 + …
weach item's weight: its share of the totalxeach item's value, such as its returnThe weights add up to 1 (100%)

The small numbers below the letters just label the items: item 1, item 2 and so on. For your funds: 0.90 × 4% + 0.10 × 20% = 3.6% + 2.0% = 5.6%.

Quick check

You buy 100 shares at $10 and later 300 shares at $14. What is your average price per share?

Worked example

Example, with made-up numbers. A $10,000 portfolio holds $6,000 in stocks returning 8%, $3,000 in bonds returning 3%, and $1,000 in cash earning 1%. What did the whole portfolio return?

Each weight is the holding divided by $10,000. The simple average of 8%, 3% and 1% would say 4%, which undersells the portfolio because most of it is in stocks.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Using a simple average when sizes differ. If the amounts aren't equal, the plain average is wrong.
  • Weights that don't add up to 100%. Check the total. If it's 90% or 110%, a weight is missing or counted twice.
  • Weighting by the wrong thing. Portfolio returns are weighted by money invested, not by the number of holdings. Margins are weighted by sales, not by the number of products.

Remember

  • A weighted average counts each item by its size.
  • Multiply each value by its weight, then add. The weights add up to 100%.
  • The result is pulled toward the biggest weight.

    Show answer

  10. Product A has sales of $10,000 at a 40% margin. Product B has sales of $30,000 at a 20% margin. What is the overall margin?

    Show answer
