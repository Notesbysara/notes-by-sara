Exponents

Compound interest, discounting and average growth rates are all written with exponents. This lesson covers the few rules finance actually uses.

Growth and equations · Lesson 1 of 7 · 6 minutesBefore this: Percentage traps

Make a guess

You invest $1,000 at 10% a year and leave it alone for 30 years. How much do you have at the end? A common guess is about $4,000: $1,000 plus $100 a year for 30 years. The real answer is $17,449.

The idea

Growing by 10% means multiplying by 1.10. Three years of growth is 1.10 × 1.10 × 1.10. An exponent is a short way to write that repeated multiplication: 1.10³, read "1.10 to the power of 3". The 1.10 is the base and the 3 is the exponent.

Each year's growth is applied to everything you already have, including last year's growth. Over 30 years, that adds up to 1.10³⁰ ≈ 17.45, so $1,000 becomes about $17,449.

On a calculator, use the xʸ or ^ key. In a spreadsheet, type =1.1^30.

Both lines start at $1,000. Adding $100 a year is a straight line. Multiplying by 1.10 every year curves upward, and the gap keeps widening.

Negative and fractional exponents

These look odd, but each one is a finance tool you'll use again and again.

Negative: go back in time1.05−3 = 1 ÷ 1.053A negative exponent means "divide by". It turns growth into discounting, which is how you find what future money is worth today.
Fractional: find the yearly rate1.211/2 = 1.10A power of ½ is a square root, ⅓ a cube root and so on. If something grew 21% over 2 years, it grew by an average of 10% a year.

Worked example

Example, with made-up numbers. A company's revenue grew from $50 million to $80 million over 5 years. What was its average yearly growth rate? Analysts call this the compound annual growth rate, or CAGR.

1. Total growth factor80 ÷ 50 = 1.6
2. Raise it to 1 ÷ years1.61/5 = 1.0986
3. Subtract 11.0986 − 1 = 0.0986
Average yearly growth9.86%

Check it: 1.0986⁵ ≈ 1.6, and 50 × 1.6 = 80. Simply dividing the 60% total by 5 years would say 12%, which overstates the yearly rate.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Multiplying instead of raising to a power. 1.10³⁰ is not 1 + 0.10 × 30. That shortcut ignores growth on growth.
  • Applying the exponent to the wrong part. 1,000 × 1.1³ means 1,000 × (1.1³), not (1,000 × 1.1)³. Powers come before multiplication.
  • Reading a negative exponent as a negative number. 1.05⁻³ is about 0.864, a positive number below 1.

Remember

  • An exponent is repeated multiplication: 1.1³ = 1.1 × 1.1 × 1.1.
  • A negative exponent divides. That's discounting.
  • A fractional exponent is a root. That's how you find a yearly rate.
