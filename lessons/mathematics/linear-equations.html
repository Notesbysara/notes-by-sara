Linear equations and graphs

Straight-line relationships are everywhere in business: a fixed cost plus a cost per unit. Solving them tells you things like how much you must sell before you stop losing money.

Growth and equations · Lesson 3 of 7 · 6 minutesBefore this: Ratios and proportions

When does the coffee cart start making money?

Example, with made-up numbers. A coffee cart pays $3,000 a month for rent and a permit, whatever it sells. Each cup costs $1.50 to make and sells for $4. How many cups a month does it need to sell to cover its costs?

The idea

Each cup adds the same amount, so the cart's costs follow a straight line. Any straight line can be written as:

y = mx + b
bthe intercept: the value when x is 0mthe slope: how much y changes for each extra 1 of x

For the cart, total cost = 3,000 + 1.50x, where x is cups sold. The intercept, $3,000, is the fixed cost. The slope, $1.50, is the cost of each extra cup. Revenue = 4x: it starts at 0 and rises $4 per cup. Slope is "rise over run": the change in y divided by the change in x.

Left of the crossing, costs are above revenue and the cart loses money. Right of it, revenue is higher and it makes a profit. The crossing is the break-even point.

Solving for the unknown

Break-even is where revenue equals cost. To find x, get it alone on one side. The one rule: whatever you do to one side, do to the other.

4x = 3,000 + 1.5xRevenue equals cost
2.5x = 3,000Subtract 1.5x from both sides
x = 1,200Divide both sides by 2.5

The cart breaks even at 1,200 cups a month, when revenue and costs are both $4,800. Finding where two lines meet like this is called solving simultaneous equations: two equations that must both be true at once.

Quick check

A company's total cost is 500 + 20x, where x is units made. What does the 20 tell you?

Worked example

Example, with made-up numbers. An online course costs $9,000 to film and host. Each student adds $10 in payment fees and support. It sells for $70. How many students does it need to break even?

1. Set revenue equal to cost70x = 9,000 + 10x
2. Subtract 10x from both sides60x = 9,000
3. Divide both sides by 60x = 150
Break-even150 students

The 60 is what each student leaves after their own cost ($70 − $10). Analysts call it the contribution per unit. That gives a shortcut: break-even = fixed cost ÷ contribution per unit.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Changing only one side. Subtracting 1.5x on the left but not the right breaks the equation.
  • Mixing up slope and intercept. The number multiplied by x is the slope. The number on its own is the intercept.
  • Ignoring the cost per unit. Dividing fixed costs by the price ($3,000 ÷ $4 = 750 cups) forgets that every cup also costs money to make.

Remember

  • A straight line is y = mx + b: b is where it starts, m is the change per unit.
  • To solve, do the same thing to both sides until x is alone.
  • Break-even is where revenue meets cost: fixed cost ÷ (price − cost per unit).
