Arithmetic and geometric sequences

Year after year, money either changes by the same amount or by the same percentage. Those two patterns behave very differently over time.

Sequences and series · Lesson 1 of 3 · 5 minutesBefore this: Exponents

Which job offer is better?

Example, with made-up numbers. Two jobs both start at $40,000. Job A gives a $2,000 raise every year. Job B gives a 5% raise every year. In year 2, both pay $42,000. Does it matter which you pick?

The idea

A sequence is a list of numbers that follows a rule. Finance uses two kinds:

Arithmetic: add the same amount40,000 → 42,000 → 44,000 → 46,000Each step adds the same difference, here $2,000. On a chart it's a straight line.
Geometric: multiply by the same factor40,000 → 42,000 → 44,100 → 46,305Each step multiplies by the same ratio, here 1.05. The steps keep getting bigger.

The two salaries are almost the same for the first few years. By year 10, Job B pays $62,053 against $58,000. By year 20, it pays $101,078 against $78,000.

Finding any term

You don't have to write out every year. Each kind has a formula for the nth term, using what you learned in Reading formulas:

Arithmetic
an = a1 + (n − 1) × d
a1 is the first term and d the difference.
Geometric
an = a1 × rn − 1
a1 is the first term and r the ratio.

Why n − 1? The first term has had no steps yet. Year 10 has had 9 raises, so Job B's year-10 salary is 40,000 × 1.059 = $62,053.

Quick check

A machine loses $2,000 of value every year. What kind of sequence are its yearly values?

Where you'll meet them

Arithmetic
  • Straight-line depreciation
  • Fixed yearly raises
  • Simple interest, where interest is paid only on the original amount
Geometric
  • Compound interest and savings growth
  • Prices rising with inflation
  • Declining-balance depreciation, which takes the same percentage each year

Compound growth is just a geometric sequence. A $1,000 deposit at 5% runs 1,000 → 1,050 → 1,102.50 →… with a ratio of 1.05. That's the same as FV = PV × (1 + r)n, counting years from 0 instead of 1.

Worked example

Example, with made-up numbers. A $30,000 car loses 15% of its value each year. What is it worth after 5 years?

1. Find the ratio1 − 0.15 = 0.85
2. Raise it to the number of years0.855 = 0.4437
3. Multiply by the starting value$30,000 × 0.4437
Value after 5 years$13,311

Here we count 5 full years of steps, so the power is 5. In an terms, that's a6: the value at the start of year 6.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Using n instead of n − 1. The 10th term has had only 9 steps from the first.
  • Writing the ratio as the rate. 5% growth means multiplying by 1.05, not by 5 or 0.05.
  • Treating a percentage change as a fixed amount. "Falls 15% a year" is geometric. "Falls $4,500 a year" is arithmetic.

Remember

  • Arithmetic sequences add the same difference. Geometric sequences multiply by the same ratio.
  • The nth term uses n − 1 steps from the first term.
  • Compound growth is a geometric sequence. Over long periods it pulls far ahead of fixed additions.

1. What kind of sequence is 3, 6, 12, 24, …?

2. What is the 10th term of 5, 8, 11, …?

3. A $1,000 deposit earns 4% a year, compounded. Which describes its yearly balances?
