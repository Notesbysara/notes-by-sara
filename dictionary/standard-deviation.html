Standard Deviation

Intermediate

Standard deviation is a measure of how much a set of values typically differs from the mean. It is the square root of the variance.

Standard deviation expresses spread in the same units as the original data, which makes it easier to interpret than variance. A low standard deviation means values tend to be close to the mean; a high one means they are more widely scattered.

For the values 2, 4, 6 and 8, the population variance is 5, so the population standard deviation is about 2.24. In other words, the values typically lie a little over 2 units from their mean of 5.

In finance, standard deviation is the most common measure of volatility. If two funds both average annual returns of 7%, but one has a standard deviation of 5% and the other 15%, the second fund's returns swing much more from year to year, making it riskier in the short term.

When data follow a normal distribution, standard deviation has a useful interpretation: about 68% of values fall within one standard deviation of the mean, and about 95% within two. For a fund averaging 7% with a standard deviation of 15%, returns between −8% and 22% would be expected in roughly two years out of three, if returns were normally distributed. Real market returns often have more extreme outcomes than this simple rule suggests.
