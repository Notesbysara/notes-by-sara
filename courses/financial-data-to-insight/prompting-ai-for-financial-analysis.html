Prompting AI for financial analysis

AI tools can clean data, write formulas and draft summaries in seconds. Used well, they save hours. Used carelessly, they can hand you wrong numbers in a very confident voice.

What you'll be able to do: write a clear, structured prompt for a finance task, using one prompt for each step of the method. You'll also check AI answers properly and protect confidential data.

Rule one: protect the data​

This comes before everything else. Don't paste confidential company figures, client names or personal information into an AI tool unless your employer allows it and the tool is approved for that data.

  • Use the AI tools your employer has approved, and follow its policy.
  • When in doubt, remove names and account numbers, or use made-up sample data with the same structure. Removing names doesn't make the figures themselves safe: unreleased company results are confidential on their own.
  • Remember that some tools may store what you type, and some may use it to train their models, depending on the tool and its settings.

Tip You can often get the help you need without the real numbers. Ask for the method or the formula using sample data, then apply it to the real file yourself.

A six-part prompt​

A prompt is the set of instructions and data you give an AI tool to produce an answer.

A vague prompt gets a guess. A structured one gets something you can use. This template has six parts:

PartWhat it saysExample for the shop
RoleWho the AI should act asYou are a financial analyst helping a small retailer.
ContextThe background and the decisionThe owner is deciding whether to open a new store for $250k.
TaskExactly what you want, in one sentenceExplain why cash fell this year even though the shop made a profit.
DataWhat you're giving it, with columns and unitsThe cash flow statement below, in $ thousands, January to December.
FormatHow you want the answerA table of the main reasons, then three bullet points.
RulesThe limitsShow your calculations. List your assumptions. If you're not sure, say so. Don't invent numbers.

Put together, that's:

Example prompt

You are a financial analyst helping a small retailer. The owner is deciding whether to open a new store for $250k. Explain why cash fell this year even though the shop made a profit. The data is the cash flow statement below, in $ thousands, January to December. Give me a table of the main reasons, then three bullet points. Show your calculations, list your assumptions, say if you're not sure, and don't invent numbers.

Example Compare that with “Analyze this.” The six-part prompt tells the AI what matters, what the units are and what a good answer looks like.

One prompt per step​

Don't ask for everything at once. Use one prompt for each step of the method, and check each answer before you move on. Mistakes are much easier to catch in small pieces.

StepExample promptCheck
Assess“Check this file for duplicates, missing values, numbers stored as text and mixed units. List each problem with its row.”Spot-check two of the problems it found in the file
Inquire“Suggest a hypothesis tree for why cost of goods rose from 60% to 61% of sales.”Does it have gaps or overlaps?
Analyze“Write the Excel formulas for gross margin, operating margin and the current ratio, using these cell references.”Recalculate one ratio by hand
Visualize“Which chart best shows this finding, and what should its title say?”Does the title match the numbers?
Interpret“Draft a one-page summary using Finding, Why, So what and Now what.”Read every claim and agree with it before sharing

Tip If the first answer isn't right, reply with what's wrong and ask again. A short follow-up such as “Use $ thousands, and show the formula for each row” often fixes it.

Check before you trust​

AI tools can make arithmetic mistakes, misread a column, or state something that sounds right but isn't. This is often called a hallucination. So check every time:

1

Recalculate a few numbers yourself

Pick two or three figures and work them out by hand or in Excel. If one is wrong, check them all.

Example An AI summary says “cash fell 17.6%”. Check: 60 ÷ 400 = 15%. The AI divided by the ending cash (60 ÷ 340) instead of the starting cash.

2

Ask it to show its working

Formulas, steps and assumptions make mistakes visible. An answer with no working is hard to check.

3

Read everything before you share it

Never send an AI-written conclusion to anyone until you've read it, checked it, and agree with it. You are responsible for the work, not the tool.

Watch out Be extra careful with facts the AI didn't get from you, such as a company's published results or an accounting rule. Check them against the original source.

Build a prompt library​

Save the prompts that worked, with a note on what they're for and what you had to fix. Next month, with new data, you can rerun the same steps in a fraction of the time, and every new project starts faster.

Monthly data checkAssessFinds duplicates, blanks, text numbers and mixed unitsTell it the grain and the units first

Try it yourself (optional)

1. Write a six-part prompt asking an AI tool to check the shop's March sales file for problems (Topic 3), Assume your employer has approved the AI tool for this data.

2. An AI tool tells you the shop's net profit margin is 7.6%. Revenue was $2,310k and net profit $140k. Is it right?

Show the answer

1. One good version: “You are a careful data analyst. I'm preparing a small shop's March sales file for analysis. Check the file below for duplicate rows, missing values, numbers stored as text, inconsistent region labels and unusual values. One row = one store on one day; Sales is in US dollars, after discounts. List each problem with its row number in a table, and say what you'd check before fixing it. Don't change or invent any values.” If the file had a column the check doesn't need, such as customer names, you'd remove it first.

2. No. 140 ÷ 2,310 = 6.1%. (7.6% is what you get from 175 ÷ 2,310, profit before tax, so the AI likely used the wrong line.)

What this looks like at work​

AI tools are becoming part of everyday finance work. For example:

  • You ask an AI tool to write a tricky Excel formula, then test it on a few rows.
  • You use it to draft a first version of a monthly commentary, then check and edit every line.
  • You build a set of prompts your team reuses each month.

Each time, protect the data, ask for the working, and check the numbers before anything leaves your hands.

How you'd say it to your manager​

If you use AI in your work, say so, and say how you checked it.

Example message

Hi Maya, I used our approved AI tool to draft the first version of the cash commentary, with the customer names removed. I've checked every number against the cash flow statement and fixed one percentage it got wrong. The final version is attached, and I've saved the prompt so we can reuse it next month.

Notice it's open about the tool, explains the checks, and makes the work repeatable.

Remember

  • Never paste confidential data into an AI tool unless your employer allows it.
  • Use six parts: Role, Context, Task, Data, Format, Rules.
  • Use one prompt per step of the method, and check each answer before moving on.
  • Recalculate a few numbers yourself, ask for the working, and read everything before you share it.
  • Save the prompts that work.
