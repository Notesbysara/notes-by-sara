## Formulas you'll use again and again

<div className="nbs-sec"><div className="steps"> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">1</span><h3>Start with SUM, AVERAGE and ROUND</h3></div> <p>Every formula starts with an equals sign. <code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=SUM(F6:F13)</code> adds the range and gives 5,284. <code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=AVERAGE(F6:F13)</code> gives 660.5.</p><p><code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=ROUND(number, digits)</code> rounds a result. For example, the average price of a pair of shoes is 4,420 ÷ 69 = 64.057…, and <code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=ROUND(4420/69, 2)</code> gives 64.06.</p><p className="tip"><b className="lab">Tip</b> ROUND changes the value itself. Formatting a cell to show two decimals only changes how it looks. If you add up rounded values, the total can differ slightly from the rounded total.</p><p className="small">Helps with almost every topic.</p> </div> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">2</span><h3>Make formulas copy correctly with $</h3></div> <p>When you copy a formula down, Excel shifts its cell references. That's usually what you want, but not always.</p><p className="eg"><b className="lab">Example</b> To find each row's share of total sales, put the total in a cell outside the table, such as N6, and write <code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=F6/$N$6</code> in G6. Copy it down. F6 becomes F7, F8 and so on, but $N$6 stays fixed. Without the $ signs, row 7 would divide by N7, which holds a different number, so every answer below the first would be wrong.</p><p className="tip"><b className="lab">Tip</b> Click a reference in the formula bar and press F4 to cycle through the $ options. On a Mac, press Command+T.</p><p className="small">Helps with Topics 6 and 7, when you copy ratio and forecast formulas.</p> </div> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">3</span><h3>Make decisions with IF</h3></div> <p><code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=IF(test, value if true, value if false)</code> returns one of two results.</p><p className="eg"><b className="lab">Example</b> With budget revenue in N7 and actual revenue in N8, <code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=IF(N8>N7,"Favorable","Unfavorable")</code> labels the variance. For cost rows, flip the test. A zero variance shows as Unfavorable, so check those by hand.</p><p className="small">Helps with Topic 7.</p> </div> </div></div>

## Add up and count by condition

<div className="nbs-sec"><div className="def"><p><b>SUMIFS</b> adds the values in one range where other ranges meet the conditions you set. <b>COUNTIFS</b> counts the rows that meet them.</p></div> <div className="tw"><table><tbody><tr><th>What you want</th><th>Formula</th><th style={{textAlign:"right"}}>Result</th></tr><tr><td>Sales in the North</td><td><code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=SUMIFS(F6:F13, C6:C13, "North")</code></td><td className="r">4,049</td></tr><tr><td>Shoe sales in the North</td><td><code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=SUMIFS(F6:F13, C6:C13, "North", D6:D13, "Shoes")</code></td><td className="r">3,185</td></tr><tr><td>Number of rows for store S02</td><td><code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=COUNTIFS(B6:B13, "S02")</code></td><td className="r">4</td></tr></tbody></table></div> <p>The order matters: in SUMIFS, the range you're adding comes first, then each condition as a pair of range and value.</p> <p className="tip"><b className="lab">Tip</b> COUNTIFS finds duplicates. In a new column, <code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=COUNTIFS($A$6:$A$13,A6,$B$6:$B$13,B6,$D$6:$D$13,D6)</code> counts how many rows share this row's date, store and product. Anything above 1 is a possible duplicate, the same check Topic 3 uses, at this table's level of detail.</p> <p className="small">Helps with Topics 3 and 6, for totals by store, region or product.</p></div>

## Bring in values from another table

<div className="nbs-sec"><p>Often the value you need sits in a different table. Suppose a store list in K5:L11 has each store and its region. To fill in a region column automatically, for example in column I:</p> <div className="tw"><table><tbody><tr><th>Excel version</th><th>Formula in I6</th></tr><tr><td>Microsoft 365, Excel 2021 and later</td><td><code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=XLOOKUP(B6, $K$6:$K$11, $L$6:$L$11)</code></td></tr><tr><td>Any version</td><td><code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=INDEX($L$6:$L$11, MATCH(B6, $K$6:$K$11, 0))</code></td></tr></tbody></table></div> <p>Both say: find this row's store in the store list, and return the region next to it. The 0 in MATCH asks for an exact match, which is almost always what you want.</p> <p className="tip"><b className="lab">Tip</b> If a lookup returns #N/A, the value usually isn't in the list, often because of an extra space. That's where TRIM, below, comes in.</p> <p className="small">Helps with Topics 2 and 3, for adding regions or product details to a sales file.</p></div>

## Summarize a table with a PivotTable

<div className="nbs-sec"><div className="def"><p>A <b>PivotTable</b> summarizes a table by grouping rows and totaling values, without writing any formulas.</p></div> <div className="steps"> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">1</span><h3>Insert it</h3></div> <p>Click inside the table, then Insert › PivotTable, and choose a new worksheet.</p> </div> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">2</span><h3>Drag the fields</h3></div> <p>Drag <b>Region</b> to Rows and <b>Sales</b> to Values. Excel shows North 4,049, South 1,235 and a grand total of 5,284. Drag <b>Product</b> to Columns to split each region by product.</p> </div> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">3</span><h3>Check it</h3></div> <p>Make sure the Values field says “Sum of Sales”, not “Count of Sales”. Excel counts instead of sums when a column contains any text or blank cells, such as a number stored as text.</p> </div> </div> <p className="tip"><b className="lab">Tip</b> A PivotTable doesn't update by itself. After changing the data, right-click it and choose Refresh.</p> <p className="small">Helps with Topics 3 and 9.</p></div>

## Clean messy columns

<div className="nbs-sec"><div className="tw"><table><tbody><tr><th>Problem</th><th>Tool</th><th>How</th></tr><tr><td>Extra spaces, like “North ”</td><td>TRIM</td><td><code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=TRIM(C6)</code> removes spaces at the start and end, and cuts repeated spaces between words down to one. It misses the non-breaking spaces some exports contain.</td></tr><tr><td>Numbers stored as text</td><td>Convert to Number</td><td>Select the cells, click the warning icon, and choose Convert to Number. Or use <code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=VALUE(F6)</code></td></tr><tr><td>Exact duplicate rows</td><td>Remove Duplicates</td><td>Data › Remove Duplicates, then check the columns that define a duplicate</td></tr><tr><td>Two things in one column, like “S01-North”</td><td>Text to Columns</td><td>Data › Text to Columns, and split on the dash</td></tr></tbody></table></div> <p className="tip"><b className="lab">Tip</b> Clean in a copy, or in new columns, and keep the original untouched. Topic 3 explains why.</p> <p className="small">Helps with Topic 3.</p></div>

## Build a chart quickly

<div className="nbs-sec"><p>Select the data, including the headers, then Insert › Recommended Charts, or pick a type from the Charts group. Then:</p> <ul style={{margin:"0",paddingLeft:"20px",display:"flex",flexDirection:"column",gap:"4px"}}><li>Click the chart, then the <b>Chart Elements (+)</b> button beside it, to add or remove titles, labels and gridlines. On a Mac, use Chart Design › Add Chart Element.</li><li>Click the chart title and type your finding as the title.</li><li>Right-click any part of the chart and choose Format to change colors or axes.</li></ul> <p className="tip"><b className="lab">Tip</b> Topics 8 and 9 explain which chart to choose and how to keep it honest. Each row's average price per item, in column H, rounded to two decimals.</p> <details><summary>Show the answer</summary><p>1. <code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=SUMIFS(F6:F13, C6:C13, "North", D6:D13, "Bags")</code> gives 864 (384 + 240 + 240).</p><p>2. <code style={{fontSize:".9em",background:"var(--cream)",padding:"1px 6px",borderRadius:"5px",overflowWrap:"anywhere"}}>=COUNTIFS(A6:A13, DATE(2025,3,3))</code> gives 5. Row 6 gives 65.00, and row 10 (S03 shoes) gives 61.75. No $ signs are needed, because each row should use its own cells.</p></details></div>

<div className="nbs-sec"><div className="remember"> <p className="eyebrow">Remember</p> <ul><li>Start every formula with =, and use $ to fix a reference when you copy it.</li><li>SUMIFS adds and COUNTIFS counts, by one or more conditions. The sum range comes first.</li><li>XLOOKUP, or INDEX and MATCH, bring in values from another table.</li><li>A PivotTable summarizes without formulas. Refresh it after the data changes.</li><li>Clean in a copy: TRIM, Convert to Number, Remove Duplicates and Text to Columns.</li></ul> </div></div>
