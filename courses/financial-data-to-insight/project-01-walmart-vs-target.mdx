## The six steps

<div className="nbs-sec"><div className="steps"> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">1</span><h3>Assess: check the data first</h3></div> <p>Before calculating anything, find the traps in this data. Open the starter workbook and check four things:</p><ul style={{margin:"0",paddingLeft:"20px",display:"flex",flexDirection:"column",gap:"4px"}}><li><b>Year ends:</b> Walmart's years end on January 31. Target's end on the Saturday nearest January 31, so its 2024 column ends on February 3, 2024.</li><li><b>Week counts:</b> Target's year to February 2024 had 53 weeks, one more than the next year.</li><li><b>Revenue lines:</b> both companies' revenue includes some non-merchandise income (Walmart: membership and other income; Target: advertising, credit card profit sharing and other revenue). Little or none of it has a matching cost of sales. This project uses total revenue for both; on net sales alone, Walmart's gross margin would be 24.2% instead of 24.9%.</li><li><b>Units and totals:</b> everything is in $ millions. Tie three numbers back to the original reports.</li></ul><div className="try" style={{marginTop:"4px"}}><p className="eyebrow">Checkpoint</p><p>Target's revenue fell 0.8% in the year to February 2025. Why might that overstate how much its business actually shrank?</p><details><summary>Check your answer</summary><p>The year before had 53 weeks and this one had 52. Target says the extra week added about $1.7 billion, or about 1.6% of that year's sales. Without it, sales rose slightly, by about 0.8%. Always check the length of the period before comparing.</p></details></div><p className="small">Practices Toolkit A and Topics 2 and 3.</p> </div> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">2</span><h3>Inquire: sharpen the question</h3></div> <p>Write a one-page question brief, then a hypothesis tree. “More efficient” could mean several things, so break it down:</p><ul style={{margin:"0",paddingLeft:"20px",display:"flex",flexDirection:"column",gap:"4px"}}><li>Does one keep more of each dollar of sales? (gross and operating margin)</li><li>Does one get more sales from its assets? (asset turnover, inventory turnover)</li><li>Does one turn profit into cash better?
