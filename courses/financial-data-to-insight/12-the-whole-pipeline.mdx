## The six steps on one question

## It's a loop

<div className="nbs-sec"><p>Notice what happened at step 5. Answering one question raised a new one: <b>did the shoe discounts bring in enough extra sales to pay for themselves?</b> That sends us back to step 2 (the data is already clean) with a new, sharper question, and around the loop again.</p> <p>That's normal. Good analysis rarely ends with a full stop. It ends with a decision and, often, the next question.</p></div>

## Your turn: a first project

<div className="nbs-sec"><p>Pick one small, real question, from your work, your studies or a public company. Don't aim for perfect. Aim for finishing once. Use this checklist:</p> <div className="tw"><table><tbody><tr><th>Step</th><th>What you'll produce</th><th>Done when</th></tr><tr><td>Assess</td><td>A schema map and a clean file</td><td>The totals tie to the source</td></tr><tr><td>Inquire</td><td>A one-page question brief</td><td>Someone else agrees it's the right question</td></tr><tr><td>Analyze</td><td>The numbers that answer it</td><td>Each hypothesis is supported or ruled out</td></tr><tr><td>Visualize</td><td>One or two charts</td><td>Each passes the five-second test</td></tr><tr><td>Interpret</td><td>A one-page summary</td><td>You can say it in 30 seconds</td></tr><tr><td>Prompt</td><td>Saved prompts for next time</td><td>You've checked the AI's numbers yourself</td></tr></tbody></table></div></div>

<div className="nbs-sec try"><p className="eyebrow">Try it yourself (optional)</p> <p>Online sales fell from $19,500 to $18,200 in a month, even though website visits rose 40% (Topic 5). Write one sentence for each of the six steps, describing what you'd do.</p> <details><summary>Show the answer</summary><p>One good version:</p><p><b>Assess:</b> check the visits and orders data covers the same days, with no duplicate orders. <b>Inquire:</b> “Why did online sales fall 7% while visits rose 40%, and what should we change on the website this quarter?” <b>Analyze:</b> compare the share of visits that buy (3% to 2%) and the average order, by traffic source. <b>Visualize:</b> a line chart of the share of visits that buy, by week, with the finding as the title. <b>Interpret:</b> size the lost sales and recommend a fix for the weakest traffic source. <b>Prompt:</b> save a prompt that runs the same check next month.</p></details></div>

## What this looks like at work

<div className="nbs-sec work"><p>At work, nobody will say “please run the six steps.” But this is what good analysts do, quietly, every time. For example:</p> <ul style={{margin:"0",paddingLeft:"20px",display:"flex",flexDirection:"column",gap:"4px"}}><li>Your manager forwards a worry and asks you to “take a look”.</li><li>You're given a week to answer a question before a budget meeting.</li><li>You join a new team and want your first piece of work to land well.</li></ul> <p>Each time, move through the steps in order, produce something at each one, and finish with a decision.</p></div>

## How you'd say it to your manager

<div className="nbs-sec"><p>At the end of a full analysis, keep the message short and point to the detail.</p> <div className="msg"> <p className="eyebrow">Example message</p> <p className="body">Hi Maya, I've finished the margin review. Margin fell from 9% to 8% because cost of goods rose a point: about $15k from lower shoe prices and $8k from supplier price increases. I'd suggest we review the shoe discount policy and talk to the suppliers who raised prices before the budget. One open question is whether the discounts paid for themselves in extra sales, which I can check next. Summary and workings attached.</p> </div> <p className="small">Notice it gives the answer, the sized causes, a recommendation, and the next question.</p></div>

<div className="nbs-sec"><div className="remember"> <p className="eyebrow">Remember</p> <ul><li>Assess the data, Inquire with a sharp question, Analyze to find the cause, Visualize the finding, Interpret with a so-what, and Prompt to repeat it.</li><li>Each step produces something the next step uses.</li><li>The method is a loop: interpreting often raises the next question.</li><li>Aim to finish one small project, not to make it perfect.</li></ul> </div></div>
