Project 01 · Free · About 3 to 4 hours

Walmart vs Target: turning sales into profit and cash

Your first real analysis. Two of America's biggest retailers, three years of their published results, and the six steps from this course.

What you'll be able to do: run the whole method on real company data, from checking the numbers to writing a one-page summary. You'll also practice the Excel and statistics skills from the toolkits on figures that matter.

The brief​

Your question

Over the last three years, which retailer turned its sales into profit and cash more efficiently, and why?

What you'll produceA clean data sheet, a ratio table, two charts and a one-page summary
What you needExcel or Google Sheets, and Topics 1 to 12 (or work through each step after its topic)
DataThree years of results for each company, in $ millions

The six steps​

1

Assess: check the data first

Before calculating anything, find the traps in this data. Open the starter workbook and check four things:

  • Year ends: Walmart's years end on January 31. Target's end on the Saturday nearest January 31, so its 2024 column ends on February 3, 2024.
  • Week counts: Target's year to February 2024 had 53 weeks, one more than the next year.
  • Revenue lines: both companies' revenue includes some non-merchandise income (Walmart: membership and other income; Target: advertising, credit card profit sharing and other revenue). Little or none of it has a matching cost of sales. This project uses total revenue for both; on net sales alone, Walmart's gross margin would be 24.2% instead of 24.9%.
  • Units and totals: everything is in $ millions. Tie three numbers back to the original reports.

Checkpoint

Target's revenue fell 0.8% in the year to February 2025. Why might that overstate how much its business actually shrank?

Check your answer

The year before had 53 weeks and this one had 52. Target says the extra week added about $1.7 billion, or about 1.6% of that year's sales. Without it, sales rose slightly, by about 0.8%. Always check the length of the period before comparing.

Practices Toolkit A and Topics 2 and 3.

2

Inquire: sharpen the question

Write a one-page question brief, then a hypothesis tree. “More efficient” could mean several things, so break it down:

  • Does one keep more of each dollar of sales? (gross and operating margin)
  • Does one get more sales from its assets? (asset turnover, inventory turnover)
  • Does one turn profit into cash better? (operating cash flow compared with net income)
  • Is one growing while the other isn't? (revenue growth)

Checkpoint

Target keeps more of each sale, so why is Walmart's return on assets higher?

Check your answer

Return on assets = net margin × asset turnover. Target's net margin is higher (3.5% vs 3.1%), but Walmart gets about $2.51 of sales from each dollar of assets, against Target's $1.76. Walmart's speed more than makes up for its thinner margin: 7.7% against 6.2%.

Visualize: show the finding

Practices Topics 8 and 9.

5

Interpret: write the one-page summary

Use Finding, Why, So what and Now what. Here's the start of a strong answer:

Sample opening

Target keeps more of each sale, but Walmart turns its assets into sales and profit faster, and its sales are growing while Target's fell. In the year to January 2026, Target's operating margin was 4.9% against Walmart's 4.2%, but Walmart turned its inventory 9.1 times against Target's 6.1 and earned a higher return on assets, 7.7% against 6.2%.

Tip Include at least one honest limit. For example, the two companies report revenue a little differently, and three years is a short period.

Practices Topic 10.

6

Prompt: check it with AI

Write a six-part prompt asking an AI tool to recalculate your ratio table from the data sheet and point out anything unusual. Then check two of its numbers against your workbook, and note any it got wrong.

Practices Topics 11 and 12.

Remember

  • Check year ends, week counts and revenue definitions before comparing two companies.
  • Break “more efficient” into margins, turnover, cash conversion and growth.
  • A higher margin doesn't guarantee a higher return. Speed matters too.
  • Finish with a one-page summary, and state its limits.
