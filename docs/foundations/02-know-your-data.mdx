<div className="nbs-head"><p className="eyebrow">Topic 2 of 12 · Step 1: Assess</p> <h1>Know your data</h1> <p className="lead">Before you do any math, you need to know exactly what you're looking at. It sounds obvious, but this is where a lot of expensive mistakes begin.</p> <p>Here's a quick example. A spreadsheet shows revenue of <b>4,218</b>. No notes, no labels. What does it mean?</p> <div className="mean"> <div><span>If it's in dollars</span><b>$4,218</b></div> <div><span>If it's in thousands of dollars</span><b>$4.2 million</b></div> <div><span>If it's in millions of dollars</span><b>$4.2 billion</b></div> </div> <p>Same number, a million times apart, and nothing in the cell tells you which. This topic shows you the four questions to ask so that never catches you out.</p></div>

## Four questions to ask any data

<div className="nbs-sec"><p>Ask these every time you open a new file, in this order.</p> <div className="steps"> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">1</span><h3>What type is each column?</h3></div><div className="def"><p>A <b>data type</b> is the kind of value a column holds. It decides which operations make sense on that column.</p><p>What counts: if a value can be meaningfully added or averaged, it's numerical. If it labels a group, it's categorical, even when it's written in digits.</p></div> <p>Most columns fall into one of four broad groups:</p> <div className="types"> <div><b>Numbers</b>Sales, units, price. You can add, average and compare them.</div> <div><b>Categories</b>Region, product, store ID. You can count and group them, but not add them.</div> <div><b>Dates</b>Sale date, month end. You can sort them and group them by month or year.</div> <div><b>Text</b>Descriptions, notes. You can read and search them, but not calculate with them.</div> </div> <p className="small">Here's how it looks in a real file. The label under each heading is the column's type:</p> <div className="tw"><table><tbody><tr><th>Sale date<br /><span className="typechip">Date</span></th><th>Store ID<br /><span className="typechip">Category</span></th><th>Product<br /><span className="typechip">Category</span></th><th style={{textAlign:"right"}}>Units<br /><span className="typechip">Number</span></th><th>Note<br /><span className="typechip">Text</span></th></tr> <tr><td>2025-03-03</td><td>S01</td><td>Shoes</td><td className="r">12</td><td>Promo weekend</td></tr> <tr><td>2025-03-04</td><td>S02</td><td>Bags</td><td className="r">8</td><td></td></tr></tbody></table></div> <p className="eg"><b>Example:</b> a store ID like 1042 looks like a number, but adding or averaging store IDs gives you a meaningless answer. It's a category.</p> <p className="tip"><b>Tip:</b> if you type a ZIP code like 02139 into Excel, it drops the zero and shows 2139. Format ID and code columns as Text before you type or import them.</p> </div> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">2</span><h3>What shape is the data?</h3></div> <p>Financial data usually comes in one of three shapes, and each one answers a different kind of question.</p> <div className="def"><p><Term slug="time-series" name="Time Series" def="A time series is a sequence of data points recorded at successive, usually equally spaced, points in time."><b>Time series data</b></Term>: a sequence of data points for one thing, recorded at successive, usually equally spaced, points in time.</p></div> <p>In plain words: one thing tracked over time, like one shop's sales year by year. One company's numbers alone can't tell you whether it's doing better than its rivals.</p> </div> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">3</span><h3>What does one row stand for?</h3></div><div className="def"><p><b>Granularity</b> (or grain) is the level of detail a single row represents: which entity and which time period each row describes.</p><p>What counts: every row in one table should have the same grain. Mixing daily and monthly rows in the same table can make the totals wrong, for example by counting the same sales twice.</p></div> <p>In plain words: is one row a single sale? A day? A month? A whole year?</p> <p>The easiest way to answer it is to finish this sentence: <b>“One row = …”</b>. For example, “One row = one store, on one day.”</p> <p>Each row should be unique at that level. If two rows match on every part of the grain (here, the same store and the same day), something is wrong: it's probably a duplicate. Topic 3 shows you how to find them.</p> <div className="mini"> <div className="tw"><p className="small">Daily, by store</p><table><tbody><tr><th>Day</th><th>Store</th><th style={{textAlign:"right"}}>Sales</th></tr> <tr><td>Mar 3</td><td>S01</td><td className="r">1,164</td></tr><tr><td>Mar 3</td><td>S02</td><td className="r">890</td></tr> <tr><td>Mar 4</td><td>S01</td><td className="r">975</td></tr><tr><td>Mar 4</td><td>S02</td><td className="r">1,020</td></tr></tbody></table></div> <div className="tw" style={{alignSelf:"center"}}><p className="small">Added up: both days, S01 and S02 (both in the North region)</p><table><tbody><tr><th>Days</th><th>Region</th><th style={{textAlign:"right"}}>Sales</th></tr> <tr><td>Mar 3–4</td><td>North</td><td className="r">4,049</td></tr></tbody></table></div> </div> <p className="eg"><b>Example:</b> your <Term slug="budget" name="Budget" def="A budget is a plan for how money will be earned and spent over a set period, such as a month or a year. You can't compare them until you add up the daily store numbers into monthly region totals.</p> <p className="tip"><b>Tip:</b> totals like sales and units add up from detail, but you can't split a total back into detail, so keep the most detailed version you can get. Averages, percentages and ratios don't add up: margins of 11% and 8% don't make 19%. Recalculate them from the totals instead.</p> </div> <div className="step"> <div className="step-head"><span className="n">4</span><h3>What unit, currency and year?</h3></div><div className="def"><p>A <Term slug="fiscal-year" name="Fiscal Year" def="A fiscal year is the twelve-month period a business or government uses for its accounting and financial reporting. It is issued by a government or central bank and used to price goods and settle debts.">currency</Term>, and the time period.</p> <p>Time periods are trickier than they look, because many companies don't use January to December. ## Let's see it in action

<div className="nbs-sec"><p>Remember the shop from Topic 1? The owner sends you its sales file. Here are the first few rows:</p> <div className="tw"><table><tbody><tr><th>Date</th><th>Store</th><th>Region</th><th>Product</th><th style={{textAlign:"right"}}>Units</th><th style={{textAlign:"right"}}>Sales</th></tr> <tr><td>2025-03-03</td><td>S01</td><td>North</td><td>Shoes</td><td className="r">12</td><td className="r">780</td></tr> <tr><td>2025-03-03</td><td>S01</td><td>North</td><td>Bags</td><td className="r">8</td><td className="r">384</td></tr> <tr><td>2025-03-03</td><td>S03</td><td>South</td><td>Shoes</td><td className="r">20</td><td className="r">1,235</td></tr> <tr><td>2025-03-04</td><td>S01</td><td>North</td><td>Shoes</td><td className="r">15</td><td className="r">975</td></tr></tbody></table></div> <p>Let's ask the four questions.</p> <div className="walk"> <div className="wrow"><p className="label">Question 1 · Types</p><p>Date is a date. Store, Region and Product are categories. Units and Sales are numbers. Store is a code, not a quantity, so we'll never add it up.</p></div> <div className="wrow"><p className="label">Question 2 · Shape</p><p>Many stores over many days, so it's a panel. That means we can see trends and compare stores.</p></div> <div className="wrow"><p className="label">Question 3 · One row</p><p>One row = one product, in one store, on one day. So each row is a daily total, not a single sale.</p></div> <div className="wrow"><p className="label">Question 4 · Unit and year</p><p>The file doesn't say whether Sales is in dollars or thousands, or whether it's before or after discounts. We ask the owner. Answer: US dollars, after discounts, before tax.</p></div> </div> <p>Now we write it all down in a small table called a <b>schema map</b>. It takes two minutes and saves hours later.</p><div className="def"><p>A <b>schema map</b> is a table that records, for every column in a dataset, its name, data type, meaning and unit. Many also add where the data came from.</p></div> <p className="small"><b>Grain:</b> one row = one product, in one store, on one day.</p> <div className="tw"><table><tbody><tr><th>Column</th><th>Type</th><th>What it means</th><th>Unit or format</th><th>Source</th></tr> <tr><td>Date</td><td>Date</td><td>Day of the sales</td><td>YYYY-MM-DD</td><td>Till system</td></tr> <tr><td>Store</td><td>Category</td><td>Which store (S01 to S06)</td><td>None</td><td>Store list</td></tr> <tr><td>Region</td><td>Category</td><td>North, South, East or West</td><td>None</td><td>Store list</td></tr> <tr><td>Product</td><td>Category</td><td>Product line</td><td>None</td><td>Product list</td></tr> <tr><td>Units</td><td>Number</td><td>Items sold that day</td><td>Count</td><td>Till system</td></tr> <tr><td>Sales</td><td>Number</td><td>Sales after discounts, before tax</td><td>US dollars</td><td>Till system</td></tr></tbody></table></div> <p className="small">This is an invented shop. ## What this looks like at work

<div className="nbs-sec work"><p>Knowing your data is usually the first thing you do in any finance task. For example:</p> <ul> <li>On your first week, someone sends you a report and asks you to “take a look”.</li> <li>You're asked to compare this month's actual results with the budget.</li> <li>Two teams send you numbers, and you need to combine them into one report.</li> </ul> <p>In each case, the four questions stop you comparing numbers in different units, different years or different levels of detail.</p></div>

## How you'd say it to your manager

<div className="nbs-sec"><p>Asking good questions about the data early shows you're careful, not slow. Keep it short and say what you'll do next.</p> <div className="msg"> <p className="eyebrow">Example message</p> <p className="body">Hi Maya, before I start on the sales analysis, two quick checks. Is the Sales column before or after discounts? And is the 2024 report a calendar year or our fiscal year? Also, the budget is monthly by region, so I'll add up the daily store sales to match before comparing. I'll send the first numbers tomorrow.</p> </div> <p className="small">Notice it asks specific questions, explains the one change you'll make, and gives a clear next step.</p></div>

<div className="nbs-sec"><div className="remember"> <p className="eyebrow">Remember</p> <ul> <li>A number without a unit, currency and time period can't be trusted.</li> <li>Four questions: what type, what shape, what does one row stand for, and what unit and year.</li> <li>Codes and IDs look like numbers, but they're categories.</li> <li>Totals add up from detail, never back down. Averages and percentages must be recalculated, not added.</li> <li>Write a schema map before you analyze anything.</li> </ul> </div></div>
