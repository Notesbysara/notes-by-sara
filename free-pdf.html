1
Compound Interest
Beginner
Interest calculated on both the original amount and the interest already earned.
Example1,000 at 5% a year becomes 1,628.89 in 10 years.
2
Diversification
Beginner
Spreading money across different assets so one loss does not sink the whole portfolio.
ExampleHolding shares in 20 industries instead of one.
3
P/E Ratio
Beginner
Share price divided by earnings per share. A quick way to compare how richly shares are priced.
ExamplePrice 50, earnings per share 5: the P/E is 10.
4
Opportunity Cost
Beginner
What you give up by choosing one option over the next best one.
ExampleSpending 500 on a trip means not investing 500.
5
Working Capital
Beginner
Current assets minus current liabilities: the cash a business has to run day to day.
ExampleAssets 600, liabilities 400: working capital is 200.
6
Inflation
Beginner
A general rise in prices over time, so each unit of money buys less.
ExampleAt 3% a year, today's 100 buys about 97 worth next year.
7
Net Present Value
Intermediate
The value today of future cash flows, minus what the investment costs. Above zero means it adds value.
ExamplePay 900 now for 1,000 in a year at 5%: NPV is 52.38.
8
Current Ratio
Intermediate
Current assets divided by current liabilities. Above 1 means short-term bills are covered.
Example600 divided by 400 gives a current ratio of 1.50.
9
Beta
Intermediate
How strongly a share moves compared with the whole market. Above 1 means it swings more.
ExampleBeta of 1.5: it tends to rise 15% when the market rises 10%.
10
Put Option
Advanced
The right, but not the obligation, to sell an asset at a set price before a set date.
ExampleRight to sell at 100 when the price falls to 80 gains 20, before the premium.
