Working with financial professionals

The kinds of financial professionals, how each is paid and why that matters, the standards they must meet in the US, the UK, India and Taiwan, how much fees cost over time, and how to check someone before you hand over money.

Life goals · Lesson 3 of 3 · 10 minutesBefore this: Big life decisions

In plain words

A good adviser can help you avoid expensive mistakes, but advice is never free. Find out exactly how someone is paid, because that shapes what they recommend. Check that they are registered with the regulator, compare total costs, and walk away from anyone who promises high returns with no risk or pushes you to decide quickly.

Different professionals do different jobs. Titles like "adviser" and "consultant" are often loosely used, so look at what the person is licensed to do:

Financial planners and advisersLook at your whole financial picture: goals, savings, investments, insurance, retirement and tax.
Brokers, distributors and agentsSell or arrange specific products, such as funds, shares, insurance or mortgages, usually paid by the product provider.
Accountants and tax advisersPrepare tax returns and plan around tax rules, especially for business owners, the self-employed and people with income in several countries.
LawyersDraft wills, trusts, powers of attorney and marriage agreements, and handle disputes.

How someone is paid creates the incentives you need to understand.

Fee-only: hourly or flatYou pay for advice directly. Few product conflicts, and you see the full cost up front.
Fee-only: percentage of assetsOften around 1% a year. Aligns with growing your money, but the cost rises as your wealth does, and it can discourage advice to pay off a mortgage or buy property.
CommissionPaid by product providers, so advice can feel free. It creates pressure to sell products, especially those paying higher commission.
Salaried at a bank or insurerUsually recommends only the employer's own products, and may have sales targets.

Each country sets its own standards:

United StatesInvestment advisers owe a fiduciary duty to act in your best interest throughout the relationship. Broker-dealers must act in your best interest when making a recommendation, under Regulation Best Interest. Check either on FINRA BrokerCheck or the SEC's adviser search.
United KingdomFirms must be authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority; check its online register. Since April 2026, authorised firms can also offer "targeted support", suggestions designed for groups of customers in similar situations, between general guidance and full advice. Complaints go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.
IndiaSEBI-registered investment advisers give fee-based advice and are kept separate from distribution. Mutual fund distributors earn commission, which is built into "regular" fund plans; "direct" plans of the same funds have lower costs because they pay none.
TaiwanAdvisers, banks and insurers are licensed by the Financial Supervisory Commission. Many investments and insurance policies are sold through bank wealth managers and insurance agents on commission. Disputes can go to the Financial Ombudsman Institution.

Sources: US SEC; Cornell Law School LII; UK FCA; Economic Laws Practice, on SEBI rules.

Check before you trust.

1Confirm registrationLook the person and firm up on the regulator's own register, not a link they send you.
2Ask how they are paid, in writingEvery fee and commission, and whether they act as a fiduciary at all times.
3Check qualifications and recordLook for recognized credentials such as the CFP, and for any disciplinary history.
4Understand what you are buyingIf you cannot explain a product, its costs and how you would exit it, do not buy it yet.

Red flags that should stop you:

Guaranteed high returnsReal investments with high returns carry risk. Guarantees of both are a classic sign of fraud.
Pressure to act nowDeadlines, "limited spots" or anger at your questions.
Payment to a personal accountLegitimate firms take money through the firm or a regulated platform, never into an individual's account.
Contact out of the blueUnsolicited calls or messages, including on social media and messaging apps, are a common start of investment scams.

Many people do not need ongoing advice. With simple finances, low-cost index funds and a written plan, you can manage on your own and pay for one-off advice when needed. Advice tends to be worth more for complex taxes, a business, an inheritance, retirement income decisions, divorce, cross-border lives, or if you know you are likely to panic in a market fall.

How professionals use it

Compliance officers
Check within firms that advice is suitable, fees are disclosed and conflicts are managed.
Regulators
License firms and individuals, set conduct and fee rules, and act against misconduct.
Ombudsman services
Resolve complaints between consumers and financial firms, often free to the consumer.
Professional bodies
Set exams and ethics codes for credentials such as the CFP, and can withdraw them.

Know how your adviser is paid, check them on the regulator's register, count every fee, and never let pressure or promises replace understanding.

9 mixed problems from lessons 24 to 26 · about 15 minutes

The problems aren't labeled with a lesson, so part of the work is choosing the right idea. Work each one on paper, then open the answer. All figures are illustrative unless a country's actual rules are named.

  1. A US college lists tuition and fees of $45,000 a year. A family's offer includes $28,000 a year in grants. What is the net tuition per year, and over four years if nothing changes?

    Show answer
    Funding education45,000 − 28,000 = $17,000 a year, or $68,000 over four years, before housing and living costs.

  2. You want 60,000 saved for a child's education in 15 years and expect a 5% yearly return. Roughly how much must you save each month?

    Show answer
    Funding educationAbout 224 a month. You pay in about 40,400; investment growth supplies the rest.

  3. An English graduate on a Plan 5 loan earns £35,000 a year. How much do they repay each year and each month, and does it depend on how much they borrowed?

    Show answer
    Funding education9% of (35,000 − 25,000) = £900 a year, or £75 a month. No: repayments depend only on income, though the balance decides whether they finish repaying within 40 years.

  4. In India, a borrower under the old tax regime paid ₹80,000 of interest on an education loan this year and pays 30% tax on that slice of income. Roughly how much tax does the deduction save, before cess?

    Show answer
    Funding education80,000 × 30% = ₹24,000. Under the new regime the deduction is not available.

  5. A home costs 300,000. You'd put down 60,000 and borrow 240,000 at 5%. Taxes, insurance and upkeep are 2% of the price a year, the deposit could earn 5% invested, and you expect the home to rise 2% a year. Ignoring buying and selling costs, which is cheaper than renting a similar home for 16,800 a year?

    Show answer
    Big life decisionsOwning: 12,000 interest + 6,000 upkeep + 3,000 lost return − 6,000 rise = 15,000, so owning is about 1,800 a year cheaper. Add buying and selling costs and a short stay could reverse that.

  6. Job A pays $80,000 with a 3% retirement match. Job B pays $85,000 with no match but costs you $4,000 more a year in health insurance premiums. Which is worth more, counting only these items?

    Show answer
    Big life decisionsA: 80,000 + 2,400 = $82,400. B: 85,000 − 4,000 = $81,000. Job A, despite the lower salary.

  7. In Taiwan, a household with NT$1 million of disposable income a year looks at a NT$15 million home. What is the house price-to-income ratio? If they save 30% of income, how long to save a 20% deposit?

    Show answer
    Big life decisionsRatio: 15, about Taipei's 2025 level. Deposit NT$3 million ÷ NT$300,000 a year = 10 years, before any investment growth.

  8. 200,000 is invested for 20 years at 6% a year before fees. Option A charges 1% a year in total, Option B 0.2%. Roughly how much more does B end with?

    Show answer
    Working with financial professionalsA: 200,000 × 1.0520 ≈ 530,700. B: 200,000 × 1.05820 ≈ 617,700. B ends about 87,000 higher.

  9. A family in India has ₹50 lakh of investments that a SEBI-registered adviser will advise on. What is the most the adviser may charge a year under the assets-based mode, and under the fixed-fee mode?

    Show answer
    Working with financial professionalsAssets-based: 2.5% × ₹50 lakh = ₹1.25 lakh. Fixed fee: up to ₹1.51 lakh per family. These are ceilings; many advisers charge less.
