Changing money behavior

Why willpower alone rarely changes money habits, and the tools that do: automation, defaults, friction, commitment devices and if-then plans. With the evidence behind them, the tools available in the US, the UK, India and Taiwan, and a simple plan to start this month.

Money psychology · Lesson 2 of 2 · 10 minutesBefore this: Your money mindset

In plain words

Good intentions fade, but systems keep working. Set up automatic saving on payday, make good choices the default and bad ones harder, decide in advance what you will do in tempting moments, and start small. When you slip, restart rather than give up.

Changing the default changed the behavior of millions:

Just over 40%of eligible UK private sector employees were saving in a workplace pension in 2012, before automatic enrollment
vs
90%of eligible employees in Great Britain were saving into one in 2025

Sources: Institute for Fiscal Studies; UK Department for Work and Pensions.

Design your surroundings instead of relying on willpower. Willpower runs low when you are tired, stressed or busy, which is exactly when most spending decisions happen. People who manage money well usually do not resist temptation more often; they arrange their lives so they face it less. The tools below change what happens automatically, so good choices need no effort.

Five tools that work:

1Automate savingSet transfers to savings and investments for the day your pay arrives, so you never see the money in your spending account. "Pay yourself first" works because it removes the monthly decision.
2Use good defaultsJoin your employer's retirement plan, accept automatic increases, and set bills to pay automatically, at least the minimum, so a busy month never brings a late fee.
3Add friction to spendingRemove saved cards from shopping sites, unsubscribe from sale emails, delete apps you overuse, and wait 24 hours or longer before nonessential purchases above a set amount.
4Commit in advanceDecide now to save more later, such as from your next raise. Keep savings in a separate account, ideally at a different bank, and tell someone your goal.
5Plan "if-then" responses"If friends suggest an expensive dinner, then I'll suggest a cheaper place." "If the market falls 20%, then I'll keep my monthly investment going." Deciding in calm moments makes it easier to act in hard ones.

In the first company to try "Save More Tomorrow", employees committed to raise their retirement saving each time they got a pay raise. Those who joined started at an average saving rate of 3.5%. Where were they after about four years?

Each country has tools that make automation easy:

United StatesMany 401(k) plans enroll workers automatically and raise contributions each year; new plans set up since the end of 2022 generally must do both from 2025. Banks and brokers offer automatic transfers and recurring investments.
United KingdomAutomatic enrollment puts eligible employees into a workplace pension unless they opt out. Standing orders to savings accounts and regular monthly investments into ISAs are widely available.
IndiaA systematic investment plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount in a mutual fund each month by auto-debit. Recurring deposits and UPI AutoPay mandates can automate saving and bill payments.
TaiwanBanks and brokers offer regular fixed-amount investing in funds, ETFs and some shares. Employees can also add voluntary contributions of up to 6% of wages to their labor pension account, deducted from salary.

Track a little, and celebrate progress. Checking your accounts once a week, or reviewing spending once a month, keeps you aware without becoming a chore. Track one or two numbers that matter, such as your savings rate or remaining debt, and mark milestones like paying off a card or reaching one month of expenses saved.

Expect slips and plan for them. New habits take weeks or months to settle, and everyone has bad months. A missed goal is information, not failure: adjust the plan, make it easier if needed, and restart the next payday. The all-or-nothing thought "I've already blown the budget this month" does more damage than the overspending itself.

Start this month with four steps:

Week 1Set one automatic transfer to savings on payday, even a small one.
Week 2Turn on automatic bill payments and remove one source of temptation.
Week 3Join or increase your workplace retirement saving, and schedule an automatic increase.
Week 4Write two if-then plans for your hardest money moments, and pick a date to review.

How professionals use it

Policymakers
Use defaults such as automatic enrollment to raise saving across whole populations, as the UK did from 2012.
Employers and plan designers
Choose default contribution rates, automatic increases and default funds for workplace plans.
Financial coaches
Help clients set up automation, if-then plans and regular reviews, and support them through setbacks.
Behavioral scientists
Test which reminders, defaults and designs actually change behavior, and which only seem to.

Make good money choices automatic, make bad ones harder, decide in advance, and restart whenever you slip.

Estate and giving · Money psychology
10 mixed problems from lessons 27 to 30 · about 15 minutes

The problems aren't labeled with a lesson, so part of the work is choosing the right idea. Work each one on paper, then open the answer. All figures are illustrative unless a country's actual rules are named.

  1. In the UK in 2026/27, a widow leaves an £800,000 estate, including a home, to her children. Her late husband's allowances were not used. Roughly how much inheritance tax is due? What if no allowances had been transferred?

    Show answer
    Estate planningWith both sets of allowances: 2 × (£325,000 + £175,000) = £1,000,000, so no tax. With only her own £500,000: (800,000 − 500,000) × 40% = £120,000.

  2. An American dies in 2026 with an estate of $9 million. Is federal estate tax due? What else should the family check?

    Show answer
    Estate planningNo federal estate tax: $9 million is below the $15 million exemption. They should check their state, since some states tax estates far smaller, such as above $2 million in Massachusetts.

  3. In Taiwan, an estate is worth NT$40 million. After the NT$13.33 million exemption and NT$6.67 million of deductions, what is the taxable estate and the estate tax?

    Show answer
    Estate planningTaxable: 40 − 13.33 − 6.67 = NT$20 million. That is within the 10% bracket, so the tax is NT$2 million.

  4. In India, a father names his elder son as nominee on a bank account. His will leaves everything equally to his two children. After he dies, who ultimately owns the account money?

    Show answer
    Estate planningBoth children equally, under the will. The nominee generally receives the money as a custodian for the legal heirs, not as its owner.

  5. A UK basic-rate taxpayer and a higher-rate taxpayer each give £40 with Gift Aid. How much does each charity receive, and what does each gift really cost the donor?

    Show answer
    Charitable givingEach charity receives £50. The basic-rate donor's cost is £40. The higher-rate donor claims back 20% of £50, so their cost is £30.

  6. In the US in 2026, a single person taking the standard deduction gives $1,500 in cash to charity. How much can they deduct? An itemizer with adjusted gross income of $150,000 gives $5,000: how much can they deduct?

    Show answer
    Charitable givingNon-itemizer: $1,000, the cap. Itemizer: the floor is 0.5% × 150,000 = $750, so $4,250.

  7. An investor sells all their shares after a 20% market fall, saying, "I can't bear to lose more." Which mental shortcut is at work, and what two tools could have helped?

    Show answer
    Your money mindsetLoss aversion: the pain of further losses outweighs the chance of recovery. A written if-then plan ("if the market falls 20%, I keep investing") and automatic monthly investing remove the in-the-moment decision.

  8. Your take-home pay is 2,400 a month and you spend 160 hours a month working and commuting. How many hours of work does a 600 item cost?

    Show answer
    Your money mindsetReal hourly pay: 2,400 ÷ 160 = 15. The item costs 600 ÷ 15 = 40 hours, about five working days.

  9. You earn 50,000 and start saving 4%, rising by 1 percentage point a year up to 10%. Ignoring pay rises, how much do you save in year 4, and in which year do you first reach 10%?

    Show answer
    Changing money behaviorYear 4 rate: 4 + 3 = 7%, so 3,500. You reach 10% in year 7.

  10. Name one tool from each of these to automate monthly investing: a US worker, an Indian investor, and a Taiwanese employee wanting more retirement saving.

    Show answer
    Changing money behaviorUS: 401(k) automatic enrollment and annual increases or a recurring brokerage investment. India: a SIP by auto-debit. Taiwan: voluntary labor pension contributions of up to 6% of wages, or regular fixed-amount investing.
