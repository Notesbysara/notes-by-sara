Loans and mortgages

How an installment loan is repaid, what the APR adds to the interest rate, fixed and variable rates in the US, the UK, India and Taiwan, how the loan term and down payment change the total cost, and what paying early saves.

Borrowing · Lesson 3 of 4 · 9 minutesBefore this: Credit cards

In plain words

A loan gives you a lump sum now, and you repay it in equal monthly payments that cover interest and part of the amount borrowed. Early payments are mostly interest. A longer loan means smaller payments but much more interest in total. A mortgage is a loan secured on a home, so the lender can take the home if you stop paying.

Two US mortgages for the same $300,000, at average rates in mid-November 2026:

$2,018a month for 30 years at 7.11%, with about $426,500 of interest in total
vs
$2,605a month for 15 years at 6.45%, with about $168,900 of interest in total

Rates: Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey, 12 November 2026. Payments and totals calculated from them.

The shorter loan costs $587 more a month but saves over a quarter of a million dollars. Choosing a loan is mostly a trade between a payment you can manage now and the total you pay over time.

Most personal loans are repaid in equal installments. Each monthly payment covers that month's interest on the balance, and the rest reduces the amount owed, the principal. As the balance falls, less of each payment goes to interest and more to principal. The payment is an annuity, calculated with the formula from the Foundations lesson on annuities and loan payments. On the 30-year loan above, the split shifts like this:

Year 1$21,234$2,983
Year 10$18,571$5,647
Year 20$12,745$11,473
Year 30$907$23,310

$300,000 over 30 years at 7.11%, the same $24,217 paid every year.

That is why, after several years of payments, many borrowers are surprised by how much they still owe.

Compare loans by APR, not just the interest rate. Lenders often charge fees to set up a loan, such as arrangement, origination or processing fees. The annual percentage rate (APR) spreads those fees over the loan and adds them to the interest, so it shows the full yearly cost. Two loans with the same interest rate can have different APRs, and a low rate with large fees can cost more than a slightly higher rate with none. Laws in many countries, including the US, the UK and India, require lenders to show the APR or an equivalent figure.

A secured loan is backed by something you own. A mortgage is secured on the home and a car loan usually on the car; if you stop paying, the lender can repossess it. Because the lender's risk is lower, secured loans usually charge lower rates than unsecured ones, such as personal loans and credit cards, which rely only on your promise and your credit record.

The rate can be fixed or variable, and what is normal differs by country. A fixed rate keeps the payment the same; a variable or floating rate moves with a benchmark, so payments rise and fall with the central bank's interest rate, as the Economics lesson on central banks described.

United StatesThe 30-year fixed-rate mortgage is the standard: the payment never changes, and borrowers can usually refinance if rates fall. It averaged 7.28% on 19 November 2026.
United KingdomMost borrowers fix for a short period, usually two or five years, then move to the lender's variable rate unless they take a new deal. In November 2026 the average two-year fix was 5.97% and the five-year fix 6.00%, a three-year high.
IndiaMost home loans have floating rates. For floating-rate loans to individuals for non-business purposes, taken or renewed from 1 January 2026, lenders may not charge anything for paying early.
TaiwanThe government's Youth Housing Loan 3.0, from 1 August 2026, offers first-time buyers under 50 up to 40 years with up to five years of interest-only payments, a subsidized rate of 1.775% for the first three years, and limits on income and home price.

Sources: Freddie Mac via GlobeNewswire; Mortgage Professional, on Moneyfacts data; Upstox; Reserve Bank of India; Focus Taiwan; Taipei Times.

A fixed rate costs certainty: it is often a little higher at the start, but it protects the budget if rates rise. A variable rate is cheaper when rates fall and painful when they climb. Whatever you choose, check that you could still afford the payment if the rate were a few percentage points higher.

An interest-only period, like Taiwan's grace period, lowers payments at the start, but the balance does not fall during it, so the payments afterward are larger and the total interest is higher.

A $300,000 loan at 7% costs $2,696 a month over 15 years. If you stretch it to 30 years at the same rate, what happens to the monthly payment?

The down payment changes both the risk and the price. The loan-to-value ratio (LTV) is the loan divided by the home's value: borrowing $270,000 on a $300,000 home is 90% LTV. A bigger down payment means a smaller loan, less interest, and usually a lower rate, because the lender is protected if prices fall. Lenders often charge more, or require mortgage insurance, at high LTVs. In the UK at the end of 2025, for example, the average two-year fix was 5.33% at 95% LTV, against 4.86% across all deals.

Source: Property Industry Eye, on Moneyfacts data.

Other common loans follow the same logic.

Car loansSecured on the car, usually over three to seven years. The car loses value quickly, so a long loan can leave you owing more than the car is worth.
Student loansRules vary most by country. Some government schemes, such as the UK's, link repayments to income and cancel what is left after a set number of years; others work like ordinary loans.
Personal loansUnsecured, with a fixed rate and term. Usually cheaper than a credit card balance, and sometimes used to consolidate card debt.
Payday and short-term loansSmall, fast loans with very high costs once fees are counted as an annual rate. They are best avoided; an emergency fund is the alternative.

Before you sign, check four things. The APR and the total amount you will repay, not only the monthly payment. Whether the rate is fixed, and for how long. What happens if you pay early or miss a payment. And whether the payment still fits your budget if your income dips or the rate rises.

How professionals use it

Mortgage brokers
Compare rates, fees and terms across lenders and match borrowers to the loan that fits their income and plans.
Underwriters
Check income, debts, credit record and the property's value to decide how much a lender will lend and at what rate.
Financial planners
Weigh paying a mortgage down early against saving or investing the same money, based on the rate and the client's goals.
Central banks
Watch how quickly rate changes reach households, which happens faster where most mortgages are variable or short fixes.

Look at the total cost, not just the monthly payment: a longer term, fees and interest-only periods all make a loan cost more.
