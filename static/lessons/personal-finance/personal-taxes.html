Personal taxes

How income tax works in most countries: tax brackets, the difference between your marginal and average rate, deductions and credits, how tax is collected and filed, and how savings are taxed, with examples from the US, the UK, India and Taiwan.

Everyday money · Lesson 6 of 7 · 9 minutesBefore this: Banking basics

In plain words

Most countries tax income in slices. The first slice is taxed at zero or a low rate, and each higher slice at a higher rate. Earning more only puts the extra money in a higher slice, so a raise never shrinks your pay after income tax. Deductions lower the income that gets taxed. Credits cut the tax bill itself.

A common worry: "If my raise pushes me into a higher tax bracket, I could end up with less money." Try it.

In the US in 2026, a single person's taxable income above $50,400 is taxed at 22%, and the slice below it at 12%. Your taxable income rises from $50,000 to $51,000. How much more federal income tax do you pay?

Income tax is charged in slices. Each slice of income has its own rate, and you pay that rate only on the part of your income inside the slice. The US calls the slices brackets, the UK calls them bands, and India calls them slabs. The structure is similar almost everywhere, but the slices and rates differ a lot by country:

United States, 2026After a standard deduction of $16,100 for a single person: 10% on the first $12,400 of taxable income, then 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37% above $640,600. This is federal tax; most states add their own income tax.
United Kingdom, 2026/27The first £12,570 is a tax-free personal allowance. Then 20% up to £50,270, 40% up to £125,140, and 45% above. Scotland sets its own bands on wages.
India, new regime, 2026-27Nil up to ₹4 lakh of taxable income, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, and 30% above ₹24 lakh, in ₹4 lakh steps. A rebate means no tax at all on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh. A 4% cess is added to the tax.
Taiwan, 2026 income5% on net taxable income up to NT$610,000, then 12%, 20%, 30%, and 40% above NT$5.19 million. Net income is what is left after an exemption of NT$101,000, a standard deduction of NT$136,000 and a salary deduction of up to NT$227,000.

Sources: IRS; GOV.UK; ClearTax; Bloomberg Tax; Ramco, on National Taxation Bureau figures.

Here is how the slices add up for a single person in the US earning a salary of $76,100 in 2026. The standard deduction takes off $16,100, leaving $60,000 of taxable income, which falls into three brackets:

Total federal income tax: $7,912. Based on IRS 2026 brackets for a single filer.

That one person has two tax rates, and they answer different questions. The marginal rate is the rate on the next dollar earned, here 22%. It tells you how much of a raise, a bonus or extra work you keep. The average rate, also called the effective rate, is total tax divided by income, here $7,912 ÷ $76,100, or about 10.4%. It tells you how much of your whole income goes in income tax.

A few systems do have ranges where the marginal rate is unusually high. In the UK, the £12,570 personal allowance shrinks by £1 for every £2 of income above £100,000 and is gone at £125,140, so in that range each extra £1 costs 60p in income tax. In India, the rebate ends at ₹12 lakh of taxable income, so a rule called marginal relief caps the tax just above that point at the amount of income over ₹12 lakh. That stops the tax from jumping by far more than the raise, but in this narrow range almost all of the extra income goes in tax, and with the 4% cess added it can slightly exceed it, until the normal calculation takes over at about ₹12.7 lakh of taxable income. Losing a benefit or allowance as income rises can have the same effect. These are the real cases behind the worry in the question above.

Sources: GOV.UK; HDFC Bank.

Inflation can raise your taxes even when tax rates stay the same. If brackets stay fixed while pay rises with prices, more of each person's income moves into higher slices. This is called bracket creep or fiscal drag. The US adjusts its brackets for inflation every year. The UK has kept its £12,570 allowance and £50,270 higher-rate threshold frozen since April 2021 and plans to keep them there until April 2031, which official forecasters expect to pull millions more people into paying tax or into the higher rate.

Sources: ICAEW; Office for Budget Responsibility.

Deductions and credits both lower your tax, but by very different amounts.

$220saved by a $1,000 deduction for someone with a 22% marginal rate. A deduction lowers the income that is taxed, so it is worth your marginal rate times its size.
vs
$1,000saved by a $1,000 credit. A credit comes off the tax bill itself, so it is worth its full amount to anyone who owes at least that much tax.

An example of a credit is the US child tax credit, worth up to $2,200 per qualifying child for 2026. Because a deduction is worth more at a higher marginal rate, the same deduction saves a high earner more than a low earner.

Source: CNBC Select.

Most systems let you take a fixed deduction or list your actual ones. In the US and Taiwan, you choose between a standard deduction and itemizing specific costs, such as charitable gifts or certain interest payments, and itemizing only helps if they add up to more. India gives a different choice: the new regime, now the default, has lower rates and few deductions, while the old regime has higher rates (5%, 20% and 30%) but allows many deductions, such as up to ₹1.5 lakh for certain savings and investments and interest on a home loan. People with large deductions sometimes pay less under the old regime, so it is worth comparing both. In the UK, employees have few deductions; the main one is tax relief on pension contributions.

Sources: PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries; Bajaj Finserv.

For most employees, tax is collected before the pay arrives. Employers withhold an estimate of income tax from each paycheck, as the lesson on income and paychecks showed, and send it to the government. After the year ends, a tax return settles the difference: if too much was withheld you get a refund, and if too little you pay the rest. How much filing you do depends on the country:

United StatesMost people file a return every year. Returns for 2026 are due by 15 April 2027. The form you give your employer, the W-4, controls how much is withheld.
United KingdomThe Pay As You Earn system usually gets employees' tax right through a tax code, so most employees never file. People with self-employment or other untaxed income file a Self Assessment, due online by 31 January after the tax year.
IndiaEmployers deduct tax at source, and people whose income is above the basic exemption limit generally file an income tax return after the financial year ends on 31 March. India replaced its 1961 income tax law with the Income Tax Act 2025 from 1 April 2026, keeping the same slabs.
TaiwanTax is withheld from salaries, and everyone liable files a return each year between 1 May and 31 May for the previous calendar year, with any balance paid at filing.

Sources: IRS; GOV.UK; MCU, on the Income Tax Act 2025; PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries.

A large refund is not a gift: it is your own money that was held without interest during the year. A large bill usually means too little was withheld, often because of a second job, freelance work or investment income with no tax taken off.

Interest on savings is usually taxed as income, but most countries exempt some of it.

United StatesBank interest is taxed at your normal income tax rates. Tax-advantaged accounts, covered later in the retirement accounts lesson, shelter savings for retirement.
United KingdomA personal savings allowance makes the first £1,000 of interest tax-free for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate and nothing for additional-rate. Interest in an ISA is tax-free, with up to £20,000 a year paid in. From April 2027, tax on savings interest rises by 2 percentage points and under-65s can put at most £12,000 a year into cash ISAs.
IndiaInterest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate. Banks may deduct tax at source from interest, which you then count against your final tax.
TaiwanA special deduction for savings and investment covers up to NT$270,000 a year of bank interest and similar income for a household.

Sources: Post Office; TaxAssist; Taiwan Ministry of Finance.

Dividends and gains from selling investments have their own rules, often with lower rates, and are covered in the lesson on taxes and your investments.

Income tax is not the only tax a household pays. Social security contributions, such as US payroll taxes and UK National Insurance, come out of wages too. Sales taxes are built into prices: Taiwan's standard VAT is 5%, the UK's 20%, and India charges GST at several rates, while US sales taxes are set by states and cities. Property taxes and local taxes add more. A full picture of what you pay includes all of them.

Source: PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries.

Good habits make tax season easier wherever you live. Keep payslips, year-end tax statements, receipts for anything you might deduct, and records of savings and investment income for several years. Check that withholding still fits after a new job, a second income or a change in family. And be wary of messages claiming to be from the tax office: tax authorities in many countries warn that scammers use fake refund offers and threats of arrest to get money or personal details.

How professionals use it

Financial planners
Plan around a client's marginal rate: when to take income, which deductions to use, and which accounts to save in.
Accountants and tax preparers
Prepare returns, choose between standard and itemized deductions or between India's two regimes, and check withholding.
Payroll teams
Calculate withholding on every paycheck from each employee's tax details and send it to the tax authority.
Economists
Compare average and marginal rates across income levels to judge how progressive a tax system is and how it affects work and saving.

Your marginal rate decides what you keep from the next unit you earn; your average rate shows what tax takes from all of it.
