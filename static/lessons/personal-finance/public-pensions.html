Public pensions

How government pensions work, what they pay and when in the US, the UK, Taiwan and India, the big decision of when to claim, why their finances are under strain as populations age, and how to check what you are likely to get.

Retirement · Lesson 3 of 4 · 9 minutesBefore this: How much you need for retirement

In plain words

Most countries run a public pension: workers pay in through payroll taxes or social insurance, and retirees receive a monthly payment for life, usually raised with prices. It is rarely enough to live on alone, but it is valuable because it lasts as long as you do and keeps up with inflation. When you start claiming changes how much you get every month for the rest of your life.

The same worker in Taiwan, with 30 years of labor insurance at the top insured salary of NT$45,800, could claim the old-age pension at three different ages:

NT$17,038 a monthclaiming early at 60, 20% less for life
NT$21,297 a monthclaiming at the normal age of 65
NT$25,556 a monthdelaying to 70, 20% more for life

Source: Storm Media, using the Bureau of Labor Insurance formula.

Claiming age is one of the biggest retirement decisions, and most systems make it permanent. A public pension is longevity insurance: unlike savings, it does not run out, so the longer you live, the more it pays in total.

Most public pensions are pay-as-you-go social insurance. Today's workers and employers pay contributions, which fund today's retirees' pensions. Your benefit usually depends on how many years you contributed and, in many systems, on your earnings. Payments are typically raised each year with prices or wages, which no ordinary savings account guarantees. They are distinct from the workplace and personal accounts in the lesson on retirement accounts, which hold your own invested money.

How the main systems work:

United States: Social SecurityBenefits depend on your 35 highest years of earnings. You can claim from 62, with a reduced benefit, until 70; for anyone born in 1960 or later, the full retirement age is 67. Benefits rise with inflation: by 3.6% for 2027, after 2.8% for 2026.
United Kingdom: State PensionThe full new State Pension is £241.30 a week, or £12,547.60 a year, in 2026/27. You need 35 qualifying years of National Insurance for the full amount and at least 10 for any. The age is rising from 66 to 67 between April 2026 and April 2028, and the "triple lock" raises it each year by the highest of inflation, earnings growth or 2.5%.
Taiwan: Labor InsuranceThe old-age pension is the higher of two formulas, mainly average insured salary × years × 1.55%, using the best 60 months of insured salary. The normal claiming age reached 65 in 2026; you can claim up to five years early at 4% less per year, or up to five years late at 4% more. It is adjusted when prices have risen 5%.
India: mostly limitedThere is no universal contributory pension for most workers. Formal-sector employees get a small pension from the Employees' Pension Scheme within the EPF. The Atal Pension Yojana, a voluntary scheme for workers aged 18 to 40 outside the formal sector, guarantees ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 a month from 60 and passed 9 crore enrollments in April 2026.

Sources: El Cronista; 24/7 Wall St.; House of Commons Library; Which?; CommonWealth Magazine; China Times Wantrich; New Kerala, on PFRDA data.

When to claim is a trade between more money sooner and more money for longer. In the US, claiming at 62 instead of 67 cuts the monthly benefit by 30% for life, while each year of delay after 67 adds 8%, up to 24% more at 70.

The right claiming age depends on your situation. Delaying tends to make sense if you are healthy, expect to live long, can afford to wait using other savings, or, in the US, are the higher earner in a couple, because a larger benefit can also mean a larger survivor benefit. Claiming earlier can make sense if your health is poor, you need the income, or you have no other savings to bridge the gap. Because a bigger lifelong, inflation-linked income protects against living longer than expected, many planners treat delay as a way to buy longevity insurance cheaply.

Public pension finances are under strain as populations age. With fewer workers per retiree, systems must raise contributions, raise ages or cut benefits. Pension ages are rising in the UK, Taiwan and many other countries. In the US, the 2026 Trustees Report projects that the trust fund paying retirement and survivor benefits will run out of reserves in late 2032, after which incoming taxes would cover about 78% of scheduled benefits unless Congress acts. Changes are likely in many countries, but public pensions are rarely abolished, so plan on receiving them, perhaps somewhat reduced, rather than nothing.

Sources: Beinsure, on the 2026 Trustees Reports; OECD, Pensions at a Glance 2025.

Check your record early, while gaps can still be fixed. Missing years or wrongly recorded earnings reduce your pension, and most can only be corrected within a time limit.

United StatesA my Social Security account at the SSA website shows your earnings record and estimated benefits at each claiming age.
United KingdomThe government's "Check your State Pension forecast" service shows your qualifying years, and you may be able to pay voluntary contributions to fill gaps.
TaiwanThe Bureau of Labor Insurance's online services estimate your labor insurance pension and labor pension account together.
IndiaThe EPFO passbook shows your EPF and pension scheme contributions; check that every employer has paid in.

Sources: UK Tax Drag; China Times Wantrich.

How professionals use it

Financial planners
Model claiming ages for clients and couples, and plan how savings can bridge the years before a delayed claim.
Actuaries
Project public pension finances decades ahead, as in the US Trustees Reports and Taiwan's labor insurance actuarial reviews.
Policy analysts
Compare reforms, such as higher ages, higher contributions or different indexing, and their effects on different generations.
Benefits advisers
Help people check their records, fill contribution gaps and claim everything they are entitled to.

Know what your public pension will pay, check your record early, and choose your claiming age as carefully as any investment.
