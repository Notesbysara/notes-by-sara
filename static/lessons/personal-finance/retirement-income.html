Retirement income

How to turn a lifetime of savings into a reliable income: withdrawing from investments, buying an annuity, or combining the two; why the order of returns early in retirement matters so much; and the lump-sum and withdrawal rules in the US, the UK, India and Taiwan.

Retirement · Lesson 4 of 4 · 9 minutesBefore this: Public pensions

In plain words

In retirement the job flips from building savings to spending them without running out. You can take money out of your investments bit by bit, which keeps control but leaves you exposed to markets and a long life, or buy an annuity, which pays a guaranteed income for life but gives up the lump sum. Many retirees combine the two, and keep a cash buffer so they never have to sell after a crash.

Two retirees each start with $1,000,000, withdraw $40,000 a year and get exactly the same returns over 25 years, two bad years and 23 good ones, only in a different order (illustrative figures, after inflation):

$646,000left after 25 years when the two bad years (−20% and −15%) come first
vs
$1,609,000left when the same two bad years come last

This is sequence-of-returns risk. Losses early in retirement hurt most, because withdrawals are taken from a shrunken pot, locking in the losses before markets recover. The same returns that are harmless while you are saving can be damaging once you start withdrawing.

There are three main ways to turn savings into income.

Withdraw from investmentsKeep the money invested and take a regular amount, called drawdown in the UK. Flexible and inheritable, but it can run out if returns are poor or you live long.
Buy an annuityHand a lump sum to an insurer in return for a guaranteed income for life. No market or longevity risk, but usually no way back and little or nothing left for heirs.
Combine themCover essential spending with guaranteed income, public pensions plus an annuity, and fund extras from investments.

Withdrawing works best with a plan. The research in the lesson on how much you need suggests starting at about 4% of the pot and adjusting with inflation, or starting higher if you are willing to cut back after bad years, a "guardrails" approach. A common way to manage sequence risk is to hold one to two years of spending in cash, a few years in bonds, and the rest in shares. In a bad year you spend from cash instead of selling shares at low prices, then refill the cash in good years.

You retire with 1 year of spending in cash and the rest invested. In your first year, shares fall 25%. What should you usually do?

Annuities turn savings into a pension you cannot outlive. The income depends on your age, health, interest rates and the options you choose. In the UK in November 2026, £100,000 could buy a healthy 65-year-old about £7,500 a year for life from a single-life annuity with level payments, but only about £5,200 if the income rises with inflation. Joint-life annuities that keep paying a surviving partner pay less, and people in poor health can get more through enhanced annuities. Quotes vary by hundreds of pounds between providers, so compare before buying.

Sources: Legal & General, 23 November 2026; Hargreaves Lansdown; Which?.

Annuities suit people who want certainty, expect to live long, or worry about managing investments as they age. They suit less well people in poor health, those who need flexibility, or those whose essential spending is already covered by public pensions. Buying in stages through retirement, rather than all at once, spreads the interest-rate risk and leaves options open.

Each country has its own rules for taking the money out:

United StatesWithdrawals from traditional 401(k)s and IRAs are taxed as income; Roth withdrawals are usually tax-free. Required minimum distributions from traditional accounts start at 73. Annuities are optional and bought from insurers.
United KingdomFrom 55, rising to 57 in April 2028, you can usually take a quarter of a pension tax-free, up to an overall cap, then use the rest for drawdown, an annuity or both; withdrawals beyond the tax-free part are taxed as income.
IndiaSince December 2025, non-government NPS subscribers with more than ₹12 lakh can take up to 80% as a lump sum at exit and must use at least 20% to buy an annuity, instead of 60% and 40% before; withdrawals can be deferred up to 85.
TaiwanThe labor pension account can be taken as a lump sum, or monthly if you contributed for at least 15 years; since 2026, those who choose monthly payments have 30 days after the first payment to switch to a lump sum, once. Labor insurance can be taken as a monthly pension or, for some, a lump sum.

Sources: Hargreaves Lansdown; Business Standard; Upstox; China Times Wantrich; Business Next.

Larger lump sums bring more freedom and more responsibility. A sum taken all at once has to last for decades, and the temptation to spend it early, lend it to family or put it into a risky scheme is real. Taking a monthly pension, or buying some annuity income, protects against both overspending and fraud in later life, a point Taiwanese planners often make about choosing monthly labor pension payments.

Source: CommonWealth Magazine.

A few practical habits make retirement income smoother.

Pay yourself monthlyMove a set amount from investments to a current account each month, like a salary, to make budgeting easy.
Mind the tax orderWhich account you draw from, and when, can change your lifetime tax bill a lot; this is worth professional advice.
Simplify as you ageFewer accounts and more guaranteed income make finances easier to manage, and harder for scammers to target.
Review every yearCheck spending, the withdrawal rate and the mix, and adjust gradually rather than in a panic.

How professionals use it

Retirement planners
Design withdrawal plans, cash buffers and tax-efficient drawing orders, and decide how much guaranteed income each client needs.
Insurers and actuaries
Price annuities from life expectancy and interest rates, and invest the premiums to meet lifetime payments.
Pension administrators
Run payouts, lump sums and annuity purchases under each scheme's rules, such as India's NPS and Taiwan's labor pension.
Regulators
Require clear information on retirement options and watch for pension scams aimed at people taking lump sums.

Cover essentials with guaranteed income, keep a cash buffer for bad years, and withdraw the rest at a pace your savings can sustain.

  1. Your employer matches 100% of what you save, up to 4% of your $50,000 salary. You save 3%. How much free money are you leaving behind each year?

    Show answer
    Retirement accountsYou get a $1,500 match on your $1,500. Saving 4% would bring $2,000, so you leave $500 a year behind.

  2. In the UK in 2026/27 you earn £30,000. Under the auto-enrollment minimum, how much goes into your pension in total each year, and how much of that must the employer pay?

    Show answer
    Retirement accountsQualifying earnings are £30,000 − £6,240 = £23,760. 8% of that is £1,900.80 in total, of which the employer pays at least 3%: £712.80.

  3. A UK retiree wants £30,000 a year and expects the full new State Pension of £12,547.60. Using a 4% withdrawal rate, roughly how big a pot do they need?

    Show answer
    How much you need for retirementThe gap is about £17,452. Divided by 4%, that is about £436,000.

  4. You will need $40,000 a year in today's money when you retire in 24 years. If prices rise 3% a year, about how much will that be in the money of that time?

    Show answer

  5. In Taiwan, a worker has 25 years of labor insurance at an average insured salary of NT$40,000. What is the monthly old-age pension at 65, and at 62?

    Show answer
    Public pensionsFormula B: 40,000 × 25 × 1.55% = NT$15,500 (formula A gives NT$10,750, so B applies). At 65: NT$15,500. At 62, three years early at 4% each: 15,500 × 0.88 = NT$13,640, for life.

  6. A US worker's full benefit at 67 is $1,800 a month. What would they get claiming at 62, and at 70?

    Show answer
    Public pensionsAt 62, 70%: $1,260. At 70, 124%: $2,232, both before future inflation increases.

  7. Two retirees earn the same average return over 25 years. Why might one run out of money and the other not?

    Show answer
    Retirement incomeSequence of returns. If losses come early, withdrawals are taken from a smaller pot and the losses are locked in before markets recover. A cash buffer and flexible withdrawals reduce the risk.

  8. A 65-year-old in the UK uses £100,000 to buy a level annuity paying £7,500 a year. Roughly how many years until the payments add up to the £100,000? What would a version rising with inflation trade off?

    Show answer
    Retirement income100,000 ÷ 7,500 ≈ 13.3 years. An inflation-linked annuity starts much lower, about £5,200 here, but its income keeps its buying power, which matters more the longer you live.
