Receivables

When a company sells on credit, it records the sale before the cash arrives. Some customers never pay, so accounting sets aside an estimate for that loss up front.

Key accounts · Lesson 2 of 7 · 5 minutesBefore this: Revenue recognition

$32 million owed. How much will actually come in?

Example, with made-up numbers. Brewline's wholesale arm sells beans to offices and hotels, who pay 30 days later. At year end, customers owe $32 million. A few are already late, and one hasn't paid in four months. What should the balance sheet show?

The idea

A credit sale creates an account receivable: Dr Accounts receivable, Cr Revenue. Since some will never be collected, the company estimates the likely loss and records it in the same period as the sale, following the matching principle:

Allowance for doubtful accountsAn estimate of receivables that won't be paid. It sits under receivables and reduces them
Bad debt expenseThe cost of topping the allowance up to the right size, charged on the income statement
Write-offRemoving a specific debt once it's clearly lost. It uses up allowance already set aside

A common way to size the allowance is an aging schedule: the older a debt, the less likely it's paid.

Most money owed is recent and safe. The small, old balances carry most of the expected loss.

Quick check

A customer owing $0.5 million goes bust and the debt is written off against the allowance. What happens to net income this year?

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Treating the write-off as the expense. Under the allowance method, the cost hits when the estimate is made, not when a debt is finally written off.
  • Missing a shrinking allowance. Cutting the allowance boosts profit. If late debts grow while the allowance shrinks, ask why.
  • Ignoring receivable days. When receivables grow faster than sales, customers may be paying slower, or sales may be pushed through on easy terms.

Remember

  • Credit sales create receivables. The allowance for doubtful accounts estimates what won't be paid.
  • Bad debt expense tops the allowance up. A write-off then uses it, with no new expense.
  • Receivable days = receivables ÷ revenue × 365: how long customers take to pay.

Three questions. Try them without scrolling up.
