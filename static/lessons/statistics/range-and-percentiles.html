Range and percentiles

An average tells you the middle. Range and percentiles tell you how wide the data runs, and exactly where one value sits among the rest.

Describing data · Lesson 3 of 8 · 5 minutesBefore this: Mean, median and mode

Is 8% a good year?

Example, with made-up numbers. Your fund returned 8% this year. Ten similar funds returned −6%, 1%, 3%, 4%, 5%, 6%, 7%, 9%, 11% and 24%. Was your fund near the top, the middle or the bottom?

The idea

Put all eleven returns in order, yours included. Three simple measures describe how they spread out:

−6   1   3   4   5   6   7   8   9   11   24

The shaded band holds the middle half of the funds. The range runs from the lowest to the highest.

RangeHighest minus lowest: 24 − (−6) = 30 points. Quick, but one extreme value can stretch it.
PercentileThe value with a given share of the data below it. The 25th percentile has a quarter below it.
Interquartile rangeThe spread of the middle half: 75th percentile minus 25th percentile, 9 − 3 = 6 points. Extremes don't move it.

The 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles are called the quartiles, because they cut the data into four equal parts. The 50th is the median you already know. Your fund, at 8%, beat 7 of the other 10, so it sits around the 70th percentile.

Quick check

What share of the data lies inside the interquartile range?

Worked example: one wild fund

The 24% fund had an unusual year. Take it out, and compare the two measures of spread:

Range, all 11 funds24 − (−6) = 30 points
Range, without the 24% fund11 − (−6) = 17 points
Interquartile range, all 119 − 3 = 6 points
Interquartile range, without it8 − 3 = 5 points
One fund changed the range by13 points

The interquartile range moved by just 1 point. When a few extreme values could mislead, the interquartile range gives a steadier picture. To find the quartiles here, take the median of the lower half and of the upper half. Spreadsheets use slightly different methods and may give 3.5 and 8.5. Both are fine.

Where finance uses them

Fund rankings"Top quartile" means better than at least 75% of similar funds.
Value at RiskA bank's 5th percentile of daily returns: a bad day that happens about one day in twenty.
Pay and pricesSalary bands and house prices are often reported as the median with the 25th and 75th percentiles.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Not sorting first. Percentiles only make sense on data in order, from lowest to highest.
  • Reading a percentile as a percent return. "The 70th percentile" is a position among the funds, not a 70% return.
  • Trusting the range alone. A single extreme value can double it. Check the interquartile range too.

Remember

  • Range = highest − lowest. Simple, but stretched by extremes.
  • A percentile tells you how much of the data lies below a value. The quartiles are the 25th, 50th and 75th.
  • Interquartile range = 75th − 25th percentile: the spread of the middle half, steady when there are extremes.
