Skewness

Average and spread don't tell the whole story. Data can lean to one side, with a long tail of rare, extreme values. In finance, which side that tail is on can matter more than anything else.

Describing data · Lesson 5 of 8 · 5 minutesBefore this: Mean, median and mode, Variance and standard deviation

Small wins or rare disasters?

Example, with made-up numbers. Strategy X gains 1% in nine months out of ten and loses 15% in the tenth. Strategy Y loses 1% in nine months and gains 15% in the tenth. Their swings are the same size. Are they equally risky?

The idea

Skewness describes which way data leans. Most values bunch up on one side, and a thin tail of rare, extreme values stretches out on the other:

The skew is named after the tail. The mean gets pulled toward the tail. The median stays with the crowd.

Negative skewLong tail to the left: frequent small gains, rare big losses. The mean sits below the median.
Positive skewLong tail to the right: frequent small losses, rare big wins. The mean sits above the median.

That gives a quick test that needs no formula: compare the mean and the median. In a spreadsheet, =SKEW() gives a number: below zero is negative skew, above zero is positive.

Quick check

In a country, the mean income is $80,000 and the median is $55,000. Which way are incomes skewed?

Worked example: strategies X and Y

Strategy X: nine months of +1%, one of −15%mean −0.6%, median +1%
Strategy Y: nine months of −1%, one of +15%mean +0.6%, median −1%
Standard deviation of each5.1%
Skewness (=SKEW)X: −3.2 · Y: +3.2

Same spread, opposite skew. X looks calm and steady for months, then gives it all back in one go. Strategies that sell insurance or options often look like X. Y is the lottery-ticket shape of a venture fund or a single small company.

Why it matters

Standard deviation treats a surprise gain and a surprise loss the same. Investors don't. A long left tail means rare but severe losses, and these are easy to miss in a few calm years of data. Stock market returns lean this way: most days are quiet, but crashes are sharper than rallies.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Naming the skew after the bulk of the data. It's named after the tail. Most values on the right with a tail to the left is negative skew.
  • Thinking a steady track record means low risk. A negatively skewed strategy can look smooth for years before its rare big loss.
  • Trusting skewness from a few data points. One extreme value decides it. Small samples can show skew that isn't really there, or miss it.

Remember

  • Skewness is the direction of the long tail: left is negative, right is positive.
  • The mean is pulled toward the tail, so mean below median suggests negative skew.
  • Negative skew means rare, severe losses, and standard deviation alone won't warn you.

Check yourself

Three questions. Try them without scrolling up.

1. A set of data has a long tail stretching to the left. What is its skew?

2. A fund's mean monthly return is 0.3% and its median is 0.9%. What does that suggest?

3. Which strategy has positive skew?
