Infinite series

Some sums never end but still add up to a fixed number. That's how finance puts a price on payments that go on forever.

Sequences and series · Lesson 3 of 3 · 5 minutesBefore this: Summing a geometric series

Can "forever" have a price?

Example, with made-up numbers. A share pays a $5 dividend at the end of every year, forever. Using a 10% discount rate, what is it worth today? The payments never stop. Does that make the value endless too?

The idea

Each payment is worth a little less today than the one before, because it's further away. In today's money they're $4.55, then $4.13, then $3.76, and so on, a geometric sequence with a ratio of 1 ÷ 1.1 = 0.909. Add them up year by year and watch the total:

The total keeps rising but slows down. After 10 years it's $30.72, after 30 years $47.13, and it never passes $50.

The sum settles to a number. Mathematicians say it converges. That happens because the terms shrink toward zero quickly enough.

The formula

Start from the sum of n terms, a × (1 − rn) ÷ (1 − r). When the ratio is between −1 and 1, rn shrinks toward 0 as n grows. For example, 0.90950 is only 0.009. Drop it, and you get:

S = a ÷ (1 − r)
afirst termrratio, between −1 and 1
Settles8 + 4 + 2 + 1 + …The ratio is 0.5, so the terms shrink. The sum is 8 ÷ (1 − 0.5) = 16.
Never settles1 + 1.05 + 1.052 + …The ratio is 1.05, so the terms grow. The sum gets bigger forever, and the formula doesn't apply.

Quick check

Which of these can you sum with a ÷ (1 − r)?

Worked example

Back to the share: $5 a year forever, at 10%.

1. First term: next year's $5 in today's money5 ÷ 1.1 = 4.545
2. Ratio1 ÷ 1.1 = 0.909
3. Use the formula4.545 ÷ (1 − 0.909)
Worth today$50

With unrounded numbers, the answer is exactly $5 ÷ 0.10. That shortcut, payment ÷ rate, is the perpetuity formula you'll meet in Time value of money. It also shows why far-off payments matter little: the first 10 years already make up $30.72 of the $50.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Using the formula when the ratio is 1 or more. Then the terms don't shrink and the sum has no limit. The formula would give a meaningless or negative answer.
  • Using the payment as the first term. With $5 as the first term you get $55, which is the value if the first $5 arrives today rather than in a year.
  • Using the rate as the ratio. At 10% the ratio is 1 ÷ 1.1 = 0.909, not 0.10.

Remember

  • An infinite series can settle to a fixed number if its terms shrink fast enough.
  • A geometric series settles when its ratio is between −1 and 1. Then S = a ÷ (1 − r).
  • That's why payments that never stop can still have a price.

Three questions. Try them without scrolling up.

  1. A salary starts at $50,000 and rises by $1,500 every year. What is it in year 8?

    Show answer
    Arithmetic sequence50,000 + (8 − 1) × 1,500 = $60,500

  2. A $20,000 machine loses 20% of its value each year. What is it worth after 3 years?

    Show answer
    Geometric sequenceThe ratio is 0.8. 20,000 × 0.83 = $10,240

  3. Is 100, 90, 81, 72.9, … arithmetic or geometric? What is the next term?

    Show answer
    Geometric sequenceThe differences change (10, 9, 8.1), but each term is 0.9 times the last. It's geometric, and the next term is 72.9 × 0.9 = 65.61.

  4. What is the 10th term of 2, 6, 18, 54, …?

    Show answer
    Geometric sequence2 × 310 − 1 = 2 × 19,683 = 39,366

  5. Add up the first 8 terms of 5, 10, 20, 40, ….

    Show answer
    Summing a geometric series5 × (28 − 1) ÷ (2 − 1) = 5 × 255 = 1,275

  6. You save $2,000 at the end of each year for 10 years, earning 6%. How much do you have right after the last deposit?

    Show answer
    Summing a geometric series2,000 × (1.0610 − 1) ÷ 0.06 = $26,361.59

  7. What does 0.9 + 0.09 + 0.009 + … add up to?

    Show answer
    Infinite seriesThe ratio is 0.1, so 0.9 ÷ (1 − 0.1) = 1.

  8. A share pays $3 at the end of every year, forever. At 12%, what is it worth today?

    Show answer
    Infinite series3 ÷ 0.12 = $25. The long way gives the same: (3 ÷ 1.12) ÷ (1 − 1 ÷ 1.12).

  9. What does 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + … add up to, forever?

    Show answer
    Infinite seriesIt doesn't settle. The ratio is 2, so the terms keep growing. The formula would give 1 ÷ (1 − 2) = −1, which is meaningless here.
