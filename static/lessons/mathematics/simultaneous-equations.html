Solving two equations at once

Some questions have two unknowns, like a price and a quantity. One equation can't pin them down, but two can, and the answer is where their lines cross.

Growth and equations · Lesson 4 of 7 · 5 minutesBefore this: Linear equations and graphs

When do two plans cost the same?

Example, with made-up numbers. Phone plan A costs $20 a month plus $0.10 a minute. Plan B costs $35 a month plus $0.04 a minute. Plan A is cheaper if you barely call, and plan B if you call a lot. At how many minutes do they cost exactly the same?

The idea

Each plan is a straight line: cost = fixed fee + rate × minutes. We want the one point that sits on both lines, the same minutes and the same cost. On a graph, it's where the lines cross:

Below 250 minutes, plan A is cheaper. Above it, plan B is. At 250 minutes both cost $45.

Two unknowns need two equations. To solve them, use substitution: get one unknown on its own in one equation, then put that into the other. Here both equations already say "cost = …", so set them equal:

1. Same cost20 + 0.10m = 35 + 0.04m
2. Gather the minutes on one side0.06m = 15
3. Dividem = 250
4. Put it back into either plan20 + 0.10 × 250 = $45

Always check with the other equation too: 35 + 0.04 × 250 = $45. If both agree, the answer is right.

Quick check

x + y = 10 and x − y = 4. What are x and y?

Worked example: a market price

Example, with made-up numbers. At a price of p dollars, buyers want 1,000 − 50p units and sellers offer 200 + 30p units. The market settles where the two match. What are the price and the quantity?

1. Demand equals supply1,000 − 50p = 200 + 30p
2. Gather the p terms800 = 80p
3. Solve for the pricep = 10
4. Quantity, from either side1,000 − 50 × 10 = 500
Market price and quantity$10 · 500 units

Check with supply: 200 + 30 × 10 = 500. You'll meet this again in Economics as the market's equilibrium.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Stopping at one unknown. Finding m = 250 is half the answer. Put it back in to get the cost.
  • Not checking with the second equation. A small slip in the algebra shows up straight away when the two don't agree.
  • Expecting an answer every time. If two lines rise at the same rate, they're parallel and never cross. Two plans with the same per-minute rate never cost the same.

Remember

  • Two unknowns need two equations. The answer is where their lines cross.
  • Substitution: get one unknown on its own, put it into the other equation, solve, then put the answer back.
  • Check the answer in both equations.

Check yourself

Three questions. Try them without scrolling up.

1. y = 2x + 1 and y = 5x − 8. Where do the lines cross?

2. You split $10,000 between a bond fund paying 4% and a stock fund paying 9%, and want $700 a year in total. How much goes into each?

3. Two plans both charge $0.05 a minute, with monthly fees of $10 and $25. When do they cost the same?
