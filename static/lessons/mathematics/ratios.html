Ratios and proportions

Margins, P/E, debt-to-equity, revenue per employee: most finance metrics are ratios. They let you compare a giant company with a tiny one on equal terms.

Numbers and percentages · Lesson 4 of 5 · 5 minutesBefore this: Percentages and percentage change

Which business is more profitable?

Example, with made-up numbers. Big Co. made a profit of $400 million on sales of $5 billion. Small Co. made $6 million on sales of $50 million. Big Co. made far more money. But which one is better at turning sales into profit?

The idea

A ratio compares two numbers by dividing one by the other. Read the result as "this much A for every 1 of B". Dividing profit by sales tells you how much profit each dollar of sales brings in, whatever the company's size.

Big Co.$400m ÷ $5,000m
8¢ of profit per $1
Small Co.$6m ÷ $50m
12¢ of profit per $1

Each grid is one dollar of sales, split into 100 cents. The gold squares are the profit kept from it. Small Co. keeps more of every dollar.

The same ratio can be written several ways: 6 : 50, 6/50, 3 : 25 once simplified, 0.12 as a decimal, or 12%. Simplifying means dividing both sides by the same number, just like reducing a fraction.

Rates per unit

Ratio = A ÷ B  →  "A per B"

When the two numbers have units, the ratio becomes a rate: price per share, cost per customer, revenue per employee. Rates are the easiest way to scale. Find the value for one unit, then multiply. If 4 shares cost $16, one costs $4, so 10 shares cost $40.

Direct proportionBoth change by the same factor. Double the shares, double the cost: if 4 cost $16, 8 cost $32. The ratio between them stays the same ($4 a share).
Inverse proportionWhen one doubles, the other halves. Split a fixed $12,000 bonus among 4 people and each gets $3,000. Among 8 people, $1,500 each. The product stays the same (people × share = $12,000).

Quick check

3 analysts review 12 reports a day. At the same pace, how many can 5 analysts review?

Worked example

Example, with made-up numbers. Company A has $36 million of revenue and 120 employees. Company B has $90 million and 450 employees. Which one earns more revenue per employee? Divide revenue by employees for each.

1. Company A$36m ÷ 120 = $300,000
2. Company B$90m ÷ 450 = $200,000
3. Compare$300,000 vs $200,000
More revenue per employeeCompany A

Company B is bigger, but each of its employees brings in a third less. Analysts use this ratio to judge how efficiently a company uses its people.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Flipping the ratio. Debt-to-equity is debt ÷ equity. Equity ÷ debt is a different number with a different meaning.
  • Comparing ratios built on different periods. Monthly sales per employee and yearly sales per employee can't be compared directly.
  • Assuming everything scales. Double the sales and costs like ingredients double, but rent usually stays the same. Not every cost is proportional.

Remember

  • A ratio is A ÷ B, read as "A per B". It makes different sizes comparable.
  • To scale, find the value for one unit, then multiply.
  • Direct proportion: both change by the same factor. Inverse: when one doubles, the other halves.
