Rate of change

A straight line rises at the same speed everywhere. A curve doesn't. This lesson shows how to measure how fast a curve is rising at one exact point, which is where calculus begins.

Calculus · Lesson 1 of 6 · 5 minutesBefore this: Linear equations and graphs, Functions

How fast is it growing right now?

Example, with made-up numbers. $1,000 grows at 8% a year. In the first year it gains $80. Between years 9 and 10 it gains $159.92. The growth keeps speeding up. So how fast is the balance growing at the exact moment it reaches year 10?

The idea

For a straight line, the slope tells you how fast it rises: rise ÷ run, the same everywhere. A curve has a different slope at every point. To find it at one point, draw a line through that point and a second point a little further along, then slide the second point closer and closer:

As the second point slides toward year 10, the grey lines turn until they settle on the gold line that just touches the curve. That line's slope is the rate of change at year 10.

The grey lines measure an average rate of change over a stretch of time. The gold line measures the rate at one instant.

Average rate of change

Between two points, the average rate is the change in output divided by the change in input. It's the same rise ÷ run you used for straight lines:

Average rate = (f(b) − f(a)) ÷ (b − a)

The units are "output per input," here dollars per year. Keep them: a rate without units is hard to use.

Quick check

f(x) = x2. What is the average rate of change from x = 1 to x = 3?

Worked example: the rate at one point

The balance is B(t) = 1,000 × 1.08t, and at year 10 it's $2,158.92. Measure the average rate from year 10 over a shorter and shorter gap:

Gap after year 10Average rate, $ a year
1 year$172.71
0.5 years$169.39
0.1 years$166.79
0.01 years$166.22
Shrinking toward 0→ $166.15
Rate of change at year 10$166.15 a year

The answers settle on one number, just as the infinite series in the last lesson settled. That number is the instantaneous rate of change. In the next lesson it gets its proper name: the derivative.

Common mistakes
Three slips beginners make.
  • Forgetting to divide by the change in input. A rise of $8 over 2 years is $4 a year, not $8.
  • Using the average as the rate at a point. On a curve, the average over a long stretch can be far from the rate at any single moment.
  • Dropping the units. "166" means nothing alone. "$166 a year" tells you what's changing and how fast.

Remember

  • Average rate of change = change in output ÷ change in input, in output-per-input units.
  • A straight line has one rate everywhere. A curve's rate changes from point to point.
  • Shrink the gap toward zero and the average rate settles on the rate at one instant.

Check yourself

Three questions. Try them without scrolling up.

1. f(x) = x2. What is the average rate of change from x = 2 to x = 5?

2. What is the rate of change of the line y = 3x + 2?

3. A share price goes from $40 to $46 over 3 months. What is its average rate of change?
