Supply and demand

How buyers and sellers together set a price, and why prices jump when something shifts on either side.

Markets and prices · Lesson 1 of 3 · 6 minutesBefore this: Linear equations and graphs

In plain words

Buyers want more of something when it is cheap. Sellers offer more when the price is high. The market price settles where the amount buyers want matches the amount sellers offer. When something changes how much people want or how much can be made, the price moves.

In 2022, bird flu swept through American poultry farms, and over 44 million egg-laying hens were lost. The average price of a dozen large eggs in US cities:

The main driver was supply: fewer eggs were available while people still wanted roughly as many. That is supply and demand at work. Not everyone saw it as the whole story: in January 2023 the farm group Farm Action asked the US Federal Trade Commission to investigate the largest producers for price gouging. The largest producer, Cal-Maine Foods, pointed to bird flu and higher production costs.

Source: Reuters, via Investing.com.

Demand is how much buyers want at each possible price. At lower prices people buy more, partly because the item is better value against alternatives and partly because their money goes further. Plotted with price up the side, the demand curve slopes down.

Supply is how much sellers offer at each possible price. Higher prices make it worth producing more, and draw in sellers who could not cover their costs before. The supply curve slopes up.

The market price sits where the two curves cross. Above it, sellers offer more than buyers want, and the surplus pushes the price down. Below it, buyers want more than is on offer, and the shortage pushes it up. This crossing point is called equilibrium.

Illustrative market. Losing supply shifts the supply curve left: at every price, less is offered.

Separate a move along a curve from a shift of the whole curve. A change in the price itself moves buyers and sellers along their curves. A change in anything else shifts a curve:

Demand shifts withIncomes, tastes, the prices of substitutes and complements, the number of buyers, and expectations about future prices.
Supply shifts withInput costs, technology, the number of producers, taxes and regulation, and disruptions such as weather or disease.

Bird flu cut the number of laying hens by roughly a tenth, yet egg prices rose by about 150%. Why did the price rise so much more than supply fell?

The same logic prices everything in finance. A share price is where buyers and sellers balance. An interest rate is the price at which the supply of savings meets the demand for loans. A currency's value reflects demand for it against supply. When a price moves, the first question is which side shifted, and why.

Back to eggs. As farmers rebuilt their flocks, supply recovered and the price fell back. By May 2023, a dozen cost $2.67 on average. Nothing about demand had changed much; the supply curve had moved back.

May 2023 price: US Bureau of Labor Statistics, via Fox News.

How professionals use it

Commodity traders
Track harvests, weather, inventories and demand to anticipate shifts in supply or demand before prices react.
Equity analysts
Forecast a company's prices and sales volumes by asking how supply and demand in its market will change.
Company pricing teams
Set prices by estimating how much customers will buy at each level, and what competitors will supply.

In everyday terms

When a price spikes after a disaster, a bad harvest or a sudden craze, ask whether the cause is temporary. Supply shocks often reverse as producers recover.

Prices are signals: a high price tells producers to make more and buyers to use less.

A price is where what buyers want meets what sellers offer.
