401(k)

Advanced

A 401(k) is a workplace retirement savings plan in the United States, named after a section of the tax code, that lets employees save part of their pay with tax advantages, often with contributions added by the employer.

Abandonment Option

Advanced

An abandonment option is a company's ability to stop a project before its planned end, and recover some value by selling assets or cutting losses, if the project performs worse than expected.

Absolute Advantage

Advanced

Absolute advantage is the ability of a country, business or person to produce more of a good or service than others using the same amount of resources.

Absorption Costing

Advanced

Absorption costing is a method of calculating product costs that includes all manufacturing costs, both variable and fixed, in the cost of each unit produced.

Accelerated Depreciation

Intermediate

Accelerated depreciation is a method of depreciation that charges more of an asset's cost in the early years of its life and less in later years, compared with spreading the cost evenly.

Accounting

Beginner

Accounting is the process of recording, organising and reporting a business's financial transactions so that owners, managers, investors, lenders and governments can understand its financial position and performance.

Accounting Equation

Beginner

The accounting equation states that a business's assets equal its liabilities plus its equity. Everything a business owns is financed either by borrowing or by its owners.

Accounting Estimate

Intermediate

An accounting estimate is an approximation of an amount in the financial statements that cannot be measured precisely and depends on judgment about future events, such as bad debts, warranty costs or an asset's useful life.

Accounting Fraud

Advanced

Accounting fraud is the deliberate manipulation of a company's financial statements to mislead investors, lenders, regulators or others about its financial performance or position.

Accounting Policy

Intermediate

An accounting policy is a specific principle, rule or practice a company chooses and applies in preparing its financial statements, such as how it values inventory or recognises revenue.

Accounts Payable

Beginner

Also known as: Trade Payables

Accounts payable is the money a business owes to its suppliers for goods or services it has received but not yet paid for.

Accounts Receivable

Beginner

Also known as: Trade Receivables

Accounts receivable is the money customers owe a business for goods or services they have received but not yet paid for.

Accredited Investor

Advanced

An accredited investor is a person or organisation that meets financial or professional criteria set by regulators, allowing them to invest in certain private offerings not available to the general public. The term is used mainly in the United States.

Accretion/Dilution

Advanced

Accretion/dilution analysis shows whether an acquisition is expected to increase or decrease the acquiring company's earnings per share.

Accrual Accounting

Beginner

Accrual accounting records income when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred, regardless of when cash actually changes hands.

Accrual Quality

Advanced

Accrual quality is the extent to which a company's accrual-based accounting profits are reliably backed by cash flows, rather than by estimates and adjustments that may later prove wrong.

Accrued Expense

Beginner

An accrued expense is a cost a business has already incurred but has not yet paid or been billed for by the end of the reporting period.

Accrued Revenue

Beginner

Accrued revenue is income a business has earned by delivering goods or services but has not yet billed or received by the end of the reporting period.

Accumulated Depreciation

Intermediate

Accumulated depreciation is the total depreciation charged on an asset since it was bought. It is subtracted from the asset's original cost to show its current book value.

Acquirer

Advanced

In card payments, an acquirer is the bank or financial institution that enables a merchant to accept card payments, processing transactions on the merchant's behalf and depositing the funds into its account.

Acquisition

Beginner

An acquisition is a transaction in which one company buys a controlling stake in, or all of, another company or its assets.

Active Investing

Intermediate

Active investing is an investment strategy in which a manager or investor selects investments and adjusts holdings in an attempt to outperform a benchmark index.

Active Share

Advanced

Active share is a measure of how much a fund's holdings differ from its benchmark index, expressed as a percentage from 0%, identical to the index, to 100%, no overlap at all.

Activity-Based Costing

Advanced

Activity-based costing (ABC) is a method of assigning overhead costs to products or services based on the activities they actually use, such as machine setups, inspections or orders processed, rather than a single broad measure such as labour hours.

Actuary

Advanced

An actuary is a professional who uses mathematics, statistics and financial theory to measure and manage risk and uncertainty, especially in insurance, pensions and investments.

Adjustable-Rate Mortgage (ARM)

Intermediate

Also known as: ARM

An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) is a home loan, common in the United States, whose interest rate is fixed for an initial period and then changes at regular intervals in line with a benchmark interest rate.

Adjusted EBITDA

Advanced

Adjusted EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, further adjusted to exclude items that management considers unusual, non-recurring or non-cash, such as restructuring costs and share-based compensation.

Adjusted Gross Income

Intermediate

Adjusted gross income (AGI) is a measure of income used in the United States federal tax system: a person's total gross income minus certain specific deductions, called adjustments.

Adjusting Entry

Beginner

An adjusting entry is a journal entry made at the end of an accounting period to update account balances so that revenue and expenses are recorded in the correct period.

Adverse Opinion

Advanced

An adverse opinion is an audit opinion stating that a company's financial statements are not fairly presented, because misstatements are both material and pervasive.
